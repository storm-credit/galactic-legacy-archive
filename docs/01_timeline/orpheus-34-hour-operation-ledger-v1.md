## 5. Competing Plans at 04:00

### Plan S — State Priority

Allocation:
- A mostly State/medical/command.
- B civilians with controlled exceptions.
- C remaining State + selected civilians.
- Grey local/independent overflow.

Model:
- preserves 85–93% of State-function group.
- moves 1.55–1.95 million civilians.
- produces strongest later war/service capacity.

Risk:
- 650,000–1.05 million civilian/irregular people left exposed.

### Plan C — Civilian Priority

Allocation:
- A medical/critical mixed.
- B/C civilians/refugees.
- State functions fragmented or abandoned.

Model:
- moves 2.15–2.55 million civilians.
- preserves only 35–55% of intact State-function packages.

Risk:
- later identity/payroll/medicine/command collapse could kill more across other regions.

### Plan G — Full Continuity Override

Allocation:
- centralized dynamic manifest optimized by Authority G/Aurel.

Model:
- lowest projected collision/unused-slot loss.
- moves approximately 2.55–2.78 million total in some models.
- preserves strong minimum State function.

Risk:
- overrides local withdrawal, consent and destination choice.
- uses forced separation/reclassification.
- creates permanent central emergency precedent.

### Plan M — Distributed Mixed Plan, selected

Core:
- dismantle State functions into minimum viable packets.
- share A/B/C across civilian and State groups.
- reserve Grey Route for local/independent/late rescue.
- hold formations and institutions accountable to different fronts.

Model before execution:
- 2.45–2.72 million arrivals.
- 180,000–300,000 organized holdout capacity.
- 450,000–800,000 dead/captured/missing/severely exposed.

Risk:
- higher coordination burden than G.
- service packets may fail after arrival.
- defense is fragmented.
- no one actor can correct all fronts in real time.

Rian’s responsibility:
- supports and commands the joint operation but recuses from sole manifest ranking.
- accepts that G may outperform M on immediate total movement.
