## 8. Red-Team Attacks

### Attack 1 — “The registry was created after the work, so the roles are invented retroactively.”

Response:
- many role codes and recurring combinations already exist in historical document headers;
- the registry does formalize some previously implicit or sparsely used codes;
- retrospective consistency is not the same as contemporaneous independent execution;
- therefore the registry may govern future work and classify past work, but cannot manufacture missing independent-session evidence.

Control:
- historical work remains Mode R.

### Attack 2 — “A huge agent list creates an illusion of expertise.”

Response:
- new-agent addition is restricted;
- roles have boundaries and cannot claim human certification;
- proportional review avoids summoning every role for every artifact;
- coverage quality, not role count, is the metric.

### Attack 3 — “The same orchestrator can repeat the same blind spot in every role.”

Response:
- true residual risk;
- red-team, opposing-benefit requirements and explicit alternatives reduce but do not eliminate it;
- Mode I/H/E can be added for high-risk domains later;
- claims of independent consensus remain prohibited.

### Attack 4 — “Working Canon is meaningless while files say REVIEW.”

Response:
- source precedence and final audit already promote integrated domains;
- effective-status manifest now resolves the discrepancy;
- historical headers are retained as process metadata.

### Attack 5 — “The world bible is complete only because deferred work is hidden.”

Response:
- deferred register and coverage matrix explicitly list scene cards, exact values, atlas totality, visuals and human tests;
- completion is defined as no need to invent foundational rules during execution, not encyclopedic enumeration.
