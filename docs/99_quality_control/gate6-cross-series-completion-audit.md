## 3. Major Cross-Series Strengths

### 3.1 Causal closure

The chain does not rely on arbitrary scale escalation:

- Academy rights create external mission capability but no independent logistics.
- the first ship/open standard creates Ardis’s premature viability.
- Ardis creates a rival political/route precedent that destabilizes succession.
- succession pluralism creates incompatible fleet mandates.
- the ownerless fleet’s success and depletion create Second Orpheus.
- Orpheus creates route-denial politics and Blood Admiral responsibility.
- identity decomposition creates current legal/record conflict.
- record war creates reasonable Seed activation.
- Seed benefits create the Preservation Regime.
- defeating the acute operator leaves P-001 holding indispensable central power.
- distribution becomes necessary and dangerous rather than ceremonial.

Verdict: PASS.

### 3.2 Early-asset return

The ending reuses rather than replaces early elements:

- 07 is a civil interoperability/service interface, not a late superweapon.
- Academy charter components scale into ship, city, fleet, route, archive and transition institutions.
- H-001 community routes become a federation and later a denial/accountability system.
- H-002’s maintenance/design rights become technical commons.
- S-001’s provenance work becomes plural current-status/Archive translation.
- M-001’s patient consent/minimum care becomes interregional service architecture.
- the first ship and ownerless fleet remain support/command institutions rather than obsolete collectibles.

Verdict: PASS.

### 3.3 Final centralized answer is credible

The Continuity Seed/Preservation Regime:

- restores identity, family claims, medicine, payroll, routes and military command;
- prevents forged orders, local hostage systems and some mass-casualty events;
- gains genuine public and institutional support;
- remains useful after monopoly ends.

Distribution is chosen because permanent ownership is illegitimate, not because centralization never works.

Verdict: PASS.

### 3.4 Protagonist growth resolves the initial flaw

P-001 begins by using future knowledge to identify valuable people and optimal outcomes.
He progressively gains responsibility over:

- self/cell;
- school/frame;
- ship/community;
- city/node;
- succession/state;
- fleet/theater;
- millions at Orpheus;
- civilizational records;
- central Seed command.

The final growth is relinquishing exclusive future indexing, central command and ownership of the collected legacy while remaining alive to bear consequences.

Verdict: PASS, subject to exact physical/legal sacrifice mechanism.

### 3.5 Independent core characters

- H-001 owns route-federation politics, one genuine D4 denial and specific Blood Admiral responsibility.
- H-002 owns technical standards/worker-user rights, including failures and safety governance.
- S-001 owns provenance/current-status/record plurality and the limits of operational truth.
- M-001 owns medical minimums, triage, consent and service continuity.
- A-001 owns the scalable central answer through human, AI, office and constituency responsibility.

They are not simply P-001’s skills or final cabinet.

Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS; exact personal/relationship endings still open.

## 4. S1 Cross-Series Risks

### S1-01. The 650–750 contraction claim is unsupported

Earlier architecture promised a complete 650–750 episode version.
Current per-grand-act contraction ranges sum to approximately:

- minimum: 846 episodes;
- maximum: 989 episodes;
- nominal: 1100 episodes.

The gap is not solvable by trimming scene length alone.
A true 650–750 version would require:

1. merging or removing whole subacts;
2. combining adjacent grand acts or relocating payoffs;
3. reducing repeated institution-building cycles;
4. consolidating regional theaters and representative cast;
5. moving some mysteries/payoffs into the same causal crisis;
6. accepting fewer separate collection arcs.

Decision:
- preserve 1100 as the complete ceiling architecture.
- relabel 846–989 as the current trim envelope.
- mark 650–750 as an OPEN deep-contraction project, not an accomplished feature.

Severity: S1 because duration was a stated macro property.

### S1-02. Repeated governance rhythm

GA1, GA3, GA5, GA7 and GA10 all build or reform plural authority after crisis.
Their primary questions differ, but scene execution can collapse into:

`crisis → stakeholder meeting → charter clauses → attack/test → provisional institution`.

Controls:

- each act must retain a distinct physical object and failure mode:
  - GA1: school custody and youth rights;
  - GA3: city/node production and citizenship;
  - GA5: armed fleet mission/pay/repair/recall;
  - GA7: destructive route authority and downstream death;
  - GA10: functional handoff under active service dependency.
- no more than one full assembly/hearing per act.
- rules must emerge from mission, repair, closure, casualty or handoff failure.
- deep contraction should merge any subacts that repeat the same rhythm.

### S1-03. Record/identity density in GA7–10

Four late grand acts repeatedly use:

- records;
- identity;
- keys;
- current status;
- institutional authority.

Distinct dramatic modes exist, but execution risk remains:

- GA7: responsibility for infrastructure destruction;
- GA8: archive/personhood/origin and activation;
- GA9: beneficial classification regime and central command;
- GA10: operational authority transfer and reconstruction.

Controls:
- attach every record decision to bodies, routes, machines, services and relationships.
- keep UI/explanation sparse.
- preserve battle, rescue, production, medical and ordinary-life modes.
- prohibit lore-only scenes.

### S1-04. P-001 still appears in every central architecture decision

Autonomous-success requirements exist, but P-001 remains the connective protagonist of all ten grand acts.
Risk:
- other actors’ institutions can feel like tools proving his final philosophy.

Controls:
- at least six grand acts have a decisive victory caused by another actor’s autonomous decision.
- GA5–10 must include fronts P-001 cannot see or correct in real time.
- final institutions act against his preference and continue after his exit.
- one regional/character ending he dislikes remains legitimate.

### S1-05. Exact chronology and resource states are not locked

The architecture has travel-time bands and campaign phases, but not a single dated master chronology.
Without it:

- fleets may recover too fast;
- characters may age/qualify inconsistently;
- political transitions and construction may occur unrealistically;
- Orpheus/denial/Seed sequences may lack required time.

Required before detailed episodes beyond the opening:

- dated series calendar;
- per-GA ship/fleet readiness and losses;
- node/route state table;
- population/food/medical timelines;
- P-001 body/health/Archive state.

### S1-06. Final named losses and individual endings remain open

The architecture requires permanent losses, but several exact answers remain unchosen:

- first ship fate;
- 07 original frame fate;
- E-001 final path;
- A-001 human and AI outcomes;
- important GA5–9 commanders/institutions lost;
- P-001 final health/memory/profession;
- exact H-001 office/accountability;
- romance/family policy.

This does not break macro causality but blocks emotional ending and scene cards.

### S1-07. Twenty mystery payoffs are registered but not scene-locked

Every CORE thread has a final material state, but the exact final reveal/action scenes are not mapped.
Risk:
- explanation clusters in GA8–10;
- payoff occurs in a document rather than character choice;
- repeated reveals steal each other’s impact.

Required:
- one exact episode range and decisive scene/action for M-001–M-020;
- setup, partial payoff, reinterpretation and final material consequence;
- deliberate ambiguity label for remaining unknowns.

### S1-08. The final transition can become too institutionally dense

GA10 distributes seven functions across seven regional states and multiple actors.
Risk:
- ending emotion diluted by constitutional completeness.

Controls:
- foreground 5–7 fronts and 3–5 epilogue places/relationships.
- combine legal structures through visible services and iconic assets.
- P-001 index/command sacrifice and the first handoff region carry the emotional spine.
- not every institution receives a standalone closure chapter.

### S1-09. Ordinary-life continuity may weaken after scale expansion

Early ordinary-life anchors are strong, but later acts can become crisis/institution heavy.
Required in every GA:

- one stable home/work/service place;
- named food, sleep, work, family or care consequence;
- one relationship scene not interrupted immediately by attack;
- one victory/failure shown months later in ordinary use.

### S1-10. Gate 1 style/prose evidence remains missing

No canonical prose should be authorized until:

- legal public Korean webnovel opening/prose reference analysis is completed;
- 1st-person vs close-3rd-person samples are compared;
- mobile paragraph rhythm, dialogue density, technical explanation and battle clarity are tested;
- first-five non-canon scene cards/prose samples are audited.

This is currently the highest direct writing blocker.

## 5. S2 Moderate Risks

### S2-01. Front-stage cast load

The architecture contains many strong representative roles.
Controls:
- only 3–5 active speakers per mini-arc;
- merge roles only when authority/constituency remains credible;
- reuse known actors rather than adding a new spokesperson for every institution.

### S2-02. Scale may reduce personal causality

Controls:
- large outcomes move through named nodes, ships, hospitals, communities and offices;
- P-001 never personally governs all 76+ billion registered people;
- regional divergence remains.

### S2-03. Technical interfaces can feel like keys solving politics

Controls:
- every Authority/Seed key still requires energy, staff, physical access, legal/affected-person standing and local implementation.
- technical access never grants political legitimacy automatically.

### S2-04. Similarity drift

Potential comparison pressure remains from:
- academy/mecha prototype stories;
- fleet political epics;
- collection systems;
- centralized-AI-vs-freedom endings.

Protected differentiators:
- 07’s value is civil interoperability rather than hidden combat power;
- collection is distributed rights/integration rather than ownership;
- protagonist flaw is hero concentration/institutional absence;
- final reward is relinquishment and plural custody;
- antagonist is a useful institution with constituencies.

These must be dramatized clearly by episodes 20, 100 and 210.

### S2-05. Final relationship policy

Long serialization needs a deliberate choice on romance/family/intimacy.
It cannot remain accidentally absent or be added as epilogue reward without prior architecture.
