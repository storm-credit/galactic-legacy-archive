## 1. Verdict

> **PASS FOR WORKING-CANON ATLAS FOUNDATION**

S0 blockers:
- 0.

The atlas converts abstract late-series institutions into inhabited regions with services, routes, resources and political constituencies. Exact local names, cast and episode calendars remain open.

## 2. Attack — Too Many New Places Too Late

Risk:
- GA8 introduces Meridian, Orsen, Ardis, five Palimpsest zones and the Assembly Constellation.
- GA9–10 then adds six/seven regional models.

Controls:
- Meridian, Orsen and Ardis are existing lineages, not late inventions;
- Palimpsest is one region with five internal zones, not five unrelated civilizations;
- GA9 regional models reuse earlier geography and institutions;
- GA10 transition regions are the same places under changed authority, not new locations;
- reader-facing act rotation limits active places to 3–4.

Required future control:
- use a master terminology ledger and map;
- retire site labels when their function is understood;
- introduce Palimpsest through one entry zone before the full region.

Verdict:
- PASS WITH MEMORY CONTROL.

## 3. Attack — Every Region Exists to Demonstrate a Political Model

Risk:
- regions may feel like essays: Core centralization, Ardis pluralism, Taris frontier, Palimpsest refusal.

Controls:
- each region has population, work, medicine, industry, family and route dependencies;
- internal factions and individual opt-in/exit conflicts remain;
- benefits and harms cross ideological lines;
- transition failures are mandatory;
- the same region contains several political preferences.

Required future work:
- named ordinary focal characters, professions, homes, leisure and relationships;
- one local conflict per region unrelated to the central constitutional question;
- show internal class and generational differences.

Verdict:
- CONDITIONAL PASS.

## 4. Attack — Palimpsest Is a Convenient Deep-Lore Dungeon

Risk:
- damaged mirrors and ancient authorities may become a quest zone delivering answers.

Controls:
- 42–78 million current residents;
- mirror economy, medicine and maintenance needs;
- current governments and AI/person communities;
- synchronization harms local services and privacy;
- no single mirror or vault contains full truth;
- Authority F/G requires consent, power, operators and multiple sources.

Required scene constraint:
- each archive operation must include a current local service consequence;
- at least one crucial source refuses or remains isolated;
- expedition actors leave resources and obligations, not only take knowledge.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 5. Attack — Seed Activation Still Looks Like One Final Machine

Risk:
- the Continuity Assembly Constellation could visually and narratively become a master computer.

Controls:
- six separate anchors and service communities;
- five-site inputs;
- physical power, cooling and operator needs;
- no central chamber;
- domain-specific activation and asymmetric confidence;
- local stop rights and refusing regions.

Required visual/story rule:
- never stage the climax as reaching a throne/server and pressing one button;
- show at least three simultaneous local operations and one missing/refused input.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 6. Attack — Preservation Regime Geography Is Too Neat

Risk:
- regions map too cleanly to P1–P5 or pro/anti-regime positions.

Controls:
- classifications are domain-specific;
- regions mix S5 central service and S2 local rights;
- internal populations support different domains;
- Core can be P1 for service and politically constrained;
- frontier residents can opt into identity/medicine while rejecting command;
- refusal zones contain individuals who seek synchronization.

Required future state sheets:
- sample households/institutions with mixed domain statuses;
- movement between regions and changing classification.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 7. Attack — Distributed Ending Is Favored by Region Design

Risk:
- Ardis and Palimpsest look prepared and ethically rich, while Core/provincial regions look dependent or captured.

Controls:
- Core and Orsen have superior service and safety;
- central intervention must prevent real cascades;
- distributed handoffs must cause preventable harm;
- Core populations may rationally choose prolonged synchronization;
- local/provincial/opt-out institutions can coerce and exclude;
- some collapsed regions need external trusteeship longer than protagonists prefer.

Required final-act balance:
- at least one distributed transition fails badly enough that centralization would have saved lives;
- at least one centralized service preserves voluntary support in the epilogue;
- protagonists do not force immediate transfer.

Verdict:
- PASS WITH MANDATORY COST.

## 8. Attack — Population and Military Numbers Are Decorative

Risk:
- broad population ranges and fleet counts might never constrain scenes.

Controls:
- ranges define service and response scale;
- routes and data classes limit movement;
- local fleets cannot occupy all systems;
- support and authentication are central;
- transition metrics require operational effects.

Required next packets:
- macro economy/trade;
- per-act military holdings/readiness;
- named resource and service state sheets.

Verdict:
- FOUNDATION PASS, QUANTITATIVE DETAIL OPEN.

## 9. Attack — Central Key Can Still Affect Everything

Risk:
- Rian may appear able to command 80 billion people through one interface.

Controls:
- central reach is geographically/domain unequal;
- local implementers remain necessary;
- refusal and low-connectivity regions respond partially or not at all;
- central key coordinates selected identity, route, command and service consistency;
- it cannot create resources or operate every local institution.

Required operational rule:
- every central command records propagation time, participating systems and nonresponding regions;
- at least one major local actor uses central order differently from Rian’s intent.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 10. Attack — AI/Composite Communities Are Abstract

Risk:
- personhood issues may remain technical categories without daily life.

Controls:
- Orsen and Quiet Harbor give physical services, embodiment, housing and resource context;
- AI persons do not inherit institutional authority automatically;
- compute/energy, copy and movement rights are specified.

Required later work:
- named AI/composite households or communities;
- work, leisure, intimacy, reproduction/copy and mourning practices;
- disputes between AI persons, institutions and non-person systems.

Verdict:
- PARTIAL PASS.

## 11. Attack — Transition Functions Are Too Bureaucratic

Risk:
- seven functions and six service-state levels can feel like project management rather than drama.

Controls:
- functions map to visible doors, medicine, pay, movement, weapons, records and emergency response;
- regional failures and material services are mandatory;
- reader-facing text need not name every code;
- author-side state tracking can remain technical.

Required scene translation:
- each mini-arc focuses on no more than 2–3 functions;
- state labels remain off-page unless natural to the institution.

Verdict:
- PASS.
