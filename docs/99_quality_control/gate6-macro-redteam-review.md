## 3. S1 Major Risks

### S1-01. GA2–3 may feel like extended setup before imperial war

Problem:
- frontier salvage, open standard, colony governance can appear like side quests between academy and succession war.

Why it matters:
- readers expecting admiral/fleet scale may feel delayed.

Current protection:
- GA2 builds first ship, crew, logistics, industrial network.
- GA3 proves a political/node model needed for final distribution.
- each victory directly triggers imperial response.

Required controls:
1. introduce strategic fleet/node consequences by early GA2 without moving to full war.
2. include one memorable ship-scale confrontation and one settlement siege.
3. show original-timeline event divergence, not only local adventures.
4. carry GA1 rivals/records into both acts.
5. ensure GA3 joint-trust node is visibly used in GA6 and GA10.

Owner: N02 / N07 / M03 / P06

### S1-02. GA4–5 can repeat “assemble coalition against central power”

Problem:
- succession compact and ownerless fleet both involve plural groups negotiating authority.

Required distinction:
- GA4 question: who has legitimate right to rule and authenticate the state?
- GA5 question: how can armed organizations coordinate without one owner?
- GA4 primary tools: law, succession, espionage, records.
- GA5 primary tools: command doctrine, ships, logistics, contracts.

Execution control:
- no extended fleet-building in GA4.
- no repeated heir/court intrigue in GA5.
- different front-stage cast and locations.

### S1-03. Second Orpheus may feel pre-solved by theme

Problem:
- readers know P-001 should choose civilians, reducing suspense.

Required controls:
1. capital/state institutions must contain irreplaceable lives and continuity, not empty symbols.
2. civilian-first option must cause concrete deaths, war expansion, and regional collapse.
3. A-001’s centralized solution must plausibly save more lives in the immediate model.
4. H-001/H-002 infrastructure must be incomplete and contested.
5. P-001 must choose procedure/authority sharing, not simply “people over state.”

Success condition:
- reasonable readers can disagree with the choice while understanding it.

### S1-04. GA7 could turn H-001 into a repeated moral test rather than protagonist

Problem:
- H-001 risks existing mainly to ask whether he becomes Blood Admiral.

Required controls:
- give H-001 separate constituency, elections/succession, internal rivals, victories, mistakes, family/community change.
- one major GA7 operation must occur outside P-001 viewpoint/control.
- H-001’s political end state must not depend on P-001 forgiving/recognizing him.
- real harmful choice remains his responsibility.

Owner: C02 / N08 / P08

### S1-05. GA8 Archive exposition could overwhelm character and action

Problem:
- civilization records, plural histories, AI law, Seed origin are concept-heavy.

Required controls:
1. every lore reveal must solve or worsen an immediate ownership, rescue, route, or personhood conflict.
2. use 2–3 living/AI representatives, not encyclopedia tours.
3. P-001 is not the sole interpreter; H-002, law/memory specialists, affected communities own conclusions.
4. distribute origin clues across GA1–7; GA8 confirms and connects rather than introduces.
5. keep one physical expedition clock and one faction race.

Drop condition:
- if a chapter can be removed without changing a character’s choice or resource state, the exposition is cut or relocated.

### S1-06. GA9–10 decentralization can become a predictable moral sermon

Problem:
- “centralization bad, decentralization good” is simplistic and contemporary-jargon heavy.

Required controls:
- Seed stability visibly improves ordinary life.
- some sympathetic heroes choose preservation through the ending.
- distributed transition fails in several regions.
- local elites can abuse autonomy.
- minimum-service and burden-sharing obligations limit pure veto.
- P-001 cannot impose decentralization universally; opt-in/delayed regions remain.
- continuity institutions retain bounded roles.

Final criterion:
- distribution is chosen because no actor may own everyone’s future, not because it is operationally superior in every case.

### S1-07. A-001 appears too late

Problem:
- formal antagonist emerges around GA4, but ending relies heavily on their answer.

Required controls:
- continuity terms/protocols in GA1.
- practical central coordination benefits in GA2–3 through subordinate offices/AI tools.
- at least one sympathetic continuity-aligned representative before A-001.
- A-001’s first appearance should resolve a real crisis, not deliver philosophy.
- do not retroactively make A-001 responsible for earlier villains.

### S1-08. P-001 may still dominate every grand-act climax

Problem:
- even with independent arcs, series architecture names P-001 as decision-maker repeatedly.

Required climax ownership:

- GA1: H-001/H-002/Neutral/student assembly make charter possible.
- GA2: H-002’s standard/community choice determines outcome.
- GA3: local council/H-001 governance determines node legitimacy.
- GA4: claimant/observer/assembly votes and evidence, not P-001 decree.
- GA5: subordinate captain wins decisive front under distributed command.
- GA6: H-001 civilian network executes evacuation.
- GA7: H-001 owns final doctrinal choice.
- GA8: AI/civilization representative controls access/consent.
- GA9: P-001 may win military control—the deliberate peak of monopoly.
- GA10: multiple fronts succeed/fail without P-001 intervention.

Gate rule:
- at least 6 of 10 grand acts must have decisive success caused by another actor’s autonomous choice.

### S1-09. Collection fun may diminish after GA5

Problem:
- later collection categories—authority, records, institutions—can feel abstract compared with mecha/heroes.

Required controls:
- retain tangible ships, tools, keys, AI embodiments, maps, cultural objects, and restored spaces.
- political/institutional gains need visible before/after experiences.
- reuse and transform early equipment.
- later acts still include selective signature machine/ship/relic discoveries without power creep.
- each abstract collection must give an operational choice within 10 episodes.

### S1-10. 1100-episode map may encourage bloat despite contraction plan

Required controls:
- episode counts are ceilings, not quotas.
- each subact must pass goal/conflict/change/payoff test.
- no grand act expands because “more characters/items would be fun” without causal need.
- maintain contractible map in parallel.
- after first 100, design next 100–150 only, while later acts remain coarser until dependencies mature.
- quarterly equivalent review: remove/merge any thread without ending contribution.

## 4. S2 Moderate Risks

### S2-01. Too many governance models

Academy charter, joint-trust node, ownerless fleet, route federation, plural Archive can blur.

Control:
- each answers a different resource/authority problem and has distinct failure mode.
- maintain comparison table in political bible.

### S2-02. Repeated legal/contract solutions

Control:
- law must be backed by physical control, funding, witnesses, legitimacy, and enforcement.
- some conflicts cannot be litigated and require war/exit.

### S2-03. Physical geography absent

Need:
- galaxy/node schematic and travel-time classes before detailed GA2–6.
- otherwise faction movement and Orpheus choice may be arbitrary.

### S2-04. Force/economic scale absent

Need:
- ship classes and fleet sizes by act.
- node populations, self-sufficiency, production, travel/repair costs.
- prevents late unlimited resources.

### S2-05. Ordinary-life stakes may disappear at scale

Control:
- recurring community/family/crew/local viewpoints.
- every GA has home/base phase and ordinary service consequence.
- macro decisions return to named people without using them only as victims.

### S2-06. Romance/family policy undecided

Not a blocker now, but long serialization needs deliberate decision:
- no romance, slow-burn, multiple relationships, or family formation.
- must not emerge accidentally after hundreds of episodes.

### S2-07. Cultural variety not designed

Need:
- factions/regions cannot differ only by political system and hardware color.
- language, ritual, labor, food, family, education, death/memory practices.

## 6. Ending Stress Test

### Scenario A — P-001 keeps central key temporarily

Risk:
- “temporary benevolent dictatorship” silently becomes endorsed.

Required consequence:
- even brief use changes institutions, dependencies, and trust.
- delayed relinquishment costs lives/rights or empowers hardliners.

### Scenario B — immediate distribution

Risk:
- technical/political implausibility and preventable deaths.

Required consequence:
- staged transition, regional choices, bounded emergency authority, failures.

### Scenario C — Archive destruction

Risk:
- simple heroic reset.

Required portrayal:
- destruction is a credible option supported by H-001 radicals and some communities, with catastrophic route/memory cost.

### Scenario D — distribution becomes new single constitution

Risk:
- central blueprint reappears under new name.

Control:
- plural implementations with shared minimum protocols, not one total institutional form.

Recommended final:
- staged distribution under war pressure, with some regions remaining temporarily centralized and future disputes unresolved.

## 9. Similarity Audit

Potential comparisons:
- Gundam: academy/youth pilots, imperial politics, prototype lineage.
- Pick Me Up / collection games: hero roster, missions, permanent costs.
- Legend of Galactic Heroes: rival commanders, empire, history/politics.
- XCOM/Battletech: roster/base/fleet/maintenance.
- common SF AI ending: centralized machine order vs freedom.

Current differentiators:
- maneuver frames as capture/control infrastructure platform.
- collection as distributed rights and integration, not summons/inventory.
- Archive as plural historical continuity system.
- former admiral’s flaw is hero concentration and institutional failure.
- first machine’s value is interoperability, not hidden firepower.
- final collection payoff is relinquishment/distribution.

Requirement:
- these differentiators must be dramatized by episode 20/100, not remain design notes.
