## 0. Audit question

The current species proposal has good **functional differentiation**. This audit asks a different question:

> Will recurring nonhuman characters still read first as people, or will the portfolio feel like seven mechanically optimized gameplay classes / encyclopedia specimens?

Two failure modes are tested:

1. **visual-cognitive overload** — too many strongly nonhuman recurring body plans create recognition and staging cost;
2. **functional-design bias** — every biological difference conveniently maps to ship, engineering, medical or military utility.

Neither problem is solved by making all species humanoid. The target is controlled diversity.

---

# 1. Reader-facing silhouette bands

These are visual/readability bands, not biological worth or canon taxonomy.

## V0 — Near-human readable

Characteristics:
- roughly familiar upright/shared-space silhouette;
- face/gesture/body language can be staged with low prose overhead;
- biology may still differ strongly internally;
- ordinary clothes, rooms and mixed group blocking remain easy to visualize.

Use:
- emotional anchor;
- frequent recurring cast;
- mixed dialogue scenes.

Risk:
- cosmetic forehead-alien problem if material consequences are weak.

## V1 — Moderate divergence

Characteristics:
- clearly nonhuman body plan;
- still easy to position in rooms, ships and conversations after one introduction;
- one or two unusual locomotion/manipulation facts matter repeatedly.

Use:
- recurring supporting/major characters;
- infrastructure difference visible without re-explaining every scene.

## V2 — Strong divergence

Characteristics:
- body plan, environment or communication substantially changes scene blocking;
- reader needs explicit spatial/service cues;
- ordinary mixed-space presence creates recurring material consequences.

Use:
- highly memorable characters/communities;
- better in controlled number per active subact.

Rule:
- V2 is not “better alien design.” Too many simultaneous V2 actors make every ordinary scene pay an exposition tax.

# 3. Four portfolio directions

| Direction | Shape | Strength | Failure risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Maximum Xenobiology | preserve every slot at most visually alien interpretation | spectacular codex | ordinary scenes become constant staging/explanation | REJECT as default |
| B — Balanced Distance | humanity anchor + roughly 2 near-human-readable nonhuman slots + 3 moderate + 2 strong | diversity + attachment + illustration clarity | needs deliberate visual restraint | **RECOMMENDED** |
| C — Humanoid Majority | most S1 V0/V1, reserve strong xenobiology for S2/S3 | easiest serialization | undermines genuine species breadth | HOLD |
| D — Function First, Visual Later | keep functional slots but leave silhouette band unresolved until actual front-stage character need | minimizes premature lock-in | can postpone useful concept-art cohesion | STRONG SUPPORTING RULE |

### Recommended working combination

> **B + D.**

Keep the functional portfolio, but do not interpret every unusual biology in its most visually complex possible form before actual recurring-character use.

This is a proposal-level reader-control rule, not a request to redesign the species now.

# 4. Portfolio-level exposure ceiling

Recommended NONCANON reader rule:

- one active 5-episode window: **new S1 name <= 1** by default;
- one active subact: no more than **2 strongly divergent V2 species** should require repeated spatial/environment explanation at the same time;
- additional already-known species may be present if no new explanatory burden is introduced;
- do not introduce a new body plan, homeworld, faction, religion and specialist machine together.

This is consistent with the existing GA exposure ladder and does not change any scene card.

---

# 5. Functional-design bias audit

Current slots were intentionally derived from consequential world needs. That solved cosmetic aliens but creates the opposite risk:

> every species starts to look as if a game designer optimized it for a shipboard job.

Examples of risky shorthand:
- S1-02 → heavy work / bracing;
- S1-03 → hull sensing;
- S1-04 → fluid/pressure environments;
- S1-05 → chemical/medical sensing;
- S1-07 → electrosense / maintenance;
- S1-06 → long institutional memory.

These are acceptable **biological consequences**, but cannot become each species' primary narrative identity.

# 6. New anti-RPG-species gate

Before any S1 is `READY FOR AUTHOR CANON DECISION`, require all four:

## G-R1 — Non-advantaged profession

At least one recurring/named role must work in a field where their signature biology gives **no meaningful professional advantage**.

Examples:
- high-g species teacher, musician, claims clerk;
- low-g mechanosensory cook, lawyer, parent;
- electrosensory species historian, comedian, mediator.

Do not make the counterexample a joke character whose only function is “surprising job.”

## G-R2 — Non-physiological social conflict

At least one meaningful internal disagreement must be driven by:
- class;
- generation;
- region;
- religion/ethics;
- family;
- labor politics;
- historical memory;
- ideology;

and **not** be reducible to body/environment needs.

## G-R3 — Non-utilitarian culture

At least one memorable aesthetic/cultural practice must have no direct combat, sensor, engineering, survival or economic optimization function.

Acceptable categories:
- humor;
- fashion;
- music;
- courtship/social ritual;
- children’s play;
- festival;
- cuisine preference beyond nutritional necessity;
- decorative art;
- hobby;
- storytelling form.

Rule:
- not every cultural feature needs a plot payoff. One or two ordinary details may exist because people live lives.

## G-R4 — Biology cannot solve its own first plot

The first front-stage species character must not resolve the scene because their species trait is exactly the missing tool.

Their biology may:
- create a cost;
- change a constraint;
- require accommodation;
- complicate a plan.

It should not arrive as a bespoke solution key.

This strongly supports the current E122–130 S1-03 caregiver proposal: their physiology adds a ship-life constraint rather than solving the rescue.

# 7. Visual-softening without biological erasure

The portfolio can reduce reader load without making everyone humanoid.

## S1-02

Keep four-limb identity but use a clean locomotion silhouette:
- two anterior manipulators;
- two posterior supports;
- compact torso.

Do not add extra ornamental appendages merely for alienness.

## S1-03

Do not add more sensory organs/limbs beyond the already unusual 3+2 plan.

If front-stage visualization proves too busy, the issue should be solved through:
- clear resting posture;
- consistent gait grammar;
- clothing/harness silhouette;
not by adding more traits.

## S1-04

Fluid dependence already supplies strong alienness. Keep facial/communication staging readable enough that every conversation does not require wet-module exposition.

## S1-05

Strong candidate for a **V0/V1 visual bridge**:
- host body may be readily readable in mixed public space;
- alien identity comes from developmental symbiosis, medicine and life history rather than maximum external complexity.

This is not a final body-plan decision.

## S1-06

Radial five-limb body is already a strong visual hook. Avoid extra exceptional senses or exotic atmosphere unless ecology truly requires them.

## S1-07

After removal of thermal-cycle overstack, keep electrosense + anchoring as the core. Do not add visible electrical organs, glow effects or machine-like anatomy unless separately justified.

## S1-08

Do not use its unresolved anatomy to “fix the portfolio ratio” mechanically.

Ontology decision comes first:
- independent engineered nonhuman;
- or human-derived clade.

Then visual design follows that history.

# 8. Character-attachment gate

For each S1 before promotion, identify four role slots as existing schema requires, but add one more check:

> If the species label is removed from the character summary, does the character still have a personal want, contradiction and relationship problem?

PASS requires YES.

A character whose complete pitch is:
- “the Braxi engineer,”
- “the aquatic medic,”
- “the electrosense scout,”
- “the ancient long-lived witness”

is not yet a character.

---

# 9. Illustration / mecha production consequence

A recurring S1 character should be drawable and stageable consistently.

Before locking a V2 body plan:
- front/side resting silhouette should be understandable;
- seated/restraint posture should be possible;
- ordinary clothing needs a stable visual grammar;
- speech/gesture focus must be visually locatable;
- cockpit/vehicle adaptation should not require a completely new chassis unless the existing equipment gate independently proves it.

Do not let concept-art novelty force technology canon.

---

# 10. Reader-memory test packet for later

When final names and first characters exist, test a small packet rather than the whole codex.

Suggested sample:
- one human/posthuman character;
- one V0/V1 nonhuman;
- one V1 nonhuman;
- one V2 nonhuman;
- one ordinary shared-space scene;
- one work/conflict scene.

Ask readers after delay:
- which person wanted what?
- which physical need mattered?
- which faction/job did they belong to?
- which fact was biology versus culture?
- could they distinguish names without a glossary?

Failure signal:
- reader remembers only “water alien / four-leg alien / electric alien.”

---

# 11. Interaction with current S1 portfolio decision

This audit does **not** reject the seven functional slots.

It changes the promotion question from:

> “Are seven biological concepts different enough?”

to:

> “Are seven recurring peoples different enough **without all being mechanical role packages or high-load silhouettes**?”

Therefore Decision C in [[species-world-expansion-author-decision-gate-v2]] remains HOLD, but future author review should evaluate **portfolio feel**, not only individual species merit.
