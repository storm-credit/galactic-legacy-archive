## 0. Audit question

The prototype program should not force new exposition scenes into a 1,100-episode architecture that is already complete.

Question:

> **Can the reader learn the conceptual grammar of the old demonstrator era through events that already exist, so that GA8 can reconstruct the history without feeling like a new late retcon?**

Target:
- no new episode;
- no new battle;
- no changed outcome;
- no early 01–20 checklist;
- no “secret prototype exposition dump.”

A useful reveal opportunity is one where an existing event already demonstrates the same principle.

# 1. Reveal ladder

## RL0 — anomaly only

Reader understands:
- 07 does not fit one clean modern category.

Does not know:
- wider program;
- slot numbers;
- origin history.

## RL1 — lineage is distributed

Reader understands:
- a technical identity can survive through components, service routines, copied credentials, labor and adapters without one intact original object.

Does not know:
- numbered program.

## RL2 — success includes governance and failure records

Reader understands:
- a standard can improve because an accident exposed limits;
- worker stop rights, training, certification and incident records are part of technology.

Does not know:
- that old demonstrator failures produced similar historical rules.

## RL3 — old authority is function-bounded

Reader understands:
- ancient/legacy keys do not equal sovereignty;
- service, identity, military and political authority can diverge.

Still no numbered-set reveal required.

## RL4 — historical program reconstructed

Reader learns:
- expansion-era crisis;
- multiple contractors/workstreams;
- numbered experimental authorization;
- failures/partial successes;
- wartime militarization;
- later standardization/reclassification.

Preferred window:
- **GA8**, after the reader has lived through RL0–RL3.

## RL5 — exact relevant slot facts only

Reader may learn a small number of P-slot outcomes because a current claim depends on them.

Rule:
- never convert RL5 into “complete all twenty entries now.”

- software/controller state and physical state can disagree;

  - medical limits;
  - EW safety law;
  - autonomy responsibility rules;

without becoming a successful production machine.

# 6. GA3 — production and labor show what ancestry becomes

GA3 does not need an ancient-history reveal.

Its existing production/labor architecture already shows that machine success requires:
- operators;
- worker stop authority;
- scheduler/authentication state;
- local jigs;
- certification;
- replacement supply;
- current user/service acceptance;
- payment and care.

Examples in E241–247:
- a certified freeze can save a line without granting full management control;
- worker/local knowledge and certified diagnostics each solve different parts of one failure;
- different labor blocs cannot run the whole line alone;
- a local provisional seal can be better **now** while a later certified replacement remains necessary;
- copied old management tokens plus local bypasses and stale state can combine into a safety incident.

Prototype relevance:
- prepares the reader for **modern lineages as institutional recombinations**, not hereditary bloodlines of machines.

If/when a first mass open-standard derivative becomes reader-facing later in GA3:
- its significance should be industrial repeatability and governance;
- not “we rebuilt legendary Prototype 07.”

### Ruling

> **GA3 should remain a consequence/reproduction layer, not historical exposition.**

# 9. GA8 — natural RL4 reconstruction windows already exist

GA8 is the first act structurally suited for deep reconstruction because the existing story is already about:
- competing archive versions;
- current versus historical personhood;
- provenance;
- translation modes;
- founding histories;
- old classifications producing present claims.

## E801–810 — provenance method

Existing Serrat arc establishes:
- authenticated records can conflict;
- no one count/classification automatically defines current persons;
- one historical term can translate into owner/trustee/custodian differently;
- translation purpose changes political subjects;
- old configuration packages can still damage current links.

Prototype relevance:
- teaches the exact epistemic discipline needed to reconstruct the old program without one master record.

## E826–835 — four authentic histories, all incomplete

Existing structure:
- Imperial, Independence, Helix and Neutral histories all contain authentic contributions and consequential omissions;
- current rights are adjudicated from present people/services plus historical provenance;
- no founding bundle becomes a master truth/title key.

Prototype relevance:
- ideal methodological template for the 01–20 history:
  - military archive may call one slot a weapon;
  - contractor archive may call it a licensed product;
  - worker/service record may call it an intervention system;
  - later Imperial register may collapse the distinctions.

### Best insertion principle if later approved

Do **not** create a separate history lecture episode.

Instead a current GA8 claim should depend on one old program fact, for example:
- whether a current service interface descends from an O2 public subsystem or a proprietary O3 chassis;
- whether one current authority claim comes from an emergency test license that was never sovereign;
- whether a modern contractor owns a design whose old article was jointly/publicly funded;
- whether a famous “failed prototype” actually produced the safety rule now excluding someone.

Then the program history is learned because the current dispute needs it.

### Ruling

> **GA8 is the natural RL4/RL5 window. Existing archival machinery is already sufficient; exact insertion remains AUTHOR HOLD.**
