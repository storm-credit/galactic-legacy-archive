## 2. S1 Major Risks

### S1-01. Succession law can become unreadable

Problem:
- seven protocols, six political solutions and multiple institutions can overload the reader.

Controls:
- foreground four personal claimants only.
- introduce each protocol through an order that changes pay, movement, protection, ceremony or command.
- never explain all protocols in one scene.
- reader-facing summary remains: crown, civil executive, military, authentication and provinces.
- Founding Covenant and Provincial Compact operate mainly as coalition alternatives.

### S1-02. There may be no emotionally clear antagonist

Problem:
- all claimants are legitimate and useful; conflict may feel abstract.

Control:
- create concrete institutional antagonism through Purge/Archive hardliners, coercive advisers and monopoly attempts without making them sole cause.
- each claimant must make one personal decision with named victims/beneficiaries.
- P-001 relationships with Protector/Custodian/Heir/Regent carry emotional mirrors.
- one claimant or major supporter rejects compact and becomes persistent GA5 opponent.

### S1-03. P-001 can still look like the decisive kingmaker

Required controls:
- Ardis responds before P-001 arrives.
- multiple-legitimacy finding authored by courts/records/witnesses, not his Archive.
- claimants make decisive compact choices independently.
- provinces/institutions must meet thresholds without his endorsement.
- P-001 refuses public deterministic endorsement and receives no crown/regency office.
- at least one compact clause he opposes is adopted and works.

### S1-04. Legal mystery may substitute documents for drama

Controls:
- evidence moves through couriers, hospitals, Registry, archive, military logs and living witnesses.
- every evidence item has a custodian and human risk.
- one authentic document causes harmful action before its scope is understood.
- public leaks and missing context alter crowds, fleets and services.
- document analysis follows physical/administrative consequence.

### S1-05. Regent and Custodian may overlap as bureaucratic centralizers

Distinction:
- Regent asks what services/administration must execute now.
- Custodian asks which identities/orders can be trusted and what must be suspended.
- Regent uses budgets, appointments and ministries.
- Custodian uses authentication, routes, records and veto.
- their alliance can be efficient and dangerous; their conflict must appear in at least two concrete cases.

### S1-06. Heir may become decorative or obviously weakest

Controls:
- give real public/provincial network and meaningful policy/mediation experience.
- partial accession materially reduces panic and improves compliance in some places.
- Heir makes at least one difficult protective decision and one coercive/pressuring decision.
- ceremonial legitimacy remains necessary in final compact rather than mere sentiment.

### S1-07. Protector may become standard “good general turns dictator”

Controls:
- military unity genuinely prevents mass casualties.
- Protector rejects at least one coup/purge and protects civil institutions.
- conflict arises from expanding emergency mandate through valid risk, not sudden ambition.
- P-001 agrees tactically and may owe lives to Protector’s command.
- even if Protector rejects compact, officers/forces split rather than all becoming villains.

### S1-08. Continuity Custodian can reveal final antagonist too early or too completely

Controls:
- GA4 establishes office benefits, methods and danger—not full Seed origin or final regime.
- human–AI pair can disagree.
- office accepts bounded role at GA4 end, making later expansion plausible.
- no retroactive responsibility for all earlier events.
- Archive origin remains incomplete until GA8.

### S1-09. Public-service middle act may repeat GA3 city management

Distinction:
- GA3 allocates scarce physical city services and local citizenship.
- GA4 resolves which national authority may issue payments, appointments and contracts across billions/provinces.
- use authentication, institutional compliance and political legitimacy rather than local ration planning.
- core-team roles must scale through institutions, not personally allocate all resources.

### S1-10. The final compact can feel like a neat technocratic solution

Controls:
- compact fails to gain universal recognition.
- military/fleet and provincial interpretation remains fragmented.
- emergency offices can renew/expand and institutions can collude.
- one claimant/constituency sees compact as theft and remains sympathetic.
- services improve but command slows and civil war still begins.
- exact sunset/removal enforcement is uncertain.

### S1-11. GA4 lacks enough mecha/fleet action

Required action envelope:
- E338–345 courier/boarding pursuit.
- E368–375 public ceremony security and disruption.
- E391–397 Intelligence transfer conflict.
- E398–404 military deconfliction/limited fleet movement.
- E405–412 route/anchor suspension operations.
- E428–434 accession/key-transfer confrontation.
- E435–443 distributed institutional activation under sabotage/refusal.

Control:
- 07 serves capture, transport and infrastructure; no royal superweapon.
- first ship is courier/witness carrier, not flagship.
- large fleets remain political deadlines until GA5.

### S1-12. Core geography can turn into location tourism

Controls:
- each major site embodies a distinct authority/resource.
- no site visit without changed evidence, service, command or alliance.
- active sites per act limited to 3–5.
- travel/communication clocks remain fixed.

### S1-13. 120 episodes may be bloated

Contractible GA4:
- 90–105 episodes.

Likely merges:
- 4A-3 + 4A-4 if claimant introductions/hearing overlap.
- 4B-3 + 4B-4 if provincial and Regent service politics repeat.
- 4C-1 + 4C-2 if Intelligence and military emergency can interlock.

Rule:
- episode counts are ceilings.
- every mini-arc changes public service, evidence custody, institutional alignment, force command or compact state.

## 3. S2 Moderate Risks

### S2-01. Sovereign death ambiguity may drift into cheap mystery

Control:
- death/irreversible incapacity itself is strongly evidenced.
- dispute centers on legal timing, capacity and document scope.
- no late reveal that sovereign secretly lived and invalidates the arc.

### S2-02. Too many seals and authentication terms

Control:
- project tracks technical details; prose uses plain consequence.
- one or two active verification questions per scene.

### S2-03. Intelligence blocs can seem omnipotent

Control:
- agency depends on institutions, routes, field offices and human cooperation.
- blocs possess incomplete information and contradict one another.
- witnesses/Neutral/provinces/local records can resist.

### S2-04. Founding Covenant can feel irrelevant

Control:
- give it one important exposure/limit on emergency power.
- do not expand into equal personal claimant unless scene testing proves necessary.

### S2-05. Provincial Compact can look automatically democratic

Control:
- powerful provinces bargain selfishly.
- weak regions can be abandoned.
- compact includes minimum obligations and anti-coercion requirements.

### S2-06. Archive/future evidence can overpower legal evidence

Control:
- P-001’s future records are contradictory and curated.
- he states source/confidence and cannot submit them as decisive public proof.
- living records and institutions settle current scope.

## 5. Collection Audit

Strengths:
- authentic protocols have scope/condition/custodian.
- no true-ruler item or full evidence set.
- compact is operational but temporary.
- ownerless-fleet problem emerges causally.

Reader-facing consolidation:
- five tracks: claims, services, military, authentication and provincial acceptance.
- evidence/document names grouped into hearings/cases rather than inventory spam.

Cut any target that:
- makes no operational difference.
- repeats another claimant’s authority.
- has no custodian/risk.
- does not contribute to compact or GA5.
