## 0. Question

GA9 intentionally gives Rian his strongest central command authority.

The design passes authority separation.
The remaining danger is **prose-level credit absorption**:

> a manuscript can preserve every legal limit on paper while emotionally narrating every success as “Rian saved everyone and others correctly followed him.”

This audit identifies what the eventual prose must preserve.

---

## 1. Existing design verdict

GA9 design already contains strong ensemble safeguards:

- central command is materially better in several named operations;
- Rian receives military/mission composition, not all five authority fields;
- local physical/medical/ship/receiving stops remain outside his unilateral control;
- affected/service delegates can pause or condition his priorities;
- outsiders can cooperate without accession;
- AI/community/current-person actors retain separate identity choices;
- one enclave node exits military command but refuses coalition membership;
- Aven remains a technically serious independent actor;
- enclave services retain real benefit;
- defeated ships do not become prize property;
- command-linked deaths and harms remain attributed in the ledger.

Structural verdict:
> **PASS.**

Prose-risk verdict:
> **HIGH ATTENTION REQUIRED.**

# 2. Five scenes/effects that must not be reframed around Rian

## P-01 E983 autonomous node exit

Card order:
1. node decides to suspend enclave military links;
2. node retains local service/physical authority;
3. node accepts neutral observation/deconfliction;
4. node refuses coalition membership;
5. Rian recognizes the protected autonomous status and replans.

Prose danger:
> “Rian granted the node independence.”

Wrong.

Required emotional ownership:
- the node chooses;
- the node bears risk;
- Rian's good decision is **not to seize or punish the choice**.

---

## P-02 E985 plural identity choices

Actors:
- Lumen;
- Orison;
- nonstandard clinics;
- mobile settlements.

They choose:
- which proofs to accept;
- which military identity fusion to reject;
- which safety inspection to accept;
- which parallel lane to use.

Prose danger:
> “Rian gave them the right to remain themselves.”

Wrong.

Correct:
- they already possess current agency;
- Rian's command system is forced to operate around that agency.

---

## P-03 E988 affected/local stop

Card fact:
- central evacuation priority is paused by service/affected authority;
- local engineers confirm current risk;
- Rian replans;
- assault loses a pursuit window.

Prose danger:
> make Rian instantly see that the stop is correct, turning the stop into proof of his wisdom.

Better:
- he may initially dislike the loss;
- the local actors own the evidence and decision;
- he adapts because the authority is real, not because he graciously agrees.

---

## P-04 E990 Aven acts against military seizure

Card fact:
- military commanders fuse service credentials to fleet compliance;
- Aven refuses broad shutdown;
- Aven releases provenance evidence;
- local operators create service forks;
- Rian authenticates protected forks.

Prose danger:
> convert Aven's action into “Aven finally understood Rian was right.”

Wrong.

Aven's motive:
- protect the continuity/service architecture from military misuse;
- preserve the part of his own project he still believes is valuable.

He can oppose military seizure **without joining Rian's ideology**.

---

## P-05 E996 defeated fleet/title separation

Card fact:
- surrendered/grounded hulls remain subject to crew/title/mission/local-defense review;
- outsiders remain independently owned/commanded;
- no prize-fleet merger.

Prose danger:
> victory montage visually presents all surviving ships behind Rian as “his fleet.”

Wrong.

Required visual/textual control:
- different transponders;
- separate command acknowledgments;
- ships departing to local custody;
- crews refusing new missions;
- repair yards receiving hulls under different titles.

The military victory must look **less owned** after the shooting stops, not more.

# 3. Rian's actual heroism in GA9

Avoiding credit absorption does not mean weakening the protagonist.

Rian's earned strengths should remain large and explicit:
- unmatched large-scale command composition;
- four-minute multi-front convergence;
- logistics synchronization;
- ability to replan around valid local stops;
- selective targeting that protects service infrastructure;
- willingness to sacrifice tactical pursuit for civilian continuity;
- ability to use the defeated system without converting it into conquest;
- refusal of permanent office despite genuine current dependence.

The distinction is:

> **Rian is exceptional at coordinating independent actors. He does not become the author of their independent choices.**

That is a stronger admiral fantasy than universal obedience.

# 4. Prose checks for every major central-command scene

Before a GA9 command scene passes revision, ask:

### C-1 Who supplied the fact?
If the answer is always Rian, fail.

### C-2 Who had legal/current stop authority?
Name the actor/domain.

### C-3 Who disagreed with Rian?
At least some disagreements must survive as reasonable even after success.

### C-4 What did another actor decide before Rian reacted?
If none, check satellite-cast regression.

### C-5 What did Rian not know?
Central map ≠ physical omniscience.

### C-6 What success happened without his order?
Local repair, surrender, service fork, protected exit, care choice, outsider deconfliction, etc.

### C-7 What cost is attributable to his order?
Do not narratively dissolve the 620 command-linked deaths / 2,540 irreversible harms into “war is costly.”

### C-8 What remains outside his ownership after victory?
People, hulls, routes, records, offices, identities.

---

# 5. Scene-ending distribution

Do not let every multi-front scene end on:
- Rian's realization;
- Rian's order;
- Rian's approval;
- Rian's reaction.

Some scene/episode closures should land on:
- local refusal;
- operator stop;
- a ship choosing surrender;
- an outsider refusing accession;
- Aven releasing evidence;
- a family receiving a loss notice;
- a service node preserving itself under another mandate.

This is not a quota.
It preserves causal ownership.

---

# 6. Offscreen state progression during central command

Even while Rian is fighting, author-side clocks should continue for:

1. enclave local governments;
2. service operators;
3. affected/current-standing communities;
4. outsider corridors;
5. surrendered/grounded crews;
6. repair yards and medical networks;
7. Aven's technical staff;
8. coalition bodies debating extension/expiry.

At least some later scenes should encounter changed states that Rian did not personally cause.

Examples already compatible with cards:
- one node exits;
- service fork survives;
- fleet crew chooses ground/surrender status;
- outside route refuses integration;
- review body narrows continuation.

Do not invent new outcomes beyond cards merely to satisfy this audit.

---

# 7. Multi-species extension control

If species expansion is later canonized, GA9 becomes a powerful payoff zone for nonstandard bodies/services.

But do not make Rian personally adjudicate:
- species medicine;
- reproductive legitimacy;
- culture;
- translation identity;
- body-standard worth;
- accommodation eligibility.

Those remain with:
- affected/current people;
- medical/service professionals;
- local institutions;
- separated rights fields.

Rian may command around those decisions.
He may not become their universal moral court.

# 8. Red-team failure examples

FAIL:
> “Only Rian could see the whole board, and everyone finally understood why they had to trust him.”

Why:
- turns separated authority into deferred obedience.

FAIL:
> “The node chose independence because Rian gave it a safe option.”

Why:
- erases node agency.

FAIL:
> “Aven joined Rian after realizing the truth.”

Why:
- erases Aven's continuing technical/political disagreement.

FAIL:
> “The surviving enemy fleet became the foundation of Rian's final armada.”

Why:
- violates title/crew/mission separation.

PASS shape:
> Rian wins the military composition problem, but the battlefield keeps returning decisions he cannot own: local stops, exits, surrender terms, service forks, affected priorities and independent routes.

---

# 9. GA9 special Rian-removal test

Because Rian is uniquely necessary during the named campaign, the test is not “same outcome without him.”

Instead ask:

> If Rian disappears at each stage, do other actors still act, even if the military outcome becomes much worse?

E976–979:
- YES: enclave hardens, Aven negotiates architecture, coalition debates, military ultimatum proceeds.

E980–993:
- YES but worse: local/affected stops, node choices, service forks, outsider corridors and surrender actors still move; the coalition loses its unmatched composer.

E994–1000:
- YES: crisis owners, title/crew processes, review bodies and local institutions continue; absence exposes dependency rather than freezing the galaxy.

Verdict:
- **PASS.**

# 10. Final ruling

GA9 detailed design:
> **PASS — central command is powerful without swallowing all authority.**

Primary manuscript risk:
> **CREDIT ABSORPTION / EMOTIONAL RECENTRALIZATION AROUND RIAN.**

Required future manuscript rule:
> **When Rian coordinates an autonomous choice, preserve the other actor's authorship of that choice.**

No scene-card, canon, death, authority or outcome change is required.
