## 1. Verdict

The three packets are usable as **working canon foundations**, with explicit limits.

Approved for downstream design:
- four opening cultural strata as social patterns rather than political factions.
- Korean-readable hybrid naming strategy for future sample testing.
- ordinary-life anchors for the opening cast.
- H-001’s community as a plural political unit with three focal characters.
- known-lattice population, node hierarchy, travel/communication delay, ship/fleet envelopes and relative cost scale.

## 2. S1 Major Risks

### S1-01. Cultural strata can become personality templates

Problem:
- Core, Frontier, Industrial and Neutral patterns may tempt the draft to assign predictable politics and voices.

Required controls:
- every culture must contain at least one prominent internal counterexample.
- character decisions derive from current interests, relationships and history—not cultural label alone.
- no dialogue may be written by swapping in a culture-specific accent template.
- faction and culture fields remain separate in every character sheet.

Mandatory counterexamples:
- a Core-registry character who supports plural records and local refusal.
- a Frontier character who prefers Seed/imperial stability.
- an Industrial worker who defends Helix certification for safety reasons.
- a Neutral member who breaks equal rules for a humanitarian exception and another who refuses.

### S1-02. Ordinary-life traits are too narratively useful

Problem:
- route games, gardening, notebooks, scrap toys and ration habits all currently echo grand themes and can feel engineered.

Required controls:
- at least one hobby or preference per core character must remain largely useless to plot.
- not every personal object becomes a relic or payoff.
- ordinary scenes may change affection or mood without advancing a mystery.
- recurring habits can be irritating, funny or banal rather than symbolic every time.

Recommended nonfunctional additions during voice testing:
- P-001 is bad at a harmless artistic/social activity.
- H-001 has one wasteful personal preference he cannot justify.
- H-002 likes something aesthetically embarrassing and technically irrelevant.
- S-001 enjoys fiction/gossip without cataloguing it.
- M-001 dislikes or neglects one healthy habit despite medical expertise.

### S1-03. The protected community still risks becoming H-001’s moral apparatus

Problem:
- PC-001/002/003 all connect strongly to H-001’s fall and Archive themes.

Required controls:
- give each focal a relationship, grievance and decision unrelated to H-001.
- at least one community vote/choice proceeds without H-001 present.
- one focal should reject the label “H-001’s community.”
- resource allocations must create conflicts among residents that H-001 cannot solve by superior logistics.
- no focal dies merely to push H-001 toward or away from Blood Admiral destiny.

### S1-04. 76–82 billion people can make personal politics feel disconnected

Problem:
- a galaxy-scale population may make academy/one-node decisions seem insignificant or make P-001’s late authority implausibly broad.

Required controls:
- strategic impact travels through node standards, law, records and reproducible institutions, not celebrity alone.
- P-001 never personally administers the full population.
- regional actors can ignore, reinterpret or reject central decisions.
- final distribution has multiple local implementations and incomplete reach.
- every grand-act scale increase retains named local communities and service consequences.

### S1-05. Fleet totals can invite meaningless number inflation

Problem:
- thousands of hulls can lead to battles resolved by larger counts and repetitive fleet descriptions.

Required controls:
- use readiness, location, role, support, ammunition, repair, authentication and political limits before raw totals.
- reader-facing battle orders should normally track 3–7 meaningful formations, not hundreds of named hulls.
- large totals belong to strategic summaries; scenes follow commanders, crews, routes and objectives.
- a 500-ship concentration must require months of warning, logistics and political cost.

### S1-06. Travel ranges remain too broad for precise causality

Problem:
- ranges such as 3–8 weeks can be bent to plot convenience.

Required controls:
- before GA2 detailed plotting, create a route graph with 12–20 named opening systems/nodes and fixed normal/priority/damaged travel classes.
- every major movement records departure, route, queue priority, transit, recovery and communication arrival.
- unusual speed requires a consumed key, cost, risk or prepositioned asset.

### S1-07. Communication being 3–8× faster than ships needs mechanism limits

Problem:
- without concrete packet size, synchronization and authentication constraints, characters may transmit whatever the plot requires.

Required controls:
- separate short authenticated orders, compressed reports, full sensor logs, neural/AI models and physical evidence.
- large/high-security data may still require couriers.
- damaged nodes reduce both bandwidth and trust.
- corrections can arrive after a forged summary has already shaped policy.

### S1-08. The first GA2 ship may be too capable as mobile base

Problem:
- a 132 m escort with frames, workshop, medicine and passengers can solve academy dependence too quickly.

Required controls:
- opening crew below minimum sustainable complement.
- only one frame berth fully functional at first; second is partial/external.
- medical capacity is stabilization, not hospital.
- cargo/workshop modules compete for power, heat and habitable space.
- debt, lease, inspection and route conditions prevent free movement.
- one serious engagement forces repair/resupply or retreat.

## 3. S2 Moderate Risks

### S2-01. Naming theme can overload identity motif

Control:
- most people use one stable reader-facing name.
- aliases/titles appear only when social/legal conflict requires them.
- no more than two active names for one character in an arc.

### S2-02. Cultural food and ritual can become exposition blocks

Control:
- show them during real eating, repair, mourning, bargaining or embarrassment.
- a cultural detail must affect interaction or sensory reality, but need not carry lore every time.

### S2-03. Community size 28–44 may be hard to stage

Control:
- use three focal characters plus 3–5 rotating named residents.
- group votes and work shifts can represent wider population without pretending unanimity.
- maintain a community roster only when GA2 convoy design begins.

### S2-04. Academy and galaxy cost indices need conversion discipline

Control:
- retain Academy annual budget = 100 as design-only index.
- do not expose raw index to readers.
- create a later price basket before writing contracts or salaries in currency.

### S2-05. Independence force range is broad

Control:
- distinguish confirmed controlled hulls, aligned local hulls and theoretical coalition totals.
- most cannot concentrate at once.
- create per-region force table for GA2/3 rather than global estimate in scenes.

### S2-06. Neutral Assembly’s civilian fleet can look like hidden superpower

Control:
- member vessels are not centrally commanded.
- commercial obligations and neutrality rules limit concentration.
- mobilizing a fraction causes trade, insurance and political damage.

## 5. Strategic Scale Stress Tests

### Why can an academy incident matter?

Because it creates reproducible precedents in:
- student/data rights.
- multi-party custody.
- open-service technical standards.
- node/mission governance.
- Archive/Continuity protocol discovery.

It does not matter because P-001 becomes famous immediately.

### Why can one open standard threaten Helix?

It cannot destroy Helix alone.
It can:
- reduce lock-in for a narrow emergency class.
- expose pre-standard interfaces.
- create political demand and competing certifiers.
- enable local repair while increasing safety/security disputes.

### Why can the Empire not simply blockade every rebel node?

- too many dispersed nodes and routes.
- blockade ships/support unavailable everywhere.
- local economies and Imperial citizens would suffer.
- node destruction/denial can damage state logistics.
- political factions and corporations resist total disruption.

### Why does a grand fleet not decide the war once?

- political authority and authentication fragment.
- fleets cannot hold hundreds of nodes without local institutions.
- ammunition, repair and crew readiness decay.
- victory can destroy the infrastructure it seeks to control.
