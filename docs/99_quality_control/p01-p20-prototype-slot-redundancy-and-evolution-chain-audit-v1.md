## 0. Question

Option B assigns twenty internal experimental slots.

A believable engineering program may revisit the same problem several times.
But repeated problems are only useful when they show:

> failure → changed assumption → different experiment → different inherited result

Otherwise they are duplicate lore.

This audit distinguishes:
- **productive iteration chains**;
- **true redundancy that should be merged/reassigned before canonization**.

---

# 1. Redundancy test

Two P-slots may both remain only if at least two are different:

1. primary engineering question;
2. operational environment;
3. institutional sponsor/user;
4. failure mode;
5. success metric;
6. whole-frame versus subsystem scope;
7. later inherited consequence.

If only the number/name differs:
- merge or reassign one slot.

---

# 2. P-01 ↔ P-20 — common frame question

## P-01
O1:
- tries broad universal load frame/common hardpoint;
- fails because scope/loads/interfaces produce an overburdened or unstable whole article.

## P-20
O3:
- bounded common structural/mass-production architecture;
- explicitly gives up universal fit;
- optimizes repeatability, production tolerance and defined mission variation.

### Why both can exist

The core lesson is historical:

> P-20 succeeds partly by **refusing P-01’s universal requirement**.

Different success metric:
- P-01 asks “can one frame accept everything?”
- P-20 asks “can a bounded family be reproduced safely and cheaply?”

### Ruling
**KEEP BOTH — STRONG FAILURE→BOUNDED-SUCCESS CHAIN.**

Do not make P-20 a literal rebuild of P-01 by default.
It can inherit institutional lessons across many intermediate programs.

# 3. P-03 ↔ P-13 — human-machine performance chain

## P-03
O1:
- high acceleration + aggressive coupling;
- whole test fails safety/medical viability.

Inheritance:
- restraint, sync and medical ceilings.

## P-13
O4:
- later/parallel high-performance architecture integrates acceleration, sensor fusion and deep coupling;
- achieves exceptional test performance;
- remains non-scalable because operator/medical/maintenance burden is extreme.

### Redundancy risk

Weak version:
> “P-13 is P-03 but finally works.”

That would make P-03 only a failed stepping stone and P-13 a hidden ultimate frame.

### Required distinction

P-13 must use a materially different design assumption, for example:
- distributed sensor/coupling architecture rather than raw command bandwidth;
- extreme operator screening rather than universal pilot requirement;
- short bounded test objective rather than field sustainability.

P-03 establishes population-wide safety limits.
P-13 proves exceptional performance can exist **without invalidating those limits**.

### Ruling
**KEEP BOTH / P-13 MUST NOT BE “FIXED P-03.”**

# 4. P-04 ↔ P-05 — endurance versus heat architecture

## P-04
O2:
- long-duration utility + emergency surge operation;
- success is duty-cycle scheduling/energy management.

## P-05
O2:
- modular/removable heat-rejection architecture;
- success is cooling module/interface knowledge despite poor whole-frame tactical profile.

### Overlap
Both involve heat/endurance.

### Distinction
- P-04 = **when/how long** the machine can operate;
- P-05 = **how heat hardware is physically serviced/reconfigured**.

P-04 can use conventional cooling with better scheduling.
P-05 can have excellent modular cooling but still fail mission endurance/defense.

### Ruling
**KEEP BOTH — DISTINCT IF PACKETS MAINTAIN DUTY-CYCLE VS HARDWARE-MODULE SPLIT.**

# 5. P-06 ↔ P-18 — repair/fallback chain

## P-06
O2:
- damaged-operation manual fallback + field repair access;
- produces procedures/physical isolation/service accessibility.

## P-18
O3:
- weak-logistics whole-frame architecture;
- designed from the start around donor compatibility, field repair and long support gaps.

### High overlap
This is the strongest redundancy risk after P-07/P-17.

### Required distinction

P-06 must be:
- **repairability of a damaged article**.

P-18 must be:
- **an entire operational/logistics ecology**:
  - common consumables;
  - donor parts;
  - low-specialist crew burden;
  - manual diagnostics;
  - long service intervals;
  - degraded fuel/power/support assumptions.

P-18 cannot merely have “easy repair panels.”

### Ruling
**KEEP CONDITIONALLY.**

If P-18 packet lacks full logistics ecology:
- merge its surviving lessons into P-06 and reassign P-18 to another under-covered historical question.

# 6. P-07 ↔ P-17 — emergency authority/interoperability chain

## P-07
O3:
- mixed-contractor service/diagnostics;
- rescue/capture;
- broad but bounded role-based emergency access.

## P-17
O2:
- cross-institution authentication + expiring purpose tokens + auditability.

### Very high conceptual overlap
Both touch emergency authority.

### Required distinction

P-07’s authority layer must be primarily **physical/service-era**:
- role-based emergency access;
- local operation;
- broad service connectors;
- imperfect security;
- immediate intervention.

P-17 must be a later **governance/security response** to abuse/conflict exposed by broad access:
- purpose scoping;
- expiry;
- provenance/audit;
- institution-to-institution translation;
- no universal physical compatibility requirement.

Historical relation can be:
> broad service authority proved useful → abuse/ownership disputes exposed → P-17 tests narrower auditable authority.

P-17 does not replace P-07’s service bus.

### Ruling
**KEEP CONDITIONALLY — SEPARATION MUST BE EXPLICIT.**

If P-17 remains just “better service keys,” it is redundant and should be reassigned.

# 12. P-19 ↔ P-20 — production chain

## P-19
O2:
- tooling, common subassembly, manufacturing-process pilot.

## P-20
O3:
- whole scalable frame/family architecture.

### Risk
Too neat a “prototype 19 directly perfects into 20” sequence can feel game-like.

### Control
P-19 should be a **production/process article**, perhaps not a complete maneuver frame at all.

P-20 combines:
- P-19 manufacturing lessons;
- earlier load-frame limits;
- cradle standards;
- bounded modularity.

Thus P-20 is not simply P-19 Mark II.

### Ruling
**KEEP BOTH — P-19 SHOULD BE NON-HEROIC INDUSTRIAL TEST ARTICLE.**

# 15. Program-history implication

The numbered program should contain a mixture of:
- intentional follow-up experiments;
- parallel contractor approaches;
- failed branches with no successor;
- later reinterpretations after war/reconstruction.

Do not write:
> P-01 failed, so P-02 fixed it, then P-03 fixed that ... until P-20 was perfect.

That would turn a distributed industrial era into a tech-tree ladder.

Preferred graph:
- many-to-many influences;
- dead ends;
- reused subsystems;
- changed institutional priorities.
