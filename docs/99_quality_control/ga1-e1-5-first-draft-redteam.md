## Attack 1 — The protagonist is morally right too often

Risk:
- Rian credits Bram, protects Haren’s people, accepts Nera’s authority, obeys Mia and rescues the capsule.
- repeated good choices could make him feel pre-corrected rather than flawed.

Counterevidence:
- E1 uses impossible knowledge and lies by omission.
- E2 initially asks probabilities before current people and treats self-sacrifice as a clean solution.
- E3 gives a repair order that would damage current 07.
- E4 predicts the wrong location and tries to preserve eligibility.
- E5 first proposes taking sole blame, again centralizing responsibility; he also needs others to stop his premature machine actions.

Decision:
- PASS.

Revision control:
- do not add speeches explaining his virtue.
- preserve his operational abstraction and irritation when others delay him.

---

## Attack 2 — Every teammate gets a perfectly timed line

Risk:
- E5 planning and hearing give Haren, Nera, Serin and Mia one domain statement each.
- this can read like a designed team tutorial.

Counterevidence:
- roles arise from already established disputes and actual stop authority.
- their goals do not fully align.
- the process still fails the official test and harms Juno’s cell.

Decision:
- PASS FOR FIRST DRAFT / S1 RHYTHM RISK.

Second-pass instruction:
- compress repeated role explanations.
- retain at least one interruption, disagreement or partial silence so the sequence does not feel like a roll call.

---

## Attack 3 — Institution is a cartoon trap

Risk:
- three official choices appear engineered to force betrayal or abandonment.

Counterevidence:
- the test includes real safety reasons, a live monitored operator, medical thresholds and independent instructor control.
- Kara does not demand injury and allows an alternate plan while preserving failure consequences.
- official classification separates human safety from performance, even if the institution values the latter wrongly.

Decision:
- PASS, conditional on later showing certified systems genuinely save people.

That later requirement is already locked in E18 and Black Ward/Helix arcs.

---

## Attack 4 — 07 becomes special too fast

Risk:
- recognition light, Archive-family protocol and first movement occur in five episodes.

Counterevidence:
- E1 07 is powered down and physically unstable.
- E3 it only stands under external support.
- E5 it moves slowly at low output, kneels once, loses the worn knee support and cannot finish.
- no weapon or ownership reward appears.

Decision:
- PASS.

## Attack 5 — Archive/UI turns the novel into a game

Risk:
- E1, E3, E4 and E5 contain displays.

Finding:
- the removed E5 `future match percentage` was an actual drift toward game-system diagnostics.
- remaining displays represent records, current rules or sparse mystery signals.

Decision:
- PASS after removal.

Rule:
- no Archive notification in E6 solely to deliver the hook.

## Attack 6 — Bram exists to validate the theme

Risk:
- ordinary worker repeatedly solves physical steps and comments on records.

Counterevidence:
- he is impatient, practical and not grateful for symbolic elevation.
- his work has limits; he cannot certify or solve the whole frame.
- later institutional costs and worker interests remain independent.

Decision:
- PASS.

Second-pass rule:
- avoid giving Bram the final thematic line in every episode.

## Attack 8 — Future-memory exposition crowds current action

Risk:
- E1 future 07, E2 future Blood Admiral, E3 future Nera, E4 Orpheus and E5 remembered schedule appear in consecutive episodes.

Finding:
- every future fragment changes a current choice, but cumulative density may make readers expect a future-name reveal every episode.

Decision:
- S1 RETENTION RISK, not blocker.

Mitigation for E6–10:
- at least two episodes should deliver reward through present repair/social action without a major future-memory reveal.
- future memory must fail or cost when used.
