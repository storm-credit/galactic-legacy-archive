## 2. Attack — Fleet Counts Become Empty Spectacle

Risk:
- hundreds of hulls and thousands of frames may become background numbers.

Controls:
- reader-facing battles track 3–7 meaningful formations;
- support, route, yard, ammunition, crew and mandate fields constrain action;
- global holdings are never one available fleet;
- theater concentration requires months and visible economics;
- local forces and withdrawal rights remain.

Required scene rule:
- name objective, support and failure condition before hull count.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 3. Attack — Rian’s Command Growth Is Too Fast

Risk:
- student ace becomes fleet/theater commander within six years.

Controls:
- GA1–4 he does not own or command a fleet;
- GA2 separates captain and mission command;
- GA3 local leaders retain authority;
- GA4 uses precision/record work;
- GA5 command arises through existing commanders and a bounded charter;
- independent commanders win without him;
- GA9 central command comes from emergency institutional conditions, not rank alone.

Required support:
- named experienced staffs and commanders;
- failures from Rian’s inexperience with ship, logistics, politics and personnel;
- current body/pilot limits.

Verdict:
- CONDITIONAL PASS, supporting-command cast required.

## 4. Attack — Mecha Are Either Pointless or Too Powerful

Risk:
- fleets dominate strategic war, making frames cosmetic; or elite frames decide everything.

Controls:
- frames perform service, capture, rescue, boarding and infrastructure work;
- ships control approach and endurance;
- dense node/habitat geometry and political objectives require frames;
- frames depend on carriers/cradles/maintenance;
- 07 has limited long-range firepower and sustained endurance;
- capital ships cannot repair or occupy infrastructure.

Required battle variety:
- at least one decisive non-duel frame action per major war arc;
- no frame destroys capital fleets alone.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 5. Attack — Independence Always Wins Through Asymmetry

Risk:
- hidden routes and local support can excuse implausible victories.

Controls:
- weak medicine, ammunition, heavy repair and sustained coordination;
- local communities can refuse cells;
- reprisals and route destruction create costs;
- Imperial/Helix sensors, intelligence and public services are strong;
- irregular concentration remains small.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 6. Attack — Imperial Forces Are Slow Straw Men

Risk:
- political authorization and conflicting orders make state fleets conveniently ineffective.

Controls:
- Imperial doctrine has strong staffs, logistics, standards, reserves and legitimate service roles;
- provincial/local commands retain autonomy;
- central/authenticated command can save lives and win efficiently;
- GA9 demonstrates superior coordination;
- delays arise from real route and legitimacy constraints.

Required victories:
- Imperial/state commanders independently save populations and defeat opponents;
- one protagonist plan must fail against competent standard doctrine.

Verdict:
- PASS WITH POSITIVE EXAMPLES REQUIRED.

## 7. Attack — Helix Is Militarily Underpowered for Its Influence

Assessment:
- Helix does not need a state-sized navy because influence comes from yards, medicine, parts, insurance, technical teams and contracts.
- direct security is concentrated at facilities and recovery operations.

Required control:
- Helix cannot openly fight large state fleets without alliances;
- state/Neutral recognition limits corporate force;
- workers and facilities can resist.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 8. Attack — Support-Ship Emphasis Becomes Repetitive Moral Lesson

Risk:
- every battle might repeat “logistics matters more than heroes.”

Controls:
- support functions vary: rescue, repair, ammo, food, sensors, evidence, transport and command;
- ace/pilot skill remains tactically meaningful;
- some battles are decided by rapid violent concentration;
- support importance is shown through options and recovery, not lectures.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 9. Attack — Replacement Lead Times Contradict Large Later Fleets

Assessment:
- later fleets mainly reuse, reactivate, repair, convert and realign existing hulls;
- Seed/Aurel improves readiness faster than new construction;
- capital-ship losses remain permanent or strategic;
- common fleet networks aggregate existing ships.

Required ledger:
- track source of every major new formation: prior fleet, provincial force, conversion, defection or construction.

Verdict:
- PASS.

Assessment:

Required control:

Verdict:
- PASS AS ORDER-OF-MAGNITUDE.

Verdict:
- PASS.
