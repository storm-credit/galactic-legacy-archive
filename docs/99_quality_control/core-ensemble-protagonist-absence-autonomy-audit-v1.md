## 0. Audit question

The main design already states:

> major characters and factions have plans, failures and succession while P-001 is absent.

This audit tests the five core recurring non-Rian actors most likely to shape the series:
- Haren Doan;
- Nera Vick;
- Serin Or;
- Mia Lentz;
- Ern Varka.

Question:

> If Rian is absent for the next ten episodes, does this character still want something, choose something, pay a cost and alter the state of the world?

---

# 1. Haren Doan

## Independent engine

Core want:
- protect communities and routes without turning survival into another hostage system.

Independent flaw:
- can abstract people through logistics even while opposing Rian's military abstraction.

## Rian-removal proof

GA2:
- negotiates separately with community and Neutral routes;
- some residents reject him or stay behind.

GA4:
- while Rian is absorbed in imperial succession politics, Haren builds federalist/provincial ties and refuses secret imperial bargain.

GA5:
- creates an independent civilian supply charter and refusal rights against military requisition pressure.

GA6:
- controls distributed route work and acquires dangerous independent power over survival corridors.

GA7:
- acts as autonomous admiral/political rival and later bears direct D4 responsibility.

GA9:
- builds opt-out corridors and refuses central-key orders even when Rian's command has measurable benefits.

## Counterfactual

If Rian disappears:
- Haren's communities still need routes;
- route authorities still bargain;
- shortages and denial choices still occur;
- Haren can still make a damaging D4-class decision under his own authority.

Verdict:
> **VERY STRONG PASS.**

Prose veto:
- do not reduce Haren to “the friend who teaches Rian decentralization.”

# 2. Nera Vick

## Independent engine

Core want:
- preserve technical authorship, repairability and worker/service rights without allowing one state/company/military owner to capture the standard.

Independent flaw:
- open interoperability can spread unsafe copies, arm people she does not support and become another standard monopoly.

## Rian-removal proof

GA1:
- secures maintenance veto and chooses technical sacrifice rather than simply maximizing Rian's combat capability.

GA2:
- publishes controlled emergency compatibility standards; accidents and litigation follow regardless of protagonist intent.

GA3:
- builds local production/worker safety architecture that locals can own without her.

GA4:
- bargains directly with houses/institutions and selectively releases interoperability information.

GA5:
- refuses single military ownership of fleet-repair standard and trains regional crews.

GA6:
- sacrifices rare/elite equipment for common evacuation capacity.

GA7:
- conditional supply can support multiple sides under civilian rules, attracting accusations from both.

GA9–10:
- develops local forks/manual fallback and later governed commons that can disagree with Rian's preferred transition.

## Counterfactual

If Rian disappears:
- workers still need standards;
- Helix still litigates/competes;
- unsafe copies still fail;
- yards still bargain over ownership and certification;
- Nera still has to decide whether to open, restrict, fork or stop a design.

Verdict:
> **VERY STRONG PASS.**

Prose veto:
- Nera is not “Rian's mechanic.”
- Rian is one user/operator among many people affected by her technical choices.

# 3. Serin Or

## Independent engine

Core want:
- preserve provenance, current standing and the ability of affected people/institutions to challenge records.

Independent flaw:
- provenance discipline can slow urgent action;
- translation/custody systems can themselves become tools of classification and control.

## Rian-removal proof

GA1:
- preserves multi-party evidence even when records make the group harder to protect.

GA2:
- provenance work continues through ship/route identity and communications regardless of Rian's immediate tactical needs.

GA3:
- translates local/worker/imperial current records inside Ardis's own institutional process.

GA4:
- succession evidence proves several commands authentic but cannot choose a ruler; Serin's custody obligation survives whichever claimant Rian prefers.

GA6 concrete proof:
- central optimization recommends replacing disputed identities with easier verified passengers;
- Serin/current-status teams preserve most disputed passengers under provisional status;
- throughput efficiency falls as a real cost.

GA7:
- Blood Admiral provenance separates person, office, key and propaganda instead of delivering one heroic/villain truth.

GA8–10:
- refuses sole archive truth/custody and helps create translation commons that remain imperfect.

## Counterfactual

If Rian disappears:
- evidence still has owners/custodians;
- records still conflict;
- institutions still attempt classification;
- people still need appeal/current standing;
- Serin still chooses what can be authenticated, disclosed, withheld or marked uncertain.

Verdict:
> **STRONG PASS — PROSE-SENSITIVE.**

Primary risk:
- scenes can reduce her to “the person who explains the evidence to Rian.”

Required prose control:
- when Serin's decision concerns custody/provenance, the consequential verb belongs to Serin/institution:
  - preserves;
  - refuses;
  - releases;
  - seals;
  - marks uncertain;
  - creates appeal path.

Not merely:
- informs Rian;
- confirms Rian;
- finds what Rian needs.

# 4. Mia Lentz

## Independent engine

Core want:
- preserve current patient consent, transportability and minimum medical service under political/military pressure.

Independent flaw:
- triage inevitably excludes/delays someone;
- medical caution can conflict with strategic windows and distributed service capacity.

## Rian-removal proof

GA1:
- consent/treatment rights exist as her institutional responsibility, not protagonist morality.

GA2:
- patients can choose routes/providers she distrusts.

GA3:
- minimum-service design and triage operate inside Ardis independently of Rian's preferred priorities.

GA4:
- hospitals continue through succession regardless of who wins legal argument.

GA5:
- rescue beds/tenders become independent strategic constraints.

GA6 concrete proof:
- Mia/Ella identify 18,400 people whose listed destination cannot support treatment;
- they block automatic transfer;
- 11,600 nonmedical State seats are displaced;
- later medical decisions determine who can be moved and who must remain with care.

GA7:
- medical/dependency evidence measures downstream denial deaths and constrains route-war narratives.

GA8–10:
- medical consent/person-state and minimum-service compacts remain separate authority domains.

## Counterfactual

If Rian disappears:
- patients still deteriorate;
- transport windows still expire;
- hospitals still run out of capacity;
- consent/refusal still exists;
- Mia still has to triage, stop transfers and assign care pathways.

Verdict:
> **STRONG PASS — PROSE-SENSITIVE.**

Primary risk:
- becoming “Rian's conscience/doctor.”

Required prose control:
- Mia sometimes makes a medically correct choice that is strategically harmful to Rian.
- the narration must preserve that the authority comes from patient/current medical state, not from Rian accepting moral advice.

# 7. Relationship rule

A strong ensemble is not five separate protagonists with no common center.

Rian remains the primary emotional/strategic anchor.

But relationships must be expressed as **intersections between independent arcs**:

```text
Rian wants A
Haren is already doing B
Nera's technical choice makes C possible
Mia blocks part of A because bodies cannot survive it
Serin preserves evidence that changes who can justify B/C
Ern chooses D outside the shared plan
→ the story outcome is the collision of these choices
```

Avoid:

```text
Rian creates plan
→ Haren handles logistics
→ Nera repairs it
→ Mia heals people
→ Serin explains records
→ Ern creates tactical complication
```

The second shape is a support-party RPG structure and should fail ensemble audit.

# 8. Manuscript audit questions

For every 5–10 episode block with at least two core ensemble members active, ask:

1. Which non-Rian character had an objective before Rian spoke to them?
2. Which decision would they have made if Rian were elsewhere?
3. Did anyone materially refuse or alter Rian's preferred plan?
4. Did that choice create a cost that cannot be attributed back to Rian?
5. Did any character's institution/community/client/patient matter more than friendship with Rian?
6. Did a successful non-Rian decision retain its own credit?
7. Did a failed non-Rian decision retain its own responsibility?

If answers 2–5 are all “none,” re-audit the block even if multiple POVs are present.
