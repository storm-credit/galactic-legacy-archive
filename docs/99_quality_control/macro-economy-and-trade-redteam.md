## 1. Verdict

S0 blockers:

The numbers are design ranges, not a hard science simulation or reader-facing price catalogue.

## 2. Attack — BSC Is Artificial and Game-Like

Risk:
- one adult-day service unit may feel like a game currency or ignore local economies.

Controls:
- BSC is author-side only;
- actual dialogue can use IRC, RSM, corporate/local credits and service rights;
- price variation and unavailable goods are explicit;
- noncash entitlements often matter more than money.

Required rule:
- never display BSC as a system stat in the story.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 3. Attack — Excessive False Precision

Risk:
- wages, ship costs and populations use numerical ranges unsupported by full input-output simulation.

Controls:
- ranges remain broad;
- exact values appear only when decisions require them;
- author-side conversion is anchored to prior Academy index and ship estimates;
- physical/institutional bottlenecks override price;
- no claim of predictive realism.

Required practice:
- scenario-specific state sheets choose a value inside the range and record assumptions;
- avoid exact decimals and stable galaxy-wide prices.

Verdict:
- PASS WITH CALIBRATION LABEL.

## 4. Attack — Ordinary People Still Cannot Understand Scale

Risk:
- millions and billions of BSC obscure household stakes.

Controls:
- reader-friendly conversions use meals, person-days, months of net income, treatment courses, ship-days and route slots;
- household and Academy examples included;
- capital and operating cost separated.

Required scene rule:
- connect every large cost to one visible household, crew or service consequence.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 5. Attack — Helix/State Economic Control Is Too Universal

Risk:
- identity, medicine, finance, certification and routes could make Helix/Empire unbeatable.

Controls:
- control is divided across institutions and regions;
- local credits, Neutral settlement, open standards, worker networks and alternate routes exist;
- sanctions require real bottlenecks and third-party enforcement;
- sanctions create worker defection, smuggling and political costs.

Required arc control:
- identify which bottleneck each faction does **not** control;
- avoid sanctions that instantly affect all 612 systems.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 6. Attack — Economic Oppression Reduces Everything to Debt

Risk:
- student, ship, treatment and family arcs can repeat “debt trap.”

Controls:
- economic power also uses capacity, timing, recognition, skilled labor, housing, route, insurance and public budgets;
- some contracts genuinely improve mobility and safety;
- local mutual obligation can coerce without formal debt;
- wealthy regions face service and political constraints.

Required variety:
- each grand act foregrounds a different bottleneck.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 7. Attack — First Ship Is Financially Impossible

Current estimate:
- distressed value 8–14 million BSC;
- total repair/claim exposure 24–40+ million;
- normal monthly operation 40,000–100,000;
- Academy annual budget 18–24 million.

Assessment:
- plausible only because no actor buys the ship cleanly;
- lease, mission obligations, liens, old crew claims, Academy/Neutral/Helix/state contributions and distressed status split the burden.

Required control:
- first contracts cannot pay off capital debt;
- one high-intensity mission must threaten solvency;
- crew pay and maintenance remain recurring.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 8. Attack — Academy Budget Is Too Low or High

Assessment:
- 20 million BSC for ~3,000 residents plus facilities and frames is viable only with:
  - student/worker productive labor;
  - sponsor-provided equipment/medicine;
  - state/mission funding;
  - hidden risk transfer and debt;
  - existing capital infrastructure not purchased annually.

Required control:
- capital replacement remains underfunded;
- loss of Helix/mission contracts creates closure threat;
- staff wages and deferred maintenance stay visible.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 9. Attack — Military Economy Is Underpriced

Risk:
- large warships and fleets may still appear affordable relative to state population/economy.

Response:
- current tables are order-of-magnitude anchors;
- full military holdings/readiness and war-budget packet is the next required batch;
- ammunition, support and yard constraints matter more than hull sticker price.

Verdict:
- ECONOMY FOUNDATION PASS / MILITARY CALIBRATION OPEN.

## 10. Attack — Refugees Are Treated as Costs

Risk:
- person-month calculations can frame displaced people only as burdens.

Controls:
- refugees bring labor, skills, culture, political standing and networks;
- host-region employment and institution formation included;
- calculations exist to prevent magical resettlement, not rank human worth;
- consent and destination acceptance remain separate.

Required later work:
- named refugee households and host residents;
- long-term contribution and conflict, not only emergency consumption.

Verdict:
- PASS WITH HUMAN FOCALS REQUIRED.

## 11. Attack — Aurel Economic Success Predetermines Political Support

Risk:
- reliable pay and medicine may make opposition irrational, or the story may minimize those gains.

Controls:
- Aurel success is explicit and geographically unequal;
- opposition may target political/identity authority while retaining service synchronization;
- GA10 allows voluntarily extended central services;
- classification and review queues create real harms.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 12. Attack — Trade Chokepoints Are Convenient Plot Levers

Risk:
- every crisis may reveal the one missing medicine, route or part.

Controls:
- bottleneck classes and advance economic indicators;
- failures require at least two layers;
- inventories, reserves and substitutes create delay rather than instant collapse;
- producers can suffer when consumers starve due transport failure.

Required scene/act state:
- record stock, burn rate, route and replacement lead time before crisis.

Verdict:
- PASS.
