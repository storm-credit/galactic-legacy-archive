## 2. S1 Major Risks

### S1-01. GA3 can feel like GA1 charter at larger scale

Problem:
- both end with multi-party authority and rights.

Required distinction:
- GA1 controls one coercive school and student contracts.
- GA3 controls public food, medicine, housing, taxation, labor, node traffic, defense and citizenship for 1.4 million people.
- GA3 must show local elite capture, public-service scarcity and military legitimacy—failure modes absent or minor in GA1.
- protagonists are outsiders without automatic constituency.

Cut condition:
- any GA3 debate that could occur unchanged inside the Academy must be rewritten around city-scale service, labor, citizenship or defense.

### S1-02. Seven factions can overwhelm reader memory

Control:
- active front-stage factions per subact: 3–5.
- cluster reader-facing geography/politics around Civic, Foundry, Transit, Node and Defense; Helix/Federalists enter when relevant.
- leaders introduced through actions and services, not one council roll call.
- use recurring district/worker/resident faces to embody faction effects.
- not every internal bloc gets named in prose.

Introduction target:
- E211–224: Civic, Transit/Garden, Node.
- E225–247: Foundry.
- E248–270: Federalist pressure and wider representation.
- E271 onward: Defense and major external Helix/Imperial actors.

### S1-03. City politics can become meeting-heavy and static

Controls:
1. no more than one full formal assembly per subact.
2. major choices occur through ration enrollment, strike, outage, inspection, docking, work tests, patrol, evacuation or combat.
3. every 3–5 episodes changes at least one clock, service, territory, route or authority.
4. P-001’s ship must leave one location/role and perform physical service/defense operations.
5. district-level consequences appear before the next policy discussion.

### S1-04. Joint trust may look like the author’s predetermined correct answer

Control:
- centralized Imperial/Helix arrangements visibly stabilize medicine, authentication and command faster.
- local plural delay causes named irreversible loss.
- some sympathetic residents oppose joint trust at E330.
- joint trust remains lower capacity, underfunded and vulnerable to local elite/technical capture.
- one later grand act must show Ardis trust failing or acting selfishly.
- local actors—not P-001—author and amend core terms.

### S1-05. P-001 risks becoming a colonial savior/founder

Required controls:
- Ardis already has government, labor, defense, service and node expertise.
- heroes bring scarce but bounded tools and precedents.
- no city renaming, statue, governorship, permanent outsider veto or protagonist-appointed local leader.
- local utility-frame/operator team owns decisive repair.
- final routine node window authorized locally after P-001 departs.
- one major local solution rejects P-001’s recommended priority and works.

### S1-06. H-001 can become de facto city planner and eclipse local politics

Control:
- H-001 supplies models/route obligations but lacks city mandate.
- Ardis-linked community subgroup may disagree with him.
- Garden, Transit and Civic service leaders control data he cannot replace.
- his preferred allocation must lose or be modified more than once.
- end role is external logistics/network treaty or bounded local contract, not executive control.

### S1-07. H-002 can become sole infrastructure genius again

Control:
- Node Custodians and Foundry teams have decades of tacit knowledge.
- H-002’s standard is only one interface/tool layer.
- local engineer/team produces an alternative she initially rejects.
- 07 handles dangerous physical interface but cannot solve phase theory, cooling, power or labor.
- local workers own implementation, training and production credit.

### S1-08. Service Authority E can feel like serial key collection/power escalation

Control:
- Authority E grants local-custody/service compatibility, not full node command.
- it is held locally under split conditions.
- physical repair, operators, energy and destination cooperation remain required.
- no combat-stat boost or instant access.
- it creates political targeting and responsibility rather than easy power.

### S1-09. External fleets may arrive only when plot needs escalation

Control:
- fixed communication, authorization and travel clocks from GA2/route graph.
- seed warnings and mobilization evidence 8–20 episodes before arrival.
- external actors send orders, offers, agents and supply pressure before ships.
- arrivals can be delayed/accelerated only through stated route/priority changes.

### S1-10. Node engineering can become incomprehensible technobabble

Reader-facing model:
- three spines with different strengths and controllers.
- shared heat/energy means not everything runs together.
- physical repair + operators + trusted identity + route agreement.
- no one key solves all.

Terminology limit:
- no scene introduces more than 2–3 new technical terms.
- technical explanation follows a visible failure/test/choice.
- diagrams/status tables remain project support, not prose dumps.

### S1-11. Distributed delay loss may feel artificially inserted to prove “not utopia”

Required chain:
- conflict must follow previously established authority gap, communication delay, spoofing, resource allocation and local human decision.
- central command must have a credible counterfactual advantage, not omniscient magic.
- named actors own decisions; blame remains disputed but evidence-based.
- loss changes charter/standing rules and later politics.

Recommended loss types:
- thermal farm or Transit habitat over a disposable military ship, because city-service stakes differentiate GA3.
- avoid killing huge numbers solely for theme.

### S1-12. Final siege may become too many simultaneous fronts

Control:
- track 3–5 front owners, not every character.
- P-001 viewpoint covers one linked layer; short reports/limited viewpoint changes handle others only if POV design allows.
- each front has one clear objective and resource link.
- action sequence alternates rather than constant cliff cuts.
- one front can resolve earlier to reduce load before the three-spine climax.

### S1-13. 120 episodes may be bloated

Contractible GA3:
- 90–105 episodes.

Likely merges:
- 3A-2 + 3A-3 if first allocation/diagnostic beats overlap.
- 3B-1 + 3B-2 if strike and residency both become meeting-heavy.
- 3C-1 + 3C-2 if defense/Helix arrival can interlock.

Rule:
- episode counts remain ceilings.
- every mini-arc must change a service, territory, authority, relationship or route.

## 3. S2 Moderate Risks

### S2-01. Ardis numbers and clocks can feel like management simulation

Control:
- reader follows named households, workers, patients, crews and districts.
- only 2–4 clocks foregrounded at once.
- numbers appear when they force choice, not every episode.

### S2-02. Provisional residency may resolve identity politics too neatly

Control:
- not all accept registration.
- services remain unequal.
- guardianship, voting, long-term citizenship and external recognition stay open.
- fraud/security incident produces real backlash without proving exclusion right.

### S2-03. Foundry strike can repeat Helix/open-standard themes

Distinct function:
- labor power and city service obligation, not only technical ownership.
- workers can exploit indispensability and must share authority with users/nonworkers.

### S2-04. Federalist/Scorched actors can become convenient violent extremists

Control:
- denial has credible anti-occupation logic.
- some Custodians/Defense professionals agree partly.
- federalist civil bloc remains distinct and participates in governance.
- coercive acts produce internal opposition and named consequences.

### S2-05. Helix receivership repeats GA2 embargo

Distinct function:
- GA2 concerned regional standard/parts access.
- GA3 concerns city debt, hospitals, factories and management control.
- local Helix employees and patients create independent constituency.

### S2-06. GA4 succession hook can overshadow Ardis resolution

Control:
- Ardis obtains a complete grand-act emotional/institutional payoff before final messages.
- succession hook arrives after a local routine operation proves the city can function.
- no immediate departure before farewell/aftermath.

## 4. Character Agency Audit

### P-001

Pass if:
- no governorship or city master key.
- one local solution overrides his model successfully.
- he refuses central emergency command despite a real casualty counterargument.
- his final action enables local actors, not substitutes for them.

### H-001

Pass if:
- local groups modify/reject allocations.
- Ardis community subgroup becomes locally accountable.
- his route federation must accept public-service and citizenship obligations.

### H-002

Pass if:
- local engineers/workers own implementation and one better solution.
- open standard has capacity/security limits and local liability.

### S-001

Pass if:
- provisional identity expansion causes both benefits and exploitable risk.
- no perfect master registry emerges.

### M-001

Pass if:
- central medical option saves lives and remains ethically tempting.
- local patient/professional representatives own final rules.

### Local leaders

Each front-stage leader must:
- make at least one beneficial independent choice.
- make at least one harmful/limiting choice.
- retain constituency beyond relationship with heroes.
- have succession/opposition that prevents personal monopoly.

## 5. Collection Audit

Strengths:
- no city/node ownership.
- L2 upgrade remains incomplete.
- Authority E bounded/local.
- major loss and opposition persist.
- services/rights tied to operational use.

Reader-facing consolidation:
- six tracks only: services, spines, production, residency, defense, joint trust.
- project registry documents are not popup inventory.

Cut any target that:
- does not alter access, service, route, authority or defense.
- repeats GA1/2 without city-scale consequence.
- has no later GA4–10 reuse or strong local payoff.

## 6. GA4 Dependency Audit

GA3 ending must provide:

- Ardis route recognized enough to matter in succession conflict.
- multiple valid authentication/succession messages.
- Imperial narrow coordination actors and local treaty limits.
- a city that can choose among claimants rather than simply be occupied.
- no full truth of Imperial founding or Continuity Seed.
- P-001’s regional credibility but no sovereign title.

If GA4 can start unchanged without Ardis’s route and legal precedent, GA3 has failed.
