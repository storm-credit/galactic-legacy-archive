## 0. Audit question

The previous proposal deliberately generated seven nonhuman S1 functional slots plus humanity/human-derived people as a stress-test portfolio.

This audit asks a stricter question:

> Does every one of those seven nonhuman slots actually deserve **core-species status**, or are some better treated as regional/S2 peoples or posthuman lineages?

A species may be interesting and biologically plausible without deserving core status.

Core status carries reader-memory, illustration, infrastructure, political-history and repeated-GA costs.

Therefore this audit treats `S1` as a **serial-narrative tier**, not a measure of biological importance.

---

## 1. Core-tier promotion standard

A nonhuman people should remain S1-core only when it passes most of the following simultaneously.

### C1 — Irreplaceable causal function

Removing the species must erase or materially weaken at least one important story problem that cannot be reproduced almost unchanged by:
- human regional variation;
- human-derived posthuman clades;
- cybernetic augmentation;
- AI/composite persons;
- another already-retained species;
- a regional S2 people.

### C2 — Multi-act reuse

The species must create meaningful consequences in **three or more grand acts** without requiring a new subplot merely to keep it relevant.

### C3 — Multiple-life-domain effect

It must matter in at least three of:
- household/family;
- medicine;
- public infrastructure;
- labor/economy;
- law/citizenship;
- military/logistics;
- politics/institutions;
- Archive/record classification;
- ordinary culture.

### C4 — Non-tool identity

Its recurring people must remain interesting when the signature biology is not useful to the scene.

### C5 — Reader-load return

The recurring narrative value must justify teaching the reader:
- one species name;
- one body grammar;
- one service grammar;
- recurring visual staging rules.

### C6 — Independence from protagonist

At least one institution/community belonging substantially to the species must have a meaningful agenda that continues if Rian disappears for ten episodes.

### C7 — No automatic dedicated machine

Core-species status does not itself justify a dedicated frame/ship lineage.

## 2. Four portfolio directions

### P1 — Keep all seven as equal S1 core

Shape:
- S1-02 through S1-08 all treated as equally important core peoples.

Strength:
- maximal codex breadth.

Failure path:
- seven species names become reader homework;
- several body/sense differences overlap in ship/maintenance scenes;
- species can read as a designed RPG roster;
- later acts feel obligated to keep all seven visible.

Verdict:
- **REJECT.**

### P2 — Six core, one regional

Shape:
- remove only the clearest redundant slot.

Strength:
- preserves most current work.

Weakness:
- still treats provisional concepts as if equal narrative demand were already proven.

Verdict:
- **BETTER, BUT STILL OVER-COMMITTED.**

### P3 — Four strong core + two conditional core + one regional demotion

Shape:
- four species have enough unique multi-act demand to justify active S1 planning;
- two retain S1-candidate status but must prove an additional condition;
- one moves to S2/regional status without being deleted from the galaxy.

Strength:
- keeps galaxy broad while reducing active reader burden;
- preserves useful biology;
- lets actual front-stage demand decide future promotion;
- supports the existing principle that the galaxy may contain more species than readers memorize.

Verdict:
- **RECOMMENDED.**

### P4 — Three or fewer nonhuman core species

Shape:
- most biological diversity moved to S2/S3.

Strength:
- lowest reader burden.

Weakness:
- risks making the 612-system civilization feel human/posthuman-dominant despite the requested species-codex breadth;
- weakens multi-species ordinary-life normalization.

Verdict:
- **HOLD / TOO CONSERVATIVE FOR CURRENT AUTHOR DIRECTION.**

# 3. Slot-by-slot substitution test

## S1-02 — High-gravity compact differentiated four-limb people

### Unique value

- body geometry changes seating, restraints, tools, furniture and mixed-space design;
- historical occupational channeling can support labor/class politics;
- visually clear nonhuman morphology without requiring extreme environmental isolation.

### Replacement test

Could posthuman high-g adaptation reproduce much of the acceleration/load story?

**Yes, partially.**

The posthuman bible already supports:
- high-g occupational adaptation;
- pilot acceleration support;
- musculoskeletal/circulatory modification;
- industrial adaptation.

What posthumanity does **not** reproduce automatically:
- four-limb mixed-space ergonomics;
- a long independent civilization and culture built around a different body plan;
- historical species stereotyping around load-bearing labor.

### GA reuse

Natural use:
- GA2 yard/ship accessibility;
- GA3 labor/public-service politics;
- GA5 fleet restraint/cooling/ration logistics;
- GA6 evacuation load/care tradeoffs.

Risk:
- each use easily collapses back into “strong heavy worker species.”

### Verdict

> **CONDITIONAL S1.**

Keep as core candidate only if final design proves:
1. a major civic/cultural identity unrelated to load-bearing work;
2. a recurring non-industrial person;
3. a political/history function not reproducible by a human-derived high-g clade.

If those are weak, move to **S2 regional-major** rather than delete.

---

## S1-03 — Low-g pressure/vibration-sensitive people

### Unique value

- low-g body creates ordinary-life gravity/rest/acceleration conflict;
- pressure/vibration sensitivity changes ship life, housing, alarms, childhood and evacuation;
- first useful exposure can be a caregiver/family rather than a technical specialist;
- GA6 throughput metrics become morally and physically different without inventing new law.

### Replacement test

Could cybernetic vibration sensors or trained hull engineers reproduce the story?

They can reproduce **technical detection**, but not:
- child/family high-g accommodation;
- whole-body acceleration vulnerability;
- housing/rest environment politics;
- population-level evacuation consequences.

### GA reuse

Strong natural reuse:
- GA2 mixed-ship ordinary life;
- GA3 housing/public service;
- GA5 fleet acceleration policy;
- GA6 evacuation throughput;
- GA9 optimization/classification.

### Main risk

“better hull engineer species.”

Control:
- first foreground role remains caregiver/teacher/civic worker;
- trained sensing may inform evidence but never function as machine diagnosis magic.

### Verdict

> **KEEP — STRONG S1.**

This slot earns core status through **habitat and life-course consequences**, not its sensor advantage.

---

## S1-04 — Fluid-supported amphibious people

### Unique value

- wet-support infrastructure has unavoidable mass, contamination, sanitation, medical and evacuation consequences;
- mixed households and public budgets become materially different;
- species can remain ordinary participants in shared cities while still carrying real habitat cost.

### Replacement test

Can humans with aquatic augmentation reproduce the same effect?

Only superficially.

A small elective/adapted human population would not create the same:
- civilization-scale water/service rights;
- species medicine;
- ship wet-module logistics;
- historical settlement ecology;
- evacuation-pod mass conflict.

### GA reuse

Strong:
- GA3 city infrastructure;
- GA5 mixed fleet;
- GA6 evacuation;
- GA9 efficiency/classification;
- GA10 plural service standards.

### Verdict

> **KEEP — STRONG S1.**

This is one of the clearest examples of biology creating politics without becoming combat power.

---

## S1-05 — Obligately symbiotic one-person biology

### Unique value

- one socially/legal person composed through obligate biological symbiosis produces a category problem unavailable from ordinary cybernetics;
- medicine must treat integrated biology without assuming “two organisms = two persons”;
- identity records, quarantine, pediatrics, reproduction and Archive compression all become materially distinct.

### Replacement test

Can AI composites or implanted humans reproduce this?

No, not without changing the question.

AI/composite personhood asks about software/process/person continuity.
Cybernetics asks about person/hardware boundaries.
S1-05 asks about **biological plurality within one ordinary person**.

### GA reuse

Strong:
- GA2/3 ordinary bureaucracy/medicine;
- GA4 personhood procedure;
- GA6 medical evacuation;
- GA8 Archive identity;
- GA9 classification.

### Complexity cost

High.

Requires later:
- development;
- reproduction;
- rare separation cases;
- medicine;
- family law.

### Verdict

> **KEEP — STRONG S1, HIGH DESIGN OBLIGATION.**

Do not simplify it to a hive mind or dual personality gimmick.

---

## S1-06 — Long-lived radial five-limb people

### Unique value

- lifespan alters office terms, punishment, inheritance, education, war replacement and living historical memory;
- five-limb radial body creates a distinct but not necessarily environment-segregated visual grammar;
- one individual can remember multiple institutional eras while still being an unreliable witness.

### Replacement test

Could human longevity treatment reproduce lifespan politics?

Partially, but a civilization with long maturation and low replacement changes:
- population recovery from war;
- youth/dependency duration;
- career and succession institutions;
- casualty politics
in a way elective longevity treatment does not automatically reproduce.

### GA reuse

Strong:
- GA3 long-horizon reconstruction;
- GA4 succession/term law;
- GA5 casualty and service politics;
- GA8 living memory versus records;
- GA10 transition time horizons.

### Main risk

“ancient wise species.”

Control:
- first strong recurring figure should be young/impatient or politically radical;
- personal memory remains biased and fallible.

### Verdict

> **KEEP — STRONG S1.**

Its value comes from **different political time**, not ancient wisdom.

---

## S1-07 — Electrosensory anchored people

### Unique value

- nonvisual electrosense creates EM-accessibility, privacy and safety questions;
- anchoring body plan adds microgravity staging difference.

### Replacement test

Can existing systems reproduce most story effects?

**Yes, too often.**

Overlap exists with:
- S1-03 mechanosensory hull/environment awareness;
- S2 acoustic-primary people;
- S2 wide-spectrum/polarization vision people;
- cybernetic sensory augmentation;
- ordinary electrical safety instrumentation;
- GA7 EW/EM doctrine already available through technology.

The biologically felt EM-noise/accessibility issue is distinct, but does not currently require repeated core-level presence across the whole series.

### GA reuse

Possible:
- GA2 maintenance/access;
- GA3 classroom/public accessibility;
- GA5 fleet equipment standards;
- GA7 EW safety.

Problem:
- almost every obvious use points back toward engineering/EW.

Even stereotype-breaking teacher/artist roles solve character breadth but do not prove galaxy-core narrative demand.

### Verdict

> **DEMOTE TO S2 REGIONAL-MAJOR CANDIDATE.**

Do not delete.

Preserve:
- electrosense;
- anchoring body grammar;
- EM-accessibility politics;
- ordinary-life roles.

Change only planning tier:
- no obligation for recurring presence across all GAs;
- introduce when a region/ship/institution actually requires it.

This is the clearest anti-overdesign cut.

---

## S1-08 — Engineered developmental free-lineage people

### Unique value

- designed-purpose history versus current personhood;
- inherited patents/asset records;
- creator-descendant claims;
- Archive classification and industrial ownership history.

### Replacement test

If S1-08 is human-derived:

> almost all of this belongs naturally in the posthuman/genetic-diversity layer.

If S1-08 is independently originated/engineered nonhuman:

> the story gains a genuinely distinct civilization whose ancestors were deliberately designed but whose present people are neither products nor human descendants.

### GA reuse

Potentially strong:
- GA3 labor/industrial-rights seed;
- GA4 legal status;
- GA8 creator-era records;
- GA9 classification;
- GA10 historical ownership cleanup.

### Verdict

> **CONDITIONAL S1 — ONTOLOGY GATE.**

- If author chooses **D1 independent engineered nonhuman**: keep S1 candidate.
- If author chooses **D2 human-derived engineered clade**: remove from nonhuman S1 roster and integrate into the posthuman bible.

No replacement species is automatically required. Four or five core nonhuman peoples can be enough if regional diversity is strong.

# 4. Recommended tier shape after audit

## Stable strong-core candidates

1. **S1-03** — low-g pressure/vibration-sensitive;
2. **S1-04** — fluid-supported amphibious;
3. **S1-05** — obligate symbiotic one-person biology;
4. **S1-06** — long-lived radial.

These currently have the strongest combination of:
- irreplaceable world effect;
- multi-GA reuse;
- ordinary-life consequence;
- law/institution consequence;
- reader-memory return.

## Conditional core candidates

5. **S1-02** — keep only if independent civic/cultural/historical value exceeds posthuman high-g overlap.
6. **S1-08** — keep only if ontology resolves as independently engineered nonhuman and its history is story-relevant.

## Regional-major demotion

7. **S1-07** — move to S2 regional-major candidate; preserve design, remove core-obligation.

## Humanity / human-derived spectrum

- remains the opening reader anchor;
- does not count as a monolithic culture;
- posthuman/cybernetic/genetic variation remains orthogonal to biological-species tier.

# 5. Reader-facing implication

This audit does **not** mean the galaxy has only four or six nonhuman species.

It means:

> only four are currently strong enough to demand repeated reader memory across the long series.

The world can still contain:
- 4 strong S1 candidates;
- up to 2 conditional S1 candidates;
- 8–12 S2 regional peoples;
- 3–5 S3 rare/isolated/extinct/disputed peoples;
- additional background biodiversity when a front-stage region requires it.

Reader-facing diversity therefore increases while active noun burden decreases.

# 6. GA exposure adjustment

Existing exposure ceilings remain valid, but interpret them as **maximums, not roster obligations**.

### GA1
- no S1 foreground required.

### GA2
- foreground 1–2 strong-core species;
- do not introduce S1-07 merely to establish electrosense.

### GA3
- normalize 3–4 known peoples in public life;
- one conditional S1 may be tested through an ordinary civic role.

### GA4
- use only species whose legal/history function is already earned.

### GA5–7
- reuse known populations;
- regional S2 peoples can become important without promotion to S1.

### GA8
- S1-05/S1-06/S1-08(if D1) have especially strong record-history payoff;
- S3 can supply deep-time breadth.

### GA9–10
- novelty pressure approaches zero.

# 7. Effect on current naming HOLD

The final naming pass should **not** generate seven equal core names anymore.

Recommended naming order after author portfolio approval:

1. name the actually foregrounded strong-core species first;
2. name conditional S1 only when they survive their gate;
3. name S2-07/electrosensory people only when a concrete regional/front-stage use exists;
4. do one collision screen per active naming batch, not one giant encyclopedia naming exercise.

This lowers alias churn and protects reader-facing IP quality.

# 9. Red-team failure tests

Reject future core promotion when any of these occur:

1. removing the species changes only costume/appearance;
2. its main function can be replaced by an implant or local human adaptation with no thematic loss;
3. every recurring member works in a profession advantaged by their biology;
4. the species appears in later acts only because the codex says it is core;
5. its political position tracks species identity one-to-one;
6. its dedicated ship/frame exists before logistics demand proves it;
7. first appearance solves the scene with a biological superpower;
8. reader must remember the species name but no recurring person;
9. Rian is the source of the species' political agency;
10. demotion to S2 would not change any important story outcome.

Failure of test 10 is particularly strong evidence that S1 status is unnecessary.
