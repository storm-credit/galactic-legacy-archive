## 1. Executive verdict

The author’s request for a true species catalogue is structurally compatible with the current setting, but **not yet safe to canonize as named species**.

Why compatible:

- 612-system population totals are not explicitly locked as all-human;
- personhood law already allows substantially modified humans and AI/composite persons;
- faction design already separates species/personhood from culture/geography;
- military doctrine already says humanoid frames are not the only possible form;
- late-arc classification themes naturally support nonstandard bodies and service needs.

Why not ready for canon promotion:

- early world documents often assume human-default ergonomics/environment without saying so;
- Imperial origin and demographic history are not specified for a multi-species state;
- first-contact chronology is absent;
- current named cast has no approved species reassignment;
- multi-species medical/ship/prison/school capacity has not been propagated into canon state sheets.

Current ruling:

> **DESIGN EXPANSION = PASS / CANON PROMOTION = HOLD.**

## 2. Major continuity traps

### RT-S01 — 612-system census reinterpretation

Problem:
- adding species can tempt a silent reinterpretation of the 76.0b registered population.

Control:
- total stays unchanged unless separately amended;
- species demographic allocation is a new layer beneath the locked total;
- no existing system population silently changes.

Severity if violated:
- S1 continuity break.

### RT-S02 — K-13 Academy demographic mismatch

Problem:
- a diverse galaxy with an all-human-coded opening can look contradictory.

Safe explanation candidate:
- K-13 recruitment/medical infrastructure and local corridor demographics skew human/posthuman.

Control:
- no named E1–100 character is retroactively changed to another species without direct scene-card/manuscript impact audit;
- background nonhuman presence requires facility/medical/visual consistency.

### RT-S03 — Empire history

Problem:
- a multi-species current Empire needs a historical answer for incorporation, citizenship and military service.

Options to later compare:
1. human-origin imperial expansion with later treaties/incorporation;
2. multi-species founding compact;
3. inherited relay-state built from several older polities;
4. layered hybrid: human-dominant early state transformed into a genuinely multi-species empire over centuries.

Recommendation:
- option 4 is likely continuity-friendliest but **author decision required** before canonization.

### RT-S04 — “one species = one faction”

Problem:
- easy worldbuilding shortcut produces racialized politics and shallow factions.

Control:
- every S1 species needs multiple factions/classes/cultures;
- species-majority states may exist, but major cross-species institutions must also exist.

### RT-S05 — body difference without infrastructure

Problem:
- visually alien species using human doors, toilets, beds, medicine, cuffs and cockpits without cost.

Control:
- S1 approval requires MS0–MS3 service mapping;
- at least one ordinary inconvenience and one emergency consequence must be demonstrated.

### RT-S06 — biological power creep

Problem:
- high-g, electrosense, longevity, fluid support or symbiosis can become free superpowers.

Control:
- every advantage carries metabolic, medical, infrastructure or tactical cost;
- no biology obsoletes professional training, ships, frames or logistics.

### RT-S07 — translation magic

Problem:
- perfect universal translation removes real multi-species consequences.

Control:
- trade/common language and machine translation can be strong, but legal/medical/historical translation retains provenance and ambiguity.

### RT-S08 — reproduction sensationalism

Problem:
- “can humans mate with X?” dominates species design or becomes surprise bloodline power.

Control:
- compatibility is pair-specific and medically governed;
- family can exist without genetic mixing;
- mixed ancestry is not automatic power-up.

### RT-S09 — posthuman/species category collapse

Problem:
- cybernetic people or enhanced soldiers are called new species despite no heritable lineage.

Control:
- species, augmentation and embodiment remain separate indexes.

### RT-S10 — precursor monopoly

Problem:
- all modern technology traced to one ancient alien people.

Control:
- multiple historical inputs;
- modern independent invention;
- precursor tech loses on support, production, law and maintainability where appropriate;
- AXIOM remains HOLD.

## 3. Prototype-program cross-audit

The 01–20 demonstrator architecture can support a multi-species world **without being redesigned around aliens**.

Safe additions to the historical question pool:

- cross-body-plan control translation;
- variable cockpit/restraint architecture;
- multi-atmosphere pilot capsule;
- nonhuman medical stop/biomonitoring;
- service-interface accessibility;
- non-auditory/nonvisual sensor presentation.

Important distinction:

> these can be secondary requirements inside existing prototype hypotheses; they do **not** require six new prototype numbers for six species.

07-specific ruling:

- 07’s `Open Service` value may later extend to mixed-species maintenance/service ecosystems;
- 07 must **not** become “compatible with every alien body and mind by default”;
- cockpit ergonomics/neural translation remain separate from service interoperability;
- a nonhuman pilot may require an adapter or may be unable to pilot 07 safely.

This protects both plausibility and 07’s established limits.

## 4. GA1–GA10 architecture impact

### GA1

Risk:
- opening overload.

Control:
- no species exposition arc;
- only subtle world breadth after scene-level review.

### GA2

Best insertion point:
- ship crews, clients, frontier ports and salvage markets.

Reason:
- mobility naturally shows food, cabins, translation and medical differences.

### GA3

Best structural payoff:
- city/public-standard design and first mass derivative.

Potential question:
- who counts as the “standard user” when Ardis publishes an open frame/service standard?

### GA4

High impact:
- succession legitimacy, houses, citizenship and founding covenant.

Hard requirement:
- species cannot become shorthand for claimant blocs.

### GA5

High payoff:
- mixed fleet logistics and berth/food/medicine/acceleration compromises.

### GA6

Very high emotional payoff:
- evacuation destination may be safe for one species and lethal/inadequate for another.

Red-team control:
- never use incompatibility to manufacture arbitrary deaths; capacity must be planted.

### GA7

Military doctrine:
- enemy adapts to species-specific sensing/environment where relevant;
- no side has a racial monopoly on a tactic.

### GA8

Deep-time species and extinct peoples can enter strongly here.

Control:
- not every Archive mystery becomes species archaeology.

### GA9

Excellent fit with existing classification/exclusion design:
- common profiles may treat uncommon physiology, family structure or service standards as low-confidence/nonstandard.

Potential gain:
- makes “보존할 가치가 없는 사람들” materially broader without changing the core theme.

### GA10

End-state payoff:
- plural standards must support biological difference without one universal body/citizen template.

## 5. Faction-gap impact

Existing QC found missing or weak coverage for:

- mercenary/pirate/black-market ecology;
- AI/nonhuman/alien civilization;
- religious/ideological/civic movements.

Species design solves **only part** of this.

Do not treat a species as the missing “alien faction.”

Required downstream split:

- species/civilization codex;
- species-majority or cross-species polities where needed;
- independent mercenary/irregular ecosystems;
- AI/composite communities;
- civic/religious movements.

One new alien empire cannot patch all missing classes.

## 6. Character-design controls

Every core species eventually needs at least:

- one ordinary worker/caregiver/citizen;
- one expert;
- one political or institutional actor;
- one character who rejects an external stereotype;
- one character who harms others for reasons not reducible to species;
- one cross-species relationship/household/crew tie.

Prohibition:
- first named member cannot permanently define the species.

---

## 7. Art/reader-memory controls

Species design must pass at three scales:

1. silhouette/body-plan recognition;
2. face/gesture/emotion readability without making them human in makeup;
3. ordinary clothing/equipment adaptation.

Reader-facing noun budget:
- no more than one major new species name in a dense early introduction sequence unless scene function demands otherwise;
- use profession/name first when species is not plot-relevant.

## 8. Decisions that remain author-level

The following must not be silently fixed:

1. exact number of S1 core species;
2. whether humanity is demographic majority and by how much;
3. Imperial founding relation to nonhuman peoples;
4. exact named species and homeworlds;
5. whether any core current species is fully non-humanoid to the point of requiring dedicated ships/frames;
6. interspecies reproductive compatibility rules;
7. whether AXIOM has any precursor-species relation;
8. whether any current protagonist/core ally is nonhuman or human-derived beyond existing canon.

Everything else can continue as reversible noncanon design.
