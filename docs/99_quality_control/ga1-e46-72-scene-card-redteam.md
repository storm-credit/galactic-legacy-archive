## 2. S1 Major Risks and Controls

### S1-01. Black Ward can become a policy seminar rather than webnovel drama

Risk:
- consent, identity, data, treatment, supply and governance create dense abstract discussion.

Required controls:
- every episode centers on a body, room, transfer, device, route, time or current decision.
- no scene introduces more than 2–3 new medical/legal terms.
- show apparatus, symptoms, loading, manual work, ordinary meals and treatment routines before institutional explanation.
- alternate modes:
  - E46–48 incident response/forensics.
  - E49–51 lockdown/public conflict.
  - E52–53 ordinary patient life.
  - E54–56 audit/technical/representation.
  - E57–58 relationships/AI interface.
  - E59–65 records/custody with E64 physical action.
  - E66–72 resource crisis/service sortie/seizure.

### S1-02. The Ward may look too balanced and designed to teach a thesis

Risk:
- every system has one clear good and one clear bad, making the institution feel author-constructed.

Controls:
- individual staff/patients disagree for history, career and current needs.
- one clinician/researcher should make a choice outside expected bloc.
- some abuses are banal inertia, understaffing or copied procedure rather than intentional philosophical control.
- some patients benefit without experiencing themselves as coerced.
- one local/adapter choice should be regretted and one proprietary choice genuinely welcomed.

### S1-03. Mia may become the always-correct moral authority

Required failures/limits:
- her allocation is clinically defensible but harms Haren/Orvan and may later prove one assumption wrong.
- she supports or seriously considers bounded Helix emergency command, opposing Nera/Rian.
- medical priority cannot solve identity, future care, representation or funding.
- one adapter/transport outcome worsens despite her process.
- licensed clinicians, patients and staff can overrule/correct her.
- she is still a trainee and cannot perform advanced medicine alone.

### S1-04. Nera’s open adapter can become the obvious heroic solution

Controls:
- compatibility bands and contraindications.
- serious Band B complication.
- high staff/monitoring load and shorter recalibration.
- Helix safety information improves the design.
- adapter does not serve all 23.
- some patients rationally choose Helix full protocol instead.
- version and incident reporting are mandatory; Nera does not own patient decisions.

### S1-05. Helix supply freeze can still look like hostage-taking

Controls:
- show debt, liability, unapproved modification and hidden-profile risk preceding the hold.
- Manufacturing/Frontier clinicians oppose total cutoff and support emergency care.
- Helix can stabilize all 23 more safely under its terms.
- its staff provide one indispensable safety correction and full-protocol outcome.
- harms arise through exclusive command, data, identity and long-term lock-in, not fake medicine.

### S1-06. Patient consent under emergency scarcity may be romanticized

Controls:
- disclose that choices are constrained by time, treatment dependency and unequal options.
- do not call every signed choice freely voluntary.
- patients can change decisions where operationally possible.
- guardian/capacity disputes remain.
- resources chosen by one patient affect others and are disclosed.
- emergency exceptions remain but are logged/reviewed.

### S1-07. Orvan may become a recovered plot device or Haren’s moral lesson

Controls:
- Orvan chooses current identity/treatment and refuses immediate return.
- has duties/relationships with other patients unrelated to Haren.
- information release remains partial.
- Haren cannot represent or command him.
- Orvan may choose a route Haren opposes later.
- survival does not instantly restore prior relationship.

### S1-08. ORA-3 can drift into omniscient AI or person-archive cliché

Controls:
- every answer specifies source type, confidence and permission.
- no complete memories/person restoration.
- one strategic fragment is genuinely deleted/withheld.
- ORA-3 contains pointers/clinical/consent fragments from several sources.
- physical records, patient testimony and external evidence remain necessary.
- E71 action is bounded by existing authority/permissions, not spontaneous god-AI awakening.

### S1-09. “Dead means legal closure” could become a one-trick reveal

New functions required:
- medicine/supply eligibility.
- current versus former identity choice.
- bundled records affecting other people.
- family/contract/debt consequences.
- correction, annotation, refusal and forgetting—not only exposing false death.

Rule:
- not every future dead person is secretly alive.
- physical death remains common and distinct.

### S1-10. Rian’s ace-pilot promise may disappear during medical arc

Required physical payoffs:
- E55 07 service diagnostic under medical purpose.
- E64 external-lock module hold.
- E69 coolant/power/medical-launch service maneuver.

Control:
- at least E69 provides clear ace movement spectacle.
- no combat power-up.
- Rian loses target/evidence under valid medical stop.
- he cannot solve interior clinical conflict from cockpit.

### S1-11. The imitation cell can feel like a contrived bridge to the Ward

Controls:
- C-9 grievances visible before/within incident: placement breakup, treatment loss and family guarantee.
- plan has a coherent nonlethal objective and actual participants.
- failure needs four causes: bypass, hidden treatment, competing reset and physical panic/error.
- no death inserted solely as plot key.
- members remain separately accountable and recur/face outcomes.
- Leta controls later disclosure/treatment choice.

### S1-12. Rian publicly denying the myth can sound like authorial scolding

Controls:
- Aven correctly accuses him of speaking after receiving special chances.
- Rian names concrete missing conditions and his own failures, not generic “don’t copy me.”
- statement costs support and helps Security.
- it does not resolve transfer inequality.
- future institutions must make safe refusal accessible, not rely on speeches.

### S1-13. The Neutral corridor can feel like a convenient rescue valve

Controls:
- only 8 high-dependency + up to 10 lower-dependency capacity.
- receiving care/identity/claims remain.
- staff/equipment leave with patients, weakening Ward.
- not asylum or free care.
- legal/custody delay and corridor threat.
- some patients refuse or are unsafe to move.

### S1-14. No deaths in the supply crisis may make stakes feel softened

Assessment:
- acceptable because White Dock already established deaths and this arc centers on treatment/agency rather than body-count escalation.

Required cost:
- one serious adapter complication.
- one deterioration/missed transfer due distributed delay.
- permanent information loss.
- staff/equipment depletion.
- institutional/funding collapse.
- some patients enter restrictive contracts or separate from communities.

Do not add a death merely to prove seriousness unless the causal design requires it and later consequences are tracked.

### S1-15. Temporary plural oversight can appear to be the predetermined correct answer

Controls:
- Helix unified control would likely reduce immediate medical risk for some/all patients.
- distributed delays cause visible harm in E71.
- split system is underfunded and unstable.
- patients choose centralized/proprietary routes too.
- emergency authority and local adapter can both fail.
- E73–100 must revise the arrangement and show capture/slow-response risks.
