## 0. Author clarification captured

The author reaffirmed the intended series behavior:

> Rian is the protagonist, but the story must also move through situations that occur without him. Characters, factions, institutions and fronts continue acting while he is absent.

This is **not a new replacement of existing canon**. It reinforces rules already present in main:

- the post-E100 production standard requires at least one non-Rian autonomous decisive action in most five-episode windows;
- secondary POV is allowed for autonomous decisions Rian cannot witness;
- final-series design reduces Rian's ownership/control even as his responsibility increases;
- late-series fronts have separate mandates and victory conditions.

This audit asks a stronger question than “does a non-Rian POV exist?”

> **Does causality itself continue when Rian is not present?**

---

## 1. Five levels that must not be confused

### L0 — Non-Rian presence

Another character is physically present.

This proves almost nothing.

### L1 — Non-Rian POV

The camera moves to another character.

Still insufficient if the character only thinks about Rian, waits for Rian or reacts to Rian.

### L2 — Autonomous decision

Another actor makes a decision without Rian's permission and accepts its cost.

This is already a main production-standard requirement.

### L3 — Independent causal chain

An actor/faction/institution has:
- a goal that existed before the current Rian intervention;
- its own resources, deadline and opposition;
- at least one state change triggered by its own pressure;
- consequences that continue even if Rian is elsewhere.

### L4 — Parallel front

Two or more L3 chains progress at the same time and later:
- intersect;
- collide;
- exchange resources;
- contradict each other;
- create a delayed consequence;
- or force a new choice when the fronts reconnect.

**Target series behavior: L3–L4.**

## 2. Existing main-authority evidence

### 2.1 Production standard

Main already requires:

> at least one non-Rian autonomous decisive action appears in most 5-episode windows.

Every episode card also tracks:
- active characters and independent goals;
- active institutions/factions and current authority;
- decisive choice and who makes it;
- carried-forward state.

Therefore protagonist-independent agency is not optional decoration after E100.

### 2.2 POV standard

Main POV calibration establishes:
- Rian close third as default;
- bounded Haren/Nera/Serin/Mia/Ern/local/institutional POV;
- secondary POV enters only for autonomous decisions Rian cannot witness;
- suggested full-series POV share keeps Rian at 55–65%, not 100%.

This means the series is protagonist-centered but structurally ensemble-capable.

### 2.3 Series endpoint

The E101–1100 final audit states:
- Rian's responsibility expands while direct ownership of people/institutions/assets decreases;
- E1100 has no standing central command or master key;
- institutions demonstrate crisis response without him.

This is the strongest confirmation that the endpoint is not “Rian becomes the galaxy.”

## 3. Adversarial failure modes

### F-01 — Satellite cast

Every named character has a job, but their real function is:
- advise Rian;
- oppose Rian;
- admire Rian;
- be rescued by Rian;
- wait for Rian's next order.

**FAIL.**

### F-02 — Reaction-only antagonist

The enemy's plan begins only after Rian acts.

If Rian sleeps for a month, the villain also effectively sleeps.

**FAIL.**

### F-03 — Permission laundering

A local leader technically makes the choice, but only after Rian explains the right answer and gives moral permission.

**FAIL unless the leader could credibly choose another option.**

### F-04 — Cutaway theater

The story switches POV only to show the reader information that could have arrived as a report, with no autonomous choice or cost.

**CUT or compress.**

### F-05 — Offscreen deus ex machina

A faction acts independently offscreen, but the action exists only to conveniently rescue Rian at the exact moment required.

**FAIL unless prior goal/resource/deadline predicts it.**

### F-06 — Credit absorption

Non-Rian actors solve the problem, but the final narration/social order attributes success to Rian because he “connected them.”

**FAIL.**

### F-07 — Frozen world outside camera

No trade, election, military movement, family decision, industrial failure, faction split or enemy adaptation occurs until the protagonist arrives.

**FAIL for a 612-system setting.**

### F-08 — False simultaneity

The design claims several fronts exist, but only one has:
- clock;
- resources;
- casualties;
- failure conditions;
- irreversible results.

Other fronts are labels, not causal systems.

**FAIL.**

### F-09 — Ensemble dilution

The opposite failure: too many POVs and parallel clocks make the reader forget whose emotional journey anchors the work.

**FAIL.**

Control:
- Rian remains primary attachment and largest POV share;
- many autonomous events may stay offscreen and arrive through consequences/reports;
- POV rotation is earned by decision value, not by world-scale vanity.

## 4. The Rian-Removal Counterfactual

For any major faction/front, ask:

> **If Rian disappeared from the story for the next ten episodes, what would this actor do anyway?**

A credible answer contains:
1. current objective;
2. current deadline;
3. current resources;
4. current opponent/problem;
5. next decision;
6. likely cost;
7. state that will be different when Rian later intersects the front.

Bad answer:
> “They wait to see what Rian does.”

Good answer shape:
> “They proceed with the payroll/route/claim/evacuation decision under their own authority. Rian later encounters a changed contract, damaged asset, new coalition or dead opportunity.”

Important:
- the counterfactual does **not** require the same outcome without Rian;
- Rian may be crucial;
- the world must still **move**, even if it moves toward a worse result.

## 5. Existing-series sample audit

### GA1 — Opening / Academy

Verdict: **PASS WITH INTENTIONAL PROTAGONIST-CONCENTRATION EXCEPTION.**

Reason:
- first 20 episodes are intentionally predominantly Rian to stabilize attachment;
- this is explicitly allowed by the POV harness.

But by E33–38 the cards already show independent current authority:
- Juno leaves active Academy-cell status rather than being recruited;
- Nera rejects the theft path on technical/ownership grounds;
- Haren forces the group to choose a component to abandon;
- Serin independently controls chain-of-custody interpretation;
- workers preserve claims and physical assets;
- Helix helps stabilize equipment because its own people/claim are at risk, not because it joined Rian.

Risk:
- GA1 prose revision must preserve these independent refusal/authority beats rather than compress them into Rian's internal realization.

### GA2 — First ship / route contracts

Verdict: **STRONG PASS.**

Examples:
- Theo accepts/rejects contract clauses under economic/legal authority;
- Maren sets the rescue diversion and retreat threshold;
- Ella's medical stop blocks the fastest intercept;
- temporary workers/passengers can refuse;
- tug occupants retain rights over life-support-dependent cargo;
- E125 explicitly prevents Rian from making the final allocation alone.

Causal structure:
- ship, patient, witness, insurer, tug and opportunist lines move on separate clocks.

### GA3 — Ardis

Verdict: **VERY STRONG PASS / MODEL CASE.**

Ardis exists before Rian:
- 1.42m permanent residents;
- ~170k transient/stranded;
- functioning worker, hospital, school, market, route and local government systems.

E219–235 repeatedly gives decisive authority to:
- Transit receiving workers;
- Foundry Shift Council;
- Civic identity/service offices;
- Garden crews;
- Toma/Anchor traffic board;
- six constituency contacts;
- local maintainers.

Rian's future priority is explicitly overridden by current local evidence.

This is the clearest template for future species normalization:
> nonhuman citizens should already be living and acting in Ardis before Rian notices their significance.

### GA4 — Succession conflict

Verdict: **VERY STRONG PASS.**

Final cross-audit lists autonomous decisive actors across:
- Ardis Trust/current fields;
- four claimants;
- provincial/affected bodies;
- cooperatives;
- advocates;
- loss families and crews;
- participating, partial, observing and outside jurisdictions.

Rian is explicitly not:
- claimant;
- judge;
- nominator;
- ratifier;
- Crown Reference candidate;
- tie-break.

The succession system therefore moves through multiple authentic authority chains without requiring protagonist control.

### GA5 — Ownerless fleet

Verdict: **VERY STRONG PASS.**

Final audit identifies at least six decisive successes dependent on autonomous non-Rian choices.

Independent captains and service actors:
- save populations/assets;
- refuse or accept missions;
- preserve routes;
- absorb losses;
- retain local command/nonmembership.

Rian links geometry/handoffs but does not own the network.

### GA6 — Second Orpheus

Verdict: **VERY STRONG PASS.**

Independent decisions include:
- physical stops;
- local command coordination;
- member refusal;
- holdout guarantees;
- Ella's medical-carrier separation;
- Ardo preserving two commands;
- Maren/Vera/Wren commitments;
- local late transfers;
- receiving/medical decisions.

Permanent deaths/losses occur through independent front decisions, not only Rian's immediate presence.

### GA7 — Blood Admiral war

Verdict: **STRONG PASS.**

The entire mystery rejects one-person causality:
- doctrine;
- staff omission;
- credential lineage;
- current decision;
- propaganda/memory
remain separate layers.

Haren receives and abuses a real bounded authority independently of Rian.

Route bodies can:
- join;
- partially join;
- observe;
- refuse;
without majority coercion.

This is parallel institutional causality, not protagonist-versus-villain causality.

### GA8 — Record war

Verdict: **PASS BY FULL-SERIES AUDIT; SPOT-CHECK RECOMMENDED BEFORE MANUSCRIPT PRODUCTION.**

Known structural requirement:
- living archives/AI communities retain independent preferences and custody;
- evidence/provenance cannot collapse into Rian's truth key.

Risk:
- mystery-heavy prose can accidentally pull every revelation back into Rian POV.

Control:
- preserve witness/archive/community decisions whose consequences reach Rian later.

### GA9 — Preservation regime

Verdict: **PASS BY FULL-SERIES AUDIT; HIGH PROTAGONIST-CENTRALIZATION RISK BY DESIGN.**

Reason:
- GA9 deliberately tests Rian's earned central command and dictator temptation.

Required counterweight:
- regional compliance/resistance/service actors must continue making decisions he does not witness;
- central command may be useful without becoming epistemically omniscient;
- front outcomes must still depend on local interpretation and refusal.

### GA10 — Distributed handoff

Verdict: **MAXIMUM PASS / ENDPOINT MODEL.**

Five autonomous fronts plus Rian reserve retain:
- separate mandates;
- separate assets;
- separate victory conditions;
- separate surrender/exit decisions.

Rian can command only his accepted reserve craft and cannot alter local production/service priorities.

This proves the intended endpoint:
> the galaxy can coordinate with Rian without becoming Rian's organization.

## 7. Proposed Parallel-Causality Harness for future expansion

This section is QC guidance for PR #188-derived work. It does not replace the existing canon production standard.

### PC-1 — Independent objective

Every recurring major:
- faction;
- house;
- service order;
- mercenary network;
- species-majority institution;
- regional government
must be able to state its current objective without using Rian's name.

### PC-2 — Independent clock

At least one real pressure must advance when Rian is absent:
- election;
- payroll;
- repair;
- succession;
- migration;
- route window;
- siege;
- disease;
- contract;
- family/care deadline;
- resource depletion;
- investigation;
- enemy operation.

### PC-3 — Independent choice

At least one relevant actor can choose an option Rian dislikes or never learns about until later.

### PC-4 — Offscreen state change

Not every autonomous event needs a POV scene.

A good world can change offscreen through:
- damaged asset;
- expired contract;
- changed officeholder;
- broken alliance;
- new debt;
- death/loss already caused by authorized current events;
- migration;
- resource movement;
- legal ruling;
- local victory/failure.

The later scene should encounter the changed state, not retroactively invent it.

### PC-5 — Delayed intersection

Preferred structure:

```text
Front A acts without Rian
        ↓
state changes / cost paid
        ↓
Front B reacts independently
        ↓
Rian later encounters consequence
        ↓
Rian must choose within a world he did not design
```

This is stronger than:

```text
Rian acts
  ↓
every faction reacts
  ↓
Rian chooses again
```

### PC-6 — No credit absorption

When independent actors succeed:
- preserve their authorship;
- preserve their authority;
- preserve their claim to the result;
- do not convert the result into Rian's inventory or political capital by default.

### PC-7 — Antagonist autonomy

Major adversarial groups need:
- internal goals;
- conflict with other enemies/allies;
- logistics;
- replacement/succession;
- plans that sometimes target objectives unrelated to Rian.

The protagonist may be one problem among several.

### PC-8 — Ordinary-life autonomy

At least some offscreen movement must be neither military nor conspiracy:
- marriage/divorce;
- school decisions;
- work stoppage;
- migration;
- trade;
- debt;
- medical care;
- inheritance;
- religious/civic activity;
- art/sport/community life.

This is particularly important for the new species layer.

## 8. Parallel-front portfolio — recommended, not a quota

For a mature subact, a useful design shape is usually **2–4 active causal clocks**, not 8–12 visible POVs.

Possible clocks:
1. protagonist/mission;
2. local/institutional or ally;
3. antagonist/competitor;
4. civilian/service/family/economic.

Only some need on-page POV.

The rest may be tracked author-side and surface as consequences.

This keeps the galaxy alive without turning the novel into a disconnected anthology.

## 9. Species / house / order implications for PR #188

### Species

A species is not alive because it has:
- anatomy;
- cuisine;
- a homeworld.

Before front-stage promotion, at least one recurring member/institution should have a problem that continues when Rian leaves.

Examples:
- housing vote;
- medical standards lawsuit;
- family succession;
- labor strike;
- route migration;
- religious schism;
- local election;
- trade dispute.

Do not make every species plot “Rian learns about their biology.”

### Houses

Each important house should have:
- succession;
- property/industry;
- allies/enemies;
- marriage/adoption/guardianship or office problem;
- one current plan unrelated to recruiting/opposing Rian.

### Knight/service orders

Each order should have:
- mission doctrine;
- budget/patronage;
- promotion/succession;
- current deployment;
- failure that can happen before Rian arrives.

### Mercenary/gray networks

They should negotiate with:
- insurers;
- ports;
- employers;
- rivals;
- dependents;
not just hunt Rian.

### Factions

Every major faction requires at least one conflict with another faction that **does not pass through Rian**.

## 10. POV control — world autonomy without POV sprawl

Use a non-Rian POV only if at least one is true:
1. the decisive action is impossible for Rian to witness;
2. the actor's choice changes future state before Rian can react;
3. emotional ownership of the cost belongs to that actor;
4. showing only the later report would erase agency or ambiguity.

Prefer report/consequence instead of POV when:
- no choice occurs;
- the only purpose is villain information;
- the scene repeats facts Rian will learn immediately;
- the cutaway exists merely to prove the world is big.

No omniscient hopping.

---

## 11. GA9 special guardrail

GA9 is the intentional danger zone because Rian finally holds powerful central authority.

Before manuscript production of each GA9 subact, ask:

1. what changed outside Rian's command since the previous appearance?
2. what local actor refused/modified/implemented his order differently?
3. what outcome did central command fail to see?
4. what local success occurred without him?
5. what cost is caused by delay between center and front?

If all five answers reduce to “Rian predicted it,” the subact requires re-audit.

---

## 12. Expansion stop conditions

Do not add another POV/front merely because:
- a new species exists;
- a new house exists;
- a new mecha lineage exists;
- a new faction name exists.

Add a front only when it has:
- independent goal;
- independent clock;
- independent decision;
- material consequence;
- later intersection or payoff.

Otherwise keep it as background ecology.

---

## 13. Final ruling

Existing series architecture:

> **PASS — protagonist-centered / ensemble-causal / increasingly multi-front.**

PR #188 expansion rule:

> **Every new major people, house, order, faction or regional institution must pass the Rian-Removal Counterfactual before front-stage promotion.**

This does not require more POV chapters.

It requires a world that continues to choose, pay costs and change while the protagonist is elsewhere.

Canon / manuscript / scene-card modification: **NONE**.
