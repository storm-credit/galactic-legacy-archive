## 1. Visual Similarity Failure Paths

| ID | Failure path | Severity | Why it matters | Guard |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| V-01 | **Same pretty-face syndrome** | S1 | Hair/color changes hide near-identical jaw/eye/nose/head proportions. Core cast becomes palette swaps. | Hair-off grayscale head test + same-light neutral-expression test. |
| V-02 | **Role stereotype becomes face design** | S2 | Mechanic=Nera with goggles/grime, medic=Mia with saintly clean face, hacker=Serin neon/cold face. | Start from existing behavior/posture identity; stereotype prop may not be the primary identifier. |
| V-03 | **Reference recombination clone** | S1 | Face from A + hair from B + coat from C still lands on a familiar famous archetype when seen together. | Whole-design collision pass after component extraction; three-source firewall is necessary but not sufficient. |
| V-04 | **Same-uniform collapse** | S1 | Academy characters are only distinguishable once accessories return. | Uniform-normalized test must still separate face/posture. |
| V-05 | **Props carry 100% of identity** | S2 | Remove Nera's tool or Serin's case and everyone becomes the same soldier. | Props-off silhouette/gesture test. |
| V-06 | **Late-series officer convergence** | S1 | Rian/Haren/Ern and others all mature into long coats, straight posture and dark palettes. | Mature-state boards reviewed together; no shared `prestige silhouette` default. |
| V-07 | **Facial damage as cheap differentiator** | S1 | New scars/injuries are invented because visual teams need variation. | Exact injury/scar must come from approved canon; otherwise `NEW_CANON_REQUIRED: YES-STOP`. |
| V-08 | **Real-person/actor leakage** | S1 | Prompting around a celebrity face creates unintended likeness and narrows originality. | Fictional structural references only by default; no `face of [actor]` production instruction. |
| V-09 | **Species/ethnicity inference by convenience** | S0/S1 | Art direction silently fixes a biological/species/ethnic trait still HOLD in canon. | Check current character/species canon before final face lock; unresolved traits remain unresolved. |
| V-10 | **AI-generated face drift across sheets** | S2 | Same named character changes facial geometry between illustrations; different characters converge on model-default face. | Maintain original character anchor sheet after author approval; compare frontal/3-quarter/profile consistency. |

### Ruling

The current project's 64px silhouette test is necessary but **not sufficient**. Final production needs both:

- **body/silhouette collision audit**, and
- **face-geometry collision audit**.

Neither audit creates canon facts; they only reject ambiguous or derivative renderings.

## 2. Reference-Process Failure Paths

### R-01 — One franchise quietly becomes the house style

Example failure:
- use Gundam for mecha silhouette,
- Gundam school-uniform rhythm for characters,
- Gundam rival framing,
- Gundam ending tableau.

Even if no single design is copied, the project begins to feel like a reskin.

Guard:
- no single franchise may be the primary source for more than one adjacent axis in the same final packet;
- closest-genre references are primarily **collision checks**, not creative sources.

### R-02 — `moodboard authority` overrides canon

Failure:
- a cool image suggests a coat, scar, prosthetic, weapon, insignia or vehicle that is not in canon;
- team treats it as harmless visual flavor;
- flavor then implies rank, injury, technology or relationship.

Guard:
- external reference can change **presentation**, never story fact;
- any fact-bearing visual element routes through normal canon change control.

### R-03 — excessive reference count destroys a coherent identity

Failure:
- every detail comes from a different source;
- result is technically non-copied but visually noisy and forgettable.

Guard:
- internal identity sentence remains the design spine;
- external references may clarify no more than 2–3 major design decisions per production brief.

### R-04 — negative reference list becomes a creativity cage

Failure:
- fear of similarity removes all coats, all white lines, all scars, all modular ships, etc.

Guard:
- common genre vocabulary is allowed;
- collision is judged by **combined salient axes**, not by one generic feature.

## 4. Hidden Interaction Risks

### 4.1 Visual maturity and ending authority can accidentally reinforce each other

If every character's `ending look` becomes cleaner, darker and more formal, the epilogue visually says:

> success = becoming an officer/elite institution.

That contradicts the series' plural-authority design.

Guard:
- ending visual change should show **different kinds of adulthood**:
  - Rian: reduced command-signaling / loosened body tension;
  - Haren: functional representative/route work without Blood-Admiral glamor;
  - Nera: readable shared-work standards;
  - Serin: plural provenance rather than one Archive brand;
  - Mia: service/public-health responsibility;
  - Ern: industrial/route competence retained despite leadership.

These are already present in the internal visual-direction document; do not replace them with one prestigious house style.

### 4.2 Collectibility can mutate into ownership

The reader should want to remember/revisit 07, Parus and major people. But the ending can fail if `collectible payoff` becomes:

- Rian owns the machine;
- Rian gathers the people;
- Rian's museum contains every relic;
- Rian's title validates everyone's future.

Guard:
- the final collection payoff is **distributed legacy**, not protagonist inventory.

### 4.3 Anti-similarity can accidentally erase iconicity

If every strong silhouette or motif is rejected because something vaguely resembles another work, designs become bland.

Guard:
- generic motifs are allowed;
- require only that the **specific combination and narrative use** remain original;
- internal recall hook must still work without naming any reference.

## 5. Mandatory Production Checks Before Final Character Art

For each core character:

1. `CANON FACT CHECK` — age, injuries, role, authority, species/cyborg state, relationship state.
2. `FACE COLLISION` — hair-off grayscale, same-light neutral head lineup.
3. `SILHOUETTE COLLISION` — 64px full body.
4. `UNIFORM COLLISION` — common institutional layer normalized.
5. `PROPS-OFF COLLISION` — tool/weapon removed.
6. `REFERENCE COLLISION` — nearest three fictional similarities named internally as veto checks; no direct copying.
7. `ARC COLLISION` — mature/ending states must not converge on the same prestige silhouette.
8. `NEW_CANON_REQUIRED` — YES means stop; art cannot invent the missing fact.

PASS standard:
- identity survives at least **three of four** non-color channels: face geometry, posture/motion, silhouette/material, work interaction;
- no single external character supplies more than one major salient axis;
- no three-axis resemblance cluster to one recognizable fictional character.
