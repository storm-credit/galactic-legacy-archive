## 2. S1 Risks

### S1-01. Opening cast count remains high

Registered people/representatives:
- P-001, H-001, H-002, S-001, M-001, I-001, E-001, Imperial, Neutral, Helix, AI witness, community focal characters.

Risk:
- 12 identities by episode 100 is manageable but only if introduction is staggered.

Control:
- E1–20 front: P-001/H-001/H-002, brief E-001/Neutral.
- E21–45 add S-001/I-001/Imperial/Helix.
- E46–72 add M-001/AI witness/community focal.
- do not introduce all senior faction leaders.
- retain 3–5 front-stage characters per mini-arc.

### S1-02. Representatives may feel engineered to cover themes

Risk:
- each person cleanly represents one issue: records, medicine, contracts, neutrality, imperial continuity.

Control:
- add ordinary desires, friendships, resentments, habits, and conflicts unrelated to formal role.
- make at least one representative act outside expected institutional position.
- cross-role competence/weakness: Neutral officer has crew/family stakes; Helix evaluator has safety history; Imperial observer has identity-restoration background.
- sample scenes must test voice without policy speeches.

### S1-03. Academy numbers can make hidden programs too visible

Risk:
- 3,000 residents and 100+ medical/administrative staff make complete secrecy implausible.

Control:
- Black Ward existence is semi-known; coercive purposes and identities are compartmented.
- deaths/transfers split across legal categories and external facilities.
- many staff know pieces, not total system.
- whistleblowing has evidence, jurisdiction, livelihood and patient-care consequences.

### S1-04. Official/actual mortality may be either too mild or too extreme

Current:
- official 0.6–1.2%, actual 1.4–2.7% annual student mortality.

Assessment:
- plausible for dangerous orbital correction/military program while preserving investment logic.
- severe injuries and coercive transfers carry much of the horror without death-game inflation.

Control:
- compare with in-world regular academy, industrial labor and frontier militia later.
- do not show fatal incident every few episodes.

### S1-05. First-100 registry still risks spreadsheet fiction

Control:
- Archive displays average below one per episode after E20.
- rights/institutions are experienced through changed access and choices, not status lists.
- codes are project IDs, not necessarily reader-facing names.
- cut/merge any target unused within 15 episodes.

### S1-06. Legal mechanisms may seem too powerful

Control:
- Neutral law gives time, evidence and transaction cost—not moral immunity.
- Imperial/Helix/Academy retain claims.
- charter is provisional, underfunded, revocable and limited to one institution.
- every right requires physical/security/funding implementation.

### S1-07. P-001 can still appear to create every institutional innovation

Control:
- D-001 route requires H-001.
- maintenance/design-right framework originates H-002 conflict.
- provenance/privacy framework originates S-001.
- medical consent/stop authority originates M-001.
- neutral custody originates R-NEU-001.
- multi-party locks require institutional vetoes.
- student assembly/preference process must have organizers beyond P-001.

### S1-08. Enemy ace restraint could reduce threat

Control:
- restraint costs operative/capture/objective.
- E-001 can use lethal force against armed targets.
- radical escort and time pressure create danger.
- future conflicts include genuine incompatible goals and possible combat.
