## 2. S1 Risks and Required Controls

### S1-01. Non-canon samples can quietly become the first draft

Risk:
- polished prose may be copied into manuscript once the gate opens, bypassing reader feedback and episode-level final review.

Controls:
- every file is marked TEST MATERIAL / NOT CANONICAL MANUSCRIPT.
- samples compress multiple episode beats and therefore cannot be treated as final episode text.
- no manuscript branch may import them verbatim without a decision-log entry and full scene/prose review.
- human feedback should revise the harness, not merely copy-edit these samples.

### S1-02. White Dock action may still depend on author-side map knowledge

Risk:
- repair arm, archive, cargo spine, connector, civilian tug and security frames produce invisible 3D complexity.

Controls:
- one main physical objective at a time.
- use stable nouns, no synonyms for the same structure.
- distance/direction introduced before rapid action.
- Rian’s line of sight and sensor limits explicit.
- human participants draw a three-object diagram.

Failure trigger:
- if fewer than 70% place Rian/Ern/connector correctly, reduce zone nouns and add one landmark/reorientation beat.

### S1-03. Medium-sentence style may still feel chopped because of one-line paragraphs

Risk:
- avoiding consecutive grammatical fragments does not automatically avoid artificial mobile staccato.

Controls:
- evaluate paragraph frequency, not only sentence length.
- merge isolated one-line explanatory paragraphs that do not mark choice/impact.
- retain short lines for reversal, decision, physical hit or emotional recognition.

Failure trigger:
- more than 40% of readers report “끊긴다/각본 같다/AI 문체 같다.”

### S1-04. Technical realism can slow action

White Dock risk:
- thruster plume, heat, tether, radiator and pressure systems all appear in one sequence.

Central-key risk:
- six domains, service wheel, physical pins and local keys.

Controls:
- every term must alter the next action.
- remove terms readers cannot recall and that do not affect decision.
- no physics explanation after the reader already understands the physical consequence.

### S1-05. Ern can still seem like a destined noble rival

Sample risk:
- he withholds lethal fire, helps a structure and appears visually competent.

Controls outside/inside sample:
- he steals partial records and leaves.
- his operation helps create entry conditions for radicals.
- Sian Tor is abandoned/injured partly due his restraint and priorities.
- he blocks Rian from capturing/controlling willing Independence people later.
- human test asks whether readers incorrectly saw alliance/recruitment.

### S1-06. Central-key sample may emotionally favor distribution despite factual balance

Risk:
- Rian is protagonist; the narration may frame key division as courageous even while admitting a casualty.

Controls:
- central control visibly saves named/current people first.
- Imperial/centralist demand is stated as rational and correct about immediate danger.
- Mia is willing to recentralize if a medical threshold is crossed.
- the delayed loss is not described as noble or necessary by omniscient narration.
- Rian orders the cost recorded.
- human test requires participants to articulate the pro-central case.

Failure trigger:
- more than 30% say central control had no legitimate value or was only villain power.

### S1-07. The central-key interface can feel like a convenient magic computer

Controls:
- physical service wheel and pins.
- preexisting conflicting keys/commands.
- 07 only translates/bridges one emergency layer.
- no new energy, repair, information or legal consent is created.
- local operators and manual keys remain necessary.
- one segment fails/delays.

Before canonical action:
- a simple author-side diagram and exact central route/timing remain required.

### S1-08. ORA-3 may still be remembered as “memory AI” despite disclaimers

Risk:
- readers simplify any system connected to dead/closed people as stored consciousness.

Controls:
- first answer explicitly rejects the category.
- interface shows four source types.
- legal identity judgment remains outside its authority.
- patient contact occurs as a current-person request, not AI simulation.
- deletion removes a data fragment, not a soul/person.

Failure trigger:
- more than 30% describe ORA-3 as copied dead people after reading.

### S1-09. Consent can be presented as clean individual choice despite dependency

Controls:
- treatment and protection dependency remain visible.
- deletion cannot erase all external derivatives.
- contact/disclosure choices affect family/community and evidence.
- no claim that a button press resolves structural coercion.
- patient representatives and clinicians disagree.

### S1-10. ORA dialogue may frustrate readers through repeated refusal

Controls:
- every refusal should redirect to a more useful answer, person or action.
- by the end, team obtains identity-closure process proof and a contact request.
- one real clue is lost, producing cost rather than endless tease.

Failure trigger:
- readers say “아무것도 안 알려준다” without recognizing the process proof.

### S1-11. Package sample compresses scenes into a briefing and may falsely validate exposition

Risk:
- internal clarity score could encourage writing E73–83 as one long meeting.

Controls:
- this is an information-density stress test, not final pacing model.
- canonical scene cards distribute options across recruiters, clinics, work shifts, routes and departures.
- final manuscript must preserve lived demonstrations before/around the summary.

### S1-12. Package labels can oversimplify faction diversity

Controls:
- Darin/Sera/Aven/Amel/Bram disagree inside and across package labels.
- Imperial and Helix internal splits remain in surrounding scenes.
- the sample gives one benefit/cost pair for recall, not a complete moral definition.

### S1-13. Numbers may create false precision and reader fatigue

Controls:
- 11 days, 1,442, 30.2% and 46/49 are the main retained figures.
- 8.7/1.6 are explicit test candidates for replacement by an intuitive fraction.
- exact author-side budget indices do not appear repeatedly in canonical prose.

Failure trigger:
- fewer than 50% understand the meaning of 8.7/1.6 or more than 40% flag them as clutter.

### S1-14. Voice distinction remains under-tested

Risk:
- clear functional authority can still sound like every character speaks in precise PM language.

Controls:
- issue #26 includes blind microtest.
- target ≥70% functional/character identification.
- later dialogue samples must include ordinary humor, conflict and nonprofessional life, not only crisis competence.

### S1-15. Clarity does not equal commercial hook

Risk:
- internally coherent institutional prose may not be emotionally addictive.

Controls:
- human test separately scores continuation interest and caring.
- ask where readers skim and what they want next.
- a sample with high factual accuracy but interest below 3.0 fails commercially.
