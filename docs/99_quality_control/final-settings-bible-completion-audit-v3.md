## 6. Red-Team Final Attacks

### Is the project simply “distributed governance is good”?

No.

Locked counterevidence:
- central command prevents real deaths and wins efficiently;
- Aurel restores medicine, identity, pay and order;
- distributed approval causes delays and preventable deaths;
- local institutions can coerce, exclude and be captured;
- some regions voluntarily retain synchronized services/defense.

### Is Rian still the hidden owner of everyone’s future?

No by design authority.

- people and factions can refuse and leave;
- major targets are integrated by others;
- successors are selected by local/crew/civic institutions;
- Rian’s future index and master authority are lost;
- 07호, 회랑새, fleets, records and institutions have independent final custody.

### Are Imperial and Helix systems straw men?

No.

- they provide real services, safety, production, law and mobility;
- their representatives and ordinary beneficiaries remain sympathetic;
- their coercion arises through real institutional power, not cartoon evil.

### Is ancient technology secretly stronger?

No.

- legacy systems offer service interoperability and fallback;
- they are insecure, inefficient and labor-heavy;
- modern certified systems outperform them in supported conditions.

### Is Palimpsest an empty lore dungeon?

No.

- inhabited 11-system region;
- local services, economies, governments, AI/person households and refusal rights;
- synchronization consumes current resources and consent.

### Does the ending solve the galaxy too neatly?

No.

- service inequality, temporary trusts, local capture and defense delays remain;
- some regions remain synchronized;
- collapsed corridors remain under outside support;
- no universal implementation or complete historical agreement exists.
