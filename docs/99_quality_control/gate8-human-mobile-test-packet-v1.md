## 1. Purpose

This packet allows a person unfamiliar with the full repository to test whether the novel’s prose harness actually communicates the intended experience.

It does not ask whether the participant agrees with the project’s political or ethical conclusions.
It asks whether they can:

- follow action and space;
- understand current objectives and costs;
- distinguish character authority and motives;
- understand incomplete AI/record evidence;
- retain package/charter choices on mobile;
- want to continue reading.

## 2. Participant Rules

Recommended participants:
- minimum diagnostic: 5.
- preferred: 10–20.
- at least half use a phone-sized display.
- participants should not read setting explanations before the sample.

Do not:
- explain terms before reading.
- correct answers during the test.
- allow access to the author-side self-audit until all answers are collected.

Record:
- device type.
- reading time.
- whether rereading occurred.
- skipped/skimmed section.
- confusion stated in the participant’s own words.

## 3. Test Order

To reduce fatigue, one participant does not need to read all four long samples.

Recommended assignment:

- Group A: White Dock action + short voice test.
- Group B: Central key + package/charter sample.
- Group C: ORA-3 + short voice test.

At least 5 independent reads per sample.

## 4. Sample A — White Dock

File:
- [[gate8-noncanon-white-dock-action-sample-v1]]

Read only the reader-facing sample first.

### Immediate recall questions

1. At the beginning, what was Rian trying to stop Ern from doing?
2. Why could 07 not fly directly at Ern at maximum speed?
3. What event changed the main objective?
4. What did Rian choose instead of capturing Ern?
5. What part of 07 was lost, and why?
6. Did Ern become Rian’s ally?
7. Name one action that belonged to a local worker or another cell rather than Rian.

### Spatial questions

8. Were the archive, damaged connector and civilian tug all in the same exact place?
9. Which direction/objective did Rian abandon, and which did he move toward?
10. Draw a very simple three-object diagram if willing: Rian/07, Ern, damaged connector.

### Reader response

- clarity 1–5.
- action excitement 1–5.
- emotional investment 1–5.
- continuation interest 1–5.
- terms/locations that caused confusion.

Pass:
- objective/cost ≥80% correct.
- spatial relation ≥70% correct.
- continuation interest average ≥3.5.

## 5. Sample B — Central Key

File:
- [[gate8-noncanon-central-key-action-sample-v1]]

### Immediate recall questions

1. What immediate crisis did central control solve?
2. Give one example of a life/safety benefit after Rian took control.
3. Which additional powers made the same system politically dangerous?
4. Why could Rian not simply announce that everyone now controls their own area?
5. What physical actions were needed to divide authority?
6. What harm occurred during the slower transition?
7. Did the scene claim Rian’s choice saved the most people immediately?
8. Who made decisions other than Rian?

### Interpretation prompt

In one or two sentences, explain why someone reasonable might want Rian to keep the key.
Then explain why someone reasonable might still want the key divided.

Pass:
- immediate central benefit ≥80%.
- physical distribution cost ≥80%.
- false inference “central control was useless/evil” ≤30%.
- false inference “distribution caused no real harm” ≤30%.

## 6. Sample C — ORA-3

File:
- [[gate8-noncanon-ora3-consent-dialogue-sample-v1]]

### Immediate recall questions

1. Was ORA-3 the restored mind of Orvan or another dead person?
2. What kinds of information did ORA-3 distinguish?
3. What useful fact/process could it prove?
4. Why did Haren not simply demand Orvan’s location and return?
5. What information did the patient ask to delete?
6. What minimum information remained after deletion?
7. Did deletion guarantee every external copy or influence vanished?
8. What did Rian actually lose?

### Reader response

- dialogue clarity 1–5.
- emotional impact of deletion 1–5.
- frustration from withheld information 1–5.
- intrigue/continuation interest 1–5.
- describe ORA-3 in one sentence.

Pass:
- ≥85% describe ORA-3 as source/clinical/record interface, not restored consciousness.
- ≥80% identify identity-closure process proof.
- ≥70% understand bounded but real deletion.

## 7. Sample D — Packages and Charter

File:
- [[gate8-noncanon-package-charter-mobile-sample-v1]]

Run primarily on phone-sized screens.

### Immediate recall questions

1. What is the strongest benefit of the Imperial option? Its main cost?
2. What is the strongest benefit of Helix? Its main cost?
3. Why can the Independence route not solve the whole school problem?
4. What can Neutral trusteeship do well, and what can it not do?
5. Why can the school not simply continue internally?
6. Why does the largest 30.2% preference not decide for everyone?
7. What did the charter combine from the different options?
8. Why is the charter still financially unstable?
9. Why does the old ship appear at the end?
10. Is the ship a free reward?

### Number recall

Without rereading, which of these did the reader remember and understand?
- 11 days.
- 1,442 responses.
- 30.2% largest group.
- 49 needed / 46 secured.
- 8.7 needed / 1.6 available.

The goal is not perfect numeric recall.
Ask which figures felt useful and which felt like clutter.

Pass:
- ≥70% differentiate all five options.
- ≥85% understand no complete majority/solution.
- ≥80% understand funding gap causes external mission need.
- fewer than 30% summarize the charter as simple student rule or freedom.

## 9. Emotional and Commercial Questions

Ask after a sample:

1. What did you want to know next?
2. Which person did you care about most?
3. Which part felt like homework or explanation?
4. Where did you skim?
5. Was the main reward satisfying?
6. Did the cost feel real or artificially added?
7. Would you read the next episode?
8. What genre did this feel like?

Target:
- continuation interest average ≥3.5/5.
- no single recurring “homework” location in more than 40% of participants.

- rerun only the affected questions with new readers where possible.

## 11. Decision Rules

### Human PASS

- all sample minimum thresholds met.
- continuation interest ≥3.5.
- no S0/S1 repeated misunderstanding.
- core voice functional identification ≥70%.

### PARTIAL PASS

- core events understood but one density/voice/spatial area fails.
- revise locally and retest.

### FAIL

- central objective/cost repeatedly misunderstood.
- ORA treated as restored dead-person AI by >30% after revision.
- centralization presented as obviously evil/useless or distribution as costless by >30%.
- package/charter explanation cannot be recalled without author explanation.
- continuation interest below 3.0 across samples.
