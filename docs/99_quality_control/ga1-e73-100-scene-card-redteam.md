## 2. S1 Major Risks and Controls

### S1-01. Five packages may feel like an author-created comparison table

Controls:
- each package is introduced through a person, queue, treatment, job, route or operational demonstration.
- Imperial and Helix solve current problems before control costs are explained.
- Independence and Neutral visibly fail to cover everyone.
- internal continuation experiences payroll/repair fatigue.
- reader-facing prose should name only the current decision and two or three relevant conditions, not show the full design table.

### S1-02. The preference survey can become dry governance exposition

Controls:
- E74/76 show lived choices.
- E75 designs around conflict and missing people.
- E77 gives only the headline counts and conditional implications.
- survey results change actual manifests and siege alignments.
- individual characters can contradict group results.

### S1-03. Rian may appear artificially excluded from leadership

Assessment:
- exclusion is justified because he is simultaneously pilot, future-knowledge risk, 07 anomaly and faction target.
- he still holds decisive mission/central access power.

Required control:
- representatives respect his competence but reject mandate.
- he may advise/propose and remains accountable for central decisions.
- no one removes him merely to prove a political thesis.

### S1-04. Darin’s Imperial choice could look like a forced “good rival faction” demonstration

Controls:
- Darin’s squad/family/citizenship/defense reasons precede the siege.
- Imperial package materially delivers supplies/command.
- Darin’s autonomous refusal follows stated protection terms rather than converting ideology.
- Darin accepts Imperial discipline and leaves anyway.
- future arcs may place him against Rian.

### S1-05. Sera may become a Helix apology device

Controls:
- she has dependent, career and safety stakes.
- criticizes Nera’s undocumented risk and Helix blanket control.
- protects a certified system and refuses Finance/Security repossession.
- remains contract-bound and may enforce standards against Rian/Nera later.

### S1-06. Six-front siege can exceed reader tracking capacity

Required front staging:
- E85 map/allocation.
- E86 Ring B.
- E87 Ring C.
- E88 Black Ward/Spine.
- E89 Dock D.
- E90 Ring A.
- E91 front convergence.
- E92 central problem.

Per episode:
- one main front, one short consequence feed and one end-hook front maximum.
- use recurring zone names and physical objectives.
- no more than 3–5 active named speakers.

### S1-07. External actors may unrealistically avoid heavy force

Controls:
- assets/people/infrastructure are the objective.
- many students/people voluntarily align with external factions.
- dense habitat/frame geometry makes heavy weapons costly.
- legal/Neutral/public evidence imposes route/trade/political costs.
- limited violence still causes five deaths, injuries and physical loss.

### S1-08. Siege deaths can be over-engineered to represent every viewpoint

Risk:
- one death per front/faction can feel symmetrical.

Controls:
- causal chains arise from current scenes, not equal quota.
- prose may foreground three deaths and deliver others through aftermath/claims.
- avoid giving every death a symbolic final line.
- victims have ordinary work/preferences and incomplete political meaning.

### S1-09. Centralized command may still be a strawman if Rian relinquishes it too cleanly

Controls:
- Rian takes command and saves lives.
- multiple allies/beneficiaries ask him to retain it.
- central actors save people in E92/E95.
- distributed transition causes Toma’s death/other harm and system loss.
- Rian remains responsible for central orders while he holds power.
- no claim that relinquishment maximized immediate survival.
- later centralized systems can cite GA1 failures legitimately.

### S1-10. Segmentation can feel technically magical

Before prose/action map:
- define physical central route, six local key/control stations and transfer time.
- show operators, wiring/links, manual locks, power and signed scope.
- Authority A–B only translates/bridges one path; does not create all permissions.
- one segment fails or arrives late.

Scene-card level passes, battle-prose level remains blocked.

### S1-11. Nine-seat charter may feel like a neat final answer

Controls:
- 46/49 funding gap.
- 17 disputed students remain offsite.
- voluntary departures stay gone.
- board legitimacy and nonresponse gaps.
- external funders/technical/medical veto capture risks.
- Security can abuse repeated six-hour emergencies.
- mission revenue can recreate black missions.
- expires in 180 days.

### S1-12. The ending may be too administrative after a siege

Controls:
- E93–96 remain physical emergency/command scenes.
- E97 clauses tied to recent damage rather than legal lecture.
- E98 centers people who do not return.
- E99 walks physical damage and first ship hull/claim.
- E100 enacts access/roles and presents the next material object/mission.

### S1-13. The first ship may feel like a conventional trophy despite debt language

Controls:
- distressed title, crew/wage/repair/liens and insufficient charter budget.
- only conditional survey/mission process accepted at E100.
- GA2 begins with inability to undock.
- no free ownership or immediate captaincy.

### S1-14. 07 can dominate every siege front through central access

Controls:
- maximum two meaningful sortie windows plus central approach.
- 07 cannot enter most interior zones or command medical/records/worker choices.
- Darin/Sera/Amel/Bram/Ern/local actors win decisive fronts.
- E93 access affects command, not output.
- E100 ends 45–50% and damaged.

### S1-15. Future-hero ranking rejection can feel like a simple moral lesson

Controls:
- Rian first builds/uses a private ranking matrix and only then is challenged.
- deleting/locking it loses real strategic advantage.
- Darin’s future value remains relevant but not determinative.
- institutions still use current aptitude/risk/skill categories; all classification is not banned.
- later crises can tempt Rian to rebuild central sorting.

### S1-16. The charter victory may erase the students who preferred Imperial/Helix/exit

Controls:
- Darin/Sera/Aven and voluntary departures remain chosen.
- 96 students leave and 17 disputed remain offsite.
- charter recognizes exit/contract review rather than declaring everyone should stay.
- external programs continue and recruit.
