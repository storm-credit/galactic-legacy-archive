## 0. Audit question

The proposed modern frame ecology has eight manufacturing lineages L01–L08.

A dangerous shortcut would be:

> L01 came from Prototype 01, L02 came from Prototype 02 ...

That structure would quietly turn the historical program into eight successful legendary ancestors and erase the author’s intended mix of:
- failures;
- cancellations;
- partial subsystem successes;
- cross-inheritance;
- later industrial reinterpretation.

This audit therefore asks:

> How few genuine O3 lineage-success prototypes are actually required to explain the later frame ecology?

---

# 1. Four ancestry models

## A — One successful prototype per modern lineage

Shape:
- 8 modern lineages → 8 clean prototype ancestors.

Strength:
- easy genealogy.

Failure:
- too many successful prototypes;
- factions become color-coded descendants;
- contradicts failure-heavy author intent.

Verdict:
- **REJECT.**

## B — One universal successful ancestor

Shape:
- one prototype architecture becomes all later frames.

Strength:
- simple.

Failure:
- 07 or another unit becomes master-source;
- faction/industry history collapses;
- approaches chosen-machine mythology.

Verdict:
- **REJECT.**

## C — Several structural roots + many subsystem transfers

Shape:
- 4–5 O3 structural/production roots;
- O2 partial successes supply cooling, sensors, authority, control, medical/service, fallback and manufacturing knowledge;
- later institutions recombine them differently.

Strength:
- realistic engineering history;
- preserves failures;
- modern ideology comes from institutions rather than ancestry.

Verdict:
- **RECOMMENDED.**

## D — Mostly unrelated modern inventions

Shape:
- prototype era leaves only weak influence.

Strength:
- avoids relic dependence.

Failure:
- makes numbered prototype program narratively unnecessary;
- weakens 07 ancestry payoff.

Verdict:
- **HOLD / TOO WEAK FOR CURRENT AUTHOR DIRECTION.**

# 4. Minimum O3 success demand

For the currently proposed ecology, only about **3–5 O3 lineage successes** are structurally needed.

### Strongest required O3

1. ROOT-A / 07 service-interoperability ancestry.
2. ROOT-B / scalable general-purpose structural-production root.

### Plausible additional O3

3. ROOT-D formation/cradle integration **or** ROOT-C degraded-logistics architecture.
4. whichever of ROOT-C/ROOT-D was not represented above.
5. one other role-specific root only if later demand proves it.

### Not required as O3

ROOT-E high-bandwidth/EW/AI work is stronger if much of it comes from:
- O1 failures;
- O2 partial subsystem success;
- one rare O4 non-scalable high-performance article.

This preserves the engineering-realistic distribution recommended in the base architecture.

# 6. Modern institutional reinterpretation

Ancestry does not determine ideology.

### Imperial

Can inherit:
- ROOT-B structural repeatability;
- ROOT-D command/cradle integration;
- selected ROOT-E safety lessons.

Then prioritize:
- fleet-scale certification;
- predictable supply;
- hierarchical command.

### Helix

Can inherit much of the same technical foundation but prioritize:
- licensed cells;
- proprietary diagnostics;
- high-performance certified interfaces;
- vendor lock-in.

### Ardis/open workshops

Can inherit:
- ROOT-C failure lessons;
- ROOT-A interface/service concepts.

Then prioritize:
- local repair;
- worker authority;
- auditable modification.

### Neutral rescue

Can inherit:
- ROOT-A rescue/service philosophy;
- H04/H10 operational lessons.

Then prioritize:
- consent;
- medical/rescue capacity;
- route interoperability.

### Frontier

Can inherit little direct prestige but many failed-program lessons:
- manual fallback;
- donor compatibility;
- weak logistics operation.

This makes the “failed” program historically valuable without making frontier technology secretly ancient-superior.

### Continuity

Can inherit:
- ROOT-E data/AI/authority lessons;
- ROOT-D coordination methods;
- possibly H17 authentication concepts.

But later institutions can centralize those ideas far beyond the original prototype intent.

# 7. 07 boundary

07 should remain the clearest historical whole-frame O3 success in the reader’s early understanding.

Its success means:
- service interoperability worked well enough to become an enduring design philosophy.

It does **not** mean:
- strongest armor;
- highest acceleration;
- best weapon;
- master key;
- source of every modern frame.

07 can receive secondary inheritance from other failed/partial prototypes just as later descendants do.

But its reader-facing identity remains ROOT-A.

# 8. AXIOM / L08 boundary

L08 must stay outside forced ancestry.

Allowed later possibilities after author decision:

- parallel isolated research lineage;
- nonhuman/pre-Imperial source;

Forbidden now:
- “AXIOM was Prototype 01 and all others copied it.”

This preserves optionality and stops universal-origin collapse.

# 9. Implication for exact numbered slot assignment

Do not allocate numbers yet by modern lineage.

Instead exact slot allocation should satisfy a distribution like:

- multiple slots attack the same hard problem after earlier failure;
- some numbers never produce a full hull;
- some O1 failures become famous because people died or law changed;
- some O2 partial successes are technically more influential than surviving machines;
- only 3–5 slots need O3 lineage-success status;
- one O4 may become a famous high-performance dead-end;
- modern lineages mix primary and secondary inheritance.

Therefore the author-provided numbers `03,05,06,07,09,10,11,13,17,18,20` must **not** be interpreted as a success list.

They are candidate remembered/important numbers only.
