## 2. S1 Major Risks

### S1-01. Fleet law and logistics can overwhelm adventure

Controls:
- every charter clause must originate in a mutiny, delayed order, repair choice, surrender, recall or casualty.
- no more than one full charter meeting per act.
- tangible ship movement/combat/rescue by E465 and in every major act.
- foreground only 3–5 active rule/resource problems.
- use recurring ships/crews/spaces to embody systems.

### S1-02. P-001 can still become de facto owner through charisma

Required controls:
- ships join mission mandates, not P-001 personally.
- at least one formation leaves/refuses him lawfully.
- at least three decisive successes belong to other commanders.
- P-001 loses one front/objective through his allocation.
- he cannot hold command, pay, title, maintenance and authentication together.
- fleet remains functional after he leaves/loses contact.

### S1-03. Fragmented command loss may look artificially inserted

Required chain:
- previously established authentication delay, recall, standing-rule ambiguity or support shortage.
- named commander makes understandable decision.
- unified command has a credible counterfactual advantage.
- loss changes charter, readiness and GA6 resources.
- no arbitrary incompetence or sudden communication failure.

### S1-04. Protector-aligned commander risks repeating GA4 Protector

Distinction:
- CLM-03 is institutional/theater emergency authority.
- FC-001 is an operational formation commander responsible for specific crews/ships.
- FC-001 may obey, reinterpret or disagree with Protector.
- conflict centers mission command and captain/crew rights, not national succession.

### S1-05. FC-005 can become another honorable rival cliché

Controls:
- local authority includes real resource seizure/exclusion and unwillingness to share burdens.
- tactical competence does not prove national legitimacy.
- FC-005 may cooperate and still refuse charter or cause harm.
- no recruitment after one battle.
- local population/officials can disagree with commander.

### S1-06. Crew representation can become simplistic democracy fantasy

Controls:
- no tactical majority vote.
- standing obligations and emergency command remain real.
- crews can protect misconduct or avoid risk.
- officers/technical specialists/civilians have distinct rights.
- refusal has procedure, cost and review.

### S1-07. Maintenance/logistics actors can look like hidden sovereigns

Controls:
- yard/worker/Helix control is bounded but consequential.
- they must provide transparent criteria and accept mission/public obligations.
- technical priority can be wrong or politically captured.
- ships can use alternative/open repair at risk, preserving agency.

### S1-08. Fleet-size growth can feel unearned

Controls:
- every growth stage tied to charter, repair, pay, rescue and mission result.
- not all ships travel together or reach full readiness.
- reader-facing focus remains formations/support, not rising count.
- surrendered/provincial ships retain conditions and may leave.
- final network is depleted and politically divided.

### S1-09. GA5 may lack a clear enemy

Control:
- conflict can include compact rejecters, opportunists, separatists and FC-005 theater claims, but a coherent third threat/operation must be defined before scene cards.
- avoid making generic pirates the sole opposition.
- each battle objective tied to depot, route, civilians, yard or command.
- internal fragmentation is not enough for every action scene.

### S1-10. Civilian stakes may disappear under fleet spectacle

Controls:
- Talren/Civilian Chain and crew families/pay remain active.
- one civilian convoy mission owned by FC-003/other commander.
- support ships/medical/rescue are visible and strategically decisive.
- losses return to families, pensions, workers and route communities.

### S1-11. GA6 setup may feel predetermined

Controls:
- Orpheus conditions arise from GA5 victories and chosen routes, not prophecy.
- alternative allocations could reduce but not eliminate crisis.
- civilian movement has independent reasons and choices.
- state/capital fleet concentration protects real institutions and people.
- Neutral capacity limits derive from prior debts/commitments.

### S1-12. 120 episodes may be bloated

Contractible GA5:
- 90–105 episodes.

Likely merges:
- 5A-1 + 5A-2 if depot/disputed ship overlap.
- 5B-3 + 5B-4 if crew and surrender rules repeat.
- 5C-1 + 5C-2 if recall/civilian convoy can interlock.

Rule:
- every mini-arc changes readiness, alignment, authority, crew state, route or GA6 resource.

## 3. S2 Moderate Risks

### S2-01. Too many formations and commanders

Control:
- reader-facing 5–7 formations, 3–5 active per subact.
- name individual ships only for character/loss/tactical function.

### S2-02. Command terminology overload

Control:
- prose distinction: mission mandate, theater/task commander, formation commander, captain.
- avoid introducing full legal vocabulary at once.

### S2-03. Repair tender may feel like obvious “important support ship” lesson

Control:
- make tender crew, condition, ownership and tactical vulnerability specific.
- its rescue should require sacrifice and create later limitation.

### S2-04. Prize/parole rules may soften war unrealistically

Control:
- serious crimes/command responsibility still investigated.
- security, victims and recidivism risk remain.
- not all crews accept or survive integration.

### S2-05. Provincial recall can repeat H-001 community split

Distinction:
- community split concerned personal/care/identity destinations.
- provincial recall concerns sovereign/public defense obligations, fleets and home populations.
- use command/resource consequences, not only emotional separation.

### S2-06. First ship may become irrelevant at fleet scale

Control:
- retains relay, authentication, rescue, repair and mission-command role.
- outclassed in firepower but essential in distributed fleet network.
- may be damaged/temporarily sidelined; institution continues.

## 6. GA6 Dependency Audit

GA5 must leave:

- depleted missiles, tenders, crews and medical/rescue capacity.
- civilian convoys concentrated toward Orpheus Approach.
- capital/state fleet protecting real institutions and route control.
- competing command chains and FC-005/provincial interests.
- Neutral capacity limited by accumulated guarantees/debts.
- fleet charter capable of multi-front action but slower than unified command.
- P-001 holding bounded theater authority and facing mutually exclusive rescue/state objectives.

If GA6 crisis appears from an unrelated surprise attack, GA5 has failed.
