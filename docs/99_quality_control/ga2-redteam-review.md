## 2. S1 Major Risks

### S1-01. 30 episodes of ship acquisition can feel like a maintenance spreadsheet

Problem:
- E101–121 contains title, liens, crew, reactors, cooling, recyclers, modules and certification.

Required controls:
1. every repair must change authority, relationship, mission access or living conditions.
2. physical ship access and first serious system test by E103–104.
3. captain/engineer conflict and one irreversible legal/financial commitment by E107.
4. minimum transit/undock by E121–123 at latest.
5. no full technical explanation before a failure, test or choice makes it necessary.
6. reader-facing active defects limited to 3–5 at one time.

Retention target:
- tangible progress or changed control every 2–4 episodes.

### S1-02. Too many veto holders can make protagonists passive and decisions bureaucratic

Problem:
- captain, engineer, medical, logistics, data, mission and legal authorities can generate repetitive permission scenes.

Required controls:
- ordinary decisions delegated by standing rules; full roundtable only for novel/high-cost conflicts.
- each authority has a clearly bounded domain and can make decisions off-screen/in briefing summaries.
- veto must force a new plan, cost or relationship change—not simply delay plot.
- at least one authority makes a wrong decision and faces consequences.
- emergency action remains possible, but creates audit/liability/repair cost.

### S1-03. P-001 may lack ace-pilot payoff during a ship/contract arc

Problem:
- GA2 emphasizes logistics, engineering and governance after a mecha-heavy promise.

Required action envelope:
- E122–130: first external ship/frame mission with P-001 tactical brilliance but incomplete outcome.
- E138–145: Marn/debris/ghost-caravan maneuver and capture/rescue challenge.
- E174–179: split-convoy protection where P-001 must choose one route/front.
- E200–205: Silex infrastructure battle with fleet, 07 and interior teams.

Control:
- no battle won through hidden 07 firepower.
- at least two fights center on capture/repair/escort rather than dueling.
- E-001 or another pilot owns one decisive action.

### S1-04. Ghost-ship identity may repeat Archive identity themes without new emotional effect

Problem:
- “one name compresses multiple people/hulls” already exists in H-001/Archive threads.

New function required:
- technical ownership and labor provenance.
- current residents/workers could be dispossessed by heroic salvage.
- forged/reconstructed part can function and become future history despite uncertain authenticity.
- the answer changes ship configuration and Silex access.

Cut condition:
- if ghost mystery only says records are false, reduce it to fewer episodes and merge with standard conflict.

### S1-05. H-001 community split can become a long ethical meeting arc

Required controls:
- destination offers arrive through action: inspections, packing, medical evaluation, transport slots, work tests and convoy departures.
- capacity and deadlines change mid-arc.
- no more than one major all-community meeting.
- PC-001/002/003/004/005/007 make independent arrangements.
- ship cannot physically remain at Doran throughout debate.
- E174–179 provides route action and consequences before final settlement.

### S1-06. Community fragmentation could feel like failure or punishment for autonomy

Control:
- multiple routes produce mixed gains and costs, not one secretly correct destination.
- one centralized/Helix route materially improves life.
- one Independence/open route creates avoidable hardship.
- one failed/delayed route does not negate consent.
- relationships and aid continue across separation.
- H-001’s emotional loss is not evidence people chose wrongly.

### S1-07. Open-standard conflict risks modern policy lecture

Problem:
- certification, open source, liability and standards can sound like an essay.

Required dramatization:
- named machine/medical/ship failure.
- workers/users with body, pay and family stakes.
- test rigs, incompatible material, signed update and field conditions.
- Helix-certified component that succeeds where open copy fails.
- open repair that saves people Helix cannot/will not serve.
- council rules emerge from concrete incident, not author explanation.

Terminology limit:
- no scene introduces more than 2–3 new governance/technical terms.

### S1-08. H-002 dominates both ghost and standard acts

Control:
- ghost provenance depends equally on S-001, current workers, Q-001, NAV-001 and ENG-001.
- standard council gives medical/user/worker/insurer/regulator authority.
- H-002 cannot unilaterally release/fix system.
- her mistake or incomplete design contributes but does not carry all blame.
- another workshop/engineer produces a valid alternative she initially rejects.

### S1-09. Ship cast can exceed reader memory

Front-stage limit:
- each mini-arc uses core trio + at most 3 of S-001/M-001/CAP/ENG/NAV/Q/E-001/representatives as active speakers.
- roles not active remain on watch or are summarized.
- CAP-001 and ENG-001 require distinct voices and decisions.
- NAV-001/Q-001 can be supporting until route/manifest issue matters.

Introduction schedule:
- E101–107: CAP-001 and ENG-001.
- E108–114: NAV-001/Q-001 through command negotiation.
- MED-001 no earlier than actual medical/crew need.

### S1-10. First ship ownership can become so fragmented it never feels like a reward

Control:
- despite split title/claims, crew gains real operational autonomy by E121 and emotional/functional home by E130.
- visible before/after: can depart, choose some contracts, carry 07, protect people and return.
- ownership constraints create stakes but do not require legal permission every episode.
- ship receives stable reader-facing name and recurring spaces/routines.

### S1-11. Regional force totals may overpower one old escort unrealistically

Control:
- protagonists avoid direct state/corporate fleet duel.
- leverage comes from jurisdiction, witnesses, local workers, route timing, limited objectives and faction conflict.
- first ship can defeat/escape small raiders/cutters, not frigates/destroyers head-on.
- Silex conflict limits heavy weapons because infrastructure/local access matters.
- major task-group arrival is a deadline, not a boss fight the heroes destroy.

### S1-12. GA2 may still feel disconnected from original timeline and final Archive plot

Required links:
- P-001 remembers a different regional sequence or ship fate and observes concrete invalidation.
- 061 identity/provenance mirrors legal death and Archive compression.
- Service Authority D and Silex show Continuity-era infrastructure.
- open standard and distributed ship command become late-series infrastructure prototypes.
- Ardis restoration changes future war/logistics beyond local reward.

No more than one new CORE mystery.

### S1-13. 110 episodes may be longer than content requires

Compression test:
- 2A can contract from 30 to 22–26.
- 2B from 30 to 20–26 if ghost mystery lacks independent character stakes.
- 2C from 25 to 18–22.
- 2D from 25 to 20–24.

Contractible GA2:
- 80–95 episodes without losing core outcome.

Rule:
- episode counts are ceilings; final scene/episode planning must delete/merge subbeats that do not create changed state.

## 3. S2 Moderate Risks

### S2-01. Route names and node count

Control:
- reader sees 3–5 active locations per act, not all 14 at once.
- use map/recurring route language sparingly.
- final names require phonetic distinction and Korean readability.

### S2-02. Ship performance may feel arbitrary

Need before detailed battles:
- one-page performance state sheet with current mass, heat reserve, acceleration, missile/interceptor count and repair state per major mission.
- exact numbers can be bands, but state changes persist.

### S2-03. Distressed ship economics

Control:
- low acquisition price paired with operating debt, repairs, wages and claims.
- mission revenue cannot instantly clear debt.
- Q-001/crew pay conflict returns after lost cargo/repair.

### S2-04. Community routes create many follow-up obligations

Control:
- only 3–4 groups remain regularly front-stage after split.
- others tracked through periodic messages/representatives.
- GA3 focuses Ardis group; other groups appear when causally relevant.

### S2-05. Helix can become default cause of every technical problem

Control:
- open workers, Imperial systems, opportunists and heroes also create failures.
- Helix certified systems genuinely save lives.
- internal Helix blocs disagree and Manufacturing/Frontier actors may cooperate.

### S2-06. Opportunists can become generic pirates

Control:
- tie each opportunist operation to a real claim, unpaid wage, corrupt official or salvage market.
- avoid faceless raider attacks without resource/legal cause.

## 4. Command and Agency Audit

### P-001

Pass if:
- loses at least one objective to a valid veto.
- makes tactical contributions without seizing captaincy.
- supports a high-value person leaving his network.
- proposes GA3 commitment through crew/local process.

### H-001

Pass if:
- community choices occur without him.
- his preferred destination plan is only partly adopted.
- route federation has representation and exit, not personal leadership alone.

### H-002

Pass if:
- forged/open failure has concrete technical chain and shared responsibility.
- she cannot certify or control all copies.
- standard becomes institution with workers/users/medical voices.

### S-001

Pass if:
- no magical remote access.
- evidence release harms team interests.
- provenance cannot produce one perfect truth.

### M-001

Pass if:
- treatment capacity genuinely limits convoy.
- centralized medical route is a valid option.
- consent does not mean ignoring resource effects.

### CAP-001/ENG-001

Pass if:
- each makes one autonomous helpful decision and one costly/wrong decision.
- neither exists only to stop P-001/H-002.

1. first mission: ship heat/authority/rescue under limited weapons.
2. Marn: debris/salvage/capture and current residents.
3. convoy: multiple routes/civilian vessels and identity targets.

## 6. Collection Audit

Strengths:
- ship systems and rights tied to use/cost.
- no full free ship, second elite frame or perfect standard.
- community not one collected faction.
- ghost ship/current workers prevent treasure entitlement.

Risks:
- project registry contains many technical substates.

Reader-facing consolidation:
- first ship’s visible progression should be 5 main tracks:
  1. command/crew.
  2. power/cooling.
  3. frame/workshop bay.
  4. life support/passengers.
  5. sensors/defense/relay service.
- legal documents grouped under active case/mission rather than named individually.

Use rule:
- every major acquired module/right used within 10–15 episodes.
