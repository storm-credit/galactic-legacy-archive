- explicit source-precedence ruling;
- all PR #35/#36 manuscript artifacts labeled non-canon and out-of-scope;
- future prose and revision forbidden;
- no deletion without author instruction.

Remaining risk:
- repository paths still contain the word `manuscript` and file headers claim canonical development.

Required future control:
- add a repository-level exclusion index or move files to an archival branch if the author requests cleanup;
- every handoff prompt must repeat design-only status.

Verdict:
- PASS, non-destructive and honest.

## 3. Attack — Templates Create Spreadsheet Fiction

Risk:
- universal metadata fields could encourage filling forms instead of making stories.

Controls:
- templates are author-side only;
- reader-facing memory limits are explicit;
- entries require emotional, tactical, institutional and loss functions;
- unused items are cut or merged;
- category display is optional.

Required practice:
- scene cards should reference only fields that change the current decision;
- do not display full entry records to readers.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 4. Attack — Counter-Collection Makes Every Faction Equally Omniscient

Risk:
- every faction now appears able to contest every target through law, money, routes, records and force.

Controls:
- faction-specific doctrines and preferred targets;
- intelligence source, age, confidence and missing layer required;
- fair-play rules demand prior access/capability;
- aligned networks are not centrally commanded;
- factions value different control layers.

Required practice:
- each arc names which factions do **not** understand the target and why;
- most operations should involve 2–4 serious collectors, not every major faction.

Verdict:
- PASS WITH DENSITY CONTROL.

## 5. Attack — Collection Still Centers Rian

Risk:
- even with counter-collection, the structure may still frame everyone as a target in Rian’s game.

Controls:
- people, factions and territories use relationship verbs rather than ownership;
- independent offers, refusal and exit fields are mandatory;
- rivals can preserve targets from the protagonist;
- allies and communities can hold integrated assets independently;
- permanent loss/dispersal is required.

Required test:
- at least one major target per grand act must be integrated by someone other than Rian;
- at least one desired target must remain outside his coalition.

Verdict:
- PASS.

## 6. Attack — Factions Become Different Administrative Skins

Risk:
- all factions may look like boards, offices, claims and distributed authority with only terminology changed.

Controls:
- geography, material culture, ordinary speech, service practices and architecture differ;
- Independence networks have no universal chain;
- Neutral authority is vessel/member based;
- worker authority is labor/service based;
- Aurel operates through service classification and executable consensus;
- Imperial legitimacy uses office and registration.

Remaining risk:
- project themes naturally make governance central, producing meeting repetition.

Required future control:
- culture packets must include sport, food, family, art, faith and informal conflict unrelated to institutional design;
- each faction arc needs physical work, travel, ceremony or crisis scenes beyond councils.

Verdict:
- CONDITIONAL PASS.

## 7. Attack — Visual Identity Relies on Familiar SF Tropes

Risk:
- Imperial arches, Helix modular factories and frontier repair marks can feel recognizable rather than original.

Controls:
- color-only differentiation forbidden;
- authority and maintenance behavior shape visuals;
- local cultures cross faction lines;
- repair and service systems affect clothing and architecture.

Required future work:
- concept-art prompts and silhouette tests;
- identify three visual motifs to avoid because of similarity to major franchises;
- show the same faction in wealthy and poor regions.

Verdict:
- FOUNDATION PASS / ART DIRECTION OPEN.

## 8. Attack — Succession Is Cosmetic

Risk:
- deputy lists may exist without changing policy or resources.

Controls:
- succession stress test separates staff, money, keys, military force, legitimacy and recognition;
- emergency powers expire;
- military authority does not automatically inherit civil authority;
- protagonists cannot appoint replacements.

Required application:
- per-grand-act succession table must record at least one actual death, resignation, defection or office split and resulting policy change.

Verdict:
- PASS AS TEMPLATE / IMPLEMENTATION OPEN.

## 9. Attack — Old Technology Is Secretly Better

Risk:

- early systems are insecure, inefficient and labor-heavy;
- certified modern systems repeatedly save lives;
- ancient systems provide emergency interoperability, not unlimited combat power;
- Compatibility C3/C4 cannot become permanent standards without governance.

Verdict:
- PASS.
