## 2. S1 Major Risks

### S1-01. Distribution may still look like the author’s unquestionably correct answer

Required controls:
- at least one region’s services materially worsen after distribution.
- one delayed-central region remains stable and legitimate at ending.
- one local/plural system suffers elite capture or serious failure.
- bounded central/residual institutions visibly remain necessary.
- characters can reasonably prefer more central continuity without being villains.
- no future narration proves distribution produced lower total deaths.

### S1-02. First handoff failure may be too convenient or easily fixed

Controls:
- failure chain planted in GA9: hidden dependency, staff/training gap, attacker/local elite and authentication/service limits.
- one irreversible death/loss.
- P-001 override saves people but does not solve local capacity.
- local actors own correction and accept external standing/oversight.
- later handoffs retain modified risks rather than “lesson learned” perfection.

### S1-03. Regional choice may allow powerful regions to externalize costs

Controls:
- affected outsiders and dependent regions have standing.
- minimum-service/burden/route/claims obligations enforceable enough to matter.
- wealthy nodes contribute resources and cannot simply opt out.
- local autonomy includes safe exit and external audit.
- one region resists obligations and faces political/material consequence.

### S1-04. Thin interoperability compact may quietly become a new central state

Controls:
- explicitly exclude unified taxation, politics, army, history and technical regime.
- residual bodies hold narrow functions and plural oversight.
- no permanent master identity/route/military/Archive key.
- regions can choose different implementations and leave selected domains under obligations.
- emergency corrective assembly expires per event.

### S1-05. Conversely, the compact may be too weak to be believable

Controls:
- retain interregional reserves, translation, emergency services, Common Fleet mandates and reciprocal recognition.
- specify contributions, enforcement, appeals and claims.
- allow sanctions/limited interventions under defined rules.
- show real operation after P-001’s departure.

### S1-06. P-001’s exclusive-index sacrifice can feel abstract

Required controls:
- define physical/medical/institutional mechanism before scenes.
- show what he can no longer remember/query/do.
- one preventable loss or uncertainty follows the sacrifice.
- no secret backup, copy or later reactivation.
- relationships and daily behavior change.

### S1-07. P-001 can still control transition through charisma after formal abdication

Controls:
- final fronts delegated with autonomous victories/one failure.
- he holds no permanent key, fleet or office.
- others publicly disagree and choose paths he dislikes.
- final institutions have succession/removal and can act without him.
- his postwar role is bounded and noncentral.

### S1-08. Final central battle may become another boss fight

Controls:
- conflict spans services, beneficiary regions, local capture, fleets/routes, technical/medical systems, archives/AI and coerced-region withdrawal.
- no core destruction ends the problem.
- hardliner/opportunist forces are defeated while staff/services and some central institutions survive.
- political/legal/technical handoffs determine victory.

### S1-09. A-001 may receive an overly clean reform/redemption ending

Controls:
- specific harmful authorizations remain in claims/records.
- useful staff/expertise can survive without moral absolution.
- human and AI outcomes may differ, but neither carries all blame.
- A-001 can resist or delay a transfer and face consequence.

### S1-10. Returning iconic assets may become sentimental inventory cleanup

Controls:
- 07, first ship, fleets, archives and institutions have practical successor roles and constituencies.
- ownership disputes/maintenance/heritage costs remain.
- at least one iconic object is retired/dismantled/lost while lineage survives.
- people choose independently rather than receive assignments from P-001.

### S1-11. The epilogue may be too broad and dilute emotion

Controls:
- choose 3–5 representative places/relationships plus final student/person scene.
- combine closures through institutions/objects/messages.
- do not give every supporting character equal montage.
- preserve one unresolved relationship and one failed region.

### S1-12. Plural history can be misread as “truth is subjective”

Controls:
- physical evidence, provenance and accountability still constrain claims.
- false records can be disproven where evidence exists.
- plural interpretations do not erase H-001/P-001/institutional responsibility.
- courts/claims/records produce specific findings without one total history.

### S1-13. Mystery payoff checklist may become expositional

Controls:
- every payoff appears through material aftermath, changed rights, object/institution fate or character choice.
- do not list/recap all mysteries in dialogue or UI.
- only unresolved intentional ambiguities remain.

### S1-14. P-001 survival may feel consequence-free compared with death

Controls:
- lasting memory/access/health/command loss.
- he lives with criticism, claims and irreversible choices.
- no universal hero worship or guaranteed peaceful life.
- survival requires ordinary responsibility and relationships, not escape.

### S1-15. Final scene may become overly neat or sentimental

Controls:
- new unlabeled person has specific present circumstance/desire, not generic child symbol.
- P-001’s question follows established role/context.
- no destiny UI, grand speech or proof the future is safe.
- surroundings show both improvement and unresolved imperfection.

### S1-16. 100 episodes may be bloated

Contractible GA10:
- approximately 78–92 episodes.

Likely merges:
- 10A-2 + 10A-3 if local correction/dependency disclosure overlap.
- 10B-3 + 10B-4 if medical/technical and identity interoperability can share one route crisis.
- 10D-1 + 10D-2 if reconstruction and legacy return interlock.

Rule:
- every mini-arc changes service, regional authority, key custody, P-001 sacrifice, asset custody or final aftermath.

## 3. S2 Moderate Risks

### S2-01. Too many regional transition models

Control:
- foreground 5–7 representative regions/entities, only 2–3 per act.
- other models mentioned through reports/epilogue.

### S2-02. Equalization/reserve institutions can become hand-wave funding

Control:
- specify contributor conflicts, shortages and limited coverage in final detailed design.

### S2-03. Safe exit/migration can create unrealistic freedom

Control:
- housing, service, identity, transport and host-region capacity remain constraints.

### S2-04. Technical interoperability can seem solved by standards language

Control:
- show training, tools, parts, accidents, multiple certifiers and local capacity.

### S2-05. Years-later epilogue can erase immediate grief

Control:
- transition from recent aftermath to later time intentionally; memorial/claims and unresolved loss remain visible.

- relinquishes exclusive index with permanent consequence.
- final central command expires physically/legal/operationally.

Pass if:
- keeps treatment continuous and allows local/patient choice despite risk.

### A-001/Transition Director

Pass if:
- transfers useful systems/knowledge and retains specific responsibility.
- may withhold/resist one function and face consequence.

Each must own at least one decisive success/failure independent of P-001.

## 6. Ending Integrity Audit

The ending passes only if:

1. central services continue in some form.
2. distribution fails or harms in some regions.
3. some regions voluntarily remain more central.
4. no one holds a permanent master key.
5. P-001 loses exclusive index/command with permanent consequence.
6. core companions have independent institutions and choices.
7. A-001/Continuity expertise is transformed, not erased.
8. evidence/accountability survive without one final history.
9. reset/retry is unavailable.
10. final ordinary-life scene contains no destiny confirmation.
