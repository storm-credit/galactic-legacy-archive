## 2. Findings

### F1 — Direct command responsibility

Haren personally authenticated and transmitted the D4 order.
He understood that the physical denial would not be rapidly reversible.

Finding:
- **responsible**.

### F2 — Foreseeable dependent-region harm

Haren’s model included several major dependent routes and service risks but underrepresented:
- unregistered populations;
- informal medical/food dependencies;
- delayed communication;
- local inability to evacuate before closure.

Finding:
- **responsible for serious foreseeable-risk undercount and negligent dependency verification**.

### F3 — Emergency necessity

Evidence supports a credible imminent threat of hostile seizure/use and a high-risk cascade into multiple regions.

Finding:
- emergency necessity existed, but did not erase proportionality, consultation, warning and alternative-route duties.

### F4 — Alternative measures

D0–D3 options were attempted or judged insufficient in part, but:
- at least one phased/sequenced alternative had not completed local consultation;
- one affected-region request was delayed or misclassified;
- central/federation time pressure contributed to escalation.

Finding:
- no perfect alternative proven; decision process still legally deficient.

### F5 — Delegated doctrine responsibility

Haren authored/approved doctrine and key distribution that enabled later denial actions.

Finding:
- responsible for reasonably foreseeable misuse where safeguards were absent;
- not automatically responsible for independent crimes beyond doctrine/key causation.

### F6 — Fabricated/aggregated Blood Admiral incidents

Several later atrocities, office decisions and propaganda incidents were falsely aggregated under Haren’s person.

Finding:
- **not responsible** absent specific command, assistance or foreseeable doctrine/key causation.

### F7 — Personal gain and malicious intent

No evidence that Haren acted for personal wealth, status or pleasure.
He intended to prevent wider military capture.

Finding:
- mitigates motive, not harm or command responsibility.

## 4. Binding Sanctions

### 4.1 Custodial/restriction period

- 14 months of secure protective detention/inquiry restriction already served or credited from CY 745-07 through CY 746-09.
- no ceremonial release scene; conditions include monitored communication and movement.

### 4.2 Operational authority prohibition

Permanent:
- may never hold a unilateral route-opening/denial master key.
- may never personally authorize D3–D5 action without the full legally required multi-party structure.

Twelve-year prohibition from order date:
- no executive command of a regional/galactic route authority.
- no direct custody of denial hardware or root authentication.
- no emergency waiver based solely on past expertise.

This prohibition extends beyond the epilogue.

### 4.3 Public-office eligibility

For 30 months after order:
- barred from elected/appointed public route office of any kind.

After 30 months:
- may serve only in nonexecutive roles if:
  - selected/approved through affected-region representation;
  - powers exclude live route denial/opening command;
  - term is fixed and removable;
  - decisions are public/auditable;
  - no personal key custody exists.

GA10/E1094 role:
- `피해지역·항로의무 조정위원`.
- negotiates reparations, service obligations and claims.
- cannot command routes or fleets.
- is not a redemption prize or return to admiralty.

### 4.4 Reparations

For 15 years:
- 35% of public salary, lecture/consulting income and qualifying royalties goes to the D4 affected-region reparations trust, above a protected living allowance.

Institutional contribution:
- Mutual Route Federation and other responsible institutions contribute larger shares based on authority, resources and causation.
- Haren is not made a symbolic individual payer while institutions escape.

### 4.5 Testimony and record duties

Haren must:
- preserve all personal/office logs.
- submit to annual public or affected-region review for five years.
- testify when new D4 incidents/claims arise, subject to victim protections.
- correct false exonerating and false total-guilt records where he has standing.

He may not:
- compel victims to meet him.
- use testimony as political rehabilitation campaign.
- demand forgiveness or final narrative closure.

### 4.6 Service obligation

For eight years:
- provide noncommand technical/logistics expertise to route repair, dependency mapping and affected-region continuity when requested by the trust.
- service occurs under local leadership and safety/consent rules.

This is not forced labor replacing imprisonment; it is a civil responsibility attached to professional eligibility and reparations.
