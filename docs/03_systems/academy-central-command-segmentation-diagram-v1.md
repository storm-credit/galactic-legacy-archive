## 1. Core Principle

The central key is not a magic administrative password.

It is temporary access to a physical emergency-coordination layer connecting:

- station rotation and life support;
- power and thermal distribution;
- doors/security routing;
- medical priority corridors;
- records/communications;
- Dock D/frame/shuttle safety.

The same layer can also:

- enforce transfer manifests;
- lock or release frames;
- isolate populations;
- centralize or erase/copy records;
- privilege one faction’s safety and custody definition.

Safety and political control are technically entangled at E92.

## 2. Physical Location

Central interface:
- Central Spine transit/safety junction, between station-control routing and T3/T4/T5 transfer lines.
- accessible from exterior/service truss by maintenance craft/frame and from interior by secured operator corridor.

Reader-facing visual candidate:
- a slowly rotating circular service wheel around a fixed emergency-control spine.
- six physical coupling assemblies around the wheel.
- each coupling routes one domain’s power/data/command link toward a local station.

Scale:
- service wheel diameter approximately 10–14 m.
- 07 can hold/manipulate external couplings but cannot enter the interior control corridor.
- human/utility teams operate local stations and manual keys.

## 3. Six Domains

```text
                         [Records / Comms]
                                S5
                                 |
                [Medical] S3 ----+---- S4 [Security / Doors]
                                 |
 [Life Support / Logistics] S1 --O-- S6 [Dock D / Frames]
                                 |
                        S2 [Power / Technical]

O = central emergency coordination wheel / Rian temporary bridge
S1–S6 = local segment stations/keys/operators
```

### S1 — Life Support / Logistics

Functions:
- air/water circulation priorities.
- habitat isolation/rotation-safe movement.
- food/essential-service routing.
- current-person evacuation support.

Local owners/operators:
- station life-support professionals.
- Haren/Yori current-person and minimum-service planning.

Cannot decide:
- medical treatment priority alone.
- security arrests/transfer.

### S2 — Power / Technical

Functions:
- grid routing.
- thermal/cooling allocation.
- workshop/industrial/critical maintenance loads.
- manual hardware fallback.

Local owners/operators:
- Nera, Bram and licensed/shift engineers.

Cannot decide:
- patient priority or population movement alone.

### S3 — Medical

Functions:
- clinic/Ward power and routes.
- patient movement priority.
- emergency clinical authority.

Local owners/operators:
- licensed clinicians, Mia, patient oversight within established rules.

Cannot decide:
- broad security/custody or station power policy alone.

### S4 — Security / Doors

Functions:
- bulkheads, internal routes and security response.
- detention/controlled transfer routes.
- local force coordination.

Local owners/operators:
- professional Security/Kara under emergency/charter limits.

Cannot decide:
- blanket patient/asset/data transfer alone.

### S5 — Records / Communications

Functions:
- public/emergency broadcast.
- command provenance and timestamp.
- current manifests, evidence mirrors and external links.

Local owners/operators:
- Serin, Neutral auditors, communications staff and local witnesses.

Cannot decide:
- physical door/power action without another domain.

### S6 — Dock D / Frames / Shuttles

Functions:
- bay locks and launch/recovery safety.
- frame/shuttle movement priority.
- exterior corridor/approach deconfliction.

Local owners/operators:
- Dock D master/field commander, technicians, mission authority and separate frame locks.

Cannot decide:
- ownership or permanent transfer alone.

## 4. Pre-Crisis Coupling

Before E84:
- domains have local controls but emergency coordination is routed through Academy Operations/Security and several external owner keys.

At E91–92:
- Imperial, Academy Operations and Helix commands conflict.
- local stations receive inconsistent priorities.
- safety systems stall or oscillate between valid commands.

Authority A–B role:
- 07 core recognizes/translates an obsolete service/emergency layer.
- permits Rian to bridge the central wheel long enough to choose coherent safety priorities.
- does not create legal ownership, power, staff or local hardware.

## 5. Rian Central Access State

Entry:
- 07 reaches/holds external service wheel.
- local interior team prevents current claimant from physically sealing/replacing the interface.
- Authority A–B handshake accepts Rian as temporary unregistered emergency-service operator.

Window:
- 12–20 minutes before overload, external reassertion or required renewal.

Immediate commands available:

- prioritize/open compatible routes.
- resolve mutually exclusive door/rotation/launch sequence.
- redirect available power and cooling.
- stop/release selected frame/shuttle systems.
- enforce or suspend manifests/records/locks.

Unavailable:

- create new power/cooling capacity.
- know every current person.
- repair damaged hardware.
- determine patient consent or legal truth.
- command local people who refuse/lose communications.

## 6. Segmentation Procedure

Goal:
- convert one master coordination layer into six bounded local authorities with logged cross-domain emergency procedures.

### Step 1 — Scope lock

Time:
- 30–90 seconds.

Actions:
- Rian declares current safety objectives and prohibited permanent actions.
- Serin/external mirrors record current central commands and scope.
- emergency auto-expiry set.

### Step 2 — Local station verification

Time:
- 2–6 minutes, parallel.

Required per segment:
- physically present operator/team.
- current local-system state.
- manual/physical key or authenticated station.
- fallback/stop condition.

Failure:
- a segment without a ready operator cannot simply be released.

### Step 3 — Physical/data decoupling

Time per first pair:
- approximately 2–3 minutes.

Later segments:
- 2–6 minutes depending damage/access.

Actions:
- 07/Nera/technical teams release or reroute physical coupling.
- local key inserted/activated.
- central priority changes to a limited cross-domain request rather than direct command.

### Step 4 — Local acceptance and test

Time:
- 30–120 seconds each under ideal conditions.

Actions:
- local operator issues a harmless/limited test command.
- adjacent domains confirm no dangerous cascade.

### Step 5 — Cross-domain emergency rules

Time:
- ongoing.

Examples:
- Medical can request priority power but cannot seize grid indefinitely.
- Security can close a door for immediate threat but must publish/current-person check and expire.
- Life support can isolate empty/dangerous sectors but uses current-person data and review.

### Step 6 — Master expiry

- Rian’s central access expires automatically.
- renewal requires multi-signature emergency action and stated scope.
- no private fallback/backdoor retained.

## 7. Recommended Segmentation Order

Order is chosen to reduce total cascade, not political prestige.

1. S5 Records/Comms — preserve scope, current state and external witness.
2. S3 Medical — protect treatment routes/priority from blanket transfer.
3. S1 Life Support/Logistics — stabilize current-person minimum service.
4. S2 Power/Technical — most physically difficult; depends on Bram/Nera local access.
5. S4 Security/Doors — delayed because premature release/lock can trap people.
6. S6 Dock D/Frames — retained longest under central deconfliction due active external combat/shuttles.

Alternative may change with actual damage; prose must state why the order changes.

## 8. Locked Delay and Loss Chain

To preserve centralization’s real benefit:

- one decoupled power path leaves Black Ward/medical support below planned level while S2 local key is delayed.
- central command could restore it immediately.
- Haren/Nera/Mia attempt local/alternative recovery under a limited threshold.
- Toma Ess remains with a patient/transport/support system during the gap.
- Toma dies or suffers the locked fatal chain while the patient/system is preserved or recovered.

Responsibility:
- damage and prior conflicting controls create the vulnerability.
- segmentation creates the delay.
- central restoration was a plausible faster option.
- no narrator claims the death was necessary or morally redeemed.
- record remains in charter review.

## 10. Prose Constraints

Show:
- one main segment handoff at a time.
- physical pin/cable/key/operator action.
- immediate before/after system effect.
- one remote/front consequence feed only.

Do not:
- recite all six domain descriptions in one paragraph.
- describe segmentation as copying keys to friends.
- imply distributed control is automatically democratic or safe.
- erase the central system’s saved lives.
- let Rian personally choose local medical/technical/security details after handoff.

## 11. Human-Test Criterion

After the non-canon sample, readers should understand:

- central access immediately solves command collision;
- same access can force transfers/locks/record control;
- six local systems need hardware/operators/time;
- distribution causes a real delay/loss;
- Rian’s central power expires rather than merely being promised away.

Target:
- ≥80% on central benefit and physical distribution cost.
