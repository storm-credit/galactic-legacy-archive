- long-term stabilization for cadets/workers who cannot use ordinary Academy medicine.

## 6. Treatment Systems

### System T1 — Proprietary neural stabilizer cartridges

Supplier:
- Helix Human Performance/medical contractors.

Function:
- suppresses dangerous interface feedback and supports adaptation.

Benefits:
- proven and individually calibrated.
- responsible for real long-term survival and function.

Costs/risks:
- expensive, identity/data-linked and subscription-controlled.
- shortages or access revocation create dependency.

### System T2 — Regulation membranes and sterile interface sets

- physical consumables linking patient, implant/interface and stabilization unit.
- incompatible substitutes can cause infection, signal drift or tissue injury.

### System T3 — Local treatment models

- patient-specific calibration/data held across Ward, Helix and Imperial/protected records.
- no complete model exists in one place for every patient.

### System T4 — Open/generic adapter path

Designed by:
- Nera + Ward maintenance/clinicians + patient/user input.

Function:
- allows some stabilization units to accept generic/older consumables.

Not universal:
- patient-specific risks vary.
- lower performance, more monitoring and shorter maintenance cycles.
- cannot safely serve every hidden protocol.

### System T5 — ORA-3 Patient Continuity Interface

Reader-facing name:
- `오라-3 / ORA-3`.

Nature:
- bounded clinical interface and fragmented record/witness system.
- not a whole-person archive and not omniscient AI.

Stores/mediates:
- treatment-state fragments.
- consent/access restrictions.
- transfer/identity pointers.
- old emergency/continuity protocol translations.
- some patient-authored notes and responses.

Limit:
- does not contain complete memories or prove one legal truth.
- may combine several historical sources around one current patient.
- some data is inaccessible or must be deleted/withheld by consent.

## 9. Access and Custody Rules

- transfer acute patients into BW-A/B under medical referral.
- view current clinical needs required for care.

They cannot automatically:
- see protected identity, full trial history or Imperial/Helix data rights.

Patient rights on paper:
- treatment explanation.
- emergency consent exceptions.
- limited refusal/appeal.
- private communication under conditions.

Practical coercion:
- refusal can mean loss of the only viable treatment, placement or identity protection.
- contract/sentence representatives may sign/pressure decisions.

Security:
- Academy Security can restrict movement/access during threat.

Research:
- can pause/discontinue studies.
- cannot safely switch off overlapping treatment systems without clinical consequences.

Neutral oversight:
- can audit medical custody/evidence and transport patients under accepted corridor terms.
- cannot take all patients or fund indefinite care.

## 11. Security and Crisis Modes

### Lockdown Mode L1

- doors/access controlled; treatment continues normally.
- visitors and data exports restricted.

### L2 — Custody conflict

- security escorts, patient movement restrictions and evidence seals.
- clinical stop remains with licensed medical authority.

### L3 — Supply/power emergency

- treatments prioritized by current clinical need and compatibility.
- research loads shut down before patient support where physically separable.
- not all research systems can be separated cleanly because of shared infrastructure.

### L4 — Purge/seizure risk

- competing actors attempt patient/data/equipment transfer.
- local staff/patients may choose stay, move, hide, disclose or delete.
- no single emergency evacuation route has capacity for all.
