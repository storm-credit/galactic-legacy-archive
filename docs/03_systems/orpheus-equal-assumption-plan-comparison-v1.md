## 3. Decision Rules by Plan

### Plan S — State Priority

Priority order:
1. advanced medicine and State medical institutions.
2. identity/payroll/command/authentication functions.
3. registered State dependents and selected critical workers.
4. civilian groups by verified priority and remaining capacity.

Authority:
- civil/State command with strong Continuity optimization for its own manifests.

Coercion:
- moderate/high for official asset and crew assignments.
- lower than G for personal destination reclassification outside State group.

### Plan C — Civilian Priority

Priority:
1. critical medical by immediate survival.
2. children/dependents and high-risk civilian groups.
3. largest achievable civilian passenger count.
4. minimum State fragments only where necessary for arrival survival.

Authority:
- civilian/Neutral/community manifest coalition.

Coercion:
- low/medium, but State crews/institutions may be compelled or abandoned by majority/emergency choices.

### Plan G — Full Continuity Optimization

Priority:
- dynamic model minimizing immediate expected deaths, collision and unused capacity while preserving a calculated minimum State function.

Authority:
- Aurel/Authority G controls traffic, manifests, defense alignment and destination assignments across domains.

Coercion:
- high.
- can split families/communities, redirect destinations, override withdrawal and suspend contested identities/appeals.

### Plan M — Distributed Mixed

Priority:
- fragment State functions into minimum viable packets.
- preserve civilian/community choice where operationally possible.
- maintain separate medical, route, vessel and local authority.
- accept organized holdouts and uneven destinations.

Authority:
- plural board and front commanders; Rian resolves only immediate cross-front conflicts.

Coercion:
- medium and uneven.
- some emergency assignments occur, but no single optimizer controls all domains.

## 5. 90-Day Direct and Downstream Mortality/Harm

This model includes:
- delayed injury/medical death.
- holdout/captive outcomes.
- destination service failure.
- wider-front medicine/identity/payroll/command effects.
- excludes speculative century-scale political consequences.

| Plan | 90-day direct + attributable downstream deaths | Severe nonfatal/displacement/identity-service harm | Confidence |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| S | 0.52–0.88m | 0.55–0.95m | medium-low |
| C | 0.65–1.16m | 0.45–0.82m | low/medium |
| G | 0.45–0.76m | 0.70–1.20m, much of it coercive/status/destination harm | medium |
| M | 0.56–0.97m | 0.58–1.02m | medium-low |

Important result:
- **Plan G plausibly produces fewer immediate and 90-day deaths than Plan M.**
- Plan S also beats some M runs on 90-day deaths because coherent State/medical systems save people elsewhere, despite higher immediate civilian abandonment.
- Plan C can produce the most immediate civilian arrivals while causing the worst downstream service losses.

The selected M plan is therefore not the mathematically proven survival optimum.

## 6. Rights, Power and Institutional Consequences

### Plan S

- strengthens dynastic/civil/military State continuity.
- creates a clear abandoned civilian class.
- likely intensifies route rebellion and future denial warfare.
- fewer institutional fragments, easier accountability in some domains and easier monopoly in others.

### Plan C

- strongest moral/public claim of civilian priority.
- weak/fragmented administration can enable local coercion, corruption and service collapse.
- future wars may worsen without command/industrial/medical institutions.

### Plan G

- strongest immediate service/survival performance.
- turns emergency optimization into cross-domain sovereignty.
- people become movable/status-adjustable inputs without meaningful appeal during crisis.
- post-crisis relinquishment is unlikely and beneficiaries have valid reasons to retain it.
- directly accelerates Preservation Regime logic.

### Plan M

- retains plural authority and creates evidence/standing for later reform.
- produces real avoidable losses through delay and fragmentation.
- no one institution can hide all failures, but no one institution can repair all failures either.
- preserves possibility of later distribution without proving it safer.

## 7. Why Rian Selects M

Rian’s locked reasoning at E681:

1. G is likely to move more people immediately, and he states this openly.
2. full G requires permanent-grade cross-domain control because no credible handback mechanism exists under current emergency.
3. S preserves crucial functions but assigns mass abandonment to people with weakest recognition.
4. C protects visible civilians but underfunds life-sustaining institutions and wider fronts.
5. M preserves enough function and more political/person choice while accepting a measurable immediate survival penalty.
6. Rian chooses M as an accountable political/ethical risk, not a hidden numerical optimum.

He cannot claim:
- that M saves the most lives.
- that anyone choosing G/S is evil.
- that later distribution proves the dead were necessary.
