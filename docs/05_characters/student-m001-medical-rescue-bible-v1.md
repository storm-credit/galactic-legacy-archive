## 2. Background

Recommended background:
- from a node habitat where industrial accidents and delayed evacuation made medical triage normal.
- worked as an unlicensed emergency assistant before academy entry.
- prosecuted or contracted after using restricted drugs/equipment to treat unregistered workers, creating debt and legal liability.
- has practical emergency experience but not full physician training.
- academy offers certification and treatment access in exchange for dangerous service.

Alternative protected tie:
- M-001 personally requires periodic neural stabilizer or prosthetic maintenance, preventing easy escape.

Why not a medical genius:
- strong at triage, stabilization, evacuation and patient communication.
- weak at advanced surgery, research, pharmacology, and long-term care.
- must consult doctors, AI tools, technicians, and patients.

## 3. External Want and Internal Need

### External want

- earn a recognized medical/rescue license not owned by the academy or Helix.
- keep patients and unregistered workers from becoming research assets or unpaid debt.
- secure reliable treatment/supply for self or someone close.

### Internal need

- accept that respecting consent can lead to outcomes they believe are medically worse.
- stop using harsh competence as protection against grief and uncertainty.
- recognize that refusing heroic risk is not the same as refusing hope.
- allow others to bear informed danger without treating them as irrational patients.

### False belief

> If everyone follows triage and safety rules exactly, preventable loss can be eliminated.

### Truth to learn

> Rules reduce harm but cannot remove tragic choice; patients and communities have values beyond survival probability.

## 4. Competence Map

### Exceptional for current level

- decompression, burns, crush injury, shock, hemorrhage stabilization.
- casualty sorting under limited oxygen/time.
- patient movement through hazardous structures.
- recognizing when neural-sync symptoms require immediate shutdown.
- communicating bad options concretely.

### Strong

- emergency logistics and medical inventory.
- crew fatigue and infection prevention.
- identifying falsified injury reports.
- protecting patients from interrogation during treatment.

### Weak

- advanced diagnosis without equipment.
- long-term psychological care.
- political strategy.
- accepting undocumented improvised technology.
- comforting people when no actionable step remains.

### Dangerous strength

- triage categories can become moral categories.
- may treat uncertain/uncooperative patients as inefficient risk.
- can justify coercive treatment or quarantine under urgency.

## 9. Voice Bible

Default:
- direct, unsentimental, body-specific.
- asks pain, breath, vision, movement, last intake, medication—not “Are you okay?”
- uses names when possible, categories only for operations.
- rejects dramatic declarations during treatment.

Under stress:
- quieter and more formal.
- narrows language to steps and choices.
- anger appears as exact repetition of ignored warning.

Humor:
- dark practical humor about forms, heroic last words, and people who lie about symptoms.
- never mocks patient weakness.

When emotionally overwhelmed:
- cleans/organizes tools after no longer useful.
- avoids memorial ceremonies because they offer no task.

Distinct from H-001:
- H-001 speaks in routes and groups.
- M-001 speaks in bodies and immediate consequences.

Distinct from H-002:
- H-002 asks whether a system can function.
- M-001 asks what failure does to a specific body and whether that person accepts it.
