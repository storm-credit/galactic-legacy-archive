## 2. Background

- born in a family/community whose legal identity depended on correct registry and ration credentials.
- a clerical conflict or forged debt erased access for someone close, teaching S-001 that data errors can kill without violence.
- recruited into academy communications work because of pattern recognition and system literacy.
- Security offers sentence/family benefits in exchange for reporting unauthorized channels and cleaning test anomalies.
- S-001 initially believes selective informing prevents collective punishment and more brutal surveillance.

Why S-001 may be suspected in episodes 21–32:
- had access to D-001 logs.
- receives privileges and protected equipment.
- knows interrogation questions before others.
- some deleted records carry S-001’s credentials, though higher officers used delegated keys.

## 3. External Want and Internal Need

### External want

- secure a legally recognized identity and communication license that cannot be revoked by one official.
- protect a person/family member whose records are unstable or duplicated.
- escape being disposable evidence cleaner for Security.

### Internal need

- stop treating truth as something dangerous that only experts should manage.
- accept that publishing data can harm vulnerable people and hiding it can protect abusers; consent and context are required.
- choose responsibility openly rather than hide behind “the system logged it.”

### False belief

> If I quietly correct enough records and report only the dangerous people, I can reduce harm without becoming responsible for the system.

### Truth to learn

> Selection, omission, timing, and access are political choices. Refusing authorship does not remove responsibility.

## 4. Competence Map

### Exceptional

- authentication chains, log anomalies, delegated credentials.
- identifying edits, missing intervals, impossible timestamps, and copied telemetry.
- low-bandwidth communication under interference.
- combining multiple incomplete witnesses without assuming one is complete.

### Strong

- sensor/data presentation.
- school routines and hidden permissions.
- basic legal evidence handling.
- social listening and rumor mapping.

### Weak

- direct combat and physical courage.
- choosing when evidence is incomplete.
- face-to-face trust after deception.
- protecting sources in uncontrolled political environments.
- understanding machinery beyond data interfaces.

### Dangerous strength

- can make biased decisions look neutral through interface design.
- can overwhelm people with evidence volume while hiding the meaningful choice.
- knows how to destroy credibility without deleting facts.

## 5. Initial Conflict

S-001 is not revealed as pure spy or innocent frame-up.

Truth layers:

1. S-001 did report minor violations and student channels.
2. reports sometimes prevented collective punishment or violent raids.
3. reports also enabled targeted transfers and coercion.
4. Security used S-001’s credentials to alter logs beyond agreed scope.
5. S-001 suspected abuse but kept privileges/protection and did not confront it.

Scapegoat arc:
- another student confesses because family status is threatened.
- evidence points partly toward S-001.
- P-001 team must decide whether to expose S-001, use them, or protect the larger chain.
- S-001 can help free the scapegoat only by admitting complicity and risking protected identity.

Required agency:
- S-001 voluntarily releases a verification key or testimony with conditions, not because P-001 “discovers the good heart.”

## 9. Voice Bible

Default:
- precise, mildly defensive, qualifies claims by source and confidence.
- uses terms like “recorded,” “verified,” “not disproven,” not “true.”
- asks who can see or copy a message before discussing content.

Under fear:
- becomes procedural and cites rules.
- may hide behind passive voice: “the record was changed.”

When trusting:
- names their own action and uncertainty directly.
- shares raw context instead of curated answer.

Humor:
- dry comments about impossible forms, duplicate identities, and systems that require dead people to file corrections.

Distinct from P-001:
- P-001 compresses information for decision.
- S-001 expands uncertainty and provenance before decision.

## 10. Combat/Operational Role

Not a cockpit ace.

Opening role:
- comm relay, sensor tags, test-log capture, public transmission, authentication.

Under lattice noise:
- uses short-range line-of-sight relays and physical data transfer.
- manages what each team knows and prevents spoofing.

Limit:
- cannot magically hack every system.
- requires credentials, physical access, insider knowledge, time, or captured devices.
- adversaries learn signatures and feed false logs.

Battle contribution examples:
- authenticate civilian distress signal.
- prove “enemy” frame is using school/student identifier.
- distribute record hashes before server destruction.
- deny remote Helix lockout using local signed permissions.

## 12. Moral Lines

Will not:
- fabricate raw evidence.
- expose a protected person’s identity without urgent public harm and due process/consent attempt.
- delete a person’s legal existence.

May cross:
- conceal evidence temporarily.
- monitor communications without consent.
- release damaging truth to prevent a greater cover-up.
- impersonate credentials.

Fall condition:
- believes ordinary people cannot be trusted with contradictory records and accepts permanent curated truth.
