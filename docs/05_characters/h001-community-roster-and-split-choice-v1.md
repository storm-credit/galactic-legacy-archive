## 1. Working Community

Working designation:
- `도란 K-7 공동배급 4조`.

Population at GA2 relocation crisis:
- 36 people formally/irregularly linked to the same guarantee account.

Legal identities:
- 27 currently recognized.
- 4 duplicated/contested.
- 3 administratively closed but physically alive.
- 2 minors/dependents registered under another adult’s file.

Age bands:
- 7 children under 13.
- 8 youth/young adults 13–22.
- 14 working adults 23–59.
- 7 older/disabled/chronic-treatment residents.

Core rule:
- the community is one financial/legal unit but not one political will.
- no route can satisfy all medical, legal, work, cultural and security needs.
- GA2 victory is not keeping all 36 together; it is enabling informed, supported, reversible choices where possible.

### Group B — Young Workers and Couriers

Skills:

Primary preference:

Likely destinations:

Conflict:

### Group C — Industrial/Repair Workers

Count:
- 8.

Skills:
- low/medium-grade maintenance, recycling, fabrication, pressure/habitat work.

Primary preference:
- work with real certification and family relocation.

Likely destinations:
- Brann, Neris, Helix apprenticeship at Orsen/Lumen, Ardis production.

Fear:
- open workshop economy lacks medical/safety support; Helix contracts recreate lock-in.

Conflict:
- split between certified Helix route and open cooperative route.

### Group D — Medically Dependent / Identity-Closed

Count:
- 6 including PC-003.

Needs:
- neural stabilizer, prosthetic care, oxygen/medication, identity correction, caregiver access.

Primary preference:
- not uniform; some value Orsen treatment, others fear Helix/Black Ward control.

Likely destinations:
- Orsen with negotiated rights, Kael/Neutral medical route, Ardis community clinic if capacity built.

Fear:
- becoming evidence/patient assets or losing care during transit.

Conflict:
- their treatment needs consume ship space, route priority and funds; they reject being used to justify group control.

### Group E — Children/Dependent Minors

Count:
- 7; overlaps with care burdens but tracked separately.

Needs:
- guardianship, education, stable air/food/medicine, identity and long-term safety.

Decision authority:
- not one adult automatically; guardianship claims may conflict.

Possible destinations:
- Kael education/resettlement, Vela family settlement, Ardis school/cooperative, remain with chosen guardian.

Conflict:
- adults may invoke children to justify incompatible routes.

## 4. Destination Packages

No destination is simply good or bad.

### D1 — Kael Neutral Cooperative

Capacity:
- can accept 8–12 immediately under guarantee; more later if housing/funding arranged.

Benefits:
- legal review, mixed work, route access, medical transfer, lower forced military risk.

Costs:
- fees/debt, crowded housing, slow identity process, political neutrality limits, no guaranteed citizenship.

Best fit:
- care group, some children, record/claims cases.

### D2 — Orsen Medical/Helix Route

Capacity:
- 4–8 patients/dependents under treatment/work contracts.

Benefits:
- best immediate medicine/prosthetics, certification/work opportunities.

Costs:
- data/treatment lock-in, contract control, family separation, identity requirements.

Best fit:
- some Group D and PC-004 route.

### D3 — Lysa/Taris Independence Route

Capacity:
- 6–15 physically, but long-term food/medical/legal support uncertain.

Benefits:
- exit from Imperial bundled contract, mobility, community/political agency.

Costs:
- route danger, weak recognition, possible militia/transport obligations, faction conflict.

Best fit:
- PC-002 and some young workers.

### D4 — Brann/Neris Industrial Route

Capacity:
- 6–12 workers plus limited dependents if workshop/guild sponsors.

Benefits:
- work, technical training, less direct military control, H-002 network.

Costs:
- uneven safety, debt/guild politics, housing/medical limits, Helix pressure.

Best fit:
- industrial workers and selected youth.

### D5 — Ardis Joint-Trust Candidate

Capacity at GA2 end:
- promises 12–20 but actual housing/medical/node crisis unstable.

Benefits:
- community representation, production jobs, future local citizenship, mixed care/work.

Costs:
- imminent political/node conflict, delayed legal recognition, resource shortage.

Best fit:
- those willing to build new institution and accept risk.

### D6 — Remain at Doran Temporarily

Capacity:
- infrastructure still exists but contract/route pressure worsening.

Benefits:
- existing home/work/social ties, no immediate transit trauma.

Costs:
- bundled-account vulnerability, ration freeze, likely future seizure or collapse.

Best fit:
- people rejecting forced relocation or waiting for proof.

## 5. Ship Capacity and Convoy Reality

First ship normal passenger capacity:
- 72.

Why carrying 36 is still hard:
- existing crew/cadets already overcrowd habitation.
- medically dependent passengers need beds, power, oxygen, caregiver space and privacy.
- belongings/tools/food/records compete with repair cargo.
- ship cannot visit all destinations without weeks of travel, route fees and changing risks.
- some people lack documents/clearance for Kael/Orsen/Lumen.

Opening convoy configuration options:

### Option A — Medical/Refugee module

- safely transports more vulnerable passengers.
- loses workshop/cargo capability needed for repair/payment.

### Option B — Split convoy with contracted ships

- preserves ship capability but relies on Neutral/merchant/Independence partners and increases separation/control risk.

### Option C — Multiple journeys

- reduces crowding but leaves some people exposed for weeks and may lose route access.

Recommended story solution:
- hybrid split convoy with community vote and independent destination groups, not a single heroic pickup.

## 6. Decision Process

### Information phase

H-001 must provide:
- destination capacity.
- travel/medical risk.
- legal/contract consequences.
- what cannot be guaranteed.

S-001 must provide:
- identity/record status and data-release risk.

M-001/medical staff:
- treatment and transit risk, without using health to remove all choice.

Q-001/CAP-001:
- ship capacity, cost, route and safety.

PC-001/002/003 and rotating focals:
- community priorities and dissent.

### Consent model

- adults choose individually after group resource implications are disclosed.
- minors/dependents use contested guardianship process with advocate/medical input.
- destination offers can be rejected.
- one person’s choice cannot automatically dissolve another’s ration/medical status without negotiated replacement.

### Vote/negotiation

Not simple majority rule:
- some resources are joint and must be allocated.
- destination subgroups negotiate shares of common funds, tools, records and caregiver labor.
- those remaining retain claims and voice.

## 8. Conflict and Failure Events

At least three of the following occur:

1. destination retracts capacity after political pressure.
2. forged/closed identity blocks boarding or treatment.
3. community member hides medical/contract fact to stay with group.
4. radical rescue cell attempts to take the whole community.
5. Helix offers treatment only with restrictive data/work contract.
6. Neutral insurer refuses high-risk passenger/cargo combination.
7. ship repair forces a stop/delay that exposes remaining residents.
8. one chosen subgroup refuses H-001’s escort and hires another carrier.
9. route attack targets records/people, not only ship.
10. a young/older member supports centralized route against expected culture/faction.

No tragedy is inserted only to show H-001 should have kept everyone together.

## 9. H-001 Character Movement

Start:
- believes a sufficiently detailed distributed plan can preserve community choice and survival.

Pressure:
- choices alter shared resources and create unequal risk.
- some people want institutions H-001 distrusts.
- some resent his school-acquired authority and future plans.

Wrong move:
- may optimize/group destinations before fully disclosing uncertainty or personal preference.

Correction:
- separates resource coordination from political representation.
- accepts multiple representatives and destination groups.
- writes/negotiates ongoing obligations between separated groups.

Gain:
- first true route federation seed: connected groups with mutual minimum obligations, not one physically unified community.

Loss:
- emotional home disperses.
- H-001 cannot count everyone in one place.
- some members choose factions or contracts he opposes.
- his protection becomes political responsibility rather than personal promise.

## 10. P-001 Role Limit

P-001 may:
- secure route/ship/ceasefire options.
- reveal relevant future uncertainty and strategic danger with source confidence.
- protect convoy tactically.

P-001 may not:
- choose destinations based on future hero value.
- override community votes because one member matters historically.
- promise safety he cannot operationally provide.
- turn every subgroup into his coalition asset.

Required moment:
- a person with high future value chooses a route outside P-001’s control, and he permits/defends that choice.

## 11. Collection/Progression Effect

The community does not become one collected faction.

Collection states:
- `Doran guarantee account`: broken/deprecated as coercive structure.
- `route obligations`: several bilateral/multilateral agreements.
- `community representatives`: independent relationships.
- `passenger/medical/identity rights`: operational templates.
- `route federation seed`: unlocked only after groups remain connected despite separation.

Reward:
- H-001’s system gains redundancy and consent.

Cost:
- fewer centralized resources, slower decisions, harder protection.

## 13. Ethics and Agency Checklist

Before each community scene:
- who wants what unrelated to H-001?
- who bears unpaid care/work?
- which information is missing or controlled?
- who cannot safely speak?
- what route/institutional capacity is real?
- what choice remains reversible?
- what shared resource does one choice remove from others?
- are illness, age or childhood being used to erase agency?
- is the story punishing someone merely for choosing stability, separation or self-interest?
