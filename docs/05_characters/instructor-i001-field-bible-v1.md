## 2. External Want and Internal Need

### External want

- keep as many students alive and employable as possible.
- preserve the academy long enough to reform it from within.
- prevent Security, Research, Helix, or Imperial factions from taking sole control.
- ensure their own past sacrifices were not meaningless.

### Internal need

- stop treating selective protection as the only realistic compassion.
- acknowledge students’ right to reject the academy even when the outside is dangerous.
- accept public accountability for manipulated evaluations and deployment choices.
- train successors and student governance rather than remain indispensable protector.

### False belief

> I cannot save everyone, so the only honest choice is to spend my influence on those most likely to survive and later change the system.

### Truth to learn

> Hidden favoritism preserves individuals while reproducing a system that decides whose future is worth investment. Protection must become a rule and right, not a private exception.

## 3. Past Compromise

- manipulated scores to move vulnerable students away from lethal missions.
- as a consequence, other less-connected cells were assigned those missions.
- falsified equipment failures and medical restrictions.
- accepted Helix or Imperial sponsorship for specific students.
- withheld proof of black missions because exposure would close the academy and disperse students.
- may have signed or failed to stop H-001’s original trial/deployment conditions.

Not responsible for everything:
- does not control family contracts, Black Ward, or all mission design.
- sometimes received false risk reports.
- saved real lives through compromise.

Required named consequence:
- at least one surviving student resents I-001 because their cell replaced a protected favorite.
- one dead/transferred student remains in I-001’s private record.

## 4. Competence Map

### Exceptional

- reading trainee fear, fatigue, team dynamics, and hidden injuries.
- adapting drills to poor equipment and diverse backgrounds.
- field command of inexperienced students.
- navigating academy bureaucracy and deployment rules.
- knowing which staff can be pressured, bribed, or trusted.

### Strong

- standard maneuver-frame operation.
- rescue/boarding doctrine.
- casualty prevention and after-action review.
- informal alumni network.

### Weak

- high politics and corporate law.
- large fleet command.
- radical transparency.
- asking students what future they want when the answer threatens the institution.
- recognizing their own paternalism.

### Blind spots

- equates survival with success even under lifelong contract bondage.
- sees exceptional students as future leverage.
- underestimates collective student organization.
- assumes graduates will reform the system later rather than be absorbed by it.

## 7. Voice Bible

Default:
- concise, practical, without ceremonial military flourish.
- addresses students by name/call sign, not inmate number when possible.
- gives reasons for tactical instructions but not political choices.
- dislikes promises and inspirational language.

Under fear:
- becomes more controlling and assigns positions without consultation.
- voice gets softer rather than louder.

When guilty:
- provides material help instead of apology.
- changes subject to next task.

Humor:
- veteran dry humor about paperwork, academy equipment, and students who think survival drills are beneath them.

Distinct from P-001:
- P-001 thinks in strategic outcomes and future roles.
- I-001 thinks in current cohort limits, injury, bureaucracy, and who can survive tonight.
