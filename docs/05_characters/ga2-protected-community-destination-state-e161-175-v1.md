## 3. DG-CARE — Glasswater/Lentz

Composition:
- 8 people requiring current or likely advanced/long-term treatment;
- 5 chosen caregivers/household members;
- no caregiver is automatically assigned by blood, debt or Haren’s decision.

Service offer:
- initial 180-day care compact;
- emergency and chronic-care access;
- one caregiver berth per approved care unit, with exceptions reviewed individually;
- identity reconciliation and restricted medical-data portability;
- independent patient appeal through Glasswater Care Fund/Long-Care Union structures.

Conditions/costs:
- treatment eligibility requires current clinical assessment;
- employer/corporate service data remains contested;
- housing is shared/temporary for the first 60–120 days;
- not all procedures are free or immediately available;
- one person’s most appropriate care remains in Lentz rather than Glasswater, requiring later subgroup separation.

Rights:
- may refuse data use beyond care;
- may change caregiver;
- may leave or request another jurisdiction;
- treatment does not create corporate/state ownership or permanent labor duty.

Transport:
- Neutral medical passage carries the first 13 rather than 회랑새 alone;
- 회랑새 contributes escort, records and transfer support only where required.

## 4. DG-ARDIS — Work and Residency

Composition:
- 9 people across 3 units;
- mix of industrial/service skills, dependent household members and one person requiring ongoing but locally manageable care.

Offer:
- 12-month provisional residence;
- paid training/work through Ardis Foundry Shift Council/Civic Technical Institute or public-service contractors;
- local ration/housing access;
- path to resident standing after work, care and local review.

Not guaranteed:
- permanent citizenship;
- one employer or district;
- immediate private housing;
- safe/stable L2 node service;
- automatic portability back to Imperial systems.

Risks:
- Ardis remains damaged and politically contested;
- work need can become coercive recruitment;
- local registry may undervalue outside records;
- transit delays and sanctions can strand the group.

Rights:
- group members sign individual/household contracts;
- workers retain wage and safety claims;
- dependents are not employment collateral;
- members may withdraw before final boarding or appeal after arrival.

## 5. DG-ROUTE — Mobile and Ship Paths

Composition:
- 7 people across 2 household/care units.

Initial paths:
- 2 people qualify for bounded trial positions on 회랑새 after training/licensing;
- 2 choose other route/convoy crews rather than the protagonist ship;
- 3 choose Open Passage/Varka mutual-aid, repair or hospitality work.

Rules:
- employment aboard 회랑새 does not make them permanent “crew family” automatically;
- temporary ship work can end at the next port;
- route households retain destination and household choice;
- no one is required to join Ern’s network or Haren’s federation.

Risks:
- irregular wages and route separation;
- weaker stable housing/medical access;
- identity and insurance gaps;
- mobile work may repeat earlier dependence under a different flag.

## 6. DG-STAY — K-13

Composition:
- 4 people across 2 units.

Reasons for staying:
- current schooling or apprenticeship;
- active wage/medical/identity claims;
- local family/relationship ties;
- inability or unwillingness to accept the risks of transit.

Protections:
- renewed K-13 local-service/charter agreement;
- no punishment for declining the convoy;
- access to future reassessment and departure;
- current claims remain active.

Narrative rule:
- staying is not cowardice, failure or lack of imagination;
- their later ordinary life and local political choices remain visible.

## 7. DG-PRIVATE — Destination Secrecy

Composition:
- 3 people in one current support/relationship unit.

Reason:
- public destination/identity records create a material security risk;
- their preferred route is legal or tolerable only through protected Neutral/private arrangements.

Disclosure layers:
- Neutral safety authority knows emergency contact and minimum life-support needs;
- Haren knows only that consent and transport viability were independently verified;
- public/community list records `private route — confirmed`;
- Rian, Academy and route sponsors receive no destination detail.

Rules:
- secrecy cannot hide abuse from independent welfare/safety review;
- family claimants may use an appeal process without receiving automatic location disclosure;
- Haren cannot retain the sealed destination “for their protection.”

## 8. Representation Structure

Five destination groups each select or rotate one contact function:
- care/service contact;
- contract/claim contact;
- transport contact;
- consent/appeal contact.

One person may hold more than one function inside a small group, but:
- no contact may sign permanent destination/identity decisions for competent adults;
- household guardianship and individual consent remain separate;
- Haren coordinates cross-group resources and route negotiations only.

## 9. Revision and Exit Windows

- before final boarding: unrestricted withdrawal subject only to current safety process;
- during transit: route change if capacity, medical and receiving jurisdiction allow;
- within 30 days of arrival: expedited placement appeal;
- within 180 days: destination compact review;
- after one year: ordinary local law or renewed cross-route agreement.

A change may cost money, time, housing or route availability. The right exists without guaranteeing a cost-free alternative.

## 10. Transport Allocation

The 36 do not travel together on one ship.

Planned first movement:
- Neutral medical passage: DG-CARE 13;
- 회랑새/associated convoy: parts of DG-ROUTE and transfer/evidence support;
- Ardis convoy segment: DG-ARDIS 9;
- K-13: DG-STAY 4 do not depart;
- sealed independent transport: DG-PRIVATE 3.

This prevents:
- 회랑새 exceeding passenger/medical limits;
- one accident endangering all 36;
- Haren or Rian controlling every destination;
- community identity being equated with permanent co-location.
