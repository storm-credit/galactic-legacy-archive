## 6. First Garden–Transit Transfer — E226

Movement:
- six cargo pods;
- three tugs;
- approximately 186 t total.

Failure:
- Transit receiving loop loses direct state on one branch;
- current temperature remains inside range;
- sponsor and market actors press to continue;
- local receiving operator exercises valid stop.

Outcome:
- three pods delivered on time;
- two pods delayed;
- one pod’s sensitive contents suffer about 18% downgrade/spoilage;
- approximately 14–18 t equivalent service/value loss or downgrade;
- no major injury or receiving-loop rupture;
- distribution becomes unequal and politically visible.

Decisive actor:
- local Transit receiving operator, not Rian, prevents the larger failure.

Lessons:
- physical state, service class, identity proof and sequence cannot be collapsed into one authorization;
- unknown must remain unknown/hold rather than be guessed green;
- stopping every unknown also creates spoilage and shortage cost.

- current value or range;
- source;
- confidence;
- current dependent people/service;
- stop authority;
- next update time;
- missing or withheld data.

Architecture:
- federated proofs and local data stewards;
- no total city control dashboard;
- stale green states become unknown/hold;
- household, medical, pay, security and route detail remain separately governed.

Benefits:

- external contributors can verify bounded claims.

Costs:

- recurring human labor;
- incomplete prediction;
- less attractive to funders seeking total control.
