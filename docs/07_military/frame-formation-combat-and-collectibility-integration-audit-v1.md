## 1. Audit Ruling

The repository already supports formation warfare. It defines frame elements, flights, squadrons, carriers, maintenance, recovery, readiness, source formations, derived formations and irreversible losses. It does not contain a scene-facing contract that says exactly what one ace may do against a healthy squadron, or a demand-derived bridge from the current 28-row phase-1 chassis sample to the existing formation registry.

The seven independent specialist passes agree on the following integration:

> An ace opens one bounded local window. A stable 6–8-frame named flight core and the surrounding 12–24-frame squadron exploit that window. Ships create approach rights; maintainers, rescue crews, infantry, engineers and technical teams complete capture, rescue, custody and recovery. No single frame replaces those layers.

This is compatible with current canon but remains a proposal. No named core, new capability, exact OOB, pilot assignment or story event is promoted by this document.

## 2. Authority Split

### LOCKED

- A frame element is 2–4 frames.
- A frame flight is 4–8 frames with leader/support pairing.
- A frame squadron is 12–24 frames plus carriers, maintenance and rescue.
- Elite/custom frames are hard to replace, repair and transfer and have no universal superiority.
- Frames need carriers or tenders for strategic movement.
- A sustained organization requires 4–12 direct maintainers per active frame plus shared specialists.
- Fleets create approach and protection geometry; frames handle precision capture, repair, boarding and rescue; infantry, engineers and technical teams complete occupation and restoration.
- Source and derived formations are not additive inventories.
- The first ship cannot sustain a 6–8-frame organic core in GA2, and the protagonist has no permanent squadron then.

### USER DIRECTION

- A healthy 12–24-frame squadron is not casually annihilated by one frame.
- A legendary or ace frame may isolate an opposing ace, interrupt one command link, delay pursuit or open one rescue/capture corridor.
- A 6–8-frame reader-facing core and its support chain turn that opening into a completed mission.

### ASSUMPTIONS

- `[ASSUMPTION FF-01]` A healthy squadron is at least R3, active/coherent, and has no mission-critical readiness field below its operating floor.
- `[ASSUMPTION FF-02]` The 6–8-frame core is a stable flight-strength subset of a parent squadron, not a new echelon and not additional inventory.
- `[ASSUMPTION FF-03]` Ace and legendary are hull-instance/story statuses, not chassis production classes.
- `[ASSUMPTION FF-04]` A fixed base or tender network may substitute for a dedicated carrier only when launch, cooling, reload, repair and recovery throughput are evidenced.

## 4. Four Directions at a Glance

| Direction | Structure | Best effect | Main risk | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Ace Spearpoint** | ace + wingman + mostly background force | strongest single-machine attachment | tournament escalation and invisible support | rare climax accent |
| **B — Wing-Pair Ladder** | 3–4 role pairs form a 6–8-frame core | clear pair synergy and display sets | every relationship becomes a two-person formula | character-heavy arcs |
| **C — Elite Core + Squadron Chronicle** | 6–8-frame core inside 12–24-frame squadron with persistent support and loss history | balances hero, team, formation and logistics collectibility | requires disciplined accounting | **selected base** |
| **D — Formation History First** | emblem, carrier, mandate, losses and rebuilds lead more than individual frames | strongest war scale and anti-obsolescence | weaker early personal machine attachment | GA5–GA10 overlay |

### Selected Hybrid

Use C as the operating structure, B for readable role pairs, D for later formation memory and A only for one bounded local window. The result is not a fixed super-team. It is a stable reader-facing flight whose actual hulls and attachments can change through consent, damage, source duties and mission need.

## 5. Combat Contract

### Mission Chain

1. **Approach:** ships, routes, sensors and long-range defense create access and withdrawal geometry.
2. **Classification:** recon/EW and local witnesses establish what may be attacked, captured or protected.
3. **Local window:** one ace or specialist interrupts one condition for a bounded period.
4. **Core action:** the 6–8-frame flight divides escort, breach, tag, capture and rescue tasks.
5. **Squadron completion:** line flights hold the perimeter, rotate losses, guard withdrawal and prevent reinforcement.
6. **Custody and recovery:** infantry, engineers, technical, medical, tow and maintenance teams finish the objective.
7. **Persistence:** heat, propellant, ammunition, damage, crew condition, authority and recovery time carry forward.

### One Frame Against Formations

| Opponent state | One ace may plausibly do | It may not do |
|---|---|---|
| isolated or damaged 2–4-frame element | defeat, bypass, capture or delay it with a demonstrated advantage and cost | reset immediately for another fresh engagement |
| healthy 4–8-frame flight | isolate one role, mission-kill one hull, break contact or open a route with allied sensing and recovery | casually destroy every hull |
| healthy 12–24-frame squadron | disrupt one command link, bind one ace or open one local corridor after the squadron is divided or pressured | annihilate the squadron, force automatic surrender or cover multiple fronts |
| task group or fleet | affect one boarding, node, rescue or command contact point | replace fleet access, logistics, command or territorial control |

Mission-kill and destruction remain separate. Loss of mobility, sensors, role equipment, command authentication, thermal margin or pilot capacity may end the current mission while leaving a recoverable hull.

## 9. Core Composition Template

A mature 6–8-frame core uses role pairs rather than six to eight unrelated hero machines:

- ace/mission leader + wing/deputy;
- line escort + perimeter/rotation partner;
- recon/EW or command relay + capture/breach specialist;
- rescue/recovery or service specialist, with one rotating slot when the mission requires it.

The exact composition may be 6, 7 or 8. The ace is included. Attached rescue craft, infantry, engineers, medical staff, maintainers and tenders remain separately accounted support and may be more important than another combat frame.

## 10. Logistics and Readiness Gate

No named core or squadron is operational merely because hulls exist. Before a model sheet, OOB or battle card claims readiness, record:

- parent and source formation IDs;
- hull instances and chassis slots;
- carrier, tender or fixed base;
- cradle interface and launch/recovery throughput;
- direct maintainers and shared specialists;
- cooling, propellant, reload and spare-parts path;
- tow, rescue and medical recovery;
- command, certification, insurance and withdrawal authority;
- current heat, ammunition, damage, crew and pilot state;
- starting/ending readiness and earliest credible return.

Six to eight active frames imply 24–96 direct maintainers before shared specialists. Twelve to twenty-four imply 48–288. These are arithmetic consequences of the existing 4–12 maintainer rule, not new staffing canon for a particular formation.

## 11. Identity and Heraldry

The public effect may feel like an elite order, but the formal structure remains a flight inside a parent squadron. Do not use `기사단`, `화랑`, generic animal/weapon orders or another property's suffix grammar as the default naming bucket.

### Identity Layers

1. stable parent formation ID;
2. stable flight/core author ID;
3. one earned public unit name after a shared mission;
4. operation-expiring radio stem and functional suffixes;
5. one nonverbal emblem;
6. reversible 5–8% mission band/plate overlay;
7. hull-instance names separate from pilot callsigns and chassis names.

The selected visual direction is **multi-witness identity**: source markings remain visible, while removable witness marks and mission bands show which communities, support crews and mandates made the operation possible. Hazard, medical, quarantine and maintenance colors override team branding. Overlay changes add zero chassis, zero lineage and zero form counts.

## 12. Collection Desire

### Discovery

- identify a repeated flight signature before its public name exists;
- recognize a familiar hull after transfer, repair or source-mark restoration;
- discover that an apparent ace victory depended on an unseen rescue or service chain.

### Acquisition

- a core forms through temporary detachment, consent, contracts and shared missions, not ownership;
- public identity is earned only after the group completes and records a mission together;
- a hull may refuse transfer, return to its source or become unavailable through support failure.

### Synergy

- ace window set: ace + wing + valid sensing/command condition;
- mission chain set: recon/EW → command/relay → breach/capture → rescue/recovery;
- formation chronicle set: parent ID + carrier/base + support chain + contested order + loss/rebuild/transfer record.

Completion grants operational choice, trust, history and reusable doctrine, not a numerical buff. Pilots, maintainers, crews, institutions and communities are participants with refusal and exit rights, never inventory.

### Loss and Anti-Obsolescence

- damage changes assignments, role pairs and readiness rather than summoning a replacement hero chassis;
- an old line frame can retain value through serviceability, compatibility, local trust and a known recovery path;
- emblems and recovered panels preserve continuity across rebuilds without creating a new machine;
- permanent losses in the loss ledger are not reversed for team completeness.
