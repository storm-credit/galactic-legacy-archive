## 1. Counting Rules

Holdings distinguish:

- **combat hulls** — cutters through capital ships assigned primarily to combat/security;
- **armed support** — tenders, tugs, rescue, cargo and command ships capable of self-defense or mission support;
- **frames/craft** — military/security maneuver frames and combat-capable small craft;
- **ready** — deployable within 72 hours for the listed theater, not merely existing;
- **aligned** — politically sympathetic or contractually available, not centrally commanded;
- **network reach** — ships/forces that can share information, routes or logistics but cannot concentrate quickly.

Global holdings are ranges. Grand-act figures focus on forces that can affect the story’s active theater.

## 2. R0 Global Baseline — GA1 Opening

### Imperial state and recognized provincial forces

- front-line combat hulls: 1,400–2,000;
- patrol/customs/security combat-capable hulls: 2,500–4,500;
- major military support/logistics/repair hulls: 2,000–3,500;
- military/security frames: 45,000–80,000 across 612 systems;
- fully ready front-line hulls: 45–65%;
- theater concentration available without stripping other regions: much smaller than global total.

### Helix and major corporate security

- direct combat/security hulls: 160–280;
- armed industrial/patrol/rescue craft: 500–900;
- security/technical frames: 6,000–12,000;
- high readiness at protected facilities, limited heavy fleet mass.

### Independence and irregular armed networks

- combined known combat-capable hulls: 250–600;
- support/smuggler/converted craft: 800–2,000 loosely aligned;
- military/converted frames: 5,000–15,000;
- no unified command; only a fraction can concentrate.

### Free Route Neutral Assembly

- armed rescue/security hulls: 50–90;
- member civilian rescue/merchant vessels: 700–1,400;
- security/rescue frames and craft: 1,000–3,000;
- central concentration limited by member autonomy and commerce.

### Local/provincial/worker forces outside clean categories

- thousands of local patrol, militia, industrial and contract assets;
- often counted legally under state/corporate/local institutions but operationally autonomous.

## 6. GA4 — Core Succession Crisis

Geographic scale:
- Core political/administrative networks, selected capital nodes, precision military and several fleets in background.

### Major claimant/security holdings — theater relevant

Dynastic/royal claimant:
- 20–45 combat ships directly loyal or ceremonially aligned;
- selected guard/cruiser/capital assets;
- 100–300 frames and security units;
- high symbolic/legal value, uneven logistics.

Regency/civil administration:
- 30–80 combat/security ships through ministries/provinces;
- stronger public-service and treasury access;
- loyalty varies by office.

Protector-General/military bloc:
- 80–180 combat ships across several fleets potentially aligned;
- only 30–90 can concentrate quickly;
- stronger staff and readiness, major coup risk.

Continuity trustee/office:
- limited direct combat, 10–30 security/command/support ships;
- enormous leverage through authentication, pay, route and services.

Provincial/federal actors:
- hundreds of ships outside Core with conditional recognition and local mandates;
- cannot be treated as one force.

### Protagonist role

- 회랑새, 07 and small escort/mission trust;
- access to Ardis/Neutral/fleet relationships;
- no decisive personal fleet;
- influence through records, precision operations and authority split.

### GA4 result

- no final sovereign victory;
- interim authority compact splits civil, military, succession and continuity powers;
- many fleets remain under conflicting lawful loyalties;
- GA5 begins because command, pay, yards and crew allegiance do not align.

## 8. GA6 — Second Orpheus

Geographic/operational scale:
- capital/state-function evacuation and civilian evacuation under one 28–34-hour route window.

### Evacuation fleet pool

Large military/state transports and capital assets:
- 25–45 large warships or capital/state-function carriers potentially sacrificed, rerouted or stripped;
- dozens of cruisers/destroyers/escorts;
- 100–220 civilian/refugee transports and major support ships;
- many small craft, tugs and local ferries;
- exact theoretical capacity exceeds realized safe capacity.

### Ownerless/Common Fleet contribution

- 40–70 combat ships available across several corridors;
- 50–110 support/transport/rescue ships;
- many ships already damaged or underloaded/overloaded;
- cannot centralize all routes without abandoning other fronts.

### Imperial/state-function convoy

- high-grade command, treasury, medical, archive and military ships;
- strong authentication and escorts;
- fewer people per strategic volume because institutions/equipment consume capacity.

### Civilian convoy

- higher passenger density;
- weak protection and life-support margins;
- mixed legal/route access;
- requires escorts, medical and destination capacity.

### Opposing pressure

- enemy fleets and route denial forces sufficient to make time window finite;
- not necessarily a single larger fleet;
- route, node, misinformation and support failures create most losses.

### GA6 result

- mixed evacuation chosen;
- 450,000–850,000 possible dead/missing/unstable range in design model;
- many capital assets and support systems permanently lost;
- 회랑새 loses strategic propulsion and becomes later service/training vessel;
- state, fleet and community continuity all damaged.

## 9. GA7 — Blood Admiral Route War

### Route-war forces

Haren/federated route commands:
- 25–60 combat ships under varying mission/route mandates;
- 40–100 support, transport and local craft;
- 200–600 frames/craft;
- control/value comes from routes, local operators and denial systems.

Central/state and opposing fleets:
- several 40–100-ship formations competing for corridors;
- heavier combat hulls but weaker local route legitimacy;
- infrastructure destruction threatens all.

Local/regional forces:
- dozens of node defense and militia formations;
- may support or oppose Haren independently.

### D4/D5 denial capacity

- only selected nodes/routes have physical and command conditions for high-level denial;
- requires operators, keys, explosives/energy and timing;
- cannot be executed galaxy-wide by one person.

### GA7 result

- one direct Haren D4 order remains his responsibility;
- other destruction attributed across delegated commands, doctrines and propaganda;
- route networks survive without one commander;
- ships and local forces split into later record-war constituencies.

## 11. GA9 — Preservation Regime

Existing hulls authenticated and restored rather than newly built.

Core/synchronized corridors:
- 300–500 major combat ships across Core and key corridors;
- 400–800 patrol/security/escort hulls;
- large support and logistic network;
- readiness often R3–R5 due pay, parts and command restoration.

Provincial/partner forces:

- mixed political loyalty but Aurel-authenticated service.

Continuity enforcement/rapid response:
- 40–100 high-readiness ships across several task groups;
- technical/security frames, boarding and node teams;
- relies on local partners for occupation/service.

### Opposition/alternative forces

Ardis/mission-trust networks:
- 80–160 combat ships across broad network;
- only 30–80 concentratable;
- support and local legitimacy, mixed synchronization.

Frontier/Independence/localists:
- 60–180 combat-capable ships dispersed;
- hidden routes and local support;
- low sustained readiness.

Palimpsest refusal belt:
- 20–40 combat ships and strong local denial.

Neutral/Helix internal splits:
- significant assets support regime services while resisting political scope.

### Rian central-key phase

- he can synchronize selected existing forces and services;

- first handoff fails or is attacked;

## 12. GA10 — Transition War and Plural Defense

### Starting regime force

- hundreds of combat/security ships remain authenticated and supplied;
- no clean division between regime and transition forces;
- units choose by domain, region and mission.

### Transition coalitions

Ardis/mission trusts:
- 60–140 combat ships across regions;
- strong support/repair and shared-warning roles.

Core reform/continuity forces:
- 100–250 ships support staged transfer while preserving services;
- some remain centrally synchronized temporarily.

Provincial treaty forces:
- hundreds of local ships, but most stay in home regions;
- potential warlord or stabilizing role.

Frontier/mobile service forces:
- cutters, escorts, rescue and small local fleets;
- strategically important for weak regions.

Palimpsest/AI/personhood defense:
- small combat mass, strong route/data/technical denial and political significance.

Hardline central and opportunist forces:
- 40–120 high-readiness ships across several blocs;
- attempt to capture transition failures or central residue.

### End-state defense

No single permanent fleet command.

Retained:
- regional defense;
- mission-specific theater commands;
- shared threat warning;
- interregional support and reserve;
- limited multi-trigger central coordination.

Lost:
- universal standing command and one master military key.

### Epilogue range

- overall combat hull count lower than R0 after years of war;
- readiness uneven;
- capital-ship losses not fully replaced;
- support, rescue and local defense receive greater institutional status;
- some regions maintain synchronized defense voluntarily.

## 14. Industrial Replacement and War Budget Bands

Design use only.

### Regional task group campaign month

- 10–30 combat ships plus support;
- 20–120 million BSC moderate operation;
- 100–500+ million high-intensity with damage/ammunition.

### Fleet campaign month

- 30–120 combat ships plus support;
- 0.3–2+ billion BSC depending intensity and replacement.

### Theater concentration

- several fleets;
- billions to tens of billions BSC per month;
- consumes regional production, route and political capital.

### Replacement limits

- cutters/corvettes and converted craft replace fastest;
- destroyers/cruisers only partially replaced during main story;
- capital ships lost in GA4–10 are strategic permanent losses;
- support-ship and skilled-crew losses can be harder to replace than combat hulls.

## 15. Force Continuity Rules

- every major battle updates holdings and readiness;
- no destroyed named formation returns without explicit reconstruction;
- captured ships enter a repair/crew/pay/legal queue;
- aligned ships do not automatically stay aligned next act;
- readiness recovery needs time, route, yard, parts, pay and rest;
- fleet counts use role and location, not one global total;
- local forces can refuse theater concentration;
- support and civilian capacity are subtracted when used for war.
