## 1. Registry Principle

The setting contains thousands of military and security hulls, but the story tracks a smaller front-stage formation set.

Each front-stage formation receives:

- stable formation ID;
- home/service base;
- lawful or practical command source;
- combat and support envelope;
- readiness state;
- doctrine and constituency;
- first activation and final state.

Unlisted forces remain in regional pools. They cannot appear as unlimited reinforcements; any activation must deduct from a named pool and record travel/support time.

## 2. State Code

- `A` active and coherent;
- `D` detached/split among missions;
- `R` refit/reconstitution;
- `L` logistics-limited;
- `P` politically immobilized;
- `C` command-contested;
- `I` interned/impounded;
- `X` destroyed/disbanded;
- `T` transformed into a different institution.

Readiness remains R0–R5 from the military bible.

## 8. Background Force Pools

Background pools account for the rest of the galaxy-scale holdings.

| Pool | R0 combat-capable envelope | Allocation rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Imperial core/inner unlisted fleets | 900–1,250 front-line hulls | tied to home defense, yards and succession legitimacy |
| Imperial provincial/frontier patrol | 2,500–4,500 small/security hulls | mostly cannot leave region without replacement policing |
| Helix unlisted security/armed industry | 600–1,000 craft/hulls | distributed across contracts and yards |
| Independence/local armed pool | 200–500 hulls | politically incompatible and locally supported |
| Neutral/merchant emergency pool | 700–1,400 vessels | mostly civilian; availability requires charter consent |

A background pool may create a new front-stage formation only through an operation sheet stating:

- source systems;
- mobilization time;
- command basis;
- support allocation;
- what duty is left uncovered;
- post-operation disposition.

## 9. Loss Event Schema

Every significant operation creates loss records in six categories.

1. **Hull** — destroyed, captured, interned, abandoned, converted.
2. **Support** — tanker, repair, hospital, cargo, tug, communication loss.
3. **Personnel** — dead, missing, injured, exhausted, detained, defected.
4. **Readiness** — ammunition, propellant, heat life, maintenance and crew cohesion.
5. **Authority** — command legitimacy, keys, charter, insurance and surrender standing.
6. **Constituency** — family support, wages, patient continuity, local security and political trust.

Required fields:

- operation/episode range;
- formation ID;
- starting and ending state;
- exact known loss;
- uncertain/missing band;
- repair/replacement source;
- earliest credible return;
- named people/communities affected;
- downstream arc references.

## 10. Replacement Time Floors

| Loss | Minimum credible restoration under stable conditions |
|---|---|
| frame ammunition/tool reload | hours–days with compatible tender |
| damaged frame | days–months |
| lost trained frame team | months–years |
| cutter/corvette repair | weeks–months |
| frigate/destroyer major repair | months–2 years |
| new frigate/destroyer | 1.5–5 years depending yard/war |
| cruiser | 4–10 years |
| capital hull | 8–20+ years |
| support ship and specialist crew | often slower than combat hull replacement |
| destroyed node hardware | months–years; trust/authentication may take longer |

The six-year main timeline therefore cannot replace major losses cleanly.

## 11. Grand-Act Persistence Rules

- GA2 losses modify GA3 arrival capability and debt.
- GA3 yard/node damage modifies GA4 succession mobility.
- GA4 command splits create GA5 ownerless formations.
- GA5 integration failures determine GA6 evacuation capacity.
- GA6 support and civilian losses constrain GA7 route war.
- GA7 denial and responsibility records shape GA8 evidence custody.
- GA8 archive/security fragmentation shapes GA9 regime adoption.
- GA9 coherent regime forces remain materially superior entering GA10.
- GA10 distribution does not erase ammunition, repair, wage or family obligations.
