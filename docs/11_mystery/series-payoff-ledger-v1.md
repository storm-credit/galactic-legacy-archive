## 1. Ledger Rules

Every major promise must have:

- `Plant`: first observable fact or absence.
- `Question`: what the reader can reasonably ask.
- `False Model`: plausible but incomplete explanation.
- `Advance`: new evidence that changes confidence.
- `Partial Payoff`: useful answer with consequence.
- `Reinterpretation`: answer gains new meaning.
- `Final Payoff`: irreversible choice/result.
- `Aftermath`: how the answer changes institutions or relationships.

Fairness rules:

1. final answer cannot require a rule introduced after GA8.
2. a reinterpreted clue must remain factually true in its original scene.
3. no mystery stays alive only because characters refuse an obvious conversation.
4. each grand act resolves at least one major thread and opens no more than two major new threads.
5. unresolved ambiguity is permitted only when the story’s answer is “multiple records remain,” not because planning is incomplete.

Thread status:
- `CORE`: ending-critical.
- `MAJOR`: grand-act critical.
- `SUPPORT`: character/world enrichment.

## M-001 — “당신은 역사에 기록되지 않았습니다”

Priority: CORE

### Plant — Episode 1

- after death/return, Archive displays that P-001 is not recorded in history.

Question:
- why would the final admiral be absent?
- is he unimportant, erased, or outside the system?

False Model — GA1

- his defeated side erased him.
- he was only an acting admiral with no official legitimacy.

Advance — GA2–3

- Archive records his battles under other heroes/units.
- people he remembers as supporting him are listed as sole heroes.
- open-service lineage contains his tactical traces but no name.

Partial Payoff — GA4

- Imperial Intelligence files show deliberate suppression of irregular officers and illegal commands.
- this explains some absence, not Archive’s response.

Reinterpretation — GA7–8

- identity-compression analysis shows P-001 appears as a connective pattern across many records but never as the ranked center.
- Continuity Seed simulations repeatedly fail using only recorded heroes.

Final Payoff — GA8/9

- Seed selected him as an unclassified connector: not the best hero, but the variable most widely linking people, systems, and decisions outside official history.

Aftermath — GA10

- P-001 refuses to replace absence with one definitive heroic record.
- official accounts remain plural; his final growth is asking current people who they are rather than retrieving future labels.

Required clues:
- irregular legal status.
- achievements attributed to units/others.
- multiple records contain unnamed command anomalies.
- Archive can track relations even when identity is absent.

## M-002 — The changing “Thirteen Heroes” count

Priority: CORE / recurring collection hook

### Plant — Episodes 6–20

- Archive displays `황혼의 열세 영웅 ?/13`.
- after D-004, count briefly becomes 12, then returns to 13 with an unnamed blank.

Question:
- are there fixed destined heroes?
- who disappeared or was added?

False Model — GA1–2

- a literal group of thirteen future heroes must be collected.

Advance — GA2

- two records point to the same person under different names.
- a ship or organization appears as a “hero.”

Partial Payoff — GA4–5

- imperial propaganda assembled the “thirteen” from different cohorts and institutions.
- the count is a preservation quota/category, not destiny.

Reinterpretation — GA7

- titles and collective actions were compressed into individuals, as with Blood Admiral.
- blanks change because historical centrality moves after divergence.

Final Payoff — GA8–10

- Continuity Seed had limited high-priority identity slots in one emergency preservation layer.
- P-001 rejects fixed quotas; plural archives stop ranking a permanent canonical thirteen.

Aftermath:
- “Thirteen Heroes” survives as a cultural story with multiple local versions, not official truth.

Fair clue requirement:
- at least one noncombat person, institution, or ship must enter the count before GA3.

Priority: CORE / hardware-payoff

- museum/hero worship is rejected in favor of living technical education.

Required reuse:
- GA1 rescue/capture.
- GA2 salvage/standard.
- GA3 mass derivative.
- GA6 evacuation.
- GA8 Seed interface.
- GA10 distributed transition.

## M-004 — H-001 / “피의 제독” identity

Priority: CORE character mystery

### Plant — Episode 2–3

- gentle current cadet has mass-casualty future record.

Question:
- how does he become a mass killer?
- can P-001 prevent it?

Reinterpretation — GA7

Aftermath:
- title remains as contested history rather than erased stain.

Required fairness:
- early use of collective/title identity in other records.
- at least one atrocity truly chosen by H-001 in changed timeline or credible near-crossing; otherwise responsibility is hollow.

## 3. Episode/Act Payoff Rhythm

- every 5–10 episodes: resolve one local question.
- every 20–30 episodes: reinterpret one earlier fact or relationship.
- every subact: close its immediate goal and advance one series thread.
- every grand act: final-payoff or major partial-payoff for at least one MAJOR/CORE thread.
- never keep a mystery alive by replacing answer with a larger unrelated mystery.

## 4. Broken-Promise Audit

Before any grand-act promotion, check:

- Did a previously promised person/object/set disappear without use?
- Did the answer change the protagonist’s or a faction’s choices?
- Can the reader identify earlier clues after the reveal?
- Did the reveal merely explain lore, or did it create a cost/decision?
- Has any CORE thread gone more than two grand acts without advancement?
- Is an ambiguity deliberate and thematically supported, or merely unresolved design?
