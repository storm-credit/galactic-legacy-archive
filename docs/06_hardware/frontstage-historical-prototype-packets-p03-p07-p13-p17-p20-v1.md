## 0. Why only five packets

A twenty-slot table does **not** justify twenty full historical bibles.

These five were selected because together they test all important outcome types and series functions:

- **P-03 — O1 failure:** human-machine performance can fail and still change medicine/doctrine;
- **P-07 — O3 lineage success:** 07/service interoperability anchor;
- **P-13 — O4 exceptional dead end:** spectacular success without scalable lineage;
- **P-17 — O2 partial institutional success:** authority/authentication survives as procedure, not machine;
- **P-20 — O3 production success:** reproducibility/mass manufacture matters more than duel performance.

They are deliberately not five hero machines.

# 1. P-03 — Interface / Acceleration Failure Article

Working author-side ID:
- `P-03`.

Outcome:
- **O1 — whole-article failure.**

Present provenance:
- HOLD; preferred pressure R0/R1, not operational survivor.

## 1.1 Historical question

> Can a maneuver article combine higher sustained acceleration with direct high-bandwidth pilot coupling strongly enough to let one trained operator control a denser maneuver envelope than conventional utility frames?

This is not yet “superpilot technology.”
It is an early attempt to make human control keep pace with increasingly aggressive maneuver hardware.

## 1.2 Why it was funded

Layered-crisis context:
- service frames are being pushed into contested rescue/security work;
- conventional controls lag under rapid attitude changes and damaged-sensor states;
- military evaluators see an opportunity for interception/recovery.

Institutional temptation:
- instead of simplifying the mission envelope, increase pilot bandwidth and acceleration tolerance.

## 1.3 What worked

Possible successful subresults:
- improved restraint/load-distribution geometry;
- faster motor-command translation;
- high-rate physiological monitoring;
- early warning for sync/load mismatch;
- better short-window sensor-to-control integration.

These can be O2-like subsystem results inside an O1 whole failure.

## 1.4 Why the whole article failed

Failure should combine at least three layers:

### Medical
- vascular/respiratory/neural stress outside sustainable population range;
- feedback injury persists after test window;
- training adaptation does not eliminate the risk.

### Operational
- performance collapses when pilot condition changes;
- emergency disconnect or degraded sensor state produces dangerous control discontinuity.

### Logistics
- medical support/calibration burden makes field deployment impractical.

### Institutional
- pressure to demonstrate performance creates unsafe test incentives.

## 1.5 Human cost requirement

Before canon promotion, define at least one:
- severe irreversible test injury;
- death;
- long-term neurological impairment;
- operator exclusion/medical dispute.

Exact person and count remain HOLD.

The human cost must create a later rule, not exist for tragedy decoration.

## 1.6 Historical inheritance

Allowed descendant lessons:
- cockpit restraint standards;
- pilot medical stop rules;
- sync-load monitoring;
- high-bandwidth certification ceilings;
- evidence that skill cannot substitute for present-body tolerance.

Forbidden:
- all modern pilot medicine descends from P-03;
- Black Ward is secretly “the P-03 program continued.”

Black Ward interaction, if ever canonized:
- P-03 can be **one old historical case** cited by later interface medicine;
- Black Ward has many later independent research, corporate and military causes.

## 1.7 Story use

Possible reveal windows:
- GA1: no number needed; present-body/future-skill mismatch already dramatizes the principle.
- GA8: P-03 can appear as one old injury/safety record when current institutions argue about interface-risk classification.

Reader payoff:
> one of the famous numbers mattered because people learned where a human body breaks.

## 1.8 Why not dominant

Because:
- later frames achieve better performance through distributed control, materials, safety and formation doctrine;
- exceptional pilots do not erase medical/logistics limits;
- sustainable force design beats one dangerous test envelope.

### Packet verdict
**STRONG O1 FRONT-STAGE HISTORY CANDIDATE.**

# 2. P-07 — AUXILIA Service / Intervention Lineage Candidate

Working author-side ID:
- `P-07`.

Outcome:
- **O3 — lineage success.**

Present provenance:
- author HOLD;
- if designation Option B later approved, rare R3 surviving/rebuilt lineage candidate through current 07.

## 2.1 Historical question

> Can one intervention frame safely connect to many contractor/regional service systems long enough to inspect, rescue, repair, isolate and transfer control without requiring one universal manufacturer stack?

Core hypotheses:
- mixed physical/data service interfaces;
- rescue/capture under unsafe infrastructure conditions;
- role-bounded emergency access.

## 2.2 Why it was funded

Expansion era:
- mixed yards and regional systems create rescue delay;
- infrastructure cannot wait for the exact vendor technician;
- damaged nodes/habitats need physical access plus state translation.

P-07 solves a service problem before it solves any combat problem.

## 2.3 What worked

- broad physical service handshakes;
- diagnostic translation across incompatible equipment;
- tool/coupler adaptability;
- manual/local fallback;
- rescue and intervention geometry;
- bounded role/service authority sufficient for emergency work.

## 2.4 What did not become a universal standard

Costs:
- heavy adapters/wiring;
- conversion loss;
- security exposure;
- certification complexity;
- ambiguous ownership/authority;
- lower peak combat specialization.

Therefore P-07 wins as a **lineage concept**, not as the one frame everyone should build forever.

## 2.5 Militarization

Later node-rebellion/security use can add:
- capture tools;
- shield/anchor equipment;
- training/combat components;
- stricter authentication.

Important:
- military use is downstream adaptation.

## 2.6 Historical inheritance

Primary:
- ROOT-A service/intervention lineage;
- open-service philosophy;
- broad diagnostic/service grammar.

Secondary influence:
- rescue institutions;
- Ardis/open work systems;
- bounded public interoperability.

Not inherited automatically:
- sovereign keys;
- combat supremacy;
- all modern frames.

## 2.7 Present-day 07 relation

If author later approves reconciliation Option B:

```text
historical Slot 07
→ AUXILIA-0 technical lineage/workstream
→ repeated rebuild/reclassification
→ AUX-07 registry
→ CTF-13/07 Academy asset
→ 07호
```

This remains a proposal, not current canon.

## 2.8 Story use

GA1:
- reader experiences effects without historical answer.

GA2–4:
- service/provenance/authority concepts acquire meaning.

GA8:
- exact historical classification may be reconstructed.

Reader payoff:
> 07 is important because its idea escaped the machine.

### Packet verdict
**CENTRAL O3 CANDIDATE / AUTHOR PROVENANCE HOLD.**

# 3. P-13 — Exceptional High-Performance Dead-End Article

Working author-side ID:
- `P-13`.

Outcome:
- **O4 — exceptional / non-scalable success.**

Present provenance pressure:
- R4/R1/R5 preferred;
- operational R3 survivor rejected by default.

## 3.1 Historical question

> If field sustainability, broad pilot compatibility and ordinary support cost are temporarily relaxed, how far can maneuver-frame response and sensor/control integration be pushed inside one bounded test mission?

This question is deliberately different from P-03.

P-03 asks whether aggressive coupling can be made operationally viable.
P-13 asks what a **narrow elite test envelope** can achieve even if it is not a viable force design.

## 3.2 What makes it spectacular

Possible combination:
- extremely low-latency sensor fusion;
- aggressive thrust/attitude response;
- deep operator calibration;
- high thermal burst;
- narrow highly prepared mission envelope.

Reader-facing myth potential:
- historical clips/data can look impossible compared with ordinary frames.

## 3.3 Why it is not O3

At least four costs required:

1. operator screening so narrow it cannot staff units;
2. long recovery/medical burden;
3. enormous calibration/service time;
4. thermal/parts life unacceptable for campaigns;
5. failure sensitivity to imperfect sensors/data;
6. cost approaching specialized craft rather than frame economics.

The article can win a test and lose the procurement argument.

## 3.4 Historical consequence

Useful inheritance:
- narrow sensor-fusion techniques;
- pilot screening lessons;
- emergency disconnect design;
- warning against confusing demonstration peak with campaign capability.

Modern specialist machines may outperform it overall while using safer descendants of a few ideas.

## 3.5 Myth versus reality

Myth:
> “P-13 was the perfect frame buried because institutions feared it.”

Better reality:
> P-13 did exactly what its extreme test asked—and proved why that test should not define a fleet.

No anti-technology moral:
- its engineers were not fools;
- its performance data remains valuable;
- some current pilots may admire it rationally.

## 3.6 Story use

Best use:
- GA8 archive/technical history;
- possible contested footage/test record;
- perhaps fragments/sealed remains if current claim demands it.

Poor use:
- final boss frame;
- Rian late upgrade;
- Ern secret destiny machine;
- Aurel body.

## 3.7 Collection payoff without operational hull

Reader reward can be:
- verified test footage;
- medical/test log;
- one sensor/control component;
- revelation that famous performance claim was real but context-stripped;
- a modern design choosing not to repeat it.

### Packet verdict
**STRONG O4 FALSE-ULTIMATE / HISTORY-REWARD CANDIDATE.**

# 4. P-17 — Cross-Institution Authority / Authentication Trial

Working author-side ID:
- `P-17`.

Outcome:
- **O2 — partial technical/institutional success.**

Present provenance:
- R1/R2 preferred.

## 4.1 Historical question

> After broad emergency service access proves useful and dangerous, can authority be made purpose-limited, expiring and auditable across several institutions without giving one root key permanent control?

This is **not** another P-07 service frame.

P-07:
- physical/service interoperability in the field.

P-17:
- authority semantics, authentication and audit across institutional boundaries.

## 4.2 Historical pressure

Potential causes:
- emergency keys reused outside intended context;
- military modification of civil service permissions;
- contractor/state dispute over who may invoke rescue access;
- post-incident liability where every actor claims another authorized the action.

No exact scandal/event is chosen yet.

## 4.3 What worked

Possible O2 inheritance:
- purpose-scoped token;
- explicit expiry;
- role/action separation;
- provenance log;
- local physical stop preservation;
- multi-party confirmation for high-risk transfer.

## 4.4 Why no O3 whole-frame lineage

Because the successful part is primarily:
- protocol;
- authority model;
- audit architecture.

A whole dedicated frame is not necessary for the idea to spread.

The test article may be abandoned, repurposed or physically unremarkable.

## 4.5 What failed/remained unsolved

- institutions still disagree about underlying title;
- emergency latency rises;
- offline/degraded networks weaken verification;
- audit does not prevent abuse in real time;
- people can be excluded when their authority source is unrecognized.

Therefore P-17 is not “perfect permissions.”

## 4.6 Modern echoes

Current series already dramatizes analogous problems through:
- Silex one-use/legacy service access;
- GA4 expiring service/evidence modules;
- GA8 archive/AI custody;
- GA9 central/local authority.

Important:
- do not canonize those modern systems as direct P-17 copies without later review.

P-17 can be one historical ancestor/problem case among many.

## 4.7 Story use

GA4:
- no exact number needed; reader understands bounded authority.

GA8:
- historical P-17 record can prove that an old emergency license was not sovereign ownership, if a current claim depends on it.

Reader payoff:
> one important prototype “succeeded” by leaving a rule, not a machine.

### Packet verdict
**STRONG O2 INSTITUTIONAL-TECH CANDIDATE.**

# 5. P-20 — Scalable Production / Bounded Common-Frame Lineage

Working author-side ID:
- `P-20`.

Outcome:
- **O3 — lineage success.**

Present provenance:
- R2 descendant-only strongly preferred.

## 5.1 Historical question

> Can lessons from an unruly experimental era be turned into a frame/family that ordinary yards can build, certify, train, repair and deploy repeatedly without pretending to fit every mission?

Success metric:
- reproducibility, not peak performance.

## 5.2 Inputs it may absorb

Without becoming a master ancestor, P-20 can integrate lessons from:
- P-01 universal-load failure;
- P-08 modularity/certification failure;
- P-09 cradle/interface commonality;
- P-19 manufacturing/tooling success;
- safety boundaries from P-03/P-11/P-15.

No one input owns it.

## 5.3 What worked

Possible lineage success:
- common structural subassemblies;
- toleranced production fixtures;
- stable cradle family;
- bounded mission configurations;
- predictable training/maintenance;
- parts interchange inside declared envelope;
- repeatable inspection criteria.

## 5.4 What it gives up

- universal contractor compatibility;
- every-environment fit;
- extreme P-13 performance;
- ad hoc emergency access;
- unlimited field modification.

That sacrifice is why it can scale.

## 5.5 Historical importance

P-20 can become one contributor to later Era-2 mass-production logic.

Do not make:
- every Imperial frame a direct P-20 copy;
- P-20 the Empire’s founding machine;
- modern engineering unable to improve it.

Later standards can heavily modify or replace its architecture.

## 5.6 Present-day reward

The strongest payoff is **not finding P-20’s hull**.

Better rewards:
- discovering that several rival modern lineages share one production ancestor;
- proving a claimed proprietary feature began in jointly funded public tooling;
- finding old tolerance/test records that explain a modern incompatibility;
- showing that mass manufacture was itself an experimental achievement.

## 5.7 GA use

GA3:
- modern open-standard mass derivative gives thematic mirror: success means repeatable production + governance.

GA4–5:
- factions fight over production/standards without knowing all common ancestry.

GA8:
- P-20 can become evidence that rival modern lineages are less historically pure than their propaganda claims.

Reader payoff:
> the prototype that changed the most machines may be the least glamorous surviving object.

### Packet verdict
**STRONG O3 INDUSTRIAL-LINEAGE CANDIDATE / ORIGINAL HULL NOT NEEDED.**
