## 0. Question

The numbered `01–17/20` experimental slots now have a plausible engineering structure.

The missing historical question is:

> **Why would an early lattice civilization fund a broad, expensive and failure-prone family of demonstrators in the first place?**

A weak answer such as “the military wanted better mecha” would conflict with existing technology history, where early maneuver-frame value comes from:
- relay expansion;
- rescue;
- node inspection;
- mixed infrastructure;
- distant repair;
- broad emergency authority.

This document compares four historical origin models before any exact date, sponsor or prototype number is assigned.

---

# 1. Existing canon constraints

The following current Working Canon/accepted design facts constrain the origin model.

### Era 1

Early Relay and Rescue-Service Age already establishes:
- lattice relays and orbital settlements expanded faster than unified standards;
- distant operators had to repair mixed local hardware;
- emergency service buses linked ships, habitats, tugs and utility frames;
- broad physical/role-based service authorities existed;
- rescue and infrastructure functions shaped early maneuver-frame design;
- AUXILIA-family service architecture belongs to this era.

### 07 / AUXILIA

Existing first-frame history says:
- AUXILIA purpose included early Imperial lattice inspection, rescue, exterior repair and control-key transport;
- it had to interact with many private yards and regional standards;
- it was not originally a combat frame;
- later node rebellions/rescue operations produced military modifications;
- broad open interfaces later became a security/monopoly problem.

Therefore the new umbrella program may **contain or coordinate an AUXILIA workstream**, but must not silently redefine all numbered experiments as AUXILIA units.

### Later technology

Era 2 Imperial Standardization must remain a later reaction/development:
- more structured command/authentication;
- mass production;
- service corps;
- narrower/hidden emergency buses;
- stronger central standards.

The prototype era therefore should help explain **why Era 2 wanted standardization**, not already be a fully standardized imperial weapons program.

# 2. Historical-origin directions

## H-A — Relay Expansion Crisis

### Core idea

Lattice routes, relays, orbital habitats and extraction settlements expand faster than local industry can converge on one hardware standard.

Repeated incidents show that:
- rescue tugs cannot connect to damaged habitats;
- work frames cannot use each other’s tools;
- different yards use incompatible cradles, cooling and service buses;
- emergency teams lose hours fabricating adapters;
- remote nodes wait too long for specialist parts/crews.

The state and large infrastructure operators fund demonstrators to test **cross-environment service and intervention**.

### Strengths

- strongest fit with Era 1 canon;
- naturally explains AUXILIA/07;
- puts civilians/workers/maintenance upstream of military use;
- makes failures materially expensive without requiring a giant war.

### Weakness

By itself it does not justify every H01–H20 line:
- high acceleration;
- EW;
- bounded AI;
- formation command;
- breach/capture.

### Verdict

**STRONG BASE LAYER, insufficient alone.**

---

## H-B — Interoperability / Contractor Fragmentation Crisis

### Core idea

Expansion creates not merely technical variety but a political-economic crisis.

Major yards, regional governments and service firms each push incompatible stacks:
- physical mounts;
- diagnostics;
- authentication;
- cradle standards;
- repair licensing;
- operator certifications.

No actor can impose a universal standard yet.

The umbrella demonstrator program is created as a **competitive test framework**:
- several contractors get numbered experimental authorizations;
- slots test overlapping problems;
- one contractor can fail while another succeeds;
- successful subsystems can be licensed/transferred/reimplemented;
- records become messy because legal ownership and technical authorship differ.

### Strengths

- best explanation for distributed builders under one broad program;
- naturally creates O0/O1/O2 abundance;
- makes later Helix/Imperial/open-standard conflicts historical rather than newly invented;
- explains why one modern lineage can inherit several old subsystem streams.

### Weakness

If too bureaucratic, the history feels like procurement paperwork rather than a dramatic era.

### Verdict

**STRONG STRUCTURAL LAYER.**

---

## H-C — War / Node-Rebellion Emergency Program

### Core idea

A major node rebellion, route conflict or early lattice-security war exposes that utility/rescue frames cannot safely perform:
- contested rescue;
- capture instead of destruction;
- breach under fire;
- distributed formation operation;
- EW;
- damaged-network command.

Existing civil/service experiments receive emergency military money and new slots.

### Strengths

- explains H03/H10/H11/H12/H13/H14/H15/H16;
- creates accidents, casualties, classification and later myths;
- explains why later military histories misremember service demonstrators as secret combat prototypes.

### Weakness

If made the sole origin:
- contradicts 07’s service-first ancestry;
- turns all 01–20 into weapons;
- encourages “legendary wartime prototype” collection logic.

### Verdict

**USE AS ACCELERATOR, NOT ORIGIN.**

---

## H-D — Post-Crisis Reconstruction / Anti-Collapse Program

### Core idea

After a large regional conflict or infrastructure collapse, authorities discover that high-performance systems fail when:
- central authentication is unavailable;
- supply routes break;
- contractors disappear;
- repair crews lack proprietary tools;
- mixed surviving hardware must be recombined.

A reconstruction program emphasizes:
- fallback;
- field repair;
- low-logistics operation;
- multi-cradle support;
- bounded authority;
- emergency interoperability.

### Strengths

- strongly explains H06/H08/H09/H17/H18/H20;
- connects failure history to later frontier/Ardis doctrine;
- makes resilience a response to actual institutional collapse.

### Weakness

If it happens too late, it can collide with Era 2 standardization sequence.
If too large, it risks becoming a second hidden founding event for the whole Empire.

### Verdict

**STRONG LATE-PHASE LAYER, chronology-sensitive.**

# 3. Recommended hybrid — H-E Layered Crisis Program

## Core model

> **Expansion created the problem. Contractor fragmentation made it systemic. Rebellion/war militarized it. Reconstruction selected what survived.**

This is the strongest fit with existing canon.

### Phase E1 — Expansion pressure

Historical condition:
- relay/habitat growth outruns standards;
- mixed civilian infrastructure produces recurring rescue/service failures.

Program behavior:
- small demonstrators/test rigs begin around H01/H02/H04/H05/H07/H08/H09/H10.

Not yet:
- one giant military project;
- twenty completed frames;
- universal Imperial ownership.

### Phase E2 — Competitive umbrella / numbered authorization

Problem:
- multiple contractors and regions cannot agree on one stack.

Response:
- an umbrella authority creates a bounded numbered experimental-slot system;
- the slot number identifies authorization/test lineage more reliably than manufacturer;
- several institutions can build radically different articles under the same broad envelope.

Important:
- **this is the best point for `01–17/20` numbering to arise.**

The exact authority, name, count and date remain HOLD.

### Phase E3 — Node rebellion / security escalation

Existing service experiments are suddenly evaluated under contested conditions.

New pressures:
- rescue under fire;
- infrastructure-safe breach;
- formation command;
- EW;
- pilot/interface acceleration;
- bounded AI/autonomy;
- emergency authority abuse.

Consequences:
- some old civilian slots are militarily modified;
- additional slots may be authorized;
- accidents and casualties create O1 failures and later safety law;
- public records start to diverge from classified military records.

07/AUXILIA relation:
- 07-family service workstream remains service/intervention first;
- military use is an adaptation, not its original reason to exist.

### Phase E4 — Reconstruction and selection

After conflict:
- not every experimental architecture survives procurement;
- useful subsystems migrate into civilian/public/military production;
- some whole-frame successes become root lineages;
- many failures become safety standards or prohibited practices;
- contractors fail/merge/lose rights;
- records are renamed/reclassified.

This is where O2 partial success can become historically more important than an intact prototype hull.

### Phase E5 — Era 2 standardization

The Empire later responds to the messy demonstrator era by standardizing:
- cradles;
- certification;
- command;
- authentication;
- supply and training.

Irony:
- standardization solves many real dangers;
- it also narrows the broad emergency interoperability that 07 preserves.

This creates the later story conflict without declaring either era simply correct.

# 4. Why the hybrid is stronger than a secret-superweapon program

## It explains failed slots

A fragmented competitive program naturally produces:
- duplicated experiments;
- abandoned designs;
- contractor bankruptcies;
- incompatible subsystems;
- political cancellation;
- useful failures.

## It explains cross-inheritance

Because the umbrella exists partly to compare/transfer solutions:
- one slot’s cooling can survive in another lineage;
- one failed autonomy trial can create law used everywhere;
- one service bus can spread without the original hull.

## It explains why exact records are messy

Mess comes from several causes:
- multiple sponsors;
- contractor renaming;
- war classification;
- safety investigations;
- later standardization;
- legal transfer;
- propaganda;
- ordinary archival loss.

No universal conspiracy required.

## It explains 07’s misremembered status

Later observers see:
- a numbered demonstrator;
- emergency authority keys;
- wartime modifications;
- mixed military parts;
- partial classified records.

They can wrongly infer:
> secret royal combat prototype / one of a superweapon set.

Actual historical role can remain:
> one unusually influential service/intervention demonstrator whose interfaces outlived its intended era.

```text
    ├─ cradle/logistics/field-repair workstreams
    ├─ security/breach/formation workstreams

```

A numbered slot can have:
- lead contractor;
- public sponsor;
- military evaluator;
- regional test site;
- secondary supplier;
- later rights claimant.

Therefore:
- program number != manufacturer code;
- program number != modern model family;
- program number != guaranteed completed hull.

# 7. Prototype outcome pressure by historical phase

This is pattern only, not exact slot assignment.

### Expansion phase tends to produce
- O0/O1 from overambitious environment compatibility;
- O2 service/cooling/tool successes;
- one or more O3 utility roots.

### War escalation tends to produce
- O1 dangerous acceleration/EW/AI trials;
- O2 sensor/control/breach subsystems;
- rare O4 high-performance non-scalable article.

### Reconstruction tends to promote
- manual fallback;
- field repair;
- low logistics;
- cradle commonality;
- bounded emergency authority.

### Standardization later selects
- fewer production roots than experimental slots;
- safety/repeatability over novelty;
- centralized certification over broad ad hoc access.

This supports the existing 3–5 O3-root recommendation.

# 9. Multi-species interaction boundary

If the multi-species setting is later canonized, species/body/environment diversity can be **one additional pressure** on Era-1 interoperability.

It cannot become the hidden reason the whole program existed.

Safe contribution:
- different body/restraint/service needs make universal equipment harder;
- some regions require different cockpit or environmental modules;
- rescue systems need broader accommodation.

Forbidden retcon:
> the 01–20 program was secretly created to build alien-compatible superframes.

This keeps current program history valid even if exact species canon changes.

# 11. Red-team attack

### R1 — Too neat a hybrid

Risk:
- “everything caused everything” becomes vague.

Control:
- when exact history is designed, each phase needs one dominant material problem and one institutional actor class.

### R2 — Imperial state exists too early in mature form

Risk:
- umbrella looks like later Era-2 procurement bureaucracy.

Control:
- early authority should coordinate grants/tests without already owning a universal production/identity stack.

### R3 — War swallows civilian origin

Risk:
- military records become the only remembered history.

Control:
- preserve civilian accident/rescue/workforce evidence and descendant public-service institutions.

### R4 — AUXILIA terminology overwritten

Risk:
- first-frame bible becomes retroactively false.

Control:
- keep AUXILIA as its own program/workstream/family; exact relationship explicitly HOLD.

### R5 — One historical crisis explains all 20 slots

Risk:
- program again looks centrally authored.

Control:
- slots may be authorized in waves under changing priorities; exact ceiling can grow over time.

### R6 — Prototype age becomes magic

Control:
- later equipment normally superior in supported performance, reliability, safety and scale; prototypes matter for architecture, access, edge conditions and history.

### R7 — Rebellion chronology silently invented

Control:
- existing first-frame bible supports node rebellions as later military-use context, but exact event/date/scale remains HOLD until chronology audit.
