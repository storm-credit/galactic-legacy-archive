## 1. Core Problem

After the Imperial Continuity Compact, a warship can simultaneously have:

- a captain appointed by one authority;
- officers promoted under another authority;
- crew oath to crown, constitution, province, fleet or ship;
- salaries paid by Treasury/regency or provincial government;
- fuel/parts from Helix or local yards;
- missiles issued by General Staff stores;
- node/route keys controlled by Continuity or local custodians;
- insurance and rescue conditions under Neutral agreements;
- mission orders from Protector, Regent, provincial compact or local defense;
- legal title belonging to the Empire, a province, a charter company or seized trust.

Therefore “who owns the fleet?” has no single answer.

GA5 tests whether ships can coordinate through shared intent, reciprocal obligations and bounded command without becoming P-001’s private navy.

## 2. Fleet Alignment Layers

### Layer A — Hull Title

Defines:
- nominal owner and disposal/major modification rights.

Common titles:
- Imperial central registry.
- provincial/naval district.
- charter/lease trust.
- Helix-financed title/lien.
- captured/disputed prize.
- local node-defense ownership.

Title does not automatically command crew.

### Layer B — Captain and Officer Commission

Defines:
- legal appointment, discipline and command succession.

Conflict:
- commissions signed by Regent, Protector, Heir/crown office, province or previous General Staff.

### Layer C — Crew Oath and Contract

Possible oaths:
- sovereign/crown.
- Empire/state and law.
- ship and crew.
- province/node/city.
- commander/fleet.
- contract/employer.

Crews are not ideologically uniform; officers, enlisted, technical workers and embarked units may differ.

### Layer D — Pay, Pension and Family Support

Controls:
- whether crew can remain deployed without abandoning families.
- payroll authority can win loyalty without legal command.

### Layer E — Maintenance, Fuel and Ammunition

Controls:
- practical readiness.
- shipyards and suppliers can immobilize nominally loyal fleets.

### Layer F — Authentication and Route Access

Controls:
- command-message trust, node priority, weapon keys and fleet-network access.

### Layer G — Mission Legitimacy

Controls:
- why the ship fights and who is protected.
- crews may obey defensive mission but reject claimant seizure or civilian attack.

### Layer H — Rescue, Surrender and Legal Protection

Controls:
- whether defeated/deserting ships and crews can survive politically/physically.
- Neutral/compact rules can reduce last-stand incentives.

Rule:
- a fleet alignment is a vector across all eight layers, not a faction label.

Size:

- many armed tugs/yard craft/support.

Alignment:

Strength:

Weakness:

### FLT-06 — Outer Meridian / Ardis Route Coalition

Size:
- first ship NR72-118.
- 8–15 local/provincial/Neutral/Independence/Ardis combat-capable ships.

Alignment:
- Ardis Joint Trust, provincial/Neutral agreements and compact mission terms.

Strength:

P-001’s starting influence is strongest here but not ownership.

### FLT-07 — Unaligned Frontier/Theater Fleets

Aggregate:
- several formations totaling 80–160 combat ships across distant fronts.

Alignment:
- awaiting clarity, local commanders, provincial governments or self-preservation.

Function:
- decisive potential recruits/rejecters for GA5 and later Orpheus.

Constraint:
- cannot all concentrate quickly; communication and supply differ.

## 4. Fleet Readiness Model

Readiness components:

1. propulsion/thermal.
2. weapons/ammunition.
3. sensors/comms/authentication.
4. crew and fatigue.
5. frame/craft complement.
6. support/repair.
7. route access.
8. command legitimacy/cohesion.

Readiness state:
- `R0 Grounded/Noncombat`.
- `R1 Transit/Local Defense`.
- `R2 Limited Combat`.
- `R3 Sustained Task-Group Combat`.
- `R4 Theater Operation`.

A ship can be physically R3 but politically/authentication R1.

GA5 rule:
- no formation’s readiness remains static after more than one major operation.

## 5. Common Fleet Conflict Cases

### Case 1 — Paid by one, ordered by another

- Regent pays salary; Protector issues order; province supplies families.

Choice:
- obey tactical order, reject political objective or demand compact authorization.

### Case 2 — Captain and crew oaths diverge

- captain’s commission from Crown; crew oath to state/province/ship.

Risk:
- mutiny, resignation, negotiated mission limits or split command.

### Case 3 — Helix maintenance lock

- hull legally loyal but cannot deploy without certified parts/software.

Choice:
- accept contract/control, use open repair or cannibalize another ship.

### Case 4 — Continuity authentication suspension

- valid order cannot pass key checks due conflicting chain.

Choice:
- local verification/physical courier, accept delay or use emergency override.

### Case 5 — Provincial withdrawal

- province recalls ships to protect home after contributing to coalition.

Choice:
- honor recall, remain under fleet pact or divide crew/hulls.

### Case 6 — Prize/captured ship

- crew survived under one side, title claimed by another, systems from a third.

Choice:
- integrate, return, neutralize or create trust ownership.

### Case 7 — Rescue and surrender

- crew will fight to destruction if surrender means purge/identity loss.

Solution:
- reciprocal rescue, parole, wage/pension and legal status agreements can reduce total war.

## 6. Ownerless Fleet Constitution — Design Target

Working name:
- `공동전단헌장 / Common Fleet Charter`.

Not a permanent navy constitution.
It is a mission/theater framework that lets ships coordinate while retaining different titles and political loyalties.

Minimum clauses:

1. **Shared Mission Intent** — specific protected routes/populations/objectives.
2. **Command Scope** — task/operation authority, duration and geography.
3. **Captain Safety and Surrender Authority** — bounded but real.
4. **Crew Oath and Refusal** — lawful refusal categories and process.
5. **Pay/Pension/Family Guarantee** — multiple contributors and fallback.
6. **Maintenance/Ammunition Pool** — allocation, labor credit and priority.
7. **Authentication** — multi-signature/physical courier and emergency rules.
8. **Rescue/Parole** — treatment of deserters, surrendered crews and captured ships.
9. **Prize/Salvage/Title** — no immediate personal/faction ownership.
10. **Audit/Casualty/Order Record** — after-action evidence and privacy.
11. **Exit/Recall** — when provinces/ships can leave and what obligations remain.
12. **Succession of Command** — if commander/captain/authority disappears.
13. **Civilian/Node/Infrastructure Limits** — prohibited targets and local consent.
14. **Sunset and Review** — charter ends or renews under conditions.

Core principle:

> Command is lent for a mission, not transferred as ownership of ships or people.

## 7. Command Model

### Strategic Assembly / Mandate Board

Participants:
- Compact military representative.
- provincial/fleet representatives.
- route/node/civilian mission stakeholders.
- logistics/maintenance/medical/audit voices.

Function:
- defines mission intent, resources and limits.

Cannot:
- micromanage battle.

### Theater/Task Commander

Function:
- allocates formations and sets operational intent.

Authority:
- only over chartered mission scope and accepted ships.

### Formation Commanders

- retain tactical control and can adapt.

### Ship Captains

- retain ship safety, surrender/abandon and internal discipline within charter.

### Technical/Medical/Logistics Stops

- bounded stop authority for defined catastrophic conditions.

### Refusal/Appeal

- immediate tactical refusal only under defined prohibited order or impossible condition.
- later audit for disputed cases.

Weakness:
- slower, vulnerable to withdrawal and ambiguity.

Strength:
- ships can cooperate without personal ownership and reduce catastrophic obedience.

## 8. P-001’s Position

Possible role at GA5 start:
- Compact-recognized mission/flight commander and mediator, not fleet admiral.

Progression:
- wins task-group authority through mission performance and negotiated charter.
- may become temporary theater commander for defined operation.
- cannot hold hull title, payroll, authentication and appointment power together.

Core temptation:
- accept Protector/Regent/Custodian-backed unified admiralty to prevent fragmentation.

Required loss:
- one formation/ship refuses or leaves because P-001’s mission authority is bounded.

Required success:
- another commander autonomously wins a front under shared intent.

GA5 end:
- P-001 may be recognized as compact fleet commander for a limited theater, but fleet remains institutionally ownerless/plural.

## 10. Shipyard and Logistics Network

Critical yards:
- Helios Core Yards.
- Brann/Ardis regional yards.
- provincial naval bases.
- Helix contract facilities.
- mobile repair tenders.

Fleet sustainability requires:
- distributed parts compatibility.
- worker/pay/legal guarantees.
- ammunition and propellant allocation.
- casualty replacement and rest.
- node access.

GA5 strategic collection targets:
- support ships, tenders, depots, route agreements and crews—not only combat hulls.

Rule:
- the fleet that controls more battleships but loses repair/fuel/crew trust can lose the campaign.

## 11. GA5 Force Growth Envelope

P-001-linked coalition starting combat hulls:
- 8–15.

Mid-GA5 accepted charter formation:
- 25–45 combat ships + 20–40 support.

End-GA5 independent/ownerless fleet network:
- 55–90 combat ships + 50–100 support/transport/rescue across several formations.

Not all travel together.

P-001 direct tactical control:
- normally one task group of 8–20 combat ships.
- larger theater operations require subordinate formation commanders.

No instant command of hundreds of ships.

---

## 12. Victory and Failure Conditions

Fleet charter succeeds if:
- enough ships coordinate to protect a strategic route/population.
- crews are paid/rescued/repaired under reproducible rules.
- another commander can act without P-001.
- ships can exit/refuse without immediate massacre or total collapse.

Fails if:
- one authority captures title/pay/keys/command.
- every crisis requires P-001 personal decision.
- withdrawal is costless and common mission impossible.
- audits/rescue/pay are ignored.
- fleet becomes a personal hero roster.
