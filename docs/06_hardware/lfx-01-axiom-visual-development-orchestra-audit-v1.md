## 4. Why A and B Feel Dated or Too Familiar

| Feature | Current problem | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| upward paired blades | reads as a substitute V crest | remove; use flush rearward structure or negative space |
| five-part crown mass | expresses flagship status through ornament count | one low shell with one landmark |
| angry separated cyan eyes | familiar humanized hero face | one deep optical trench with restrained apertures |
| white nose, mouth vent and red chin | reconstructs a masked human face | short shear-cut lower mask without lips or red chin |
| pointed cheek fangs | creates samurai/animal aggression | one continuous cheek-to-jaw shear plane |
| dense trapezoid panels | every area claims equal attention | large shell / service panel / micro detail hierarchy |
| red-white mosaic | reads as painted model-kit parts | concentrate red in a few structural inlays |
| uniform semigloss | makes ceramic, frame and metal look like plastic | separate roughness and edge response by material |
| distributed cyan lights | removes sensor hierarchy | cyan at optics and one sword status channel only |

The failure is not that the image is a humanoid robot. The failure is that the head's **brow, crest, eyes, cheeks, mouth and chin all use the same familiar answer at once**.

## 5. Selected Visual Development Rules

These rules are locked only for the next noncanon image comparison. They are not hardware canon.

### 5.1 A/B face preservation

- Preserve the v1 A/B-family separated cyan dual eyes, low wide brow and readable heroic face.
- Do not replace it with the v2 alien/industrial pod or the v3 closed tactical helmet.
- Keep the forehead blades short and integrated into the shell; no long central spear, unicorn horn or demon-like pair.
- Originality changes, if pursued later, must be shown as a new side-by-side comparison and cannot silently replace this selected baseline.

### 5.2 Head and neck proportions

Let `H` be crown-shell to chin height and `W` the head width including temple armor.

| Measurement | Target |
|---|---:|
| head width / head height | `1.06–1.12` |
| optic trench width | `0.56–0.62W` |
| optic aperture height | `0.05–0.08H` |
| upward optic angle | maximum `3–6°` |
| chin projection | maximum `0.08H` |
| visible neck height | `0.22–0.28H` |
| neck exterior width | `0.48–0.52W` |
| front collar opening | `0.62–0.68W` |
| neutral rear head/collar gap | minimum `0.10H` |

The neck uses one dark inner rotation joint and two broad armor collars. Repeated rings, vertebrae, exposed cable stacks and a thin human neck are prohibited. The v14 sheet is the current proportion reference, not final hardware canon.

### 5.3 Surface and CMF

| Layer | Whole body target | Head target | Treatment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| ceramic white | `48–58%` | `48–60%` | large satin shells |
| deep graphite | `28–36%` | `28–36%` | load frame and optical surround |
| brushed titanium | `6–11%` | `8–11%` | real contact and shear surfaces |
| deep crimson | `7–12%` | `4–8%` | non-emissive recessed structure only |
| cyan emissive | `≤1.5%` | `≤1%` | optics and one short status channel |

- Detail area rhythm: large shells 65%, service panels 27%, micro-detail maximum 8%.
- Reduce current head panel count by about 50% and body panel count by at least 40%.
- A major shell may have at most one seam crossing its hero-facing surface.
- No black wash on every seam, random rivets, decorative vents or uniform edge chipping.
- Armor remains 95% clean; wear appears only at actual contact and cutting surfaces.

## 8. War-Weapon Architecture

The setting's existing combat logic does not support choosing one universal answer between gun and sword.

| Layer | Primary answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| fleet and open-space war | missiles, lasers, kinetic guns, electronic warfare | detection, fire control, interception and ammunition decide approach battles |
| normal frame combat | coil carbine plus limited missiles/decoys | frames still need ranged suppression before contact |
| node, dock and habitat assault | contact cutter, capture tool, tether and selective close weapon | destroying the target destroys its strategic value and harms civilians |
| AXIOM identity | ranged weapon for ordinary fighting, physical sword for decisive contact | preserves military plausibility while retaining the author's sword preference |

Recommended noncanon loadout direction:

1. **Primary:** compact coil carbine or equivalent setting-compatible ranged weapon.
2. **Signature:** one physical two-hand sword/breaching blade with no assumed energy edge, transformation or magic return.
3. **Control:** decoy, sensor tag, point-defense or tether package selected by mission.
4. **Limited strike:** small missile/rocket pod only when authorization and collateral envelope allow it.

Narrative rule:

> Guns and missiles win approach control; the sword appears when AXIOM must end the fight without destroying the ship, node, archive or person being fought over.

This also preserves the protagonist's locked `Battlefield Editor` identity: he creates the shot for allies and does not take every final blow personally.
