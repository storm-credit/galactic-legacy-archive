## 0. Scope and source boundary

This document records the author’s 2026-08-19 direction and the PM orchestra’s brainstorming + blind-spot audit. It does **not** silently amend Working Canon.

Current author direction carried forward:

> `01~17/20` is not a list of seventeen or twenty modern production models. It is a numbered experimental/prototype program. Each numbered unit tests a different frame/system hypothesis. Not every unit succeeds. A successful or partially successful prototype may become the ancestor of multiple later production frames, while failed units may leave only a component, doctrine, accident record or no usable inheritance. `07호` is one important example, not the only prototype and not necessarily the strongest. Major characters’ signature machines must not all share one origin; true legacy machines, surviving prototypes, modern prototypes, production-derived customs, heirloom frames and rebuilt machines may coexist.

The exact ceiling (`17` versus `20`) is **not locked here**. For planning, `01–20 authorized slots` is used as a reversible envelope, not as a claim that twenty physical frames were completed.

### Canon facts that constrain this proposal

1. 07 is the only stable named protagonist-adjacent frame currently in effective canon.
2. 07’s accepted identity is service/interoperability first, not a hidden royal superweapon.
3. Era-1 technology already contains an AUXILIA-family rescue/service architecture and broad emergency interfaces.
4. Old technology is not automatically stronger; later systems usually win efficiency, safety, scale and automation.
5. GA3 explicitly requires the first mass-produced derivative frame rather than another personal superframe.
6. E1–20 should expose only 07 as the principal machine; deep world history may exist offstage but cannot flood the opening.
7. AXIOM is currently a separate user-preferred legacy-machine candidate and remains `PROPOSED — NONCANON`; pilot, acquisition, function, cost and loss are HOLD.

## 1. Problem being solved

The repository already has strong answers for:

- why maneuver frames exist;
- why 07 matters;
- how open service can spread into GA3 mass production;
- how factions operate frame formations;
- how many reader-facing machine names the series can carry.

It does **not** yet have one integrated answer for:

- where the major historical frame architectures came from;
- why different modern factions can share some deep mechanical ancestry but diverge in doctrine and service ecology;
- how prototypes fail, partially succeed and feed industry;
- how 07 relates to other experimental units without becoming one of a set of collectible superweapons;
- how true legacy machines such as AXIOM differ from human/modern prototype ancestry;
- why major characters can have distinct signature machines without every officer receiving a unique legendary chassis.

Without that layer, the current proposed manufacturing lineages and reader-named-model envelope risk reading as a catalog assembled later rather than a technology history produced by wars, failed projects, industrial standards and institutional choices.

## 3. Two-axis prototype classification

### 3.0 Red-team correction

The first pass incorrectly placed `lost / sealed / disputed` on the same axis as `failed / partial / lineage success`. That is structurally wrong: a successful prototype may later be destroyed or lost, while a failed prototype may survive intact in a museum or sealed hangar.

Therefore every prototype must carry **two independent classifications**:

1. `DEVELOPMENT OUTCOME` — what happened to the experiment;
2. `PRESENT-DAY PROVENANCE` — what remains and how trustworthy the evidence is.

This correction is required before any 01–20 slot assignment.

### 3.1 Development outcome axis

#### O0 — AUTHORIZED / NOT BUILT

- slot approved or design work opened;
- full frame never completed;
- funding, materials, contractor collapse, safety or politics stopped it.

Possible inheritance:
- requirements document;
- test rigs;
- rejected parts;
- later engineers learning from the attempt.

#### O1 — TEST FAILURE

- physical article existed and ran;
- primary hypothesis failed or total cost exceeded value;
- no direct production chassis descends from the whole architecture.

Possible inheritance:
- accident rule;
- safety standard;
- one subsystem;
- counter-doctrine.

#### O2 — PARTIAL TECHNICAL SUCCESS

- one or more important subsystems succeed;
- whole frame remains commercially/militarily nonviable;
- later lineages inherit selected technology.

This should be the **most common useful historical result**.

#### O3 — LINEAGE SUCCESS

- architecture proves reproducible enough to seed production families;
- descendants are redesigned for cost, safety, maintenance, doctrine and certification;
- the prototype itself is not the production model.

One O3 prototype may generate several descendant families, and one modern family may combine secondary inheritance from multiple prototypes.

#### O4 — EXCEPTIONAL / NON-SCALABLE SUCCESS

- performs its intended role extremely well;
- cannot support ordinary mass production because of material, pilot, maintenance, legal, thermal or industrial cost;
- may seed a limited-run elite line or remain a one-off.

Control:
- O4 is rare. It is not the default explanation for named characters’ machines.

### 3.2 Present-day provenance axis

#### R0 — RECORD ONLY

No physical article is known to survive. Evidence is plans, logs, budgets, testimony or descendant references.

#### R1 — FRAGMENTARY

Core, limb, test article, software image, tooling or partial wreckage survives.

#### R2 — DESCENDANT ONLY

No original article is available, but the lineage is strongly evidenced through later production hardware and process records.

#### R3 — SURVIVING HULL

A physical prototype or substantially continuous legal/mechanical hull survives.

Rule:
- survival does not imply combat readiness or superiority.

#### R4 — SEALED / ACCESS-CONTROLLED

Existence is plausible or proven, but custody/access prevents independent inspection.

#### R5 — DISPUTED

Multiple records disagree on whether the slot was built, which hull was authentic, or which later technology descended from it.

These provenance states can change during the story without changing the original development outcome.

## 4. Outcome-distribution scenarios — 20-slot planning envelope

These are planning tests only. They do not lock the final number of slots or physical prototypes.

| Scenario | O0 not built | O1 failed | O2 partial | O3 lineage | O4 non-scalable | Character |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A — Triumph-heavy | 1 | 3 | 5 | 8 | 3 | too many legendary ancestors; weak failure history |
| B — Engineering-realistic | 3 | 5 | 6 | 5 | 1 | strong industrial plausibility; failures and partial wins dominate |
| C — Mystery-heavy | 4 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | many holes/one-offs; risks relic mysticism |
| D — Collapse-era | 6 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 1 | strong institutional-collapse texture; may underproduce enough ancestry |

### Recommended working distribution: B

For a 20-slot envelope:

- 3 never completed;
- 5 full-article failures;
- 6 partial successes;
- 5 lineage-generating successes;
- 1 exceptional non-scalable success.

Why:

- only about one quarter become true root ancestors;
- most useful history comes from partial successes and failure lessons;
- there is room for a few famous missing numbers without turning the program into a relic hunt;
- 07 can be one of the O3 lineage successes without implying that all numbered machines were peers of equal importance.

If the author later selects 17 slots, preserve the **ratio and asymmetry**, not these exact counts.

## 6. Core lineage rule

Do not model ancestry as `one prototype → one modern model`.

Preferred graph:

```text
legacy / industrial / military inputs
            ↓
    experimental slot P-xx
            ↓
  successful subsystem bundle
            ↓
 production root / standard family
            ↓
 ┌──────────┼───────────┬──────────┐
 mass unit  support     command    specialist
            ↓
 regional / faction / licensed / rebuilt branches
            ↓
 individual hull histories and ace customs
```

Cross-inheritance is allowed:

```text
Prototype A propulsion  ─┐
Prototype B cooling      ├→ later production family
Prototype C service bus  ┘
```

Controls:

- every production family has one **primary structural parent** for reader clarity;
- secondary inherited systems may come from other prototypes;
- a new chassis still has to pass the existing independent-chassis gate;
- paint, weapon, software and reversible packs remain states, not new machines;
- descendant factions may deliberately reject part of an ancestor’s philosophy.

Example of the last rule:
- one lineage may inherit a physical frame but replace open service with strict certification;
- another may inherit its service bus but reject its pilot interface;
- ancestry does not dictate ideology.

## 7. 07 integration

### 7.1 What 07 should be under this architecture

07 is a numbered demonstrator whose decisive success is **not maximum combat power**.

Its successful hypothesis is the ability to keep mixed infrastructure, tools and machines operable across incompatible service environments.

The existing canon already supports:

- rescue/service ancestry;
- broad service interfaces;
- mixed contractor compatibility;
- later security/monopoly pressure;
- open-standard descendants;
- final public training/service legacy.

Therefore the prototype-program architecture should deepen 07 rather than replace it.

### 7.2 AUXILIA boundary

Do **not** automatically rename the entire 01–20 program `AUXILIA`.

Current canon uses AUXILIA as an Era-1 service architecture/family attached strongly to 07. The safer relationship is:

```text
[Foundational Demonstrator Program — name HOLD]
        ├─ slot ?? / other experiment lineage
        ├─ slot ?? / other experiment lineage
        ├─ slot 07 / AUXILIA-family demonstrator
        └─ ...
```

This preserves the meaning of AUXILIA while allowing the larger program to contain radically different experiments.

### 7.3 Existing designation conflict to resolve later

The repository currently contains two legacy presentations:

- `first-frame-bible-v1`: school designation `CTF-13/07`, hidden original lineage `AUXILIA-0`;
- `academy-and-07-opening-operational-state-v1`: formal registry `AUX-07 다목적 상호운용 훈련 프레임`.

The lineup proposal already flags a 07 designation conflict.

Recommended resolution candidate, **not canonized here**:

1. original demonstrator identity contains the historical `07` slot;
2. later owners repeatedly re-register the surviving frame as utility/training equipment;
3. the Academy’s `07` designation preserves or accidentally repeats the old number;
4. `AUXILIA` remains the service-architecture lineage, while the exact original model code is resolved once the larger program naming grammar is selected.

This turns a current metadata conflict into a possible historical clue rather than inventing a second unrelated “07.”

### 7.4 Why 07 survives when others do not

Strong candidate explanation:

- it was not valued as a decisive combat weapon;
- its robust service interfaces made it useful for low-status utility/training work;
- later owners stripped, mixed and re-registered it rather than preserving it as a museum piece;
- survival came from **being useful and underestimated**, not from being protected as a sacred relic.

This is thematically stronger than “secret royal prototype hidden in a school.”

### 7.5 M-003 mystery compatibility

The current payoff ledger’s `1/7` false royal-line reading does **not** need to become “one of 20 sibling machines.”

Recommended compatibility rule:

- `1/7` remains a false or partial interpretation tied to service authorities/keys;
- the numbered prototype program is a different historical layer;
- later evidence can show that the same `07` designation was repeatedly misread because registration, authority count and prototype number were compressed together.

This protects an existing locked mystery while adding deeper technology history.

## 8. AXIOM boundary and ancient/legacy inheritance

### 8.1 What is already safe

AXIOM is separate from 07 and currently outside effective canon. Its selected visual direction and working name do not establish origin, pilot, ability, acquisition, cost or GA placement.

### 8.2 Four possible relationships to the numbered prototype program

| Option | Relationship | Strength | Risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AX-A | AXIOM is the master template copied into all 01–20 | simple mythic origin | makes ancient tech the answer to everything and devalues modern inventors | REJECT |
| AX-B | AXIOM has no historical connection at all | clean separation | gives up some archaeology resonance but causes no structural damage | SAFE HOLD |
| AX-C | fragments/data/principles from one or more true legacy finds, potentially including AXIOM-related material, were only **one input among many** to the broad experimental era | links mysteries while preserving human invention | requires careful provenance so AXIOM does not become universal ancestor | **RECOMMENDED PROPOSAL** |
| AX-D | AXIOM is itself one of the numbered human prototypes | simple numbering | conflicts with current “separate legacy machine” direction | REJECT unless author reverses |

### 8.3 AXIOM isolation test

Stress test question:

> If AXIOM has **zero historical relation** to the numbered prototype program, does GA1–GA10 technology history and 07’s payoff still work?

Result: **PASS**.

- GA1 07 mystery works through AUXILIA/service ancestry alone;
- GA2 open-standard conflict works through surviving service records/components;
- GA3 mass derivative requires industrialization of 07 principles, not AXIOM;
- GA4–7 faction, fleet and command histories work through modern descendants;
- GA8 can reveal the prototype era without AXIOM;
- GA9–10 interoperability/authority climax is already rooted in service/continuity protocols.

Therefore:

> AXIOM is **optional resonance**, not a structural keystone of all mecha technology.

This is a hard anti-centralization control. Future AXIOM lore must not become necessary to explain why all modern frames exist.

### 8.4 Ancient-tech rule

True legacy technology must never equal universal superiority.

A legacy frame may have:

- an irreplaceable material;
- an interface nobody fully understands;
- exceptional local performance;

while losing badly in:

- replacement parts;
- ammunition;
- pilot training;
- fleet networking;
- certification;
- repair time;
- scalable production.

AXIOM can be a terrifying one-off without being a better civilization than the mass-production ecosystem created from successful modern prototypes.

## 9. Signature-machine origin taxonomy

Major characters should not all receive ancient/legendary machines.

Allowed origin classes:

1. **True Legacy Machine** — genuine old/unknown-origin artifact; extremely rare; support ecology largely gone.
2. **Surviving Numbered Prototype** — one of the program’s physical survivors; may be obsolete, partial or dangerous rather than simply stronger.
3. **Prototype-Descended Special** — later limited-run machine built from a successful historical lineage.
4. **Modern Prototype** — current Empire/Helix/local/AI-era experiment with no need for ancient origin.
5. **Mass-Production Ace Custom** — common chassis accumulated into a famous individual hull through pilot, maintenance, damage and field modifications.
6. **Institutional Heirloom** — guard/knightly/house/academy machine maintained across generations; history matters more than secret power.
7. **Frontier/Rebuilt Unique Hull** — donor frames and local engineering create one mechanically distinct legal hull without making it a lost superweapon.

PM rule:

> A signature machine exists because the character/institution’s history created it, not because named characters are entitled to a unique chassis.

Haren remains an important counterexample: his strongest “equipment identity” should stay route, logistics, ships and formations rather than an automatic ace frame.

## 11. GA1–GA10 reveal ladder

The program can exist deeply in world history without becoming front-loaded exposition.

### GA1 — 07 as one strange machine

Reader-facing:
- 07 registration/core mismatch;
- odd service grammar;
- false royal/superweapon model remains plausible and then weakens;
- no parade of sibling prototypes.

Offstage design:
- the larger experimental era may exist in design history, but is not named/explained on page.

### GA2 — ancestry traces, not a prototype hunt

Possible payloads:
- component provenance that does not match 07’s whole-frame history;
- another obsolete technical standard;
- false “prototype relic” markets;
- evidence that several later factions inherited different old experiments.

Rule:
- do not retrieve “Prototype 08” immediately after “Prototype 07.”

### GA3 — production payoff

This is the first major public answer:

> 07 was important because a technical philosophy could be made reproducible.

The first open-standard mass derivative proves that history can move from one damaged test frame into workers, factories, training and squadron-scale equipment.

### GA4 — political ownership of technical ancestry

Succession factions may claim:
- old design rights;
- contractor archives;
- guard/house descent;
- authentication authority.

This is a natural place to show that a prototype’s history can be a political claim without giving every claimant the original hull.

### GA5 — mixed lineages become fleet reality

The ownerless/common fleet should visibly combine mature descendants from multiple industrial histories. The reader recognizes older silhouettes and support burdens instead of receiving a new catalog for the fleet arc.

### GA6 — failed design philosophies return as costs

Mass evacuation/Orpheus provides an ideal place for:
- endurance failures;
- maintenance limits;
- command-network fragility;
- safety lessons inherited from old failed prototypes.

The past matters as engineering memory, not as a secret weapon drop.

### GA7 — military history is decompressed

Competing admirals, propaganda and title compression can reveal how different prototype descendants were assigned false heroic histories. This parallels Blood Admiral identity compression without making machines the sole mystery.

### GA8 — full archaeology/history payoff window

Only here should the series be free to resolve:
- why the experimental program existed;
- what outside/legacy inputs it used;
- which slots never existed physically;
- which technologies survived under other names;
- whether AXIOM has a real relation to any of those inputs.

### GA9 — recombination temptation

A central authority may be able to reconcile many incompatible lineages. The danger is not “build the ultimate frame” but using compatibility/authority to centralize the entire technological ecosystem.

### GA10 — descendants outlive originals

Final payoff:
- multiple regions keep different descendants and standards;
- no one inherits a permanent master chassis/standard;
- museums/archives can preserve prototypes, but living service and production remain plural.

## 13. Blind-spot / adversarial audit

### B-01 — Numbered relic scavenger hunt

**Failure:** 01→02→03 acquisition loop dominates the series.

**Control:** many slots never leave a recoverable complete hull; reveal through standards, descendants, archives, lawsuits, accidents, testimony and manufacturing traces.

### B-02 — Old-tech supremacy

**Failure:** every historical prototype outperforms modern mass equipment.

**Control:** later production must usually win reliability, efficiency, safety and support. Historical value comes from preserved functions or unusual tradeoffs.

### B-03 — Named-character entitlement

**Failure:** every important pilot receives a unique superframe.

**Control:** preserve the existing reuse-first/formation ecology. Mass-unit hull history and crew identity can be collectible without a new chassis.

### B-04 — AXIOM becomes god-source

**Failure:** all important technology secretly comes from AXIOM/ancients.

**Control:** any legacy input is one contributor among human industry, crisis-driven engineering, captured technology, local invention and failed experiments. AXIOM isolation test must continue to pass.

### B-05 — 07 loses its theme

**Failure:** another prototype takes public open service, distributed standard and education payoff.

**Control:** reserve 07’s distinctive long arc for service interoperability and its descendants. Other prototypes need different historical questions.

### B-06 — Designation contradiction

**Failure:** `CTF-13/07`, `AUX-07`, `AUXILIA-0` are treated as three machines or silently overwritten.

**Control:** create one provenance chain after the program naming decision; until then flag all exact original-designation work as HOLD.

### B-07 — Reader noun overload

**Failure:** program number + prototype name + root family + production model + variant + ace nickname are all spoken.

**Control:** separate author-side genealogy from reader-facing name. Most historical prototypes can remain numbers/archives until plot-relevant.

### B-08 — Prototype success without industry

**Failure:** one recovered design instantly enables mass production.

**Control:** retain collection-bible technology stages: theory → prototype → process → people → supply → doctrine → social spread.

### B-09 — Universal conspiracy

**Failure:** one villain erased every prototype record.

**Control:** fragment provenance through war loss, contractor failure, standard replacement, classification, legal renaming, propaganda and selective monopoly pressure. Deliberate erasure can exist locally but not explain everything.

### B-10 — Production descendants are clones

**Failure:** mass units are visually/weapons-wise just cheaper prototype copies.

**Control:** descendant design must show what was removed for cost/safety/maintenance and what each faction reinterpreted.

### B-11 — Ancient = alien = magical

**Failure:** “고대유산” becomes a free pass for unexplained abilities or a surprise alien origin.

**Control:** keep `ancient`, `pre-imperial`, `lost-human`, `nonhuman`, `unknown provenance` as separate evidence categories until explicitly proven.

### B-12 — Genealogy replaces faction identity

**Failure:** knowing prototype ancestry tells the reader everything about a modern faction.

**Control:** modern doctrine, certification, labor, law, training and supply must matter at least as much as structural ancestry.

### B-13 — Failed prototype as late ability warehouse

**Failure:** a previously “failed” machine produces exactly the needed hidden subsystem during a crisis.

**Control:** failure records must state what worked, what failed and why; any later reuse pays the known cost and is planted before need.

### B-14 — Rights vanish behind engineering

**Failure:** whoever finds an old prototype automatically owns the design/descendants.

**Control:** track physical custody, mission use, maintenance, data/design rights, certification, inheritance and public-interest claims separately.

### B-15 — Too much architecture too early

**Failure:** adding this history forces rewrites of E1–20 exposition.

**Control:** preserve opening events. The first task is backward-compatible worldbuilding; only later, if needed, propose small clue reinterpretations rather than new exposition scenes.

### B-16 — Success/provenance axis collapse

**Failure:** “lost” is treated as a development result, hiding whether the experiment actually worked.

**Control:** every slot always records both O0–O4 outcome and R0–R5 provenance separately.

### B-17 — One prototype per faction

**Failure:** each modern faction gets a neat ancient ancestor, turning history into color-coded houses.

**Control:** ancestry crosses borders; factions inherit, license, steal, reject and recombine technologies. Institution and doctrine create the modern identity.

### B-18 — Program becomes more important than the war/history around it

**Failure:** readers are asked to care about engineering numbers without human consequences.

**Control:** every prototype history must connect to at least two of: workers, pilots/patients, budgets, military doctrine, safety law, faction rights, later mass production, a named disaster or a later institutional conflict.
