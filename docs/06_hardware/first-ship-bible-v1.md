## 1. Identity

Official class:
- `NR-72 다목적 계전호위선 / Multi-Role Relay Escort`, early production block.

Hull registry:
- `NR72-118`.

Working reader-facing name:
- `회랑새 / Corridor Wren` — provisional crew nickname, not final canon name.

Previous role:
- frontier relay tender, customs/rescue escort, light convoy protection.

Current condition at GA2 start:
- 31 years old.
- laid up after reactor-cooling damage and insurance dispute.
- stripped of two major modules and most current weapons.
- nominally operable only under tow/yard restriction.

Core promise:

> 강한 전함을 얻는 것이 아니라, 서로 다른 권리와 부족한 인력으로 ‘움직이는 학교·공방·피난처’를 운영하는 첫 경험이다.

## 2. Dimensions and Performance Envelope

Length:
- 134 m.

Maximum beam:
- 31 m with radiators folded.

Dry mass:
- approximately 15,800 t.

Normal mission mass:
- 21,000–25,000 t.

Maximum emergency mass:
- approximately 29,000 t with external cargo/passenger modules; handling and node slots worsen sharply.

Crew:
- legal minimum transit crew: 42.
- sustainable mixed-mission crew: 62–76.
- original design crew: 88.
- opening available trained crew: 34–39, plus 8–16 partially trained cadets/contract workers.

Passengers:
- normal: 72.
- austere short mission: 110.
- emergency 72-hour evacuation: 180–220 with cargo/maintenance and hygiene collapse.

Frames:
- two internal cradles by design.
- opening: one cradle functional at 70–80%; second bay lacks full cooling/launch equipment.
- one external utility hardpoint for damaged frame/cargo, reducing acceleration/thermal options.

Conventional acceleration:
- efficient cruise: 0.035–0.08 g depending load.
- sustained tactical: 0.12 g for tens of minutes with radiator deployment and high propellant/heat cost.
- short tactical: 0.28 g for 90–180 seconds.
- emergency structural limit: 0.45 g for less than 20–30 seconds; risks old mounts, crew injury and cooling failure.

Rotation/artificial gravity:
- small 24 m-radius crew drum for approximately 0.35–0.45 g during cruise/berth mode.
- drum locked during high acceleration/combat.
- most workspaces use thrust, straps, handholds and magnetic footwear.

Endurance:
- 75–100 days for core crew under low-intensity patrol with full stores and functional recyclers.
- opening realistic independent endurance: 32–48 days because of recycler inefficiency, spares and crew shortage.
- high-intensity combat endurance: one serious engagement or 2–4 minor contacts before missile/countermeasure/repair depletion.

Node transition:
- compatible with standard L2/L3 routes.
- old phase-conditioning hardware increases queue inspection and recovery time.
- damaged Silex transition requires external calibration, reduced load and trained engineer.

## 3. Power, Propulsion and Heat

### Main power

- two medium fusion-electric reactor modules, originally redundant.
- Reactor A operational at 82–90% rated stable output.
- Reactor B partially operational as emergency/peak support; coolant manifold and control certification disputed.

Opening limit:
- running both at high output risks common cooling trunk failure, so “redundancy” is partly false.

### Propulsion

- four primary plasma/mass-driver engine clusters.
- twelve distributed attitude/vector thruster groups.
- two engine clusters have mismatched replacement control hardware.

Strength:
- fine docking and relay-tender maneuvering.
- can hold relative position around structures and damaged ships.

Weakness:
- poor high-acceleration pursuit.
- asymmetric response under some damage states.
- external cargo/frame worsens torque.

### Heat rejection

- two main folding radiator wings plus smaller distributed panels.
- one main wing has microfractures and patched fluid channels.
- opening safe heat reserve lower than records claim because Helix/insurer model assumes parts no longer installed.

Combat rule:
- weapons, sensors, frame support, life support and acceleration compete for heat budget.
- radiator deployment improves endurance but increases target silhouette and limits rapid maneuver.

### Thermal emergency options

1. dump hot working fluid — loses endurance/expensive consumable.
2. shut noncritical systems — affects passengers/workshop/medical.
3. store heat in sacrificial sink — limited one-use modules.
4. reduce acceleration/weapons/sensors.
5. vent or abandon hot module — physical loss.

No invisible heat reset between scenes.

## 4. Hull Layout

### Deck/Zone A — Forward Sensor and Docking Head

Functions:
- optical/active sensors, docking control, customs/rescue airlocks, small observation/inspection bay.

Weakness:
- old long-range fire-control hardware; strong close docking and material scan.

Story use:
- approach tension, inspection, E-001/Neutral docking, exterior repair.

### Zone B — Command Spine

Functions:
- bridge, CIC/mission room, secure comms, data vault, captain’s emergency station.

Layout:
- bridge handles navigation/ship safety.
- separate mission/CIC allows P-001 to command frame/operation without occupying captain’s chair.

Rule:
- P-001 cannot casually override helm/reactor/life-support command.

### Zone C — Crew Drum and Habitation

Functions:
- 42 standard bunks, 16 fold-out bunks, galley, hygiene, communal area, small quiet/medical rooms.

Opening problem:
- crew and cadets exceed comfortable capacity.
- some sleep in cargo/workshop modules on rotation.

Ordinary-life effects:
- no private officer luxury except tiny captain/master cabin.
- smells/noise/shift schedules and gravity changes create friction.

### Zone D — Medical/Rescue Module

Capacity:
- 4 stabilization beds, 8 monitored recovery berths, 20–30 short emergency casualties in cargo conversion.

Limits:
- no advanced surgery, long neural treatment or isolation hospital without modular support.
- M-001 can stabilize/transport, not replace Orsen/Neutral hospital.

### Zone E — Workshop and Frame Bay 1

Functions:
- one full frame cradle, machine tools, parts storage, external access, utility drones.

Opening state:
- limited heavy lift, incomplete coolant service and outdated diagnostic interfaces.
- H-002 can improve but needs Brann/Neris tools/crew.

### Zone F — Frame Bay 2 / Missing Module Space

Original:
- second frame cradle and relay-maintenance equipment.

Opening:
- cooling rails and launch collar removed.
- used as mixed cargo/sleep/work area.

Progression:
- cannot become fully functional without sacrificing cargo/habitation or acquiring major module.

### Zone G — Cargo and Mission Modules

- three internal standard module bays.
- opening installed:
  1. worn relay-repair module;
  2. general cargo/habitation conversion;
  3. empty/damaged service frame for leased mission package.

Collection/game function:
- module choice changes what missions are possible; ship cannot carry workshop, hospital, refugee habitat and heavy weapons simultaneously.

### Zone H — Engineering and Reactor Section

- separated reactor rooms but shared coolant/energy trunk.
- engineering control, recyclers, water/air, propellant feed.

Risk:
- common trunk damage and poor access around old modifications.

### Zone I — Aft Engines and External Truss

- primary drives, radiator roots, utility hardpoint, tug equipment.

Story use:
- high-risk EVA repair and towing/salvage.

## 5. Opening Weapon and Sensor Suite

The ship is an escort/tender, not a pocket cruiser.

### Point-defense lasers

- 8 mounts; opening 5 reliable, 2 intermittent, 1 stripped.
- effective against drones/debris/limited missiles within geometry.
- high heat use under saturation.

### Interceptor/decoy cells

- 24-cell distributed launcher system by design.
- opening load: 10–14 mixed interceptors/decoys due cost and compatibility.

### Multi-role missile tubes

- 4 small tubes.
- opening load: 6–10 missiles total, not full magazines.
- can carry sensor, rescue beacon, electronic or breaching payload rather than all anti-ship.

### Utility beam/cutter

- high-power industrial emitter for debris, towing and emergency hull work.
- combat use against exposed structures at close range; not long-range capital weapon.

### Kinetic/coil equipment

- one light utility coil projector or cargo launcher, low military lethality.
- can deploy tags, chaff, cable anchors, small kinetic interceptors.

### Electronic warfare / relay suite

Strength:
- close regional comms, convoy coordination, node-service protocols, rescue beacon authentication.

Weakness:
- obsolete encryption and limited high-end jamming.
- modifications create compatibility/security vulnerabilities.

Sensors:
- good close material/thermal/docking resolution.
- modest long-range passive suite.
- poor modern fire-control unless linked to picket/drone/07 tags.

Combat identity:
- survives, coordinates, screens and completes mission; does not win by main-gun duel.

## 6. Ownership and Legal Stack

Physical control is not ownership.

### Nominal hull title

- held by a provincial relay-defense authority or dissolved charter company under Imperial registry.

### Senior financial lien

- Helix/insurer claims reactor, cooling, parts and unpaid overhaul value.

### Academy lease

- Academy charter obtains a 180-day educational/mission lease in exchange for taking specified relay/escort contracts and repair debt.

### Neutral insurance/rescue covenant

- Neutral member insurer covers limited hull/passenger claims only if rescue, medical and audit conditions are followed.

### Crew wage/salvage claim

- old crew and new workers hold unpaid wage/salvage rights over proceeds and some removable modules.

### Imperial security restrictions

- node-service keys, secure comms and weapons require permits and inspections.

### P-001 team rights

- mission-use rights and assigned operating roles under charter.
- no title ownership.
- cannot sell, permanently modify, weaponize or flee without triggering claims and criminal status.

Working mission trust:

- title remains external.
- captain/master holds navigation and ship-safety command.
- academy mission board approves contracted mission scope.
- technical board/H-002/engineer sign off on modifications.
- Neutral observer/auditor monitors passenger/medical conditions.
- Helix retains inspection and lien but not remote unilateral command.
- P-001 commands designated field/tactical operations only.

Why all accept:
- nobody wants the distressed hull and debt alone.
- each expects leverage or data from successful operation.
- failure allows repossession/termination.

## 7. Command Authority Matrix

### Captain/Master

Final authority over:
- navigation, collision, reactor/propulsion safety, internal discipline, surrender/abandon ship.

Cannot alone:
- change mission contract, hand over protected passengers/evidence, use restricted legacy keys, permanently modify shared assets.

### P-001 — Mission/Flight Commander

Authority over:
- frame/boarding/rescue tactical plan within accepted mission.
- combat priorities approved by captain and mission mandate.

Cannot alone:
- drive ship, spend strategic reserves, override medical/engineering safety, accept political contract.

### H-001 — Logistics/Passenger and Route Planning

Authority over:
- manifest, ration/evacuation plan, convoy allocation under mission charter.

Cannot alone:
- compel passengers/communities to relocate or deny captain safety decisions.

### H-002 / Chief Engineering chain

Authority over:
- maintenance certification, modification, equipment release and technical stop.

H-002 limitation:
- not opening chief engineer; shares authority under licensed senior engineer until later qualification.

### M-001 / Medical officer chain

Authority over:
- treatment, quarantine, medical stop and casualty movement.

M-001 limitation:
- cadet/practitioner under licensed medical officer or Neutral authorization at opening.

### S-001 / Data-Comms audit

Authority over:
- authentication, logging, privacy tiers and release chain within charter.

Cannot alone:
- censor captain/mission communications without legal/operational basis.

Emergency central override:
- possible through old ship systems but requires physical action and creates legal/technical trace.
- its temptation mirrors later central command; use only with cost.

## 8. Opening Defect and Repair Ledger

### D1 — Common coolant trunk degradation

Effect:
- simultaneous reactor/weapon/frame support limited.

Fix path:
- Brann heavy replacement or Neris custom bypass.

Tradeoff:
- certified expensive durable fix vs open custom lower-cost/security risk.

### D2 — Reactor B certification and manifold damage

Effect:
- peak output unavailable/restricted.

Fix:
- Helix-certified parts or open reverse-engineered repair.

### D3 — Frame Bay 2 missing service collar

Effect:
- one active frame only; second carried cold/external.

Fix:
- recover ghost-parts ship module or sacrifice cargo/hab space.

### D4 — Crew drum bearing vibration

Effect:
- noise, wear, gravity limits; ordinary-life discomfort.

Fix:
- yard alignment, not combat salvage alone.

### D5 — Obsolete comms encryption

Effect:
- spoofing/inspection risk.

Fix:
- S-001 + Neutral/Imperial multi-signature overlay; no one key.

### D6 — Recycler efficiency 68–74%

Effect:
- water/air consumables reduce endurance and passenger capacity.

Fix:
- Vela biological unit or Helix membrane module.

### D7 — Stripped PD mount and missile scarcity

Effect:
- saturation vulnerability.

Fix:
- missions/trade/escort formation, not instant upgrade.

### D8 — Hull ownership software locks

Effect:
- some modules/doors/maintenance rights restricted or contested.

Fix:
- legal keys + physical rewiring + provenance; each method has consequences.

## 9. Mission Module Choices — GA2

Only three internal mission bays; one is often required for core life support/cargo.

Possible modules:

### Workshop Module

Benefits:
- frame/ship part fabrication and repair.

Costs:
- power, heat, crew, less passenger/cargo space.

### Medical/Refugee Module

Benefits:
- 12–24 monitored beds or 80–120 short-term passengers.

Costs:
- water/air, privacy, slower quarantine/inspection.

### Relay/Node Service Module

Benefits:
- calibration, service keys, Silex/Ardis work.

Costs:
- specialized crew, no heavy combat value, political sensitivity.

### Cargo/Salvage Module

Benefits:
- money/parts and towing support.

Costs:
- hazards, center-of-mass/heat/insurance.

### Missile/EW Module

Benefits:
- combat survival.

Costs:
- legal escalation, less humanitarian/repair capacity.

GA2 rule:
- the team must visibly leave useful capability behind when choosing modules.

## 10. Crew Life and Conflict

Opening habitability:
- crowded and under-repaired.
- rotating bunks, shared work/hab zones, limited fresh food, water discipline.
- rank/ownership conflicts over cabins and access.

Core social divisions:
- old skeleton crew vs Academy cadets.
- certified professionals vs H-002/open repair workers.
- Neutral/medical rules vs mission urgency.
- passengers/community vs combat/security crew.
- captain’s legal command vs P-001’s battlefield competence.

Ordinary-life scenes:
- crew drum vibration and sleep.
- repair noise, food module, laundry/water allocation.
- one communal space converted between meeting, meal, patient overflow and sleeping.
- personal items must fit a strict volume/weight allotment.

No instant found-family:
- contracts and competence create cooperation before affection.
- crew members may leave at Kael/Brann or after failed pay.

## 11. GA2 Progression Track

### Stage 0 — Distressed berth / E101

- cannot depart legally or safely.
- crew below minimum, one reactor/cooling chain, one frame bay partial.

### Stage 1 — Conditional tow and survey / E101–108

- tug/yard survey reveals hidden common-trunk problem.
- title/lien/crew wage claims conflict.

Reward:
- access and technical truth.

Cost:
- repair exposure exceeds initial estimate.

### Stage 2 — Minimum transit certification / E109–118

- restore Reactor A, recycler, comms, one drive pair and one frame cradle.
- acquire captain, chief engineer, licensed medical/route support.

Reward:
- K-13→Lumen/Kael transit right.

Cost:
- debt, route mission obligations, inspection and passenger limits.

### Stage 3 — First failed/partial mission / E119–130

- ship survives contract but cannot complete all rescue/cargo/combat objectives.
- crew command conflict exposed.

Reward:
- accepted as operational mission unit.

Cost:
- one module/component/claim worsens; reputation mixed.

### Stage 4 — Ghost-parts restoration / E131–160

- recover second-bay/service/relay components or choose alternatives.
- false/forged parts and old crew history.

Reward:
- second bay partial or relay module.

Cost:
- malware/claim/casualty/route damage.

### Stage 5 — Community convoy / E161–185

- passenger capacity and medical/cargo modules force choices.
- ship becomes home/political space.

Reward:
- community split destinations and route relationships.

Cost:
- overcrowding, repairs, trust/crew burnout, lost contract.

### Stage 6 — Open-standard conflict / E186–210

- ship itself becomes demonstration of interoperability and its risks.
- major engagement/relay crisis uses all accumulated systems.

Reward:
- sustainable but imperfect regional support network and GA3 access.

Cost:
- Helix sanctions, new vulnerabilities, public responsibility for failures.

- old multi-party command/technical architecture becomes prototype for distributed node/Archive transition.

Possible fate:
- survives through upgrades but not invulnerably;
- may be retired, destroyed with evacuation completed, or turned into public training/route ship.
- if destroyed, crew/institutional lineage persists.

## 13. Anti-Power-Creep Controls

- no hidden capital weapon.
- old hull cannot match modern frigate/destroyer in direct fight.
- every upgrade consumes module space, power, heat, crew or legal freedom.
- more passengers reduce acceleration/endurance/combat.
- second frame bay is a meaningful long-term choice, not automatic.
- crew and support skills are required; P-001 cannot solo the ship.
- new ship later does not erase this vessel’s route, training, workshop and legal value.

## 14. Battle-Writing Checklist

Every ship engagement tracks:
- ship mass/load and current acceleration envelope.
- radiator/heat reserve.
- reactor/coolant state.
- missile/interceptor count.
- crew readiness and current module configuration.
- frame launch/recovery time.
- mission objective and protected cargo/passengers.
- captain vs mission authority.
- nearest node/berth and travel/repair consequence.
