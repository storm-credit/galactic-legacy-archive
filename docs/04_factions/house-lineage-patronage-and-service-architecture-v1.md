## 0. Core distinction

`House`, `family`, `bloodline`, `legal lineage`, `political bloc`, `species`, and `corporation` are different things.

A house can survive through:
- biological descent;
- adoption;
- registered succession;
- marriage/partnership;
- office succession;
- apprenticeship/client incorporation;
- merger with another house;
- trust or foundation continuity.

Therefore a house is better defined as:

> a durable legal-social network that transmits some combination of name, assets, obligations, reputation, archives, offices and patronage across generations.

Blood may matter, but blood alone is never sufficient to explain power.

---

## 1. Why houses still exist in a high-technology empire

Houses survive where long-lived assets and obligations benefit from continuity:

- route concessions;
- habitat/land trusts;
- shipyard equity;
- node-service contracts;
- court/ceremonial offices;
- military endowments;
- archive custody;
- education and professional patronage;
- debt/insurance networks;
- local representation.

They do **not** survive simply because society forgot modern institutions.

Modern state, firms and houses overlap and compete.

## 2. Six functional house archetypes

These are role archetypes, not final named houses.

### HSE-A — Founding Covenant House

Historical base:
- descended legally/institutionally from one of the early signatories or recognized successor bodies of the Imperial founding compact.

Real power:
- old archives, ceremony, court standing, land/trust income, historical claims.

Weakness:
- legitimacy may be ancient but current productive capacity can be limited.

Military relation:
- may patronize guards or a service order;
- should not automatically field a private fleet.

GA4 use:
- can invoke old-law interpretations without being automatically correct.

### HSE-B — Military-Service House

Historical base:
- repeated service in fleet, border command, logistics or strategic defense.

Real power:
- officer networks, veteran foundations, academies, defense-region relationships.

Succession tension:
- professional merit versus inherited reputation.

Military relation:
- often uses standard state equipment;
- one institutional heirloom hull possible but not required.

Blind spot control:
- “good genes make better pilots” is prohibited as objective truth.

### HSE-C — Industrial / Yard House

Historical base:
- shipbuilding, frame manufacturing, materials, maintenance or logistics.

Real power:
- ownership stakes, patents, skilled-worker networks, creditor relationships.

Weakness:
- workers, regulators, banks and Helix certification can constrain it.

Military relation:
- influence equipment supply rather than personally command all units.

Story value:
- gives GA3–GA5 industrial politics named human continuity without turning Helix into one family.

### HSE-D — Node / Route Steward House

Historical base:
- long-term operation of strategic nodes, routes, convoy services or traffic institutions.

Real power:
- navigation knowledge, local legitimacy, emergency access, route estates/trusts.

Weakness:
- central standardization can strip privileges; local populations may resent tolls and hereditary access.

Military relation:
- escorts, rescue service, local defense, route-protection orders.

### HSE-E — Civic / Provincial Political House

Historical base:
- governorships, elected/appointed offices, local institutions and patronage.

Real power:
- provincial networks, tax coalitions, schools, hospitals, civic organizations.

Weakness:
- public accountability, rival districts, term/office rules.

Rule:
- not all members hold titles; house identity may function like a durable political family/network.

### HSE-F — Archive / Medical / Professional House

Historical base:
- medicine, law, archives, research, education or a protected profession.

Real power:
- credential networks, trusted expertise, foundations and professional institutions.

Potential harm:
- expertise becomes inherited access or gatekeeping.

Story value:
- shows that elite continuity is not only military/aristocratic.

## 3. House power vector

Every story-visible house must be scored separately on:

1. liquid wealth;
2. productive assets;
3. land/habitat/route rights;
4. political office access;
5. legal/ceremonial legitimacy;
6. military relationships;
7. information/archive access;
8. public constituency;
9. professional networks;
10. debt and obligations.

No house may be “powerful” in every dimension without a specific setting-level reason.

---

## 4. Succession models

Allowed models can coexist.

### S-H1 registered blood descent
- biological descent matters but eligibility is legally registered.

### S-H2 designated heir
- current holder chooses among eligible kin or members.

### S-H3 adoption / incorporation
- non-biological successor can inherit house standing.

### S-H4 council confirmation
- family/house council must confirm successor.

### S-H5 office-linked succession
- some house prestige follows an office but office itself is not inherited automatically.

### S-H6 trust/foundation continuity
- assets remain in a legal trust; descendants govern but do not personally own everything.

### S-H7 branch rotation
- several branches alternate or compete for representation.

Recommended world texture:
- major houses should use at least two mechanisms, creating real legal conflict.

---

## 5. Species and lineage rule

A house may be:
- mostly one species because of regional history;
- deliberately multi-species through marriage/adoption/incorporation;
- species-neutral as a professional or institutional lineage.

Hard veto:
- a species cannot be represented by one ruling house.
- mixed-species house membership does not require biological interbreeding.

Kinship can be legal/social without genetic compatibility.

---

## 6. Genetic enhancement and bloodline politics

Existing genetic medicine allows narrow prevention and rare experimental lineage engineering, but the setting must reject biological determinism.

Possible house behaviors:
- preserve hereditary disease treatment records;
- buy privileged enhancement access;
- advertise “healthy lineage” status;
- hide failed or abusive engineering programs;
- sponsor pilot/medical optimization;
- maintain fertility or gestation trusts.

But:
- no house has a reliable gene for genius, loyalty, courage or ace-pilot ability;
- inheritance claims may use genetics as propaganda even when scientifically weak;
- genetic data can become evidence in succession law without becoming destiny.

This allows bloodline politics without turning the setting into eugenic magic.

## 7. House–knightly-order relationship patterns

### P-HK1 Patronage
House funds an order but does not own its charter or members.

### P-HK2 Founding sponsor
House helped create an order; later law converted it into an independent service institution.

### P-HK3 Shared trust
House and public institutions jointly appoint trustees.

### P-HK4 Retainer model
This is not a knightly order at all; it is a private retainer corps.

### P-HK5 Rival claim
House claims historical ownership while the order claims public/service independence.

Recommended dramatic use:
- GA4 succession can turn P-HK5 into a real legal-military conflict over who may command a famous order.

---

## 8. House–equipment relationship

A house can possess:
- money;
- factories;
- patents;
- shares;
- service rights;
- ceremonial hulls;
- licensed security equipment.

It does not automatically possess:
- state fleet units;
- knight-order frames;
- pilots as personal property;
- proprietary rights to a whole historical technology lineage.

Heirloom machine rule:
- one surviving hull is a legal/mechanical object with maintenance costs, ownership records and replacement limits.
- its symbolic importance may exceed combat value.

---

## 9. House conflict generators

Good house conflict should arise from combinations of:

- valid but incompatible inheritance documents;
- debt versus ceremonial status;
- industrial wealth versus old legitimacy;
- public duty versus private patronage;
- adopted heir versus blood claimant;
- multi-species legal kinship versus genetic proof traditions;
- military branch loyalty versus civilian house council;
- archive ownership versus public evidence rights;
- hereditary medical/genetic privacy versus succession transparency.

Avoid:
- “evil noble family wants throne” as a complete motivation.

## 10. GA integration

### GA1
- houses are background institutions, sponsors, officer families and contract names; do not overload opening.

### GA2
- route/yard families and local patron networks become visible through contracts and first ship acquisition.

### GA3
- industrial and civic houses can support or oppose open production standards.

### GA4
- maximum visibility: succession law, founding covenants, Guard/Order patronage and claimant coalitions.

### GA5
- house-owned shares and obligations complicate “ownerless” fleet assembly.

### GA6
- evacuation tests whether status-based priority survives catastrophe.

### GA7
- regional houses can back different route federations without acting as one aristocratic bloc.

### GA8–GA10
- archives, genetic records, inheritance and distributed authority decide whether old houses survive as institutions, reform into trusts or lose privileges.

---

## 11. Red-team stop conditions

Do not canonize a named house if:

- its power comes only from “ancient blood”;
- every important member shares one personality or ideology;
- it owns an unexplained private fleet;
- it has an ancient superframe with no maintenance ecology;
- species and house are treated as synonyms;
- genetic purity objectively predicts competence;
- killing the head instantly erases the network;
- the house exists only to give one claimant money/troops.

---

## 12. Recommended named-house envelope

Do not build dozens now.

Working reader-facing envelope across the whole series:
- 4–6 major recurring houses/networks;
- 6–10 regional/act-specific houses;
- additional names only when scene demand proves they are useful.

At least one major house should be:
- non-military;
- financially weakened but legally prestigious;
- politically important without being genetically homogeneous;
- internally split over privilege reform.

This prevents the house layer from collapsing into feudal-war aesthetics.
