## 1. Succession Design Principle

- 제국의 승계는 단순 장자상속이 아니다.
- 전쟁·노드 단절·AI 붕괴 이후, 서로 다른 비상상황을 대비한 여러 승계 프로토콜이 누적됐다.
- 평시에는 이 규칙들이 우선순위로 정리되어 있지만, 이번 위기는 **서로 다른 조건을 동시에 충족**해 여러 답이 합법적으로 나온다.
- 누군가 문서를 위조해서만 혼란이 생긴 것이 아니다. 실제 법과 기록이 충돌한다.
- GA4의 답은 ‘진짜 왕을 찾아내기’가 아니라 어떤 권한을 임시로 누구에게 주고, 어떤 기록·기관이 그 권한을 제한할지 결정하는 것이다.

## 2. Trigger Event — Recommended

Working event:
- reigning sovereign suffers confirmed biological death or irreversible incapacity during a secure but partially isolated transit/medical event.

Recommended ambiguity:
- body/death evidence is strong enough that “still alive” is not the main mystery.
- disputed point is **when incapacity legally began**, whether a final designation was valid, and which emergency condition applied.

Why this is stronger than secret survival:
- avoids cheap missing-emperor twist.
- forces legal/institutional conflict.
- allows multiple documents to be authentic.

Potential event stack:
1. sovereign enters medical stasis after neural/phase injury.
2. a final sealed designation is transmitted but arrives out of order.
3. military emergency protocol activates during route/command disruption.
4. no publicly acknowledged direct heir can immediately assume all offices.
5. Registry, Court, General Staff and Continuity systems timestamp different start points.

## 3. Succession Protocols

### SP-01 — Blood and Registered Line

Rule:
- nearest eligible registered descendant/house heir assumes crown after death confirmation and ceremonial accession.

Strength:
- clear public tradition and dynastic continuity.

Weakness:
- eligibility, adoption, sealed births, disqualification and guardianship records are contested.

Primary claimant benefit:
- dynastic heir.

### SP-02 — Sovereign Designation

Rule:
- reigning sovereign may designate successor or regent through sealed multi-signature instrument.

Strength:
- direct intent and crisis flexibility.

Weakness:
- timing, capacity and transmission order disputed; designation may be for regency, not crown.

Primary claimant benefit:
- designated administrator/regent.

### SP-03 — Council Regency

Rule:
- if successor is minor, incapacitated, disputed or unavailable, senior civil, judicial and royal institutions form a regency council.

Strength:
- keeps administration functioning without premature crown decision.

Weakness:
- council can become permanent oligarchy or deadlock.

Primary claimant benefit:
- civil-service compact.

### SP-04 — Military Continuity Command

Rule:
- during existential military/route breakdown, General Staff may appoint an Emergency Protector until civilian succession is certified.

Strength:
- immediate command and defense.

Weakness:
- protector can become dictator; “existential” threshold can be manipulated.

Primary claimant benefit:
- senior military commander.

### SP-05 — Anchor/Continuity Custodianship

Rule:
- if command authentication and civil records diverge, a Continuity Custodian may suspend high-level orders and preserve the state until keys/records reconcile.

Strength:
- prevents forged sovereign commands and institutional collapse.

Weakness:
- unelected technical/archival office can govern through access denial.

Primary claimant benefit:
- Continuity Office/A-001 predecessor institution.

### SP-06 — Provincial Consent Compact

Rule:
- if central institutions split beyond a defined period, a qualified majority of provinces/node authorities may recognize a temporary sovereign/regency compact.

Strength:
- state legitimacy includes governed regions, not only Core institutions.

Weakness:
- slow, vulnerable to coercion and regional bargaining; can formalize breakup.

Primary claimant benefit:
- provincial/federal coalition and Ardis precedent.

### SP-07 — Founding Covenant / Reversion

Rule:
- under catastrophic continuity failure, original founding houses/institutions may invoke a temporary covenant restoring powers to signatory bodies until a new accession.

Strength:
- deep historical legitimacy and distributed emergency structure.

Weakness:
- signatories no longer map cleanly to modern society; records are incomplete and elite-heavy.

Primary claimant benefit:
- old court/founding coalition.

## 4. Why Multiple Protocols Validate

At GA4 opening:

- death/incapacity timestamp differs across Registry, medical, military and node systems.
- designation document is authentic but may have been signed before or after legal incapacity threshold.
- direct-line eligibility record contains sealed correction/adoption/renunciation conflict.
- military emergency threshold is objectively met in some theaters and not others.
- Continuity systems detect authentication divergence and activate custodianship.
- provincial compact threshold begins because central institutions issue contradictory orders.
- founding covenant can be invoked because several modern signatories descend from merged/split offices.

Result:
- at least four claimants/solutions can produce authentic, legally grounded orders.

## 5. The Succession Message Packet Arriving at Ardis

### MSG-A — Registry Accession Notice

Sender:
- Central Registry + Court seal.

Claim:
- recognizes dynastic heir as presumptive sovereign, orders provinces to maintain existing administration and await ceremony.

Authenticity:
- valid signatures and lineage snapshot.

Problem:
- snapshot predates or excludes a sealed correction.

### MSG-B — Final Designation / Regency Instrument

Sender:
- sovereign’s sealed personal office and medical witness chain.

Claim:
- appoints a designated regent/administrator with broad emergency authority.

Authenticity:
- cryptographically and ceremonially valid.

Problem:
- wording can be read as temporary regency or succession; capacity/timestamp disputed.

### MSG-C — General Staff Protector Order

Sender:
- Imperial General Staff emergency quorum.

Claim:
- appoints military protector, freezes disputed civil/route commands until security restored.

Authenticity:
- valid military chain under SP-04.

Problem:
- not a crown accession and risks suppressing civil institutions.

### MSG-D — Continuity Suspension Directive

Sender:
- Continuity Custodian/Anchor authority.

Claim:
- suspends all irreversible accession and master-key transfers; orders institutions into preservation mode.

Authenticity:
- valid under SP-05 because conflicting command hashes are real.

Problem:
- grants unelected office broad veto and route control.

### MSG-E — Provincial Consultation Request

Sender:
- coalition of governors, node cities and provincial assemblies.

Claim:
- invokes SP-06 and requests Ardis’s authenticated participation in a temporary compact.

Authenticity:
- enough valid signatories to begin process, not yet reach threshold.

Problem:
- some provinces are coerced or self-interested; slow response.

### MSG-F — Founding Covenant Claim

Sender:
- Old Court/founding-house coalition.

Claim:
- says throne is temporarily vacant and powers revert to covenant signatories.

Authenticity:
- authentic old seals and some valid institutional descendants.

Problem:
- excludes most current population/provinces and relies on disputed institutional genealogy.

Rule:
- none is a simple forgery.
- secondary forged summaries and selective leaks exist, but the core packet is genuinely contradictory.

# Claimant/Political Solutions

## 6. CLM-01 — The Dynastic Heir

Working title:
- `등록계승자 / Registered Heir`.

Profile:
- adult or near-adult royal descendant with recognized public identity.
- not incompetent or secretly evil.

Core belief:

> A visible lawful sovereign can stop institutions from turning every emergency into permanent power.

Constituency:
- royal household, dynastic bloc, ceremonial institutions, citizens exhausted by ambiguity, some provinces.

Resources:
- Crown Habitat, court/regalia, public recognition, portions of Registry and Guard.

Weakness:
- limited administrative/military experience; eligibility correction/renunciation dispute; can become instrument of advisers.

External want:
- complete accession and keep state intact.

Internal need:
- separate personal legitimacy from ownership of every institution and future.

Helpful action:
- publicly limits retaliation or accepts temporary shared regency.

Harmful action:
- uses ceremonial/public pressure to force institutions before evidence review.

Possible end:
- becomes bounded monarch/claimant within compact, loses sole claim, or withdraws.

## 7. CLM-02 — The Designated Regent

Profile:
- senior civil administrator, royal relative, minister or provincial executive named in final instrument.

Core belief:

> The sovereign chose competence and continuity over blood because the state cannot pause for ceremony.

Constituency:
- ministries, Civil Service Hub, some provincial governors and service institutions.

Resources:
- administration, budget execution, policy expertise and designation document.

Weakness:
- may lack public/ceremonial legitimacy; designation scope disputed; bureaucratic base.

External want:
- obtain regency and execute emergency program.

Internal need:
- accept time limits, shared audit and possible return of authority.

Helpful action:
- keeps salaries, hospitals and logistics functioning.

Harmful action:
- classifies evidence and uses administrative necessity to sideline opposition.

Possible end:
- time-limited regent in compact or head of civil executive under a separate crown.

## 8. CLM-03 — The Emergency Protector

Profile:
- respected senior admiral/general with real war record and broad officer support.

Core belief:

> A state that cannot issue one military order will be partitioned before lawyers finish reading the seals.

Constituency:
- General Staff factions, Legion Muster, military families, threatened provinces.

Resources:
- deployable forces, command network and security legitimacy.

Weakness:
- civil/economic governance, fear of coup and officer splits.

External want:
- freeze succession, secure Core and routes, defeat insurgency/foreign exploitation.

Internal need:
- submit military power to a civilian outcome that may be less efficient.

Helpful action:
- prevents mass coup/attack and protects public institutions.

Harmful action:
- arrests/suspends civil actors and treats disagreement as operational threat.

Possible end:
- bounded military protector under compact, rival defeated but institution remains, or tragic refusal.

## 9. CLM-04 — The Continuity Custodian

Profile:
- current or predecessor-stage Continuity Director/college representative; may be A-001 or an earlier officeholder depending chronology.

Core belief:

> No claimant should inherit command while identity and orders disagree; preservation must precede politics.

Constituency:
- node operators, authentication officials, continuity civil servants, citizens dependent on stable records.

Resources:
- Meridian Anchor, command suspension, authentication analysis, archival memory and emergency procedures.

Weakness:
- unelected, low public warmth, overvalues reversible institutional survival over current rights.

External want:
- suspend accession and place keys under Continuity custody.

Internal need:
- reveal uncertainty and accept plural custodians rather than one preserving office.

Helpful action:
- blocks forged/irreversible transfer and stabilizes routes/services.

Harmful action:
- freezes identities, funds and movement for “risk containment.”

Possible end:
- gains bounded role in temporary compact and becomes long-term ideological rival.

## 10. CLM-05 — The Provincial Compact

Profile:
- not one person; coalition led by 2–4 governors/node-city representatives, potentially including Ardis delegates.

Core belief:

> The Empire exists because provinces and routes continue to recognize it; the Core cannot decide legitimacy alone after issuing mutually exclusive orders.

Constituency:
- provinces, node cities, regional fleets, local administrations and federal/autonomy movements.

Resources:
- taxes, food, routes, local forces and recognition.

Weakness:
- slow, self-interested, internally unequal and vulnerable to secession.

External want:
- establish temporary compact that splits crown, executive, military and continuity powers.

Internal need:
- accept obligations to weaker regions and central minimum standards, not only provincial veto.

Helpful action:
- prevents immediate civil war and broadens legitimacy.

Harmful action:
- bargains recognition for concessions and can abandon weak regions.

Possible end:
- foundation of temporary compact; not a permanent federation yet.

## 11. CLM-06 — Founding Covenant Coalition

Profile:
- Old Court houses, judicial historians, religious/ceremonial institutions and former signatory bodies.

Core belief:

> Emergency institutions have accumulated power beyond the original covenant; legitimacy must revert to the founding balance.

Constituency:
- hereditary houses, old institutions, traditionalists, some civil-liberties critics of modern central bureaucracy.

Resources:
- old records, courts, ceremonial authority, wealth and patronage.

Weakness:
- elite bias, outdated institutional identities, fragmented modern support.

External want:
- declare vacancy and convene covenant assembly.

Internal need:
- include modern citizens/provinces/workers and accept that historical continuity is not property.

Helpful action:
- exposes hidden emergency powers and legal history.

Harmful action:
- excludes modern institutions/people and uses historical ambiguity for aristocratic restoration.

Possible end:
- contributes legal limits and archives but fails to regain sole authority.

# Imperial Intelligence Internal War

## 13. Institution Role

Imperial Intelligence is not neutral verifier.
It controls:
- sealed records and witness chains.
- claimant protection/surveillance.
- counterintelligence and leaks.
- strategic candidate/cultivation files.
- parts of Archive/legacy investigation.

The agency’s internal split determines which evidence arrives and who survives.

## 14. Blocs

### INT-DYN — Dynastic Protection Bloc

Goal:
- secure Registered Heir and complete accession before military/Continuity offices become permanent rulers.

Methods:
- household protection, suppress eligibility challenge, public ceremonial evidence.

Correct concern:
- prolonged emergency erodes civilian monarchy and public legitimacy.

Blind spot:
- treats inconvenient records/people as attacks on state.

### INT-CON — State Continuity Bloc

Goal:
- preserve administration, route and command even if crown is split or replaced.

Methods:
- negotiate Regent/Custodian/Protector balance, compartment evidence, secure keys.

Correct concern:
- one heir cannot make institutions obey.

Blind spot:
- emergency compartmentalization becomes rule by office.

### INT-CUL — Cultivation/Plural Talent Bloc

Goal:
- protect useful irregular officers, provinces and systems; prevent purge/monopoly.

Methods:
- covert safe passage, selective leaks, negotiated local autonomy.

Correct concern:
- purge wastes future leaders and pushes them to rebellion.

Blind spot:
- manipulates people “for their own future role.”

Opening representative R-IMP-001 likely aligns here with mixed Continuity ties.

### INT-PUR — Purge/Security Bloc

Goal:
- remove ambiguous claimants, rogue node keys, provincial leaders and Archive-linked actors before civil war expands.

Methods:
- arrests, discrediting, targeted assassination/identity closure and asset seizure.

Correct concern:
- multi-claimant ambiguity is exploitable by hostile forces.

Blind spot:
- creates the rebellion and fragmentation it fears.

### INT-ARC — Archive/Legacy Bloc

Goal:
- use legacy records, 07/service authorities and Archive anomalies to identify the historically stable outcome.

Methods:
- seize records, monitor P-001 network, compare future/continuity models.

Correct concern:
- standard legal evidence may not detect systemic continuity failure.

Blind spot:
- assumes historical survival ranking equals legitimacy.

## 15. Intelligence Agency Independent Clock

0 — secure witnesses and duplicate archives.
1 — protect selected claimant while monitoring all.
2 — leak/suppress evidence to prevent one rival’s early victory.
3 — Purge and Archive blocs target P-001/Ardis/07 network.
4 — field offices split and issue contradictory protective/capture orders.
5 — agency fractures into claimant-aligned commands or accepts temporary plural oversight.

If protagonists do nothing:
- witnesses disappear or are sealed.
- selected evidence creates one premature accession.
- military/Continuity response challenges it.
- civil war begins with less public evidence and more purges.

## 17. GA4 Recommended Resolution Direction

Not final crown settlement.

Recommended outcome:
- no claimant completes uncontested accession.
- a time-limited `Imperial Continuity Compact` separates:
  - ceremonial crown/household legitimacy;
  - civil regency/executive administration;
  - bounded military protectorate/General Staff;
  - Continuity authentication custody;
  - provincial consultation/recognition;
  - public audit/judicial review.
- some regions and units refuse, making GA5 civil-war/fleet problem inevitable.

Why this works:
- resolves immediate collapse without claiming one true heir.
- makes GA5 “ownerless fleet” necessary: military forces need coordination beyond personal claimant ownership.
- preserves all major factions for later.

Cost:
- slow and ambiguous command.
- claimants retain supporters.
- compact depends on personalities and emergency terms.
- field fleets/provinces may reject Core compromise.

## 18. Anti-Cliché Controls

- no secret illegitimate baby solves succession.
- no single DNA test/document reveals the true ruler.
- claimants have real constituencies and useful competence.
- dynastic heir is not automatically foolish.
- military protector genuinely saves lives.
- continuity custodian blocks real forgeries.
- provincial compact can be selfish and exclusionary.
- old covenant critique has legitimate civil-liberty value despite elitism.
- P-001 is not crowned, regent or sole kingmaker.
