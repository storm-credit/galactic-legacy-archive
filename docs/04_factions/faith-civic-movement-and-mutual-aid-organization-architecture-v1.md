## 1. Purpose

The existing faith bible already defines philosophical and devotional families. This document does **not** create replacement religions.

It fills the institutional gap:

> If people actually live by Continuance, Passage, Maker/Steward, Witness, Quiet/Refusal or secular civic ethics, what organizations do they build, who pays for them, what services do they provide, what can they coerce, and how do they survive political pressure?

The design target is not “one church per belief.”

The target is a layered civic ecology in which:

- one belief family can produce mutually hostile organizations;
- one organization can contain several species and political alignments;
- secular and religious bodies compete for the same service roles;
- material service, law, property and succession matter as much as doctrine;
- no institution is morally correct because its philosophy sounds humane.

## 2. Hard Boundaries

1. No supernatural intervention is confirmed.
2. Regression is not evidence for a universal afterlife.
3. No species has one mandatory religion.
4. No religion maps one-to-one to one faction.
5. Religious office does not automatically grant civil or military authority.
6. Civic movements do not receive free logistics, media reach or legitimacy.
7. Refugees, patients, workers and AI/composite persons remain agents, not charity props.
8. New named church/order/leader is HOLD until story demand and author approval justify it.
9. Existing Canon/Working Canon faction authority remains unchanged.

## 3. Organization Taxonomy

### FCO-01 Memorial & Continuance Trust

Derived from:
- Continuance traditions;
- public memorial/service ethics.

Material base:
- endowments;
- family/service donations;
- public maintenance contracts;
- memorial property;
- pension/claim administration fees where lawful.

Typical services:
- casualty and missing-person rolls;
- grave/memorial stewardship;
- survivor claims assistance;
- maintenance of one public service in a dead unit/community's name;
- archive custody under restricted terms.

Power source:
- trusted records;
- property/endowment;
- long family/institutional relationships;
- moral authority in casualty disputes.

Shadow:
- can freeze inherited roles into obligation;
- can suppress inconvenient corrections to preserve donor legitimacy;
- may become a gatekeeper for “whose death counts.”

Multi-species consequence:
- memorial practice must support different body disposal, mourning intervals, names, kinship and legal-death definitions.

### FCO-02 Passage House / Route Hospitality Network

Derived from:
- Open-Void / Passage traditions;
- route and exile cultures.

Material base:
- berth fees;
- mutual-aid dues;
- donated cargo space;
- port concessions;
- refugee-service grants.

Typical services:
- temporary lodging;
- translation and route information;
- missing-person notices;
- safe transfer for minors/dependents;
- neutral witness for departure/arrival obligations;
- limited emergency food, medicine and suit support.

Power source:
- distributed local nodes;
- reputation across routes;
- trust between crews who do not share governments.

Shadow:
- smuggling or witness laundering can hide inside hospitality;
- mobile ideology can neglect people who cannot travel;
- local leaders can quietly control who receives onward passage.

### FCO-03 Steward Guild / Repair Covenant

Derived from:
- Maker and Steward traditions;
- professional technical ethics.

Material base:
- guild dues;
- certification work;
- repair contracts;
- apprenticeship assets;
- tool/yard trusts.

Typical services:
- apprentice care;
- independent failure review;
- public repair days;
- maintenance records;
- emergency repair teams;
- tool inheritance mediation.

Power source:
- scarce competence;
- certification legitimacy;
- access to workshops and historical failure data.

Shadow:
- hereditary technical caste;
- guild capture of certification;
- treating users as less legitimate than makers;
- withholding support from non-member or non-standard communities.

### FCO-04 Witness Circle / Testimony Commons

Derived from:
- Witness and Plural Memory traditions.

Material base:
- civic grants;
- legal-aid funds;
- member dues;
- archive service contracts;
- university/media partnerships.

Typical services:
- consent-managed testimony collection;
- rights education;
- competing-record comparison;
- correction petitions;
- witness protection support;
- memorial mediation.

Power source:
- testimony networks;
- procedural credibility;
- access to victims and local archives.

Shadow:
- process paralysis;
- witness credential hierarchy;
- data custodians becoming unaccountable brokers;
- trauma repeatedly activated for political leverage.

### FCO-05 Quiet Sanctuary / Refusal Cooperative

Derived from:
- Quiet/Refusal traditions;
- anti-surveillance and right-to-forget practice.

Material base:
- member labor;
- private donations;
- isolated property;
- low-connectivity infrastructure;
- sympathetic legal-defense networks.

Typical services:
- temporary offline residence;
- identity/data boundary counseling;
- low-network medical care;
- refuge for people or AI-derived persons refusing compulsory linkage;
- mediation with outside authorities.

Power source:
- secrecy discipline;
- geographic and network isolation;
- community trust.

Shadow:
- abuse can be hidden as privacy;
- leaders can prevent exit;
- lack of external services can become coercive dependency;
- illegal actors may exploit sanctuary rules.

### FCO-06 Secular Mutual-Aid Federation

Derived from:
- civic/professional ethics rather than metaphysical belief.

Material base:
- worker dues;
- cooperative insurance;
- local tax matching;
- volunteer labor;
- emergency pooled inventory.

Typical services:
- strike support;
- disaster relief;
- care work;
- food and medicine pooling;
- translation and disability access;
- legal-defense funds.

Power source:
- membership volume;
- service reliability;
- labor withdrawal;
- local legitimacy.

Shadow:
- factional capture;
- patronage distribution;
- majority species/class dominance;
- emergency committees that never relinquish power.

### FCO-07 Personhood & Embodiment Rights League

Derived from:
- secular rights movements;
- Witness/Refusal overlaps;
- existing AI/composite and substantially modified-person law.

Constituencies may include:
- prosthetic/cybernetic persons;
- AI-derived persons;
- high-modification humans;
- disputed-continuity persons;
- species facing interface/access discrimination.

Typical demands:
- hardware ownership separation from personhood;
- repair and migration rights;
- bodily/embodiment autonomy;
- data portability;
- appeal from automated risk classification;
- access to public interfaces and emergency systems.

Shadow:
- expensive high-modification elites may dominate poorer disability/access agendas;
- vendors may astroturf “rights” campaigns to weaken safety regulation;
- personhood questions can be used to avoid labor or liability questions.

### FCO-08 Habitat & Biosphere Stewardship Coalition

Derived from:
- stewardship ethics;
- local survival politics;
- species/environmental dependence.

Typical constituencies:
- habitat technicians;
- farmers and food-system workers;
- native-ecology communities;
- species with narrow environmental tolerances;
- public-health networks.

Typical demands:
- life-support reserve floors;
- contamination transparency;
- protected biosphere zones;
- no military requisition below survival thresholds;
- ecological repair liability.

Shadow:
- exclusionary localism;
- “ecological protection” used against migrants;
- wealthy habitats externalizing pollution to frontier systems.

### FCO-09 Veteran / Survivor Obligation Association

Derived from:
- Continuance;
- military/service oath culture;
- secular survivor advocacy.

Typical services:
- disability claims;
- missing-unit records;
- burial/memorial support;
- testimony on command failures;
- family assistance;
- retraining.

Political diversity:
- some defend military institutions;
- some become reform blocs;
- some reject the state that employed them.

Shadow:
- service prestige used to override civilians;
- hierarchy reproduced after discharge;
- victim competition.

### FCO-10 Inter-Species Civic Access Coalition

Purpose:
- ensure that public systems designed around dominant-body assumptions do not silently exclude other recognized persons.

Typical issues:
- atmospheric zoning;
- gravity accessibility;
- food/medical compatibility;
- transit and evacuation;
- translation;
- school and prison accommodation;
- frame/industrial workplace access.

Important rule:
- this is **not** a “species unity faction.”
- members disagree over cost sharing, universal standards, local autonomy and affirmative accommodation.

Shadow:
- dominant core species can become spokespeople for everybody;
- standardization may erase small-population requirements;
- corporate vendors can turn access into proprietary lock-in.

## 4. Institution Scale Ladder

### L0 — household/local circle
- 5–50 active participants;
- one room, workshop, berth cluster or local archive;
- no galaxy-wide consequence by itself.

### L1 — station/system organization
- dozens to thousands of members/users;
- owns or leases physical service points;
- can matter in local elections, strikes, relief or public hearings.

### L2 — route/cluster network
- federated rather than centrally commanded by default;
- shared credentials, emergency reciprocity and dispute process;
- different nodes may refuse central directives.

### L3 — multi-region federation
- large enough to lobby Imperial, Neutral or regional authorities;
- vulnerable to internal blocs, donor capture and legal fragmentation.

### L4 — galaxy-symbolic institution
- extremely rare;
- reputation exceeds direct organizational control;
- must not be treated as one omnipotent church/NGO.

## 5. Property, Money and Labor

Every front-stage faith/civic organization must answer:

1. Who owns its buildings, ships, archives and tools?
2. Who pays staff?
3. Which services are free, subsidized or charged?
4. What happens when donations collapse?
5. Does it employ unpaid care labor?
6. Does it receive state/corporate contracts?
7. Can it strike, close, relocate or refuse service?
8. Who audits it?
9. What debt does it carry?
10. What asset cannot be replaced if lost?

An organization without material answers is a lore label, not a functioning institution.

## 6. Leadership and Succession

Do not default to “high priest.”

Possible leadership forms:
- elected steward board;
- rotating witness panel;
- hereditary trustee constrained by board law;
- licensed guild master with independent auditor;
- local delegates to a federation;
- paired ritual and administrative offices;
- emergency coordinator with expiry clause;
- AI/human mixed council where law permits.

Succession failure modes:
- founder cult;
- donor capture;
- archival key concentration;
- hereditary credential monopoly;
- emergency office becoming permanent;
- species-majority bloc capturing representation.

## 7. Relation to Major Factions

### Imperial sphere

Can:
- recognize public-service bodies;
- subsidize memorial, care and relief organizations;
- impose registration/accounting rules.

Cannot be assumed to:
- command all religious bodies;
- define all legitimate mourning;
- own all charity networks.

Conflict engines:
- registration versus sanctuary;
- casualty record control;
- requisition of relief assets;
- succession legitimacy rites.

### Helix / corporate sphere

Possible relations:
- contract care/rehabilitation;
- sponsor professional stewardship associations;
- fund access technology.

Conflict engines:
- vendor lock-in;
- patient data;
- charitable branding versus real liability;
- corporate-funded rights groups.

### Independence / successor polities

Strength:
- locally rooted mutual aid;
- flexible sanctuary/passages.

Risks:
- local majority pressure;
- informal coercion;
- weak appeal outside the community.

### Neutral Assembly / route communities

Strength:
- cross-jurisdiction witness, hospitality and ship covenants.

Risks:
- neutrality can hide refusal to confront abuse;
- service access may depend on route reputation.

### Ardis / standards networks

Natural overlap:
- repair covenants;
- access standards;
- witness/process groups;
- cooperative relief.

Risk:
- standards language becoming moral monopoly.

## 8. Military and Crisis Use

These organizations must matter during war without becoming secret armies by default.

They may control:
- shelters;
- casualty lists;
- evacuation manifests;
- repair volunteers;
- donated cargo;
- neutral witnesses;
- family contact networks;
- food/medicine reserves;
- local knowledge.

They may resist through:
- refusal of labor;
- public testimony;
- sanctuary;
- litigation/appeal;
- route boycott;
- distributed records;
- non-cooperation.

Armed wings are **not automatic**. If a body maintains armed protection, it must have a separate legal/logistics doctrine and must be audited against [[middle-layer-organization-role-demand-matrix-v1]].

## 9. Multi-Species Design Requirements

For any front-stage organization serving multiple species, record:

- environment zones supported;
- translation modes;
- food/medical segregation or compatibility;
- ritual/body handling constraints;
- kinship/guardian recognition;
- voting/representation method;
- accessible meeting interfaces;
- evacuation priority conflicts;
- whether one species bears disproportionate service cost;
- whether leadership mirrors user demographics.

No organization gets to claim “universal” simply because it has one alien member.

## 10. Story Deployment by Grand Act

### GA1
- keep background-light;
- one or two local service/memorial practices are enough;
- do not unload the galaxy's religions at the Academy.

### GA2
- passage houses, mutual aid, veteran/survivor claims and gray-market-adjacent sanctuary become useful;
- reveal that civil society moves goods/information where states do not.

### GA3
- habitat/biosphere stewardship and repair covenants become material in city/node governance.

### GA4
- memorial trusts, dynastic rites and civic legitimacy bodies can complicate succession without deciding the “true” claimant.

### GA5
- route federations and personhood/access coalitions become part of ownerless/common-fleet governance.

### GA6
- relief networks and species-access coalitions become operationally decisive in evacuation;
- not every shelter supports every biology.

### GA7
- veteran, survivor and witness organizations contest military legitimacy on both Rian and Haren sides.

### GA8
- Witness/Quiet institutions become central to record ownership, refusal and testimony.

### GA9
- preservation regime can selectively recognize “approved” civic bodies and suppress incompatible service networks.

### GA10
- ending should show civic institutions capable of persisting without Rian as permanent patron or master-key holder.

## 11. Red-Team Questions

Before promoting any named organization, answer:

- What indispensable service does it provide?
- Why does the state/corporation not simply replace it?
- Who dislikes it for legitimate reasons?
- Who benefits materially from its existence?
- Who does unpaid or dangerous work inside it?
- How can it fail without becoming cartoonishly corrupt?
- What asset or authority can it lose permanently?
- What does it do when Rian is absent?
- Can a member leave?
- Can a minority species or dissenting believer appeal?
- What happens after its charismatic leader dies?

## 12. Current Gap Closure

This proposal closes the **architecture gap** for:
- religion/ideology/civic-movement faction class;
- named-organization readiness;
- faith-to-service linkage;
- multi-species civic organization requirements.

It does **not** close:
- exact named institutions;
- exact leaders;
- exact membership numbers;
- canonical political endorsements;
- character-specific faith identity.

Those remain story-coupled or author-choice decisions.

## 13. Gate

Current result: **PASS FOR NONCANON ARCHITECTURE**.

Canon promotion requires:
1. story demand;
2. no conflict with existing faith/faction authority;
3. material-base and succession check;
4. species-access check where relevant;
5. independent red-team/continuity review;
6. author approval for any major named institution with cross-act power.
