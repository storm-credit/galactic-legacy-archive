## 0. Design rule

A knightly order in this setting is an **enduring service institution with oath, privilege, training, stewardship and public duty**.

It is not defined by:
- medieval costume;
- hereditary pilots;
- one species;
- one ancient superframe per member.

The word “knight” may be a translation/register used by readers for several historically distinct institutions.

---

## 1. Four recommended order archetypes

### K-01 Route Shield Order

Original public problem:
- early long-distance routes lacked enough regular-state coverage to protect convoys, rescue stranded crews and arbitrate emergency passage.

Charter:
- protect specified corridors and answer distress calls inside defined service zones.

Privileges:
- emergency docking priority;
- limited inspection authority;
- right to maintain fortified service depots;
- old convoy toll/endowment income.

Obligations:
- rescue enemies and civilians under charter conditions;
- publish loss and service records;
- cannot abandon assigned corridor solely for political convenience.

Equipment ecology:
- standardized escort frames;
- rescue/service frames;
- corvettes/frigates;
- repair tenders;
- a handful of old institutional hulls.

Prototype inheritance candidates:
- H07/H10/H17/H18.

Conflict:
- Crown/Imperial centralization wants command access;
- merchants resent toll privilege;
- locals defend the order because it actually rescues them.

### K-02 Bastion / Node Custodian Order

Original public problem:
- strategic nodes needed defenders who understood infrastructure well enough to avoid destroying what they protected.

Charter:
- defend, stabilize and if necessary safely disable designated nodes and anchor facilities.

Privileges:
- restricted access to node engineering areas;
- technical stop authority during emergencies;
- dedicated training sites.

Obligations:
- cannot use catastrophic denial without multi-party authorization;
- must preserve evacuation routes and evidence where feasible.

Equipment ecology:
- breach/capture frames;
- service frames;
- command/EW specialists;
- hardened local ships;
- strong engineering staff.

Prototype inheritance candidates:
- H06/H11/H12/H14/H17.

Conflict:
- military command wants rapid denial;
- custodians may refuse, appearing disloyal while protecting civilians/infrastructure.

### K-03 Witness / Custody Order

Original public problem:
- high-value prisoners, claimants, witnesses, archives and medical evacuees crossed jurisdictions where no single state was trusted.

Charter:
- escort and custody under multi-party legal mandate.

Privileges:
- neutralized custody zones;
- recognized evidence seals;
- limited cross-border passage.

Obligations:
- protect persons in custody regardless of faction;
- maintain chain-of-custody and medical standards;
- cannot convert custody into intelligence ownership.

Equipment ecology:
- security/rescue frames;
- low-collateral weapons;
- medical transport;
- sensor/evidence systems;
- few heavy combat assets.

Prototype inheritance candidates:
- H10/H12/H17.

Conflict:
- everyone values them until they protect the wrong person.

### K-04 Expeditionary Service Order

Original public problem:
- distant frontier settlements required survey, emergency construction, defense and diplomacy before full state infrastructure existed.

Charter:
- support new or isolated settlements for bounded terms.

Privileges:
- temporary mission authority;
- salvage and survey rights;
- access to reserve equipment.

Obligations:
- transfer governance to local civil institutions after stabilization;
- maintain multi-species service capability where population requires it.

Equipment ecology:
- modular service/fleet frames;
- scouts;
- mobile workshops;
- colony support craft;
- adaptable life support.

Prototype inheritance candidates:
- H02/H07/H08/H18/H20.

Conflict:
- orders that stay too long become frontier aristocracies;
- orders that leave too quickly create dependency collapse.

## 2. Membership models

Possible combinations:
- competitive academy selection;
- apprenticeship under serving members;
- transfer from regular military/service corps;
- sponsored candidates from houses/provinces;
- species/environment specialist recruitment;
- limited hereditary preference for dependents, never automatic command.

Recommended rule:
- rank advancement must require demonstrated service/certification, not blood alone.

---

## 3. Ownership models

Possible frame ownership:
- order trust owns chassis;
- state lends chassis;
- house endowment owns but order operates;
- individual owns only personal equipment, not strategic hull;
- jointly owned service trust.

Heirloom hull rule:
- a famous frame can remain associated with an office or order, but pilot succession and hull ownership are separate.

---

## 4. Why regular fleets do not replace them

Each order must preserve at least two advantages regular forces cannot cheaply reproduce:

- deep local/technical knowledge;
- politically trusted cross-border status;
- legal continuity across regimes;
- specialized rescue/custody expertise;
- endowment-funded peacetime readiness;
- apprenticeship/maintenance traditions;
- service obligations that survive command changes.

If an order is merely “better soldiers,” it fails this test.

---

## 5. Failure and corruption modes

Knightly orders can decay through:
- hereditary capture;
- house patronage becoming ownership;
- service fees excluding poor regions;
- ritual/status outrunning practical competence;
- old charter becoming legally unaccountable;
- equipment worship replacing maintenance reality;
- political neutrality becoming protection of the powerful;
- emergency authority becoming permanent.

At least one major order should contain a real internal reform movement.

---

## 6. Species integration

Orders can be multi-species institutions.

Required adaptations where relevant:
- barracks environments;
- diet;
- medical staffing;
- armor and suit geometry;
- frame cockpit/control variants;
- acceleration doctrine;
- death/custody ritual differences.

A species-majority historical branch may exist, but the order itself cannot be objectively defined by “racial temperament.”

---

## 7. Reader-facing machine distribution

For a 12–24-frame order squadron:
- 70–90% common/standard chassis or close derivatives;
- 10–25% specialist variants;
- 0–2 historically unique/heirloom hulls in the entire formation, often not simultaneously active.

This keeps formations visible as institutions rather than superhero teams.

---

## 8. GA placement candidates

GA1:
- only references, insignia, old service records; no major order exposition.

GA2:
- Route Shield or Expeditionary order can first appear through contract friction or convoy rescue.

GA3:
- Bastion/Node Custodian logic becomes useful around Ardis infrastructure warfare.

GA4:
- Guard/order charter and house patronage become succession leverage.

GA5:
- orders split over whether to lend assets to Common Fleet mission trusts.

GA6:
- charter obligations are tested under mass evacuation.

GA7:
- old orders fragment, reform or become regional service institutions rather than one imperial bloc.

---

## 9. Stop conditions

Reject a named order if:
- it has no peacetime civilian/public function;
- its members are all hereditary;
- all pilots use unique relic frames;
- it has unlimited funding;
- one species defines all members;
- it exists only to supply a cool rival;
- its charter has no revocation/audit mechanism;
- regular forces could replace it with no political, technical or economic cost.
