## 0. Problem

Current material references `변경방위군`, local defense and regional commands, but the phrase can hide several legally different forces.

Required split:

```text
Provincial Regular Defense
≠ Civic/Habitat Militia
≠ Imperial/State Garrison
≠ Reserve/Mobilization Pool
≠ Contract Security
≠ Emergency Coalition
```

This distinction is necessary because control of troops, evacuation, ammunition, node denial and civilian authority cannot all belong to the same actor.

---

## 1. FD-01 Provincial Regular Defense

Legal basis:
- provincial/regional charter under Imperial or successor law.

Mission:
- routine defense of inhabited systems, local routes, provincial convoys and public infrastructure.

Personnel:
- full-time officers and crews;
- local recruits;
- professional maintainers;
- limited reserve integration.

Funding:
- provincial taxes/transfers;
- central grants;
- local service fees where lawful.

Assets:
- corvettes/light escorts;
- patrol/security and fleet maneuver frames;
- a small number of frigate/destroyer-class assets in richer provinces;
- support/tender ships.

Authority:
- can conduct local defense and escort;
- cannot automatically abandon a province for central campaigns;
- catastrophic infrastructure denial requires higher or emergency legal trigger.

Political tension:
- provincial government may refuse central redeployment during succession crisis.

---

## 2. FD-02 Civic / Habitat Militia

Legal basis:
- municipal/habitat emergency law, cooperative covenant or civil-defense statute.

Mission:
- station/habitat defense;
- evacuation;
- fire/damage control;
- local anti-raider protection;
- civil emergency support.

Personnel:
- part-time volunteers/reservists;
- workers with dual-use skills;
- retired service members.

Assets:
- utility frames;
- converted patrol frames;
- small cutters;
- fixed batteries;
- rescue/medical craft.

Authority limits:
- no independent strategic campaign;
- cannot seize civilian assets beyond bounded emergency rules;
- militia leader does not become mayor/governor automatically.

---

## 3. FD-03 Imperial / State Garrison

Legal basis:
- central military deployment order.

Mission:
- protect strategic nodes, bases, state facilities and treaty obligations;
- reinforce provincial defense.

Personnel:
- regular military chain.

Assets:
- standardized fleet units;
- stronger logistics than locals;
- may include elite/security detachments.

Authority conflict:
- base commander controls military assets;
- local governor controls many civilian services;
- node custodian may hold technical stop authority.

Succession risk:
- contradictory central orders can split garrisons by legal chain.

---

## 4. FD-04 Reserve / Mobilization Pool

Legal basis:
- registered reserve duty, local defense obligation, retired-service recall or contract reserve.

Mission:
- expand crews, maintenance, local security and logistics during crisis.

Key reality:
- reserve manpower is useless without equipment, refresher training, medical screening and command slots.

Assets:
- laid-up/older frames;
- transport craft;
- depot stocks;
- civilian vehicles prepared for conversion.

Weakness:
- readiness varies sharply;
- political resistance to prolonged mobilization;
- employers and families bear cost.

---

## 5. FD-05 Contract Security Layer

Legal basis:
- licensed security, convoy or facility contracts.

Mission:
- fill gaps that public forces do not cover continuously.

Actors:
- Helix security;
- private recovery contractors;
- mercenary/security firms;
- merchant cooperative escorts.

Authority:
- contract-bound;
- cannot assume sovereign police/military authority without emergency delegation.

Risk:
- wealthy districts/assets receive stronger protection than poor communities.

---

## 6. FD-06 Emergency Defense Coalition

Trigger:
- invasion, route collapse, civil war, major disaster or command fragmentation.

Composition:
- provincial regulars;
- garrison units;
- militia;
- contract ships;
- merchant/rescue assets;
- allied/neutral support.

Command:
- mission trust or bounded emergency command.

Requirements:
- explicit objective;
- duration;
- withdrawal rules;
- ammunition/repair settlement;
- civilian authority boundaries;
- surrender and evacuation policy.

This is a precursor logic for later Common Fleet structures but is not permanent unification.

## 7. Critical authority matrix

| Decision | Provincial regular | Civic militia | State garrison | Reserve | Contract security | Emergency coalition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| local patrol | YES | limited | YES around mandate | no independent | contract only | YES |
| civilian evacuation support | YES | YES | support | support | contract/emergency | YES |
| force civilian evacuation | bounded civil law | NO alone | only emergency mandate | NO | NO | bounded mandate |
| destroy node/yard to deny capture | NO alone | NO | only lawful high authority | NO | NO | only explicit emergency authority |
| requisition civilian ships | provincial emergency law | very limited | central emergency law | NO | NO | only delegated civil authority |
| leave province for distant campaign | political authorization | NO | central order | recall terms | client contract | mission agreement |
| arrest civilians | limited military law | civil-defense only | military/security scope | NO | contract-site scope | only specified law |
| surrender local formation | commander within mandate | local civil/defense rule | military chain | attached command | contract/crew rules | coalition charter + unit rights |

This table prevents one generic commander from possessing every wartime power.

## 8. Equipment demand by layer

### Provincial regular
- rugged standardized frames;
- local-environment variants;
- escorts;
- tenders;
- command/sensor craft.

### Civic militia
- utility/industrial conversions;
- rescue equipment;
- cheap patrol frames;
- fixed defense.

### Garrison
- current standardized state models;
- better secure authentication;
- deployable support.

### Reserve
- older but maintainable production families;
- simplified training variants;
- depot compatibility.

### Contract layer
- high maintainability and mission modularity;
- recovery and security variants.

### Emergency coalition
- interoperability bridges;
- mixed-species casualty support;
- common ammunition/service agreements;
- command translation.

## 9. Species and environment effects

A frontier defense force can require structural equipment differences because of:
- local gravity;
- atmosphere/pressure;
- aquatic/fluid habitat;
- population body geometry;
- multi-species evacuation needs.

But:
- the force remains a legal regional organization;
- a species-majority system can contain minority units/citizens;
- outside reinforcements require adaptation and cannot instantly fight at full readiness.

This creates strategic geography without species determinism.

## 10. Academy connection

The Academy can receive missions from a **provincial defense contracting/operations office** without implying that one monolithic `Frontier Defense Force` controls the entire frontier.

Recommended interpretation candidate:
- early Academy mission orders come from one regional/provincial defense authority operating inside a broader frontier-defense institutional family.

Do not rename current canon issuer until exact jurisdiction is audited.

## 11. GA escalation

GA1:
- local defense mission issuer and garrison/legal distinctions appear only as needed.

GA2:
- first full display of provincial regulars, militia, private security and reserve weaknesses.

GA3:
- Ardis forces must distinguish civic defense, Foundry assets, node custodians and external forces.

GA4:
- succession creates genuine garrison/provincial command conflict.

GA5:
- units enter mission trusts without surrendering home obligations.

GA6:
- evacuation tests whether local defense stays to protect home or joins wider rescue.

GA7:
- regional defense bodies can become route-federation members rather than dissolve into one navy.

---

## 12. Stop conditions

Reject a force design if:
- `local defense` explains every armed actor;
- military commander automatically controls civilian government;
- reserve units appear fully ready overnight;
- militia has heavy fleet capability without industry;
- central garrison ignores local logistics indefinitely;
- species-majority force becomes racial army shorthand;
- emergency coalition has no expiry/withdrawal rules.
