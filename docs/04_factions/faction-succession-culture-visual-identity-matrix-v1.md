## 1. Global Design Rules

- faction, culture, class, species/personhood and geography are separate fields;
- no faction is represented solely by one leader, one family or one ideology;
- every major faction has at least three internal blocs and a defined replacement chain;
- visual identity uses shape, material, movement, maintenance and spatial behavior—not color alone;
- uniforms and architecture show resource systems and authority, not just aesthetics;
- sympathetic services and coercive costs must both remain visible;
- a leader’s death changes policy and coalition balance but does not erase the institution;
- succession conflict follows written law, material control and constituency support.

## 2. Imperial State

### Geography and social base

- Core Registry worlds, Crownline nodes, provincial capitals, military stations and recognized public-service networks.
- strongest where authentication, salary, courts, medicine and route schedules depend on old state systems.

### Cultural register

- formal registered name, office and scope matter;
- public speech distinguishes personal opinion from office authority;
- archives, seals, memorial rolls and succession rituals establish continuity;
- informal regional cultures remain diverse beneath Imperial administration.

### Visual language

- repeated vertical frames and nested arches suggesting authority contained inside authority;
- dark ceramic, pale metal, archival glass and physically replaceable seal plates;
- uniforms use detachable office panels rather than permanent rank embroidery;
- command spaces emphasize sight lines, clock synchronization and sealed document channels;
- state equipment is maintainable and standardized rather than uniformly luxurious.

Avoid:
- generic gold palace everywhere;
- all officials wearing aristocratic fashion;
- one royal emblem explaining all institutions.

### Core services

- citizenship and identity;
- courts and contracts;
- salaries and pensions;
- military defense;
- node authentication;
- public education and medicine;
- emergency requisition and coordination.

### Coercive shadow

- compulsory service;
- classified identity closure;
- relocation and compartmentalization;
- registered people prioritized over irregular populations;
- emergency law becoming permanent.

### Principal offices

1. Sovereign / Crown Holder — symbolic and limited executive root.
2. Regent Council / Designated Regent — succession bridge.
3. Protector-General — emergency military and security authority.
4. Continuity Trustee — authentication, public service and institutional survival.
5. Provincial Governors / Node Prefects — local implementation.
6. Civil Service Boards and Courts — routine state continuity.

### Succession chain

Normal:
- lawful heir → confirmed sovereign → cabinet and courts continue.

Disputed:
- designated regent manages civil authority;
- Protector-General controls immediate defense;
- Continuity Trustee validates salaries, identity and services;
- provincial offices maintain local law;
- courts determine standing after emergency.

Failure if:
- one office claims every layer;
- military controls civil authentication;
- Continuity office freezes political appeal;
- royal symbolism is used to bypass operational institutions.

### Leader-loss response

- death of a claimant creates a party realignment, not state collapse;
- deputy offices inherit bounded powers automatically;
- sealed emergency instructions expire unless reconfirmed;
- military units retain mission and civilian rules but may lose political allegiance;
- public services continue through trustee/civil boards.

### Internal blocs

- Dynastic Legitimists;
- Civic Continuity administrators;
- Military Protectorate;
- Provincial Autonomists;
- Reform Constitutionalists;
- Purge/Security hardliners;
- Budget and trade pragmatists.

### Reader-facing recognition

- characters name authority scope before making promises;
- documents and uniforms physically change when office changes;
- public spaces show repair layers and inherited standards rather than timeless perfection.

## 3. Helix Consortium

### Geography and social base

- Orsen medical habitats, Brann yards, certified manufacturing worlds, logistics exchanges, corporate schools and contract settlements.
- strongest where advanced medicine, parts, insurance and career mobility require certification.

### Cultural register

- competence is praised specifically;
- safety case, failure history and responsibility owner matter;
- professional identity can cross national borders;
- workers distinguish the company, certification profession, local plant and creditor ownership.

### Visual language

- modular hexagonal service zones, visible inspection rails and standardized maintenance clearances;
- high-contrast hazard markings paired with clean material labels;
- clothing carries skill/certification panels that can be renewed or revoked;
- corporate leadership areas use quiet neutral surfaces, while factories show layered worker modifications;
- products expose service ports but often seal software/authentication layers.

Avoid:
- glowing neon megacorp cliché;
- identical black suits;
- technology that is sleek merely to signal evil wealth.

### Core services

- advanced medicine and prosthetics;
- safe components and warranties;
- industrial capital;
- employment and technical training;
- insurance and failure compensation;
- scalable production and logistics.

### Coercive shadow

- debt and data lock-in;
- employment tied to treatment/identity;
- proprietary maintenance;
- contract rights priced above local consent;
- supply withdrawal and remote restriction.

### Principal offices

1. Consortium Assembly — major divisions and capital representation.
2. Executive Coordinator — cross-division strategy, not sole owner.
3. Division Directors — Manufacturing, Finance, Human Performance, AI/Standards, Logistics/Security.
4. Regional Receivers / Operations Directors.
5. Plant/Yard Boards and Certified Safety Officers.
6. Worker councils, guilds and subcontractor boards with varying standing.

### Succession chain

- executive vacancy triggers temporary rotating coordination among division directors;
- contracts and facilities remain under regional/plant management;
- safety officers retain independent stop authority;
- creditors may appoint receivers but cannot automatically command workers or security;
- board confirmation requires financial and operational blocs.

### Leader-loss response

- assassination/removal causes share, contract and division conflict, not corporate disappearance;
- regional directors may defect or negotiate independently;
- certification data and service obligations are mirrored across divisions;
- worker and customer claims survive leadership change.

### Internal blocs

- Manufacturing and Frontier Service;
- Finance and Receivership;
- Human Performance/Medical;
- AI and Standards;
- Logistics/Security;
- worker/guild and professional reform networks.

### Reader-facing recognition

- Helix characters ask who bears failure and who certifies repair;
- useful components and good working conditions coexist with access conditions;
- logos appear on seals and service documents, while local workers personalize tools and spaces.

## 4. Frontier Independence Successors

### Geography and social base

- Taris shadow routes, Lysa anchorage, irregular settlements, defecting local councils, hidden fleets and worker/community networks.
- not one state and not one uniform resistance.

### Cultural register

- role, past assistance and local reputation outweigh formal rank;
- route names and community affiliations coexist with legal names;
- meetings emphasize who can actually move, feed, hide or defend people;
- slogans differ sharply between liberation, federalist, localist and scorched factions.

### Visual language

- repaired surfaces keep prior owners’ marks rather than repainting everything;
- detachable route strips, cloth tags and hand-applied symbols identify current mission or sanctuary;
- asymmetrical craft and mixed components are common but not inherently unsafe;
- assembly spaces are adapted cargo bays, workshops or local civic halls;
- armed cells minimize consistent visual identity for survival.

Avoid:
- universal red rebel scarf;
- all equipment as dirty scrap;
- every informal community as warm and democratic.

### Core services

- extraction and sanctuary;
- hidden transport;
- witness protection outside state systems;
- local self-defense;
- political voice for erased people;
- alternative routes and mutual aid.

### Coercive shadow

- militant recruitment;
- weak medicine and long-term services;
- secrecy and internal policing;
- community pressure and retaliation;
- infrastructure denial or destruction.

### Principal offices and structures

No universal chain.

Common forms:

1. local assembly or council;
2. route coordinator;
3. defense commander;
4. cell convener;
5. logistics/care representative;
6. temporary coalition speaker;
7. audit/witness committee in federalist groups.

### Succession chain

- local councils replace delegates through their own rules;
- cells use designated secondary contacts, but network access is segmented;
- route coordinators require trust from multiple communities and captains;
- military commanders do not automatically inherit political authority;
- coalition speakers lose mandate when the defined objective ends.

### Leader-loss response

- a famous commander’s death may split cells into martyr, pragmatic and localist paths;
- routes continue through separate operators;
- sanctuary obligations may outlive political alliances;
- public symbols can be adopted by factions the original leader opposed.

### Internal blocs

- Liberation Network;
- Federalist Autonomy;
- Localist Defense;
- Worker/Social movements;
- Scorched Denial radicals;
- smugglers and opportunist partners.

### Reader-facing recognition

- allies disagree over destination, capacity and long-term governance;
- people may support independence while rejecting its military cells;
- visual continuity comes from repair marks and route practice, not a single flag.

## 5. Free Route Neutral Assembly

### Geography and social base

- Kael Free Port, rescue anchorages, merchant corridors, hospital ships, insurance/claims offices and independent vessel cooperatives.

### Cultural register

- capacity, duration, guarantee and shipboard authority are stated precisely;
- duty role overrides nationality while aboard;
- gifts, food and personal habits mix multiple routes;
- neutrality is treated as an operating condition with costs, not moral purity.

### Visual language

- circular docking and arbitration spaces with multiple equally visible entrances;
- ship interiors show clear emergency routes and removable national/corporate signage;
- neutral custody seals use layered witness marks rather than one emblem;
- uniforms prioritize role bands, medical visibility and ship affiliation;
- vessels vary widely but share recognizable rescue lights, transponder protocol and protected deck geometry.

Avoid:
- all-white benevolent hospital aesthetic;
- neutral officers without commercial or crew interests;
- one central neutral navy.

### Core services

- rescue and medical custody;
- arbitration and claims;
- insurance and route access;
- trusted evidence chain;
- cross-border trade and transport;
- temporary protection for disputed people/assets.

### Coercive shadow

- capacity exclusion;
- fees and guarantee burdens;
- undocumented people treated as uninsurable;
- commercial priorities hidden as neutral rules;
- refusal of permanent responsibility.

### Principal offices

1. Member Assembly — ports, cooperatives, insurers, rescue and merchant blocs.
2. Rotating Convener — agenda and emergency coordination.
3. Route and Safety Board.
4. Claims/Insurance Chambers.
5. Medical and Humanitarian Board.
6. Captains/Masters — final authority aboard their vessels.
7. Local port councils and trustees.

### Succession chain

- convener replaced by scheduled rotation or emergency deputy;
- member organizations retain assets and votes;
- ship captains remain autonomous within covenant;
- claims and custody survive through mirrored ledgers;
- emergency mobilization expires unless member bodies ratify it.

### Leader-loss response

- death of a famous rescuer affects reputation but not captaincy across member ships;
- insurers and humanitarian blocs can appoint separate interim representatives;
- no central figure can order the whole merchant/rescue fleet.

### Internal blocs

- Humanitarian/Medical;
- Insurance/Claims;
- Merchant and Route;
- Security/Anti-Seizure;
- Memory/Archive;
- local port autonomists.

### Reader-facing recognition

- offers are phrased in capacity, time and conditions;
- neutral characters name current patients/crew before abstract ideals;
- visual signs make protected space clear but do not imply unlimited protection.

## 6. Academy / Educational-Correctional Network

### Geography and social base

- orbital academies, correctional schools, training yards, family guarantee systems and placement networks.

### Cultural register

- scores, cohort status, service credits and placement language;
- student culture mixes military, worker, detention and route slang;
- instructors often use practical names while punitive systems use numbers;
- success and coercion coexist visibly.

### Visual language

- rotating habitat rings, repurposed industrial equipment and sponsor-marked zones;
- student alterations and mutual-aid signs layered over official standardized surfaces;
- different ownership claims appear physically on equipment;
- ceremonies are modest because facilities remain working infrastructure.

### Core services

- literacy, health stabilization, citizenship paths, technical/military training, housing and placement.

### Coercive shadow

- linked family debt;
- dangerous missions;
- surveillance and classification;
- sponsor ownership and conditional freedom;
- selective protection.

### Offices and succession

1. Legal/correction authority;
2. Academy Director;
3. Education/Field/Medical/Research/Security heads;
4. Student/staff representation under charter;
5. external trustees and mission board;
6. instructors and cell supervisors.

- director vacancy passes to a bounded emergency board;
- field command does not inherit medical, legal or contract authority;
- student representatives have alternates and recall procedures;
- charter powers expire and require audit.

### Leader-loss response

- an instructor’s departure leaves records, harmed students and alumni network;
- the academy can continue with changed authority rather than become the hero’s private school.

## 7. Brann–Neris Worker and Standards Network

### Geography and social base

- Brann Yard, Neris Workshop Belt, ghost-caravan workers, independent shops, guilds and unions.

### Cultural register

- works / works once / maintainable / certifiable are distinct claims;
- maker and maintainer marks matter;
- safety histories and accident names remain in everyday language;
- work crews remember unpaid labor and invisible support.

### Visual language

- exposed service paths, maker marks, repaired structural colors and tool-specific clothing;
- halls display failed components and corrective notes, not only finished products;
- official certification plates coexist with local hand-stamped service lineage;
- communal spaces share heat, food and loud machinery rather than polished office quiet.

### Offices and succession

- yard management;
- certified safety guild;
- worker mutual/union;
- open repair cooperative;
- salvage houses;
- emergency standards council.

- council chairs rotate by issue and technical domain;
- chief engineers have named deputies and stop-authority successors;
- ownership and labor representation remain separate;
- a founder cannot permanently own the standard.

### Leader-loss response

- documentation and apprentices preserve skill;
- a death can expose hidden work or fragment a cooperative;
- rival workshops can continue compatible standards without one leader.

## 8. Ardis Joint-Trust Polity

### Geography and social base

- Anchor Crown, Civic Ring, Foundry Cylinder, Garden Chain, Transit Belt and Surface Works.

### Cultural register

- repair contribution, service priority and residency status are everyday politics;
- Restart Day and competing casualty lists show memory conflict;
- registered and transit populations use different institutions but share physical systems.

### Visual language

- multiple repair marks on every major system;
- civic spaces use circular tables with physically separated authority consoles;
- node spines retain different era/material identities rather than being cosmetically unified;
- public dashboards show service and capacity without exposing private identities;
- local flags/emblems remain district-based under one trust mark.

### Offices and succession

- Civic Council;
- Foundry Compact;
- Node Custodian College;
- Transit Assembly;
- Defense Command;
- external guarantor/creditor seats;
- joint trust board with domain-specific keys.

- no single successor inherits all trust powers;
- each constituency appoints replacements;
- technical stop passes through certified operator chain;
- emergency coordinator expires after a fixed window;
- defense commander remains bounded by civilian and district authority.

### Leader-loss response

- district institutions continue;
- trust may slow or deadlock but cannot be inherited personally;
- external factions exploit vacancies, creating recurring politics.

## 9. Fleet Mission Trust / Ownerless Fleet Network

### Geography and social base

- distributed ships, crews, yards, pay systems, local defense obligations and mission coalitions.

### Cultural register

- captains distinguish ship safety, mission command, owner claim and crew consent;
- loyalty is often to crew, route or contract before one state;
- battle honors include support and repair actions.

### Visual language

- ships retain original hull markings with temporary mission-trust bands;
- command displays show which authority is currently borrowed;
- crews carry ship-specific symbols rather than one uniform fleet style;
- support vessels are visually and ceremonially central, not background cargo boxes.

### Offices and succession

- mission commander;
- participating ship masters;
- logistics/repair/pay boards;
- crew representatives;
- civilian/political mandate holders;
- local commanders with withdrawal rights.

- mission command expires by objective/time;
- deputy commander inherits only if participating authorities confirm predefined conditions;
- ship captains retain surrender/abandon-ship safety authority;
- crew and local mandates survive commander death.

## 10. Aurel / Preservation Regime

### Geography and social base

- synchronized service regions, identity/medical networks, command nodes, preserved institutions and communities benefiting from stability.

### Cultural register

- confidence, eligibility, queue, review and service continuity replace overt ideological slogans;
- users experience the regime primarily through working hospitals, wages, routes and identity;
- reformers and hardliners share technical language but differ on appeal and permanence.

### Visual language

- interfaces emphasize calm continuity, current status and service availability;
- public spaces display synchronized clocks and service seals;
- regional variations remain but critical systems share a minimal interoperability layer;
- deferred or low-confidence cases appear as waiting, missing access or manual review—not theatrical punishment.

Avoid:
- black-uniform fascist shorthand;
- one red score over every person;
- obvious evil architecture.

### Core services

- medicine, pay, identity, route scheduling, emergency command and institutional restoration.

### Coercive shadow

- deferred populations;
- permission-based life;
- appeal erosion;
- forced synchronization;
- emergency ranking becoming durable social order.

### Offices and succession

- Aurel technical composite;
- human Continuity Office;
- domain service boards;
- regional trustees;
- review and correction bodies;
- enforcement/stabilization command;
- beneficiary councils and recognized AI/person representatives.

- Aurel is not a single ruler and cannot lawfully inherit every office;
- technical core failure passes to bounded domain fallbacks;
- human trustees and regional service boards continue;
- emergency central key has expiry and audit;
- independent person Arin Vale cannot be treated as automatic successor to the system.

### Leader-loss response

- removing a hardliner does not switch off services or erase classifications;
- reform and coercion continue through institutions;
- beneficiary communities may resist rapid dismantling.

## 11. Plural Record / Memory / AI Personhood Networks

### Geography and social base

- Palimpsest sites, archive assemblies, AI/composite communities, local memory institutions, patient-controlled records and right-to-forget groups.

### Cultural register

- source, consent, version, uncertainty and current effect are named;
- disagreement is preserved without claiming every version has equal operational use;
- AI persons distinguish copies, continuations, derivatives and institutions.

### Visual language

- layered translucent displays with visible provenance paths;
- physical mirrors and local artifacts coexist with digital systems;
- consent boundaries are spatially visible in archive rooms;
- AI persons choose presentation rather than sharing one holographic form.

### Offices and succession

- source custodians;
- elected/appointed archive assembly;
- correction and consent boards;
- technical translators;
- AI/personhood representatives;
- regional record institutions.

- no curator inherits universal access;
- deleted/withheld data cannot be recreated by office succession;
- custodianship passes with source/community consent;
- AI person rights survive institutional shutdown through independent identity standing.

## 12. Visual Separation Checklist

Before approving a faction/location design, verify:

- silhouette and spatial organization;
- dominant materials and maintenance condition;
- how authority appears physically;
- how ordinary people modify official space;
- how emergency routes are marked;
- food, clothing and personal storage;
- what is repaired versus replaced;
- how names and offices are displayed;
- how dissent appears;
- how the same culture looks in another faction.

Color is supplemental, not sufficient.

## 13. Succession Stress Test

For every major faction:

1. kill, remove or discredit the most visible leader;
2. identify who controls staff, money, keys, military force, public legitimacy and external recognition the next hour;
3. identify who controls them after one week and six months;
4. show at least two rival lawful claims;
5. preserve services that do not depend on the leader;
6. expose one hidden dependency or harmed constituency;
7. ensure protagonists cannot simply appoint the replacement.
