# 2. Decision A — Imperial Multi-Species Historical Model

## Problem

A true multi-species Empire needs a reason current institutions can be both:
- genuinely plural in CY 742;
- historically human-default in many standards and founding structures.

## Options

### A1 — Human expansion empire

Early human Empire later annexes/treaties nonhuman civilizations.

Pros:
- lowest retcon cost.

Cons:
- risks making other peoples appendices to human history.

### A2 — Multi-species founding covenant

Several species jointly found Empire.

Pros:
- plural from origin.

Cons:
- high conflict with Founding Covenant/dynasty/current human-default infrastructure.

### A3 — Service federation became Empire

Cross-species route/service alliance centralizes into imperial state.

Pros:
- thematically elegant.

Cons:
- too similar to GA10 end-state and needs major founding rewrite.

### A4 — Layered transformation

Early Imperial Core is human-dominant/human-led; several nonhuman civilizations already independently exist. Centuries of contact, war, treaty, migration and service integration transform the Empire into a genuinely multi-species state. Old human-default path dependence remains.

Pros:
- best continuity/depth balance;
- preserves dynasty/SP-07;
- allows independent alien history and later co-authorship.

Cons:
- requires nuanced historical chronology.

## Recommendation

> **A4 — Layered Transformation (IMP-D)**

## Ripple if approved

Would require controlled updates to:
- decision log;
- Imperial history authority;
- succession/founding-covenant interpretation;
- species contact chronology;
- language/standards history;
- selected military/technology-history notes;
- GA1 local-demography explanation.

Would NOT require:
- rewriting the GA1–GA10 macro plot;
- reclassifying existing characters;
- changing 612-system population totals.

### Author status

`HOLD — explicit approval required`

# 3. Decision B — Demographic Planning Model

## Problem

If nonhuman people are normal, their total presence must be large enough to matter but not so large that every current human-coded document becomes implausible.

## Options

### B1 — Human-heavy
- human/human-derived 68–76%;
- core nonhuman 18–25%;
- regional/rare 4–9%.

### B2 — Human-majority plural
- human/human-derived 50–60%;
- core nonhuman combined 30–40%;
- regional/rare 8–15%;
- no single nonhuman necessarily >10–12%.

### B3 — No biological majority
- human/human-derived 30–42%;
- nonhuman combined majority.

## Recommendation

> **B2 as planning architecture.**

Important:
- exact percentages are unnecessary now;
- keep ranges rather than canonized point estimates;
- AI/composite census treatment remains separate.

### Author status

`OPTIONAL NOW`

Safe default if unanswered:
- continue using B2 as `[ASSUMPTION]` only.

# 5. Decision D — Verin Ontology

## Problem

Verin currently occupy an engineered lineage slot.

Two coherent versions exist.

### D1 — Independent engineered nonhuman species

Their biological ancestry is not baseline humanity; a historical civilization deliberately engineered a stable people from nonhuman/unknown biological roots.

Pros:
- preserves seven genuine nonhuman slots;
- strongest creator/property/personhood theme.

Risk:
- creator-history complexity.

### D2 — Human-derived engineered clade

Verin descend from engineered humans and have diverged enough to need distinct medicine/social recognition.

Pros:
- strong posthuman continuity;
- lower exotic-origin burden.

Risk:
- overlaps posthuman bible and reduces genuine alien breadth.

## Recommendation

> **D1 is slightly stronger for the current species-codex goal**, provided the creator does not become a universal precursor civilization.

Alternative if user prefers stronger human/posthuman thematic focus:
- choose D2 and replace S1-08 with a different genuinely independent biological species later.

### Author status

`HOLD — needed before S1 roster canonization, not needed for current design work`

# 6. Decision E — Interspecies Reproduction Rule

## Problem

Universal biological interbreeding would be convenient but biologically flatten the galaxy and create hidden lineage/citizenship consequences everywhere.

## Options

### E1 — Universal compatibility

Reject.

### E2 — Mostly incompatible independent species; case-by-case assisted reproduction

- independent species usually cannot produce shared-genome offspring naturally;
- advanced reproductive medicine may create limited, regulated possibilities only where biology permits;
- human-derived clades have broader compatibility;
- adoption, guardianship and mixed households are normal regardless of genetic compatibility.

### E3 — No cross-species genetic reproduction at all

Pros:
- clean biology.

Cons:
- unnecessarily rigid for advanced medicine and future story flexibility.

## Recommendation

> **E2 — mostly incompatible, limited case-by-case medicine, family not defined by genetics.**

### Author status

`HOLD — high social/legal ripple if made canon`
