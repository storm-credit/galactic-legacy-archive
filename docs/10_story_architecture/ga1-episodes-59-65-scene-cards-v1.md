# Episode 60 — 공개하면 사라지는 사람

Date:
- CY 742-05-16.

Episode function:
- dramatize privacy versus accountability through specific current risks.
- show a leak/publication can harm patients and families.
- keep Serin from treating authentication as automatic publication.

## Scene 60.1 — The leak opportunity

POV:
- Rian with Serin.

Situation:
- enough process evidence exists to make a public accusation.
- publishing person-level fragments could force an external inquiry before records are sealed.

Benefits:
- prevent quiet transfers.
- bring families/advocates.
- weaken Research/Security control.

Risks:
- reveal protected patients, undocumented relatives and treatment weaknesses.
- void current identities/contracts.
- attract Imperial/Helix/Independence seizure teams.

## Scene 60.2 — Soma’s refusal

Soma:
- refuses publication of the old legal identity/name connection.
- permits current treatment-access and correction claim to be used.

Reason:
- former identity is tied to a contract/family/custody situation Soma does not want restored.

Serin:
- accepts a record that proves structural harm without proving Soma’s personal past to the public.

## Scene 60.3 — A family that wants everything

Another family/guardian requests full release/restoration for a patient.

Patient:
- disagrees or cannot currently consent cleanly.

Mia/clinician:
- separates emergency contact, legal standing and patient preference.

Conflict:
- family harm is real; refusing disclosure also perpetuates disappearance.

No universal rule solves all cases.

## Scene 60.4 — Limited publication

Decision:
- release/process-proof and aggregate/current harm through witnessed channels.
- keep person-level keys in consent-limited custody.
- create correction/appeal requests rather than full dump.

Cost:
- the public proof is less dramatic and easier for authorities to minimize.

End hook:
- someone outside the Ward uses the aggregate proof to identify likely protected patients anyway.

Reward:
- accountable but bounded evidence release.

Cost:
- inference/exposure risk remains.

# Episode 61 — 지워달라는 기록

Date:
- CY 742-05-17.

Episode function:
- make deletion/forgetting a real patient choice.
- force Rian/Archive to lose potentially valuable information.
- separate treatment continuity from permanent historical preservation.

## Scene 61.1 — Deletion petition

POV:
- Rian.

Patient request:
- delete an optional research/neural-behavior fragment after treatment use ends.
- preserve only minimum clinical safety information.

Research objection:
- data may prevent future injury and is tied to funded study/other patients.

Archive implication:
- fragment has high future significance or links to a hero/continuity set.

## Scene 61.2 — What can actually be deleted

Serin/ORA-3 distinguish:
- local copy.
- external Helix/Imperial research copy.
- derived model contribution.
- clinical minimum.
- audit proof that deletion occurred.

Truth:
- deleting one copy is not erasing all influence.
- claiming perfect forgetting would be false.

## Scene 61.3 — Rian’s opposition

Rian:
- argues the fragment may reveal future deaths/Seed/hero identity.
- frames preservation as protecting others.

Patient:
- asks whether their mind/body data belongs to every future person who could benefit.

Mia:
- supports preserving minimum safety and the patient’s current choice.

Haren:
- notes communities also depend on shared knowledge but cannot turn one body into permanent infrastructure.

## Scene 61.4 — Irrecoverable gap

Decision:
- patient-authorized local fragment deleted/locked beyond ordinary use.
- derived safety minimum retained and external copies challenged/audited.
- Archive set remains incomplete.

Rian consequence:
- loses a real strategic clue; no later secret backup restores it conveniently.

End hook:
- ORA-3 reports a separate protected transfer is scheduled before the new consent/oversight rules can apply.

Reward:
- first enforceable refusal/forgetting right.

Cost:
- permanent information loss and conflict with future utility.
