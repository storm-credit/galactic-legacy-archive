## 1. Grand-Act Contract

Working title:
- `두 번째 오르페우스`.

Range:
- Episodes 571–690.

Primary promise:

> P-001이 평생 다시 하겠다고 믿어온 선택을 실제로 다시 마주하지만, 이번에도 모두를 살리는 답은 없고 더 많은 사람과 기관이 결정권을 나눠 가진다.

Grand-act question:

> 당장의 민간 생명과 장기적인 국가·의료·군사 연속성이 같은 항로를 요구할 때, 누구를 어떤 기준으로 먼저 보내고 어떤 미래를 포기할 것인가?

Visible goals:

1. verify route, fleet, population and life-support facts without hiding uncertainty.
2. prevent State, civilian, Neutral, local node or hostile actors from seizing all windows.
3. distribute essential institutions into smaller ships/nodes.
4. execute mixed evacuation and holdout across A/B/C/Grey routes.
5. preserve enough defense and services for survivors and wider fronts.
6. accept public accountability and aftermath without declaring moral victory.

Final reward:
- more immediate lives and plural institutional functions survive than in original Orpheus.
- mixed/distributed evacuation proves GA1–5 infrastructure matters.
- Common Fleet Charter, route federation, open standards, plural records and local custody work together at civilizational scale.
- original Orpheus truth is substantially revealed.
- P-001 does not repeat centralized abandonment or use full Continuity override.

Final cost:
- hundreds of thousands killed, captured, missing or left at severe risk.
- major combat/support ships and institutions destroyed or dispersed.
- Compact central capacity and wider war position worsen.
- one front/region suffers later because resources were spent here.
- P-001’s choice remains condemnable from several perspectives.
- Archive may label the outcome historical/continuity loss despite higher immediate survival.

Control:
- foreground 5–8 recurring ships/communities/institutions and decision actors.

- State institutions contain civilians/hospitals and projected wider consequences.

### Risk: action fatigue across 30 episodes

Control:
- 6C divided into distinct windows/fronts and POV/scene objectives.
- recovery/decision beats between operations.
- not every episode ends on explosion.

### Risk: 120 episodes too long
