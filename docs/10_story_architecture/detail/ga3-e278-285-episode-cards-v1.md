# Episode 278 — 잠긴 냉장고 안의 약

Date:
- CY 742-12-28.

POV:
- Seva Lok, depot workers, Mia and Helix convoy service staff.

Visible goal:
- keep the certified cold-chain depot operating after the receivership credential activates its remote service lock.

Hidden pressure:
- disabling the lock may void certified support; accepting it gives convoy command control over current medicine and worker access.

## Scene units

1. **Lock state** — external credentials freeze outbound inventory, revoke three local scheduler roles and require convoy approval for maintenance changes; core cooling continues for now.
2. **Patient clock** — the depot feeds three hospitals and eleven mobile/Transit clinics; multiple therapies and emergency reserves cannot wait for the ownership dispute.
3. **Employee clause** — Seva invokes a patient-continuity clause allowing 12 hours of bounded local release and maintenance if every movement is audited.
4. **Split access** — current medicine leaves under local medical/service custody while title, payment and disputed inventory remain sealed.

Decisive choice:
- Seva uses Helix’s own service rule to keep patient supply moving without surrendering the whole depot.

Reward:
- immediate care continues and certified cooling remains online.

Cost:
- only 12 hours are secured; Helix finance/security marks Seva’s action for review and limits further credentials.

Hook:
- convoy command offers full medicine and part release if armed security frames receive dock/facility access and the provisional board recognizes receivership custody.

Reward:

Cost:
- security corvette and frames remain in coercive position; remote-control dispute continues; fees/debt accumulate.

Hook:
- Line 3’s certified contract band receives a remote work order that would bypass the essential-production protocol.

# Episode 282 — 가장 안전한 잠금이 만든 사고

Date:
- CY 743-01-01.

POV:
- Seva, Mia, depot technicians, hospital/mobile clinic receivers and Helix remote service.

Visible goal:
- stop a cooling/oxygen-transfer cascade caused by conflicting remote failover and local bypass states.

Hidden pressure:
- each action is individually safety-motivated; combined state is not represented in one current controller.

## Scene units

1. **Cascade** — remote lock shifts cooling to redundant loop; local bypass keeps the damaged trunk isolated; a shared service bus overheats and two distribution branches lose stable cooling/power.
2. **Patient/stock impact** — 74 patients require transfer or therapy change; 9 are critical; 6.4 t temperature-sensitive stock enters downgrade/quarantine review.
3. **Response conflict** — convoy service wants full remote reset; local teams know one valve remains physically jammed; Mia/Seva order manual branch isolation and patient-first transfer.
4. **Outcome** — no death, but one patient suffers permanent hypoxic neurological injury; 6 staff/technicians are injured, 2 seriously; part of the 6.4 t stock is unusable or uncertain pending test.

Decisive choice:
- preserve current patient transfer and physical branch isolation rather than force a fast full-system reset.

Reward:
- wider depot/hospital cascade is stopped and most patients/stock are protected.

Cost:
- permanent patient injury, staff injuries, medication loss, service outage and shared liability across remote lock, corridor damage, local bypass and incomplete current state.

Hard ruling:
- the accident is not proof that certification or local control alone is wrong; it is a mixed-authority/current-state failure.

Hook:
- convoy command orders Seva removed and full security custody; local Helix employees refuse to abandon the active care recovery.
