# Episode 287 — 방어를 위해 누구를 끊는가

Date:
- CY 743-01-06.

POV:
- Savo, Garden/Transit operators, defense safety staff and Varen.

Visible goal:
- calculate the current population and service cost if D-B or D-C is used during capture or cascade.

Hidden pressure:
- “denial” sounds military, but most direct costs fall on workers, patients, households and food/water systems.

## Scene units

1. **Current dependency map** — D-B would cut or degrade propellant/industrial power for 41,000–56,000 workers/residents and remove two transfer routes; D-C could isolate 3 Transit blocks, 2 Garden service modules and approximately 18,600 present people for 22–47 minutes before reserve limits become critical.
2. **Defense case** — Sella’s staff shows that losing the node/Anchor intact could expose far larger regional populations and routes.
3. **Service case** — Savo/Garden operators show that current evacuation/medical capability cannot guarantee the isolated population’s survival under fire or sabotage.
4. **No neutral simulation** — the board records outcome bands and unknowns; no one declares a morally clean threshold.

Decisive choice:
- affected service/user delegates gain mandatory current-consent and stop roles for any future D-C use; Defense retains the right to propose but not execute alone.

Reward:
- destruction cost becomes attached to named current constituencies and real reserve times.

Cost:
- denial response becomes slower and external military actors view the structure as unreliable.

Hook:
- Varen reveals that a third legacy condition exists: a service-preservation path inside Spine C that may isolate command without destroying every physical route.

# Episode 288 — 보존을 위한 파괴

Date:
- CY 743-01-07.

POV:
- Eda, Varen, Custodian technicians and local utility-frame teams.

Visible goal:
- inspect the claimed Spine C service-preservation path without treating it as a hidden master key.

Hidden pressure:
- Federalist and Custodian actors both gain power if they alone define what the old system means.

## Scene units

1. **Old interface** — Spine C branch contains separate fields for physical isolation, current service custody, command denial and later restoration evidence; the fields are incomplete and partly damaged.
2. **No master authority** — available records show at least four independent role classes and an expired external witness field; no single legacy office could both activate and own the result under current evidence.
3. **Physical verification** — local frames open a service panel and confirm that one path can disable remote command/authentication while preserving a low-bandwidth emergency service conduit.
4. **Limit** — conduit can support state/medical/emergency coordination, not normal traffic, fleet command or full city service.

Decisive choice:
- classify the path as a candidate local-custody/service-preservation mechanism and prohibit operational use until current authorities and affected-service conditions are defined.

Reward:
- denial is reinterpreted as potentially segmented command refusal plus service preservation, not only destruction.

Cost:
- external and local factions now know Spine C may protect a noncentral command architecture; seizure pressure rises.

Hook:
- an unannounced current test attempts to pair D-A isolation with the newly identified command-denial path.

Reward:

Cost:

Hook:
