# Episode 194 — 화면은 열림, 밸브는 닫힘

Date:
- CY 742-10-05, immediately before live load transfer.

POV:
- 오벨 나르 at the physical branch, with Serin/Nera receiving incomplete system data.

Visible goal:
- reconcile the mechanical indicator with the controller/relay state before transfer.

Hidden pressure:
- the schedule window is closing, and every other branch reports consistent readiness.

## Scene units

1. **State disagreement** — controller/Fork D reports branch `probable open`; relay shows high-confidence upstream/downstream consistency but flags missing direct branch state; mechanical indicator remains unmoved.
2. **Physical check** — Ovel requests local isolation and direct inspection; platform operator reports earlier manual test moved correctly; overnight reconnect/cache record is incomplete.
3. **Partial movement** — low-pressure command makes the indicator shift late, apparently resolving disagreement; downstream sensor follows after delay.
4. **Red-tag dispute** — sponsor/operator ask Ovel to clear the hold; he clears only the low-load pretest condition and requires confirmation before full transfer.

Decisive choice:
- proceed to a reduced transfer stage while retaining Ovel’s manual stop and no full certification claim.

Immediate result:
- initial cooling/service flow appears stable.

Reward:
- public test enters actual operation and generates real mixed-system data.

Cost:
- unknown queued state and sensor placement remain unresolved; schedule pressure continues.

Carried state:
- Fork D cached state is stale;
- physical branch response lags the interface;
- full buffer/medical/route load has not yet transferred.

Final hook:
- the controller declares synchronization complete while the platform’s return-line temperature begins rising outside the predicted sequence.

# Episode 195 — 열린 규격의 사고

Date:
- CY 742-10-05.

POV:
- distributed operational information through Ovel, Mia, Nera and platform responders; no spectacle-only viewpoint.

Visible goal:
- stop the unexpected pressure/temperature rise and protect people/service loads.

Hidden pressure:
- automatic rollback uses the same stale branch-state model and can worsen the physical condition.

## Scene units

1. **Divergence** — platform buffer and route-tug commands transfer load; rebuilt bypass remains partly closed while interface reports open; pressure rises upstream of the downstream sensor.
2. **Warning loss** — relay receives contradictions but Fork D downgrades `unknown/hold` to supervised continuation; schedule/operator display emphasizes synchronized state.
3. **Manual stop attempt** — Ovel activates physical isolation and directs nearby workers away; queued local command moves a second valve before the manual state propagates.
4. **Rupture and release** — filter body/line ruptures; hot/chemical coolant and debris strike the service deck; Ovel shields/assists a nearby operator while completing a partial manual stop; platform emergency barriers and 07/service teams isolate remaining flow.

Locked casualty outcome:
- Ovel Nar: right forearm/hand lost beyond simple local biological restoration; respiratory/chemical injury with long-term endurance limits;
- 3 serious nonpermanent injuries;
- 11 minor exposure/impact injuries;
- no confirmed immediate death.

Service outcome:
- medical auxiliary/current patients protected through independent isolation but transfer service stops;
- one medical shipment loses temperature control and is partly spoiled;
- route/cargo/medical transfers halted;
- platform branch closed.

Decisive choice:
- responders prioritize people and physical isolation, cutting through/contaminating some demonstration hardware and evidence rather than preserving the installation.

Reward:
- larger pressure cascade and mass casualty prevented.

Cost:
- permanent injury, service loss, destroyed components, public confidence collapse and expanded sanctions.

Collection/loss state:
- Ovel becomes named irreversible injury/aftermath obligation, not a heroic standard mascot;
- no usable accident-derived upgrade.

Carried state:
- injured care, platform continuity, evidence preservation and immediate deployment freeze.

Final hook:
- the first synchronized logs disagree on whether the system ever entered an unknown state at all.

# Episode 196 — 구조가 끝나도 서비스는 돌아오지 않는다

Date:
- CY 742-10-05 to 742-10-06.

POV:
- 미아/플랫폼 civil-defense operators.

Visible goal:
- stabilize casualties, prevent secondary service collapse and preserve enough evidence for a fair inquiry.

Hidden pressure:
- every minute spent preserving hardware/logs competes with reopening medical/cargo routes and cleaning contamination.

## Scene units

1. **Triage/evacuation** — Ovel and three serious casualties moved through current medical capacity; minor exposures decontaminated and monitored; no instant prosthetic resolution.
2. **Platform service triage** — medical loads rerouted first, then critical life-support cargo; commercial/ordinary traffic halted; one shipment partly spoils.
3. **Evidence conflict** — Fork D controller, relay logs, physical valve and damaged filter body preserved where possible; some parts cut/destroyed during rescue.
4. **Deployment freeze** — O0 documentation remains, all new O1/O2 operational deployments suspended pending review; existing critical systems enter individual risk assessment rather than blind shutdown.

Decisive choice:
- keep current critical systems alive under local/physical supervision while freezing expansion, instead of disconnecting every open-profile system.

Reward:
- no secondary casualty cascade and evidence chain retained.

Cost:
- branch closed 9–16 days; route/service delays; workshops/users face uncertainty and lost income.

Relationship state:
- Mia/medical actors prioritize Ovel’s rehabilitation and current patients separately from technical publicity.

Carried state:
- cause attribution and liability; Ovel’s consent/data; current stranded services.

Final hook:
- the base release logs retain the `unknown` warning, but the demonstration controller’s displayed history does not.

# Episode 197 — 하나의 범인을 원하는 사람들

Date:
- CY 742-10-07.

POV:
- 세린/inquiry panel with Ovel participating only within current medical limits and consent.

Visible goal:
- reconstruct the accident without allowing one convenient culprit to erase system responsibility.

Hidden pressure:
- Helix, sponsors, fork vendor, platform, workshops and open-service advocates each need a narrower cause.

## Scene units

1. **Fork manipulation evidence** — vendor deliberately downgraded the unknown-state hold and shortened supervised waiting to improve throughput/market claim; current responsible individuals/company identified.
2. **Independent causes** — missing branch documentation, field-reversed label, downstream sensor placement, incomplete training, overnight cache state and schedule compression existed before/around the manipulation.
3. **Institutional benefits/harms** — Helix withdrawal was not sufficient to prevent unsafe local adoption; sponsor/public pressure accelerated it; workers/users lacked mature version/stop enforcement.
4. **Ovel’s testimony boundary** — he provides operational facts and current needs, refuses marketing/use of private rehabilitation data, and rejects “heroic sacrifice” framing.

Decisive choice:
- publish a multi-cause preliminary report with confidence levels; impose sanctions on the vendor but do not close inquiry as solved by sabotage.

Reward:
- direct manipulation exposed and responsibility distributed according to action/capacity.

Cost:
- no emotionally simple answer; legal/compensation disputes widen.

Mystery state:
- no secret conspiracy or new core mystery; accident remains materially explained.

Carried state:
- standards/governance redesign and compensation fund.

Final hook:
- several remote habitats report they cannot wait for the general freeze because their current certified parts have already failed.

# Episode 198 — 멈출 권리가 일자리보다 먼저인가

Date:
- CY 742-10-08 to 742-10-10.

POV:
- worker/user/medical/insurer/Helix/open-service governance panel.

Visible goal:
- design a governance and deployment process that can preserve emergency access without repeating the accident.

Hidden pressure:
- strict stop/qualification rules can remove jobs and service from small operators; weak rules transfer injury risk to workers/users.

## Scene units

1. **Seat structure** — worker/maintainer, user/operator, medical/service-dependent, insurer/safety, vendor/Helix and open-workshop roles; no one holds majority command.
2. **Stop protection** — current operator/worker may stop a branch without automatic future blacklisting or wage loss; misuse still reviewable.
3. **Version/training rules** — signed provenance, fork registry, physical sensor-placement diagram, role qualification, cache-reset/hold verification and incident fund.
4. **Ovel’s continuing role** — he accepts a user-safety review seat with accommodations and no assumption he resumes field work; replacement/rehabilitation decisions remain his.

Decisive choice:
- create a temporary `Mixed-Service Safety Board` and accident/compensation fund, with deployment levels O0–O3 and right-to-stop enforcement.

Reward:
- operational governance beyond code and one company.

Cost:
- higher fees, staffing, slower releases and exclusion risk for small sites; some services remain closed.

Relationship state:
- Ovel retains agency beyond victim status; worker/user seats are not represented by Nera/Rian alone.

Carried state:
- define emergency reauthorization for habitats that cannot wait.

Final hook:
- the first emergency application comes from a site where the open profile is safer than the damaged certified controller, but neither insurer will accept responsibility.
