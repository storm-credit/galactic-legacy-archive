# Episode 248 — 불탄 방에 살았다는 증명

Date:
- CY 742-11-29.

POV:
- Della Wen, Savo Nir, displaced households and Civic housing staff.

Visible goal:
- place people displaced from the fire blocks without inventing beds or erasing irregular residents.

Hidden pressure:
- the building roster records leases and official occupants, not caregivers, rotating workers, children between households or sealed residents.

## Scene units

1. **Three counts** — 167 confirmed current occupants, 24 additional habitual-use claims and 9 disputed/duplicate claims compete for 132 immediately available replacement berths.
2. **Physical proof** — medicine lockers, work-shift logs, school pickups, utility use and neighbor testimony establish current habitation without requiring one perfect identity record.
3. **Priority conflict** — medical vulnerability, dependent children, night-shift proximity and current fire loss produce different rankings; no one list satisfies all.
4. **Split placement** — 132 receive immediate berths, 35 enter paid temporary dorm/ship-hostel arrangements and 24 receive day-access/storage/transport support while claims continue; disputed claims remain individually reviewed.

Decisive choice:
- Della accepts current-use evidence and appeal instead of lease-only clearance, while refusing to declare all claimants permanent residents.

Reward:
- no displaced person is left without a documented current path.

Cost:
- household separation, longer travel, temporary fees and registered wait-list delay become visible.

Hook:
- twelve children placed outside their original district are denied school entry because service standing does not equal school residence.

# Episode 249 — 학교 문에서 시작되는 도시

Date:
- CY 742-11-30.

POV:
- displaced children/caregivers, school staff, Mia and Civic families already waiting.

Visible goal:
- keep children in school during temporary relocation without erasing capacity and care constraints.

Hidden pressure:
- opening places for one group displaces transport, meal and support capacity already promised to registered families.

## Scene units

1. **Door refusal** — twelve displaced children and four existing provisional-service children lack the correct district residence code despite current presence and prior attendance.
2. **Capacity facts** — one school has 11 classroom places but only 7 meal/transport/support slots; another has space but is 74 minutes away by current Transit–Civic route.
3. **Family conflict** — registered caregivers oppose queue jumps because their own children have waited for disability support and safe transport.
4. **Service solution** — sixteen children receive temporary attendance rights; seven receive full support immediately, five use rotating transport/meal funding and four remain in supervised remote/community study pending capacity.

Decisive choice:
- grant temporary attendance as a service right while preserving separate support queues and public displacement accounting.

Reward:
- no child is excluded solely because the fire changed an address code.

Cost:
- support remains unequal; registered families lose some schedule priority and oppose expansion.

Hook:
- Transit utility crews show that 280 workers maintaining fire, water and pressure systems still lack legal work/rescue access after their shifts end.

# Episode 250 — 도시를 고치지만 도시에 없는 사람들

Date:
- CY 742-12-01.

POV:
- Savo, Transit utility workers, Surface/Anchor service managers and Haren.

Visible goal:
- create legal work and emergency-response standing for workers whose current records do not grant ordinary district access.

Hidden pressure:
- service access can become coerced labor: the city may recognize people only while they keep dangerous systems running.

## Scene units

1. **Current roster** — 280 workers across water, pressure, fire, sanitation and cable response; 91 have provisional service standing, 63 have conflicting records and 126 rely on shift-specific passes.
2. **After-shift trap** — one pressure worker cannot access family housing after a schedule change; another can enter a fire zone but not the adjacent clinic.
3. **Terms** — service residency must include wages, injury claims, ordinary emergency/medical access and the right to stop/leave work; dependents are not labor collateral.
4. **Pilot expansion** — 280 workers and 412 named dependents/household members enter a 30-day service-residency cohort with appeal and independent welfare review.

Decisive choice:
- recognize workers and defined households as current residents for services, not merely as labor credentials.

Reward:
- utility coverage and fire response stabilize; workers gain ordinary access and claims.

Cost:
- 692 people add to overloaded service and housing queues; some managers lose shift-pass leverage.

Hook:
- 73 potential participants refuse the required identity linkage or household disclosure, including people with credible safety reasons.

# Episode 251 — 등록하지 않을 권리

Date:
- CY 742-12-02.

POV:
- Serin, Savo, private/sealed households and security reviewers.

Visible goal:
- preserve service access for people who cannot or will not enter a unified identity/household registry.

Hidden pressure:
- anonymous or sealed access can hide exploitation, duplicate claims or security intrusion.

## Scene units

1. **Refusal types** — witness/security protection, abusive-family separation, employer retaliation, conflicting old identities, ideological refusal and deliberate fraud cannot be treated as one category.
2. **Layered proof** — local custodian confirms current person and service need; sealed emergency contact and welfare reviewer hold different data; public systems receive a revocable service token, not full identity.
3. **Limits** — high-risk node/defense authorization still requires stronger identity and role proof; ordinary medical/fire/food/sanitation access does not.
4. **Choice split** — 49 of 73 enter sealed-proof service access, 11 choose ordinary registration after protections improve, 8 remain outside nonemergency programs and 5 claims are paused for material inconsistency.

Decisive choice:
- the city accepts federated/sealed proof for ordinary service while keeping higher authorization thresholds separate.

Reward:
- vulnerable people gain access without one central identity surrender.

Cost:
- slower review, recurring local-custodian labor and incomplete fraud/security prediction.

Hook:
- Civic wait-list groups publish the exact services and places they lost or delayed after the pilot expansion.

# Episode 252 — 먼저 등록한 사람들의 분노

Date:
- CY 742-12-03.

POV:
- registered Civic residents, Della, service workers and trial delegates.

Visible goal:
- confront registered-resident backlash without dismissing it as prejudice or allowing collective punishment.

Hidden pressure:
- some leaders exploit fear, but the queue and tax/service losses are real.

## Scene units

1. **Cost ledger** — 38 registered housing placements delayed, 27 school-support schedules changed and two clinic queues lengthened after emergency/provisional expansion.
2. **Public testimony** — registered families describe years of payment, waiting and compliance; Transit residents describe exclusion while operating the same services.
3. **Bad proposal** — one populist bloc demands status suspension for all duplicate/sealed records; another demands instant equal citizenship without capacity plan.
4. **Budget/appeal response** — trial funds temporary transport, adds evening clinic/school service and publishes who bears each delay; no group receives cost-free priority.

Decisive choice:
- preserve provisional access while compensating and reviewing displaced registered claims individually rather than ranking whole populations.

Reward:
- immediate service conflict is reduced and registered losses remain politically visible.

Cost:
- budget margin falls; populist and immediate-citizenship blocs both grow.

Hook:
- district delegates must decide whether the temporary access structure receives representation or remains an administrative program without voice.

# Episode 253 — 시민이 아닌 대표

Date:
- CY 742-12-04.

POV:
- Transit/service cohorts, Civic delegates, Foundry observers and Haren.

Visible goal:
- define temporary representation for people affected by service decisions without granting or denying permanent citizenship by shortcut.

Hidden pressure:
- one Transit Assembly cannot represent workers, sealed households, temporary traders, children, caregivers and people who prefer private contracts.

## Scene units

1. **Cohort map** — emergency-only users, 30-day service residents, 180-day provisional residents, registered residents and contract-only workers have overlapping but different interests.
2. **Delegate method** — service-area and district contacts are selected by affected current users/workers, with rotation, recall and appeal; no delegate can sign citizenship or identity changes for competent adults.
3. **Package choice** — centralized-contract workers may select their own service contact and are not excluded for choosing Helix support.
4. **Trial amendment** — two Transit/service delegate seats and one registered-wait-list review seat join the 30-day pact for service, housing and access matters only.

Decisive choice:
- create operational representation without pretending it resolves citizenship or city constitution.

Reward:
- defined groups can alter service decisions and challenge unequal delivery.

Cost:
- voting and review slow further; excluded blocs prepare legal/political challenge.

Hook:
- during the first delegate-reviewed node-access roster, two physically different people resolve to the same legacy identity credential.

# Episode 254 — 같은 이름으로 열린 문

Date:
- CY 742-12-05.

POV:
- Serin, Toma, node security operators and the two affected people.

Visible goal:
- contain a duplicate-identity authorization incident without using it to cancel provisional standing for thousands.

Hidden pressure:
- one duplicate credential has already entered the B-3 authentication diagnostic chain.

## Scene units

1. **Duplicate resolution** — a living Transit maintenance worker and an externally contracted technician share a legacy credential lineage after employer registry reuse and later copy migration; neither person is automatically fake.
2. **Physical consequence** — one B-3 diagnostic door opens for 41 seconds under combined service/legacy authorization; a sealed tool cart moves into the access bay and one current inspection key is copied or exposed.
3. **Containment** — local operators close the physical branch, revoke the specific token lineage and preserve both persons’ ordinary service access while high-risk authorization is paused.
4. **Evidence split** — the contract technician used a valid current assignment; the Transit worker did not initiate the access. A third use of the copied lineage is probable but not yet attributed.

Decisive choice:
- isolate the compromised authorization chain rather than suspending all sealed/provisional identities or handing B-3 to one central authority.

Reward:
- no node command or weapon access is lost; both affected people retain personhood and appeal.

Cost:
- B-3 diagnostics pause, the first Spine B work window slips and security backlash strengthens centralized-control demands.

Subact result:
- provisional service residency operates through housing, school, medical, work and emergency access;
- defined Transit/service delegates gain limited representation;
- registered losses and backlash remain real;
- duplicate identity creates a current security crisis without proving inclusion itself was wrong.

Final hook:
- Spine B’s next thermal alignment window arrives in five days; without B-3 diagnostics, local repair must choose between a slower multi-signature bypass and the Imperial/Helix temporary command key.
