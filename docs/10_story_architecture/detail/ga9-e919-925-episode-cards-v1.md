# Episode 919 — 선택하지 않으면 비싸지는 선택

Date:
- CY 746-11-06 to 11-10.

Goal:
- distinguish legal noncoercion from practical compulsion.

Evidence:
- outsider insurance premiums rise 18–46% depending on route;
- coalition-certified repair parts receive earlier allocation;
- two neutral carriers lose contracts because clients require coalition verification;
- outsider medical transfers remain possible but slower and more expensive;
- no coalition order directly compels accession.

Decision:
- classify these as dependency and market-pressure effects requiring disclosure and mitigation, not proof that every accession is invalid.

Reward:
- voluntary participation is assessed against real alternatives.

Cost:
- coalition members resist subsidizing systems that refuse common obligations.

Hook:
- Aven challenges the federation settlement that suspended central functions while permitting the coalition to rebuild them by treaty.

# Episode 920 — 금지된 기능을 조약으로 되살릴 수 있는가

Date:
- CY 746-11-11 to 11-15.

Goal:
- test the conflict between the Seed Functional Separation Settlement and coalition treaty authority.

Aven’s position:
- members may jointly authorize functions they individually possess;
- common credentials are needed for the demonstrated gains;
- treaty exit and audit distinguish the coalition from unilateral Seed power.

Federation/affected response:
- distributed signatures do not cure merged practical power;
- service dependence may make exit nominal;
- score reuse can recreate citizenship and culture classification without saying so.

Interim ruling:
- treaty key remains lawful within narrow operational functions;
- secondary status use, opaque cross-domain reuse and unreviewable dependence are prohibited;
- expansion requires new affected-party approval.

Reward:
- useful coordination survives.

Cost:
- the coalition must accept slower and more visible controls.

Hook:
- a live escort dispute requires a named commander without transferring the key.

# Episode 921 — 열쇠 없는 지휘

Date:
- CY 746-11-16 to 11-19.

Goal:
- demonstrate Rian’s bounded command under the new system.

Mission:
- escort three medical convoys through a corridor where coalition schedules, outsider traffic and local defense overlap.

Rian’s authority:
- named mission command for ships that accepted the order;
- no credential issuance or cancellation;
- no control over civilian receiving systems;
- no override of local physical or medical stops;
- no central continuity key.

Decision:
- Rian sequences escorts using coalition facts but accepts one local delay and one outsider refusal.

Reward:
- all three convoys arrive; no ship is seized or destroyed.

Cost:
- one delivery is six hours late and coalition commentators blame the absence of unified command.

Hook:
- the delayed ship’s local stop proves justified when a hidden structural fault is found.

# Episode 922 — 늦춘 사람이 맞았을 때

Date:
- CY 746-11-20 to 11-22.

Goal:
- preserve current local stop authority against performance pressure.

Incident:
- a local engineer stops a high-priority transport for a nonstandard frame-coupling anomaly;
- coalition confidence score marks the vessel low risk;
- inspection finds a fault that could have caused pressure loss during transfer.

Decision:
- classify the stop as justified and require local-current evidence to update coalition models.

Reward:
- the system learns without punishing the person who slowed it.

Cost:
- operators demand clearer standards for when local expertise can override high-confidence schedules.

Hook:
- a consolidated mortality and harm ledger reveals that the controlled region is safer overall but not harmless.

# Episode 924 — 완벽함을 평가하는 표

Date:
- CY 746-11-28 to 12-02.

Goal:
- conduct the first 90-day operational review.

Review fields:
- piracy and security;
- delay and receiving capacity;
- shortage and maintenance;
- insurance, pay and family service;
- credential accuracy and class-specific errors;
- appeals and claimant support;
- exit and dependency costs;
- outsider displacement and cooperation;
- local stop and current-evidence protection.

Decision:
- retain the treaty key for the remainder of the 180-day term;
- freeze expansion into citizenship, reproduction, culture and technology approval;
- require portability, class-specific audit and urgent-impact review;
- add two conditional participants but no forced accession.

Participation at review:
- 15 full;
- 4 conditional;
- 2 observers;
- 3 outside/reject;
- total 24.

Reward:
- the coalition earns continued authority through measured performance.

Cost:
- the system’s practical scope keeps growing even while legal scope is frozen.

Hook:
- leaders begin publicly naming the controlled network `완벽한 항로 / Perfect Route`.

# Episode 925 — 완벽한 항로

Date:
- CY 746-12-03 to 12-05.

Goal:
- close the first GA9 batch with a real success and a clear administrative threat.

Final B09-01 results:
- 24 service nodes and 37 corridors remain in the planning envelope;
- treaty key active on 31 corridors;
- 18.6m current service-dependent people;
- 17.94m receive regular minimum service;
- 520k remain conditional/manual/appeal dependent;
- 140k remain severe, uncertain or prolonged-disruption;
- 17.94m + 520k + 140k = 18.6m;
- deaths are included within the affected categories.

Performance:
- piracy events 214 reference to 61 matched actual;
- high-severity losses 47 to 11;
- median delay 31.4h to 9.2h;
- shortage burden 18.6 to 6.1 days per 100k service-days;
- maintenance backlog 23.8% to 8.7%;
- insurance settlement 29 to 8 days.

Legal state:
- participation remains plural and bounded;
- no galaxy-wide key;
- no Rian key;
- no personhood/citizenship/culture/reproduction/technology authority;
- administrative scores nevertheless shape access through markets and reused credentials.

Reward:
- the Perfect Route is genuinely safer, faster and more reliable.

Cost:
- people and communities with low scores, missing records or nonstandard forms increasingly live in the slow lane.

Final hook:
- E926 opens when a low-score community loses route access, legal identity recognition and reproductive/technical permissions through several formally separate administrative decisions that share the same continuity profile.
