# Episode 691 — 같은 증거로 닫히고 열린 길

Date:
- CY 744-12-19.

Goal:
- establish why two receiving regions using the same Orpheus evidence issue opposite route decisions.

Region A:
- closes after medical, housing and security load exceeds current safe limit.

Region B:
- keeps a quota corridor open because cutting medicine, workers and family contact would cause greater downstream loss.

Decision:
- Common Fact successors record current capacity, affected people, duration, exceptions and appeal rather than label one region loyal and the other criminal.

Reward:
- denial becomes a current decision with reasons and consequences.

Cost:
- neither choice is safe; people and cargo divert toward already weak routes.

Hook:
- 230k holdout ledger shows 93k people have less than 72 hours of critical supply under current closures.

# Episode 692 — 남은 사람들의 사흘

Date:
- CY 744-12-20.

Goal:
- establish holdout supply clocks and who can legally/physically move aid.

Holdout clocks:
- 93,000 under 72-hour critical medicine/oxygen/food/power risk;
- 82,000 with 4–7 day current reserve;
- 55,000 with 8–21 day reserve but weak defense/contact.

Decision:
- prioritize current emergency supply/contact corridors without converting them into evacuation priority or outside command.

Reward:
- holdouts are treated as living communities and operators, not failed passengers.

Cost:
- aid ships and escorts are removed from missing-person search and arrived-region stabilization.

Hook:
- one local technical closure blocks the safest route to 18,400 critical holdouts.

# Episode 693 — 안전해서 닫힌 길

Date:
- CY 744-12-21.

Goal:
- test a physically justified local closure against urgent regional need.

Finding:
- damaged relay/thermal trunk has a 22–31% failure risk under heavy convoy load;
- opening it could collapse three neighboring local systems;
- the alternate route adds 19 hours and hostile exposure.

Decision:
- local operators maintain the heavy-load stop but allow small medical/contact craft under current physical challenges.

Reward:
- major infrastructure cascade is avoided and 4,600 critical supply equivalents move.

Cost:
- 13,800 people remain on alternate/late paths; 37 die or suffer irreversible treatment loss before substitute supply arrives.

Hook:
- families call the closure a death sentence while military actors cite sabotage risk on the alternate route.

# Episode 694 — 적을 막는 봉쇄

Date:
- CY 744-12-22.

Goal:
- distinguish military/security denial from collective punishment.

Threat:
- hostile recovery/route-denial remnants use missing-person and supply traffic as cover;
- two captured ships transmit valid distress codes under coercion.

Decision:
- Sira/Iven/current defense actors establish inspection, escort and weapons-separation conditions rather than a total closure.

Reward:
- one hostile boarding group is isolated and seven aid/search ships pass.

Cost:
- 11-hour delay, one small rescue craft damaged 2–4 months and six injured, one serious.

Hook:
- insurers and secured creditors refuse coverage/release for dozens of damaged ships even after security clearance.

# Episode 695 — 보험이 닫은 항로

Date:
- CY 744-12-23.

Goal:
- expose creditor/insurance denial as different from physical or military safety.

Current effect:
- 38 major/small service ships cannot depart because title, salvage, liability and family-compensation coverage conflict;
- 12 carry medicine/repair cargo already allocated to holdout/receiving systems.

Decision:
- create a temporary mission lien and current-victim/crew priority without voiding every legitimate creditor claim.

Reward:
- 17 ships receive bounded release for named missions.

Cost:
- 21 remain grounded; creditors lose security and workers/victims still lack full compensation.

Hook:
- a political authority orders a route closure not for capacity or security, but to force recognition and payment.

# Episode 696 — 충성을 요구한 통행증

Date:
- CY 744-12-24 to 12-25.

Goal:
- test a recognition-based denial that conditions medicine, pay and family passage on political allegiance.

Order:
- route access requires recognition of one Compact/federation/State authority and transfer of local authentication control.

Decision:
- Haren, Messa, Meira and affected bodies separate urgent services/earned pay/family contact from political recognition;
- refuse the master-control transfer while offering current audit and reciprocal obligations.

Reward:
- emergency medicine and 6,200 family/contact movements pass without compelled recognition.

Cost:
- ordinary commercial and nonurgent traffic remains blocked; the issuing authority retains a lawful political dispute.

Hook:
- the first seven days show 1.14m arrived people also depend on cross-region systems that can fail through closure.

# Episode 697 — 도착한 사람도 고립된다

Date:
- CY 744-12-26 to 12-27.

Goal:
- map arrived-region dependencies and prevent “arrived” from becoming “safe and complete.”

Current dependency set:
- approximately 1.14m of 2.57m arrived people depend within 14 days on cross-region medicine, food, power, payroll/identity, family contact or technical support;
- several receiving regions counted the same supply/bed/work capacity twice.

Decision:
- add arrived-service dependency to route emergency planning alongside holdout, captured/unstable and missing claims.

Reward:
- route policy sees downstream service deaths and not only transit passengers.

Cost:
- corridor demand exceeds available ships, escorts and receiving capacity; every opening can overload another region.

Final hook:
- E698 begins the first dependency cascades as closures propagate through food, medicine, identity, work and family systems.
