# Episode 724 — 한 사람이 동시에 있을 수 없는 곳

Date:
- CY 745-02-25 to 02-27.

Goal:
- test whether one hidden person directly authored and commanded all 29 strong-match denials.

Finding:
- 11 decision pairs overlap in time beyond plausible communication/review windows;
- signers and staff were physically separated across multiple route regions;
- several packet assemblies began before later alleged central approvals.

Decision:
- reject the hypothesis that one person personally drafted, reviewed and issued every strong-match order.

What remains open:
- one person may have authored doctrine, selected key staff, approved a subset or later become the symbolic owner.

Reward:
- the investigation stops searching for an impossible omnipresent admiral.

Cost:
- public/victim groups fear the finding will dissolve responsibility into bureaucracy.

Hook:
- office-cluster analysis shows three institutional families repeatedly exchange staff and models.

# Episode 725 — 세 개의 사무실이 만든 한 목소리

Date:
- CY 745-02-28 to 03-01.

Goal:
- test whether one central office generated the shared signature.

Institutional clusters inside the 29 strong matches:
- military/route-security planning: 9;
- Continuity/service-preservation and authentication: 7;
- provincial/local emergency offices: 6;
- hybrid staff/contract/temporary command assemblies: 7;
- total 29.

Finding:
- no one office controls all clusters;
- staff rotations, shared emergency manuals and cross-certified model services connect them.

Decision:
- classify office responsibility by model adoption, staff supervision, key custody, current review and final implementation.

Reward:
- institutional pathways become visible without inventing a master headquarters.

Cost:
- office leaders can blame shared doctrine or local implementation; victims face diffuse accountability.

Hook:
- the shared doctrine/tool also appears in decisions that prevented real infrastructure or military cascades.

# Episode 726 — 사람을 죽인 계산이 사람을 살린 경우

Date:
- CY 745-03-02 to 03-04.

Goal:
- test whether the shared risk model itself is inherently criminal or useless.

Control cases:
- 8 partial/control decisions using related fields prevent verified relay, epidemic, hostile-entry or receiving-system cascades;
- they include current exceptions, shorter horizons, local evidence and residual duties absent from many strong matches.

Finding:
- predictive denial can save lives and infrastructure;
- harm increases when downstream horizons narrow, omitted groups remain invisible, escalation is automatic or appeal/exception disappears.

Decision:
- retain the doctrine/tool as evidence and a potentially legitimate instrument; investigate governance and use rather than demand deletion.

Reward:
- central/predictive-denial benefit remains credible.

Cost:
- simple abolition is not available; future institutions must govern a dangerous useful tool.

Hook:
- credential analysis reveals repeated copying and delegated signing across formally separate decisions.

# Episode 731 — 아직 피의 제독이 아니다을 실시간으로 막다

Date:
- CY 745-03-13 to 03-14.

Goal:
- apply the investigation to a current high-risk denial before harm occurs.

Current proposal:
- close a receiving corridor under a legitimate overload/security model;
- packet omits two downstream hospitals, unregistered workers and a 36-hour residual-duty obligation;
- automatic escalation would extend closure beyond the local physical need.

Decision:
- retain the physical/security closure, add current exceptions, shorten duration, publish downstream effects and require residual missions.

Outcome:
- infrastructure overload avoided;
- 18,600 dependent people receive substitute medicine/power support;
- no confirmed death from the corrected decision;
- two missions delayed 7–12 hours.

Reward:
- signature analysis proves useful before final attribution.

Cost:
- faster central automation is slowed by review; officials argue the correction may fail under wartime time pressure.

Hook:
- public accounts still credit a single “Blood Admiral” with both the original danger and the corrected decision.

Cost:
