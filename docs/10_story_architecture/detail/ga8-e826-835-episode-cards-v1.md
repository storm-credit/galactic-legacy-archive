# Episode 826 — 네 개의 문이 같은 방으로 열린다

Date:
- CY 746-03-01 to 03-04.

Goal:
- open a common evidence chamber where the four founding-history networks must state not only what happened, but what present right or debt they claim follows.

Claims entered:
1. Imperial Continuity — lawful emergency-state reserve and continuity office;
2. Independence Exodus — refugee-built autonomous sanctuary;
3. Helix Contract Founding — licensed infrastructure financed and maintained through contract;
4. Neutral Custodial — multi-party trust with no complete owner.

Decision:
- every claim must identify current affected parties, requested remedy, omitted evidence and translation mode.

Reward:
- history becomes testable against present service, residence, AI personhood, contract and custody consequences.

Cost:
- four networks reject a single common burden of proof and begin parallel public campaigns.

Hook:
- Imperial records contain authentic state-reserve seals that predate the oldest Independence settlement register.

# Episode 827 — 제국은 실제로 사람을 살렸다

Date:
- CY 746-03-05 to 03-09.

Goal:
- test the Imperial Continuity history without reducing it to propaganda.

Authentic evidence:
- emergency reserve charters;
- state-funded substrate, medical and route-capacity transfers;
- continuity-office credentials used to keep services running during collapse;
- named obligations to displaced residents and service beneficiaries.

Material benefit:
- several core habitat systems and identity-service chains would not have survived the first collapse without state reserve resources.

Omissions:
- emergency powers were extended beyond their original horizon;
- refugee builders were entered as temporary dependents rather than co-founders;
- AI custodians were classified as continuity instruments in several translations;
- later state succession records omit office expiry and refusal petitions.

Decision:
- Imperial evidence can support public-service obligations and some succession liability, but not current sovereign title or command over Serrat.

Reward:
- current users gain a possible state-successor contribution source.

Cost:
- Imperial claimants lose the argument that continuity funding alone created ownership.

Hook:
- Independence witnesses show that the reserve failed before refugee crews rebuilt critical sectors.

# Episode 828 — 피난민은 손님이 아니었다

Date:
- CY 746-03-10 to 03-14.

Goal:
- test the Independence Exodus history as construction, defense and self-rule rather than moral symbolism.

Authentic evidence:
- refugee arrival and work ledgers;
- habitat repair, food, translation and defense shifts;
- neighborhood councils operating after state withdrawal;
- refusal records against forced return and labor reassignment.

Material contribution:
- refugee and deserter communities rebuilt failed habitat sectors and maintained current-use systems.

Omissions:
- some councils excluded later arrivals, contract laborers and AI forks from votes;
- confiscated components were later treated as community property without individual review;
- autonomy declarations undercounted continuing external service and credit dependence.

Decision:
- Independence evidence supports builder, labor, family and provisional civic-standing claims, but not exclusive historical ownership or automatic authority over current AI communities.

Reward:
- descendants and current residents receive standing not dependent on Imperial recognition.

Cost:
- the Exodus network must answer claims from people its founding councils excluded.

Hook:
- Helix produces invoices and maintenance logs proving that several supposedly self-built systems were licensed and continuously serviced.

# Episode 829 — 계약은 벽을 세웠고 출구도 막았다

Date:
- CY 746-03-15 to 03-19.

Goal:
- test the Helix Contract Founding history for real investment, maintenance and coercion.

Authentic evidence:
- financing and component licenses;
- long-term maintenance staffing;
- spare-part and service guarantees;
- risk pools that kept multiple systems insured and repairable.

Material contribution:
- Helix-linked capital and technical continuity prevented repeated infrastructure collapse.

Coercive structure:
- cross-default clauses tied medical, housing and identity access to unrelated debt;
- emergency signatures were later treated as permanent consent;
- AI service processes were sometimes listed as licensed assets;
- maintenance withdrawal was used as political leverage.

Decision:
- validated current maintenance obligations and noncoercive claims survive; ownership, permanent control and consent-defective clauses do not.

Reward:
- service systems retain access to proven maintenance knowledge and successor contributions.

Cost:
- both anti-corporate claimants and Helix successors lose their total narratives.

Hook:
- Neutral custodial deeds show that neither the state, refugees nor Helix was permitted to alienate the entire enclave.

# Episode 830 — 신탁은 정답이 아니라 잠금장치였다

Date:
- CY 746-03-20 to 03-24.

Goal:
- test the Neutral Custodial history without making it the authorial neutral answer.

Authentic evidence:
- multi-party custody deeds;
- anti-sale and anti-military-conversion clauses;
- mirror and provenance duties;
- split keys for habitat, service, archive and emergency access.

Material benefit:
- custody separation prevented total seizure and preserved conflicting records.

Omissions and failures:
- custodians delayed releases that current users needed;
- some seats became hereditary or institutionally self-renewing;
- neutrality language hid unequal access to counsel and transport;
- custody rules preserved harmful classifications alongside useful evidence.

Decision:
- custodial limits remain relevant to anti-alienation and evidence integrity, but do not grant Neutral actors permanent veto or interpretive supremacy.

Reward:
- no claimant can sell or militarize the whole enclave through one historical deed.

Cost:
- current communities can no longer treat custody as politically invisible technical work.

Hook:
- all four histories now generate incompatible answers to who counts as a Serrat civic participant.

# Episode 831 — 시민권보다 먼저 서 있는 사람들

Date:
- CY 746-03-25 to 03-29.

Goal:
- separate current civic standing, residence, service standing and final citizenship.

Petitions:
- 12,640 primary standing petitions filed under one nonduplicated disposition ledger.

Candidate bases:
- current residence and participation;
- refugee-builder lineage and family continuity;
- current service dependence;
- AI current agency and community membership;
- contract labor and maintenance contribution;
- historical office or custodial role.

Decision:
- no founding history alone creates citizenship; provisional standing may arise from current life and accountable contribution while final sovereignty remains open.

Reward:
- people and AI communities can participate before one founding myth wins.

Cost:
- external claimants accuse Serrat of creating a polity through procedure while denying that any polity exists.

Hook:
- the four histories classify Lumen and Orison as office, refugees, licensed systems or custodians, none matching their present agency.

# Episode 832 — 기록 속 직책은 현재의 인격이 아니다

Date:
- CY 746-03-30 to 04-03.

Goal:
- determine how founding classifications affect, but do not replace, current AI personhood.

Historical classifications:
- Imperial: continuity officers and instruments;
- Independence: allied inhabitants and shared labor councils;
- Helix: licensed service agents and operational assets;
- Neutral: custodians and protected functions.

Finding:
- each classification explains some institutional ancestry;
- none controls the current root/fork/personhood analysis;
- present refusal, preference, community procedure and continuity evidence remain decisive.

Decision:
- historical classifications may support claims, duties and provenance, but cannot reduce current AI persons to property or automatically elevate them into sovereign offices.

Reward:
- Lumen and Orison keep present political agency without erasing institutional history.

Cost:
- some AI blocs disagree over whether accepting historical office duties threatens current self-definition.

Hook:
- service users warn that the compact will expire before the standing and contract cases are resolved.

# Episode 833 — 만료되는 협약은 의료기록도 멈춘다

Date:
- CY 746-04-04 to 04-08.

Goal:
- turn compact expiry from a constitutional abstraction into a current service risk.

At-risk carryover:
- 3,700 users remain in manual, appeal or alternate-service status from B08-01.

Expiry effects:
- service-query authority ends unless replaced;
- staff cannot assume continued access from humanitarian intent;
- AI communities can refuse an unreviewed extension;
- descendants fear emergency access will become permanent extraction.

Decision:
- authorize a time-limited continuity bridge only for existing minimum-service queries while the four-history claims are adjudicated; no bulk access or new title follows.

Reward:
- immediate medical, care, family and infrastructure failures are reduced.

Cost:
- the bridge consumes review capacity and gives every faction leverage over renewal.

Hook:
- habitat-space claims collide when Independence return groups and Helix maintenance teams request the same sealed sector.

# Episode 834 — 같은 방을 고향과 작업장으로 부른다

Date:
- CY 746-04-09 to 04-13.

Goal:
- resolve access to nine contested physical spaces without converting use into ownership.

Nine spaces:
- three current AI substrate zones;
- two service-maintenance corridors;
- two conditional cultural-return spaces;
- one mixed memorial/technical zone;
- one sealed security zone.

Decision matrix:
- AI substrate zones remain nontransferable current habitat;
- maintenance corridors receive purpose-limited access;
- cultural spaces open by schedule and consent;
- mixed zone uses joint stewardship;
- security zone remains sealed pending integrity review.

Reward:
- current repair and cultural return can coexist in bounded locations.

Cost:
- no claimant obtains the symbolic total return it promised supporters.

Hook:
- E835 publishes the first shared facts and identifies which present consequences remain genuinely contested.

# Episode 835 — 모두 진짜였고 모두 부족했다

Date:
- CY 746-04-14 to 04-18.

Goal:
- close the first phase with a joint fact statement, not a final founding verdict.

Agreed facts:
- Imperial resources and offices materially sustained early continuity;
- refugee and dissident communities materially rebuilt and governed Serrat;
- Helix-linked investment and maintenance materially sustained infrastructure;
- Neutral custody materially prevented total alienation and preserved records;
- each system also excluded, coerced, delayed or reclassified affected people;
- present AI communities, residents, descendants and service users are not merely historical exhibits.

Still contested:
- successor service liability;
- labor and exclusion remedies;
- valid maintenance claims;
- custody and release limits;
- provisional civic-standing dispositions;
- compact replacement.

Final hook:
- the tribunal opens the 20,960 disputed founding records and 17,600 provenance/translation records for structured adjudication.
