# Episode 604 — 배별 명단과 사람별 명단

Date:
- CY 744-11-24.

Goal:
- reconcile civilian ship/community manifests without transferring captain or council authority over individuals.

Finding:
- ship manifests preserve current location and life-support reality;
- household/community lists preserve care and missing-person links;
- person records preserve choice, treatment and destination eligibility;
- none is sufficient alone.

Decision:
- create linked but separately appealable ship, household/community and person layers.

Reward:
- current location and social dependencies become usable without one ownership list.

Cost:
- reconciliation is slower and exposes contradictory guardianship, debt and contract claims.

Hook:
- several families request different routes inside the same ship community.

# Episode 605 — 가족이 갈라질 권리

Date:
- CY 744-11-24.

Goal:
- define household unity as a strong presumption, not compulsory collective movement.

Cases:
- adults choosing different destinations;
- children/current caregivers vs registered parents;
- medical treatment requiring separation;
- coercive household/community leaders;
- people refusing a ship’s collective plan.

Decision:
- preserve current caregiver/child preference, adult individual choice, protected contact and later reunification claims; no household head owns all movement decisions.

Reward:
- family/community protection no longer erases individual agency.

Cost:
- manifest complexity, escort and receiving-service burden increase; some communities lose cohesion.

Hook:
- destination matching shows one civilian cluster has seats but no compatible long-term food/work system.

# Episode 606 — 도착한 뒤 굶는 사람들

Date:
- CY 744-11-25.

Goal:
- distinguish physical reception from sustainable reception.

Audit example:
- one destination offers 48,000 short-term berths;
- only 21,000 have six-month food, work, medical and housing support;
- remaining capacity depends on Soa-type agricultural/service cargo and trained operators.

Decision:
- destination capacity receives short-term arrival, treatment, six-month service and institutional-startup fields rather than one seat number.

Reward:
- cargo and skilled-community value enter destination matching.

Cost:
- apparent capacity falls; passenger-max advocates accuse planners of manufacturing scarcity.

Hook:
- a medical transfer begins under a written destination approval that predates the new audit.

# Episode 607 — 승인서가 남긴 두 사람

Date:
- CY 744-11-25 to 11-26.

Goal:
- stop and account for a medical transfer using stale destination/treatment approval.

Event:
- 1,240 patients/caregivers are loaded for a destination whose specialty beds and oxygen interface were reassigned;
- correction arrives after two transfer craft depart.

Outcome:
- 2 confirmed deaths during diversion/transfer;
- 17 patients/caregivers worsened or injured, 5 serious;
- 1,221 remaining people accounted and returned/reassigned;
- no transport hull lost;
- destination approval process suspended for current revalidation.

Causality:
- stale approval, overlapping bed claims, State/civilian data separation and urgency all contribute.

Decision:
- require current treatment/destination handshake immediately before movement and preserve emergency local stop.

Reward:
- later medical transfers gain current verification.

Cost:
- permanent deaths/injuries and a 9–18 hour delay across multiple medical queues.

Hook:
- civilian councils demand differentiated guarantees instead of one promise that every manifest entry will move.

# Episode 608 — 보장할 수 있는 것만 적는다

Date:
- CY 744-11-26.

Goal:
- define truthful current guarantees under uncertain capacity.

Guarantee classes:
1. current care/shelter/basic identity and no-retaliation;
2. verified route/destination/treatment match;
3. conditional late movement with named dependencies;
4. holdout/stay support and contact;
5. unresolved/appeal protection;
6. no guarantee of exact timing or complete family/community unity.

Decision:
- publish differentiated guarantees and prohibited discrimination rather than universal evacuation promise.

Reward:
- people can compare actual commitments.

Cost:
- political legitimacy declines because many receive conditional or stay-path guarantees rather than seats.

Hook:
- organized councils propose community blocks that may exclude isolated/unregistered people.

# Episode 609 — 공동체 블록의 바깥

Date:
- CY 744-11-27.

Goal:
- use community manifests without letting organized groups capture all capacity.

Decision:
- allow bounded community blocks for care, language, work and destination viability;
- reserve independent/isolated/unregistered access and current appeal quotas;
- no council can bind people who opt out.

Reward:
- communities can preserve mutual care and startup capacity.

Cost:
- block sizes reduce packing efficiency and create hard boundaries; isolated-person review remains labor intensive.

Hook:
- Grey/local routes attract people whose destinations reject centralized or documented entry.

# Episode 610 — 회색길을 선택한 사람들

Date:
- CY 744-11-27 to 11-28.

Goal:
- record voluntary Grey/local interest separately from desperation or forced overflow.

Initial interest:
- approximately 118,000 people request information or provisional Grey/local paths;
- only 31,000 have currently verified endpoint, craft and service consent;
- many seek family/community autonomy, avoid central identity systems or have small-craft compatibility.

Decision:
- create voluntary, conditional, coerced-by-scarcity and ineligible/unsafe states; no one is assigned Grey merely for being unregistered.

Reward:
- Grey becomes a chosen path for some people, not a disposal category.

Cost:
- endpoint risk, exploitation and missing-person probability remain high; central planners lose control of some movement.

Hook:
- final civilian/current readiness pass must classify millions without turning classification into priority.

# Episode 611 — 준비되었다는 말의 여섯 뜻

Date:
- CY 744-11-28.

Goal:
- define current manifest-readiness categories before route and plan comparison.

Categories:
- ready for one or more verified route/destination combinations;
- conditional/late pending route, destination, care or equipment;
- local/holdout/required operator;
- unresolved/appeal/identity/destination conflict;
- present but not yet assessed;
- voluntary Grey/local interest tracked inside relevant categories, not added separately.

Decision:
- readiness classification cannot itself assign priority or route.

Reward:
- later plans can compare what they actually move and leave.

Cost:
- every plan inherits unresolved and required-local groups; no clean passenger pool exists.

Final hook:
- military, medical, Neutral and local actors now negotiate first/late/holdout rules against the same categories.
