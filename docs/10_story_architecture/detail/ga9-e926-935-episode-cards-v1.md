# Episode 926 — 아무도 추방을 명령하지 않았다

Date:
- CY 746-12-06 to 12-09.

Goal:
- reveal a coordinated exclusion outcome without inventing one secret master order.

Trigger:
- one mobile settlement cluster loses route priority, identity recognition, insurance, repair access and family-service permissions within forty-eight hours.

Finding:
- six systems cite different legal authorities;
- all reuse the same low-continuity profile and confidence note;
- no individual decision alone amounts to civil death;
- together they make ordinary life nearly impossible.

Decision:
- open a linked-case inquiry across domains instead of six isolated appeals.

Reward:
- cumulative administrative effect becomes legally visible.

Cost:
- each agency argues that joint review improperly transfers responsibility to it.

Hook:
- route operators defend the first restrictions as necessary schedule protection.

# Episode 927 — 느린 차선에서 사라지는 항로

Date:
- CY 746-12-10 to 12-13.

Goal:
- examine route-access restrictions.

Action ledger:
- 186,400 route-access restrictions, manual reroutes or suspended priority decisions;
- repeated decisions and overlapping users exist.

Evidence:
- some cases involve real forged cargo or unstable receiving data;
- many arise from missing histories, mobile residence and nonstandard credential chains;
- manual routes are technically available but too slow for perishable, medical and rotating-crew needs.

Decision:
- require current-danger evidence for urgent denial and create time-bounded continuity warrants for missing-history cases.

Reward:
- route safety remains enforceable without making documentation absence permanent.

Cost:
- common schedules lose some efficiency and manual capacity becomes scarce.

Hook:
- the same profile has already migrated into legal identity systems.

# Episode 928 — 신분은 없어진 적이 없다고 적혀 있다

Date:
- CY 746-12-14 to 12-17.

Goal:
- show how legal identity becomes unusable without a formal revocation.

Action ledger:
- 72,800 identity, benefit, residence or family-record verification suspensions.

Findings:
- no statute cancels personhood or citizenship;
- benefit, housing, travel and family systems refuse to rely on low-confidence proofs;
- reconstructed disaster records receive lower weight than coalition-native records;
- AI-human households are split across incompatible categories.

Decision:
- recognize `current standing warrants` that preserve service and legal participation while historical proof is reviewed.

Reward:
- missing records no longer erase present existence.

Cost:
- several member governments argue that coalition review is intruding on domestic status law.

Hook:
- insurers use the same suspensions to reprice or cancel coverage.

# Episode 929 — 보험이 먼저 국경을 만든다

Date:
- CY 746-12-18 to 12-21.

Goal:
- test private and quasi-public insurance effects.

Action ledger:
- 118,600 premium surcharges, collateral holds, coverage limits or cancellations.

Evidence:
- common verification genuinely reduced fraud and settlement delay;
- low-score categories are priced as group risk before individual review;
- coverage loss makes route access and employment impossible even without coalition prohibition;
- outsider insurance alternatives exist but cost 35–82% more in the affected cases.

Decision:
- prohibit permanent group pricing from unreviewed continuity profiles and create a temporary risk pool.

Reward:
- market solvency and individual review are both retained.

Cost:
- coalition members and insurers must finance claims from uncertain cohorts.

Hook:
- repair yards have already begun refusing parts and certification.

# Episode 933 — 여섯 개의 정당한 결정

Date:
- CY 747-01-06 to 01-09.

Goal:
- assign layered responsibility for cumulative exclusion.

Agency defenses:
- route office protects schedules;
- status office demands proof;
- insurer prices risk;
- yard protects safety;
- clinic protects consent and funding;
- technology board protects infrastructure.

Finding:
- several individual decisions are reasonable;
- profile reuse, absent expiry and circular evidence transform reasonable decisions into a systemic barrier;
- data designers, adopting agencies and current reviewers have distinct responsibility.

Decision:
- no collective acquittal and no one-agency sole blame;
- require linked-impact review whenever three or more domains reuse one profile.

Reward:
- responsibility follows actual control and benefit.

Cost:
- linked review is slower and politically threatening.

Hook:
- affected communities organize their own joint council rather than waiting for coalition representation.
