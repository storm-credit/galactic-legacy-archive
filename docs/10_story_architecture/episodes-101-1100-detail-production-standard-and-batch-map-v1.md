## 1. Scope

This standard converts approved act/subact architecture into episode-level design without writing manuscript prose.

Output is:
- episode cards;
- scene-state cards;
- operation and resource updates;
- character/faction state changes;
- collection and mystery ledger updates;
- cliffhanger/reward cadence controls.

Output is not:
- chapter prose;
- dialogue draft;
- publication-ready length;
- final platform pacing claim.

## 2. Episode Card Schema

Every episode card must contain:

1. episode number and working title;
2. viewpoint or controlled information source;
3. opening state inherited from previous episode;
4. visible goal;
5. hidden/secondary pressure known to the design but not necessarily the reader;
6. 3–6 scene units;
7. location and travel-time feasibility;
8. active characters and independent goals;
9. active institution/faction and current authority;
10. hardware, fleet, route, medical and economic state when material;
11. obstacle/escalation;
12. decisive choice and who makes it;
13. immediate result;
14. reward/information gain;
15. cost/loss/debt;
16. collection state change;
17. mystery/clue/decoy state change;
18. relationship/status change;
19. irreversible or carried-forward state;
20. final hook/question;
21. dependencies and downstream episodes;
22. specialist review panel.

## 3. Scene Unit Schema

Each scene unit records:

- place and local time;
- entering physical/institutional state;
- actor goal;
- opposition;
- action, not merely discussion;
- value/state change;
- exit condition;
- information ceiling;
- objects/people moved;
- damage, expenditure or obligation created.

A meeting scene must alter at least one of:
- authority;
- evidence;
- allocation;
- relationship;
- deadline;
- physical access;
- liability.

## 4. State-Ledger Updates

At the end of every episode update only fields that changed.

### Character
- location;
- health/fatigue;
- authority;
- affiliation;
- relationship;
- knowledge and confidence;
- debt/obligation;
- exit/death status.

### Asset and formation
- custody/operation/title;
- readiness;
- ammunition/propellant/thermal state;
- crew/maintainer availability;
- damage and earliest repair;
- route position;
- command legitimacy.

### Economy/service
- cash/credit;
- physical inventory;
- payroll/claim;
- medicine/food reserve;
- route/insurance standing;
- who loses allocation.

### Mystery/collection
- discovery/access/custody/operation/meaning;
- clue confidence;
- false-answer status;
- active-window reader load;
- retirement/payoff status.

## 5. Episode-Level Hard Rules

- no new foundational world rule is created inside a card;
- no character is present without travel feasibility;
- no ship/frame changes state without time, labor and parts;
- no institution acts without authority, staff, money, keys, force, legitimacy or recognition as applicable;
- no future-memory answer remains perfect after major intervention;
- no major reward arrives without integration and support cost;
- no major loss disappears from the next relevant card;
- no person/faction/territory becomes owned inventory;
- at least one non-Rian autonomous decisive action appears in most 5-episode windows;
- every 10 episodes contain a physical mission/result, not only council or record debate;
- exact battle cards use the weapons/sensors calibration and formation registry;
- active proper nouns obey the terminology window.

## 6. Review Levels

### E-card standard review

- N03 episode structure;
- N04 causality;
- C-role for active characters;
- world-domain specialist when material;
- X04 continuity;
- X02/N07 reader load/reward;
- O01 canon check.

### Battle/operation episode

Add:
- T02/T03/T04;
- H02/H03/H04/H06;
- M01–M06 as required;
- M08 for retreat/denial/surrender.

### Law/economy/institution episode

Add:
- P01/P02/P03/P06;
- M05/T03 where resources are material;
- X03 for consent/coercion.

### Mystery/payoff episode

Add:
- N05/N06;
- G01/G02/G07;
- O01/X04.

## 7. Batch Completion Definition

A batch is complete only when:

- every episode in range has a card;
- opening/closing states connect without gap;
- all travel, damage and resource states reconcile;
- active names/terms pass lint;
- reward, loss and cliffhanger cadence is mapped;
- secondary mysteries opened in the batch are closed or explicitly carried;
- relevant core payoff ledger entries are updated;
- red team lists S0/S1/S2;
- batch status and next-batch handoff are recorded.

## 9. Production Order

Strict order:

1. B02-01 through B02-05;
2. GA2 cross-batch audit;
3. B03-01 onward sequentially;
4. one grand-act audit after each GA;
5. final E101–1100 continuity and payoff audit.

Do not produce later cards first merely because the ending is exciting. Later locked payoff episodes may be refined only after preceding clue/asset states exist.

- denominator: 1,000 episodes (E101–E1100);
- numerator: cards committed and audited;
- batch or act maps do not count as episode cards.
