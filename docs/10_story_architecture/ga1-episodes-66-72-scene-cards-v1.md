# Episode 66 — 열한 번의 치료

Date:
- CY 742-05-22.

Episode function:
- present exact scarcity and competing full-safety plan.
- establish patient information/choice process before allocation.
- give Helix a medically stronger immediate offer with political costs.

## Scene 66.1 — The cabinet count

POV:
- Rian observing Mia/clinicians/patient representatives.

Physical process:
- current stock is counted, seals checked and false/expired assumptions removed.
- full-protocol cycles: 11 for 23 time-critical patients.

Important distinction:
- a cycle is not interchangeable across every patient without calibration/membrane/staff.

Rian:
- initially looks for the highest-value people or widest downstream benefit.

Mia:
- states future strategic value/faction cannot enter clinical allocation.

## Scene 66.2 — Helix’s full-safety offer

Helix representative/clinical staff present:
- enough emergency stock and technical support can be released to cover all 23.

Conditions:
- Helix clinical command/audit.
- full modified-interface/outcome data.
- ORA-3 compatibility/research rights.
- patient identity/contract verification.
- unapproved adapter suspension.
- Academy debt/priority restructuring.

Benefits:
- lower immediate failure risk and known treatment.

Patient responses:
- some want the offer.
- some fear transfer/data/identity lock-in.
- no unified rejection.

## Scene 66.3 — The mixed plan proposal

Nera/Ward staff:
- present adapter compatibility bands.

Neutral:
- presents limited transport capacity and receiving-care conditions.

Haren:
- maps patients, beds, staff, transport and remaining Ward capacity.

Patient representatives demand:
- individual risk/options.
- no secret prioritization.
- right to choose Helix/full protocol, adapter, transfer or waiting where feasible.

## Scene 66.4 — Orvan is not first

Preliminary clinical list:
- Orvan does not receive an immediate full-protocol cycle because his current time-to-harm and alternative options are less urgent than an acute reversible patient.

Haren reaction:
- challenges the list based on Orvan’s risk, history and importance.

Mia:
- says those factors do not make the cartridge work better or another patient less urgent.

End hook:
- the full cycle is assigned to Leta or another acute patient whose survival could be stabilized now, while Orvan must choose a riskier route.

Reward:
- actual crisis options and patient information rights.

Cost:
- no morally painless allocation; Haren/Mia conflict.

# Episode 67 — 위험을 고르는 사람

Date:
- CY 742-05-23.

Episode function:
- make patient consent operational under scarcity.
- separate medical recommendation from political/technical ownership.
- begin adapter trials with visible labor and risk.

## Scene 67.1 — Orvan’s options

POV:
- Rian with Haren/Orvan/Mia.

Options:
- high-risk generic adapter with monitored bed.
- wait under tighter support for a later full cycle, risking deterioration.
- take a Neutral high-dependency transfer slot.
- accept Helix’s full-protocol/contract route.

Haren preference:
- Neutral transfer or full cycle.

Orvan choice:
- selects the high-risk local adapter or delayed local path because he wants to remain with the patient group/equipment/evidence through the immediate transition.
- choice is informed but shaped by others’ needs and past identity constraints.

Mia:
- confirms understanding and records refusal/choice.

## Scene 67.2 — Nera’s bridge v0.1

Workshop/medical preparation:
- Nera, Ward technicians and clinicians build/test the bridge.
- Kiro’s failed adapter evidence supplies one exclusion/failure lesson.
- version/material/patient compatibility are recorded.

Helix technical actor:
- provides or insists on one known safety limit that prevents a worse design.
- refuses responsibility for unapproved use beyond that information.

## Scene 67.3 — The first patients

Band A patients who consent:
- adapter applied under monitored conditions.
- increased alarms/staff workload become visible.
- one succeeds cleanly, one develops a manageable instability.

Rian:
- sees no triumphant “open solution”; staff must stay and recalibrate.

## Scene 67.4 — The complication

A selected Band B patient develops serious instability or equipment contamination/failure.

Consequences:
- consumes a high-dependency bed and rescue resources.
- Helix warning gains credibility.
- Nera cannot fix it alone; clinicians and patient-specific data are required.

End hook:
- the corridor must open earlier because adapter capacity is lower/slower than planned.

Reward:
- real alternative treatment begins.

Cost:
- serious complication, staff overload and lower projected capacity.

Episode function:
- turn medical evacuation into a route/capacity/identity operation.
- assign first cutter seats by current clinical/consent criteria.

POV:
- Rian, with Haren/Mia leading.

- transport benefit versus movement risk.
- destination acceptance.
- patient preference.

Academy Security or Imperial custody authority holds selected protected patients pending legal clearance.

Argument:

End hook:

# Episode 71 — 환자를 지키는 포위

Date:
- CY 742-05-25.

Episode function:
- climax the Ward arc through patient/data/equipment/security conflict.
- distribute action across medical, interior, records and external corridor.
- suspend coercive authority without destroying care.

## Scene 71.1 — Seizure/purge order

POV:
- Rian coordinating one layer, not all.

Order objectives:
- secure ORA-3/continuity data.
- transfer protected patients/equipment.
- close unauthorized adapter/workshop work.
- preserve or erase sensitive research under one responsible authority.

Justification:
- real security, liability and patient safety concerns.

Overreach:
- no patient-specific consent and no shared current-person/service plan.

## Scene 71.2 — Independent fronts

Mia/clinicians:
- keep treatment and triage; determine which movement is medically allowed.

Haren/patient representatives:
- maintain current manifest, destinations and consent decisions.

Nera/Ward workers:
- isolate treatment from research loads where possible and protect adapter/version evidence.

Serin/ORA-3:
- preserve consent/access logs and prevent bulk export/deletion.

Kara/students:
- hold corridors without entering clinical spaces as armed rulers.

Rian/07:
- remains external or grounded after E69, coordinating limited service/approach rather than leading interior fight.

## Scene 71.3 — The imperfect override

Crisis:
- centralized Security/Research command can restore faster unified control or isolate a failing system.
- distributed approvals delay action and one patient/system deteriorates.

Required loss:
- one serious complication, missed transfer or damaged system occurs partly because authority is split/slow.

Counterpoint:
- centralized order would have removed patient choices/data control and possibly preserved more immediate stability.

Decision:
- licensed clinicians invoke medical stop/custody.
- Neutral witness/corridor and public/process evidence make indiscriminate seizure costly.
- some consenting transfers continue; disputed blanket seizure stops.

## Scene 71.4 — ORA-3 chooses a limited action

Within its bounded authority and source permissions:
- ORA-3 preserves current clinical/consent state.
- rejects or limits bulk copy/erase.
- may release only process proof needed to validate patient decisions.

Not personhood climax:
- this is a constrained interface action, not full free AI awakening.

End hook:
- Academy Research/Helix/Imperial funding streams are suspended or withdrawn pending new governance, leaving the Ward and school near financial collapse.

Reward:
- patients/care remain under temporary plural oversight.

Cost:
- slower response harm, damaged systems and funding loss.

POV:
- Rian observing/participating in review.

Locked mixed outcomes:

- some through open adapter with ongoing monitoring and at least one serious complication.

Final hook:

Reward:
