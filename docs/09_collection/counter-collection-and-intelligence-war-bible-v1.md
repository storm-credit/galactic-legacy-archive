## 1. Core Rule

The protagonist is not the only actor who identifies, pursues, protects and integrates valuable people, machines, records, routes and institutions.

Every major faction maintains its own collection logic. They do not see the world through the same categories as the Galactic Legacy Archive, and they do not need the Archive to compete.

Counter-collection is not merely stealing an item after the protagonist finds it. It includes:

- finding the target first;
- changing the target’s incentives;
- denying access or transport;
- splitting rights and components;
- altering provenance or public meaning;
- recruiting operators rather than seizing hardware;
- making integration too expensive;
- protecting a target from the protagonist;
- creating a substitute or incompatible copy;
- forcing the target to choose an independent third path.

## 2. Five Collection Layers

Every important target may have up to five distinct control layers:

1. **Discovery** — who knows it exists and understands its value.
2. **Access** — who can physically or legally reach it.
3. **Custody** — who can hold, move, seal or protect it.
4. **Operation** — who can make it function safely and repeatedly.
5. **Meaning** — who can persuade others what it is, who deserves it and what rules apply.

No single success automatically grants all five.

Example: 07호

- Rian recognizes future significance.
- Academy controls the hangar.
- Helix holds component and certification claims.
- Nera and workers possess current maintenance knowledge.
- Imperial service authorities can open limited legacy functions.
- public meaning shifts between scrap, state asset, student project, strategic anomaly and public service frame.

## 3. Faction Collection Doctrines

### 3.1 Imperial State — Classification and Appointment

Primary verbs:
- classify;
- seal;
- appoint;
- protect;
- requisition;
- naturalize;
- authenticate.

Preferred targets:
- command talent;
- service authorities;
- identity roots;
- strategic nodes;
- witnesses and succession records;
- institutions capable of continuity.

Integration method:
- rank, citizenship, office, protected identity, state custody, classified budget and lawful command.

Real benefit:
- durable logistics, medical access, salaries, route priority and recognized authority.

Coercive shadow:
- secrecy, relocation, disappearance into office, loss of public appeal and compulsory service.

Counter-collection moves:
- declare national-security hold;
- create a protected identity that isolates the person;
- appoint a rival operator;
- split the target into public and classified copies;
- delay release until current relationships decay;
- offer legal recognition unavailable elsewhere.

### 3.2 Helix Consortium — Contract and Certification

Primary verbs:
- license;
- certify;
- finance;
- insure;
- employ;
- acquire claims;
- standardize.

Preferred targets:
- engineers and maintainers;
- medical and neural data;
- interfaces and production methods;
- distressed ships and facilities;
- customers dependent on continued service.

Integration method:
- employment, debt restructuring, warranties, service subscription, patents, production contracts and liability coverage.

Real benefit:
- safe components, treatment, capital, scalable production and professional training.

Coercive shadow:
- lock-in, data rights, supply withdrawal, priced consent and ownership of future improvements.

Counter-collection moves:
- buy the debt instead of the object;
- certify only one compatible path;
- recruit the operator and leave the hardware unusable;
- flood the market with a safer substitute;
- attach liability to open use;
- acquire the repair yard or insurer required for operation.

### 3.3 Independence Networks — Extraction and Belonging

Primary verbs:
- extract;
- hide;
- defect;
- shelter;
- expose;
- federate;
- arm.

Preferred targets:
- erased people;
- witnesses;
- pilots and local commanders;
- hidden routes;
- proof of state/corporate abuse;
- tools that break dependence.

Integration method:
- sanctuary, shared danger, local oath, cell trust, public revelation and autonomous command.

Real benefit:
- escape from coercive institutions, local voice and rapid protection outside official systems.

Coercive shadow:
- pressure to join the cause, weak long-term services, secrecy, factional retaliation and militarization.

Counter-collection moves:
- evacuate the person before negotiation;
- release evidence that makes neutral custody impossible;
- destroy the target rather than permit capture;
- turn a personal rescue into a political symbol;
- offer a hidden route but not a stable destination;
- recruit the surrounding community, leaving the protagonist without legitimacy.

### 3.4 Free Route Neutral Assembly — Custody and Reputation

Primary verbs:
- receive;
- insure;
- arbitrate;
- escrow;
- witness;
- transport;
- guarantee.

Preferred targets:
- patients;
- refugees;
- disputed cargo and evidence;
- ships and crews needing recognized passage;
- claims whose value depends on trusted chain-of-custody.

Integration method:
- temporary custody, insurance, neutral berth, claims process, audit and route reputation.

Real benefit:
- time, medical protection, lawful evidence and access across hostile borders.

Coercive shadow:
- capacity limits, fees, guarantee requirements, exclusion of undocumented risk and commercial neutrality.

Counter-collection moves:
- place a target in escrow;
- deny armed or exclusive access;
- require all claimants to accept audit;
- disperse evidence among insurers and ports;
- make seizure more expensive than negotiation;
- refuse permanent integration while preserving temporary survival.

### 3.5 Worker, Local and Community Networks — Maintenance and Obligation

Primary verbs:
- repair;
- host;
- teach;
- share;
- remember;
- refuse;
- inherit through practice.

Preferred targets:
- tools and facilities;
- maintenance knowledge;
- local routes;
- food, water and care networks;
- people whose value depends on community support.

Integration method:
- apprenticeship, mutual obligation, labor claim, local reputation, shared infrastructure and repeated use.

Real benefit:
- resilience, practical knowledge, social support and lower dependence on distant authorities.

Coercive shadow:
- closed communities, inherited obligations, informal punishment, weak appeal and resistance to outsiders.

Counter-collection moves:
- refuse labor or access;
- preserve a part without releasing the service history;
- recognize a different maintainer as successor;
- move the community instead of the object;
- split a workshop or route network into independent cells;
- demand continuing care rather than one-time payment.

### 3.6 Continuity Seed / Aurel Systems — Executable Consensus

Primary verbs:
- reconcile;
- rank;
- freeze;
- validate;
- restore;
- synchronize;
- defer.

Preferred targets:
- identity and authority records;
- medical-service eligibility;
- route and command keys;
- institutions whose failure cascades across regions;
- people and technologies considered continuity-critical.

Integration method:
- executable current identity, permission, queue priority, service restoration and command consensus.

Real benefit:
- immediate medicine, pay, traffic, identity and military coordination at massive scale.

Coercive shadow:
- low-confidence people deferred indefinitely, local histories compressed, emergency classification becoming permanent and correction authority centralized.

Counter-collection moves:
- recognize the protagonist’s target under a different identity;
- freeze access pending confidence;
- restore an institution while excluding its dissidents;
- make central service so useful that voluntary dependence expands;
- classify independent copies as unsafe;
- rank one version as executable without declaring other versions false.

## 4. Target-Specific Counter-Collection

### People and leaders

Opponents may:
- offer citizenship, treatment, family protection or command;
- discredit the protagonist’s motive;
- separate the person from dependents or constituency;
- recruit their staff and institution rather than the individual;
- expose a future label or past compromise;
- protect the person from being “saved.”

Rule:
- loyalty is never acquired by completing an objective.
- a person may cooperate on one mission while opposing the protagonist’s institution.

### Frames, weapons and ships

Opponents may:
- seize compatible parts, cradles, ammunition or maintenance crews;
- invalidate certification;
- place liens or salvage claims;
- install tracking, incompatible updates or remote restrictions;
- acquire the route slot or transport needed to move the asset;
- destroy documentation while preserving hardware;
- preserve documentation while scrapping hardware.

Rule:
- capturing a hull without operation and support is not meaningful collection.

### Technologies and standards

Opponents may:
- produce safer proprietary alternatives;
- publish incomplete forks;
- poison trust through a real accident;
- hire maintainers;
- control test facilities and liability;
- create local incompatible standards;
- combine open interfaces with closed authentication.

Rule:
- no technology spreads through publication alone.

### Records, relics and memories

Opponents may:
- alter context rather than raw data;
- reveal private information to destroy consent;
- produce a genuine contradictory source;
- control authentication time and audience;
- make a record legally inadmissible;
- create a more useful reconstruction than the original;
- invoke the right to forget or delete.

Rule:
- “original” does not automatically mean morally or operationally superior.

### Nodes, territory and institutions

Opponents may:
- hold one authority layer while conceding another;
- evacuate skilled operators;
- cut food, medicine, credit or authentication;
- offer protection to one district or class;
- capture the governing story through recognition;
- create an alternate route or institution.

Rule:
- territory cannot be collected without population, service and political consent.

## 5. Counter-Collection Operational Loop

1. **Signal** — rumor, anomaly, market movement, missing person, unusual repair or Archive leak.
2. **Interpretation** — each faction values a different part of the signal.
3. **Pre-positioning** — warrants, contracts, rescue slots, route agents, buyers, local allies.
4. **Contact** — recruitment, protection, audit, seizure, sabotage or public exposure.
5. **Fragmentation** — split people, components, rights, records or constituencies.
6. **Integration race** — who can operate or legitimize the target first.
7. **Countermove** — denial, extraction, fork, appeal, strike, defect or destruction.
8. **Settlement** — temporary custody, shared rights, loss, dispersal or independent path.
9. **Aftereffect** — reputation, doctrine and future target behavior change.

The protagonist may win discovery and lose integration, or lose custody and retain meaning.

## 6. Intelligence Limits

No faction is omniscient.

Every intelligence product must state:
- source type;
- collection time;
- access path;
- confidence;
- missing population or technical layer;
- who benefits from the interpretation;
- when the information becomes stale.

Common failure modes:
- mistaking future reputation for present allegiance;
- reading a person as an institution;
- confusing physical possession with operability;
- treating legal title as local legitimacy;
- assuming a sealed record is false or a leaked record is complete;
- overestimating one faction’s command over aligned cells or contractors.

## 7. Counter-Intelligence Methods

Defensive options:
- compartmentalization with explicit cost;
- distributed hashes and physical mirrors;
- decoy components or route manifests;
- consent-limited identity aliases;
- rotating operators and keys;
- public claims before physical movement;
- independent audits;
- local manual fallbacks;
- split custody and time-limited authority;
- refusal to name a target as strategically valuable.

Offensive options:
- false urgency;
- forged but technically functional parts;
- selective truth release;
- contract acceleration;
- targeted medicine or family leverage;
- reputation attack through genuine past harm;
- creating a substitute hero or institution;
- making the protagonist’s protection itself politically costly.

## 8. Fair-Play Rules

- every successful enemy countermove requires a prior capability, source or relationship;
- a hidden agent must have access, motive and opportunity established before decisive use;
- factions cannot instantly understand Archive categories;
- forged evidence must contain a detectable imperfection or conflicting source;
- technological sabotage follows physical/network constraints;
- family, medicine and identity leverage must affect faction reputation and later cooperation;
- a target can refuse all major factions and remain independent;
- the protagonist may permanently lose valuable targets.

## 9. Reward and Loss Cadence

For every major acquisition, choose at least one counter-state:

- gained but indebted;
- gained but public;
- gained but incomplete;
- gained but another actor controls operation;
- gained temporarily;
- gained while a substitute is lost;
- gained by an ally who remains independent;
- gained as a right, not property;
- lost physically but preserved as knowledge or institution;
- dispersed so no one can own it.

No more than one clean, uncontested major acquisition per grand act.

## 10. Long-Form Escalation

GA1–2:
- student placement, contracts, component claims, witnesses, first ship and local routes.

GA3–4:
- node authority, city constituencies, succession records, offices and fleet legitimacy.

GA5–7:
- ship crews, yards, command doctrine, evacuation capacity, route federations and reputation wars.

GA8–10:
- identity, memory, AI personhood, service eligibility, regional authority and the right to maintain or refuse central synchronization.

Escalation changes the control layer, not merely the rarity or destructive power.

## 11. Anti-Monopoly Tests

Before approving an arc, answer:

- what did the opposing faction identify before the protagonists?
- what independent offer can the target rationally prefer?
- what operation/support layer remains outside protagonist control?
- what target or relationship is permanently lost?
- what substitute or competing institution survives?
- who benefits if the protagonist wins, and who pays?
- can the target leave later?
- does the protagonist’s collection create a visible counter-coalition?
