## 1. PM Ruling

The portfolio count must follow this order:

1. count protagonist, core allies, core antagonists/rivals, recurring support, important extras and background role groups;
2. identify the operational and story function each role must express;
3. assign an existing shared asset ecology wherever possible;
4. create a new model, hull, weapon, relic, ship or technology only when reuse fails a documented function;
5. count entities, physical instances, forms/states, formations and sets in separate ledgers;
6. apply reader-memory, logistics, loss and art-production controls;
7. only then approve a portfolio capacity.

The previous `28-row target first` approach is withdrawn. The existing rows remain useful as a phase-1 coverage sample, but they do not determine the final machine count.

## 8. Collection Design Consequences

### Frames

Collect model lineage, individual hull history, pilot pairing, source formation, support ecology, damage/refit state and earned markings separately. Reusing a mass chassis with a distinctive repair and service history is a feature, not missing content.

### Weapons and Modules

The 40–64 front anchors combine about 18–26 signature weapons with named tools, modules and military-rights anchors. Ammunition, paint, software and reversible mission packs do not become new independent items.

### Relics and Records

Service Authority A–G is one custody/authority lineage, not seven legendary treasures. Completion can be return, distributed custody, deletion by the subject or acknowledged loss.

### Ships

The major current gap is antagonist flagships, carriers and recurring support hull lineages. A hull is not operational without crew, captain authority, wages, title, insurance, route rights, yard support and recovery.

### Technology

Blueprints do not create industry. Technology counts must preserve parent/fork, process, tooling, labor, facility, certification, training, maintenance and failure state.

### Factions and Places

Organizations and communities are participants with consent, refusal, succession and exit, never owned collection inventory. Regional visual variation should usually be an adaptation profile on a shared ecology rather than one new chassis per place.

## 9. Reader-Memory and Release Controls

- one subarc foregrounds 3–5 total collection targets;
- one battle activates no more than four machine names unless the scene is explicitly an OOB overview;
- one arc foregrounds 5–8 organizations and 3–4 places at a time;
- a new ace model displaces or pauses another front-stage machine unless the cast role requires a confrontation pair;
- each new model must receive at least three meaningful appearances or remain background-only;
- forms and refits reuse the parent name until their structural and story difference earns a separate public label;
- support, loss and recovery scenes are part of the collectible payoff, not optional bookkeeping.

### DEV-RD-02 Reuse-first result reopened by semantic red team

1. **Problem:** a passing validator still allowed title-only role evidence, GA1/GA3 demands mapped to later machines, an unsupported 26-model lower bound and arbitrary non-frame targets.

5. **Impact:** the role-first process remains usable, but it no longer claims that 26–32 independent machines or any C3–C8 exact target has been established.

### DEV-RD-03 Meaningful evidence and compatibility lock

1. **Problem:** F13 found that H2/person-name citations and self-authored `PASS_` strings could still satisfy the validator.
2. **Cause:** a non-title check rejected only H1, and the crosswalk did not lock source text or require its system claims to match the lineup index.
