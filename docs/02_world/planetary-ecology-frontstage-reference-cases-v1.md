## 1. Purpose

The ecology architecture should be proven by a small number of usable worlds, not by inventing 612 encyclopedias.

These five cases test distinct environment/economy/story patterns:

1. native mixed biosphere;
2. inherited terraforming;
3. closed habitat ecology;
4. damaged/recovery biosphere;
5. extreme species-majority home environment.

They are generic design fixtures. They do not add new canon planets.

# CASE ECO-R1 — Native Mixed-Biosphere Trade World

Planning tags:
- likely Inner or Middle;
- `ENV-1 / BIOE-3 / ECO-N or ECO-M / MS1–MS2`.

## Physical/ecological premise

- atmosphere and temperature broadly usable by several common species;
- native multicellular biosphere is biochemically only **partly** compatible;
- local water is physically drinkable after treatment but native dissolved compounds require filtration;
- imported agriculture occupies only selected managed zones because open release threatens native ecosystems.

## Native life package

Only six front-stage organisms are needed:
- one dominant producer analogue forming low canopies/mats;
- one decomposer network essential to soil chemistry;
- one small mobile mutualist that spreads reproductive structures;
- one economically useful native organism producing medicine/fiber/resin analogue;
- one crop nuisance species;
- one culturally meaningful harmless animal/organism.

No giant apex predator is required.

## Economy

Exports:
- native pharmaceuticals/material feedstocks;
- processed agricultural products;
- biosphere expertise.

Imports:
- some specialist diets;
- industrial nutrients/minerals not safely extracted locally.

## Political conflict

Three legitimate constituencies:
- conservation zones protecting native ecology;
- farmers/settlers needing land and pest control;
- indigenous/historical local communities whose livelihoods use managed native organisms.

No side is simply “nature versus greed.”

## Story function

Useful for:
- GA2 contract involving cargo quarantine;
- GA3/5 supply disruption;
- later argument that preserving a living system can be more valuable than capturing a facility.

Failure cascade:

`route embargo → imported crop inputs delayed → settlers expand into native zone → mutualist population falls → native medicinal harvest collapses → hospital shortage`.

# CASE ECO-R2 — Inherited Terraforming World

Planning tags:
- Inner/Middle;
- `ENV-3 / BIOE-4 or BIOE-5 / ECO-T / MS1`.

## Premise

Several centuries ago, planetary atmosphere/water/substrate were deliberately shifted toward a settlement target. Present residents did not choose the original target environment.

The world now looks “natural” to residents but still depends on:
- orbital shading or albedo management;
- water-routing infrastructure;
- engineered decomposer communities;
- atmospheric trace-gas control.

## Core social fact

Terraforming maintenance is not ancient magic. It is a public utility whose failure appears slowly enough for politicians to defer spending.

## Ecological tension

The engineered ecology has become its own hybrid system. “Restore the original planet” and “preserve the inherited biosphere” are mutually incompatible goals.

Some later-arriving species want:
- slightly different atmospheric composition;
- more local environmental modules instead of whole-world retuning.

## Economy

- large agriculture base;
- climate-control industries;
- water/public-infrastructure debt;
- insurance tied to planetary maintenance ratings.

## Disaster case

A damaged climate-control segment does not cause instant apocalypse.

Timeline:
- first season: heat/water anomalies;
- 1–3 years: crop shift and migration;
- longer: soil/decomposer and reservoir changes.

This prevents “terraforming machine off = planet explodes tomorrow.”

## Story use

Strong for:
- GA4 legitimacy debate over who owns inherited infrastructure debt;
- GA6 refugee destination capacity;
- GA9 Preservation claim that centralized climate control genuinely improves stability.

# CASE ECO-R3 — Closed Habitat Food-Web Cluster

Planning tags:
- Core/Inner/Middle/Frontier possible;
- `ENV-0 / BIOE-5 / ECO-H / MS1–MS2`.

## Premise

A dense station/moon habitat cluster has no meaningful open native biosphere. Every calorie, oxygen loop and waste stream depends on engineered ecology plus imported buffers.

## Ecological stack

- microbial waste processors;
- algae/producer bioreactors;
- controlled crops;
- fermentation protein;
- pollination/reproductive support where needed;
- human/nonhuman microbiome management;
- emergency seed/genetic libraries.

## Critical asset

The most irreplaceable “machine” may be a living microbial/decomposer culture bank or seed stock rather than a reactor.

## Crime/gray economy connection

Black markets can trade:
- unauthorized crop strains;
- stolen symbiont cultures;
- counterfeit disease-free seed stock;
- food-system firmware plus proprietary biological starter cultures.

## Failure case

`power rationing → grow-light cut → fresh crop reduction → reliance on fermentation food → waste chemistry changes → decomposer imbalance → water-treatment load rises`.

The crisis crosses engineering and biology; fixing only power does not instantly restore food.

## Story use

Strong for:
- GA2 ship/frontier logic scaled up;
- GA5 fleet-support habitats;
- ordinary-life stakes under siege.

# CASE ECO-R4 — Damaged Recovery Biosphere

Planning tags:
- Middle/Frontier;
- `ENV-2 or ENV-3 / BIOE-6 / ECO-D / MS0–MS2`.

## Premise

A once-productive inhabited world suffered combined wartime and industrial ecological damage years or decades before the current story.

Damage may include:
- reservoir contamination;
- soil/substrate heavy-metal or chemical burden;
- loss of a keystone mutualist;
- abandoned military propellant/toxic material;
- invasive opportunist released during refugee logistics.

## Important rule

The war is over but ecology is not “healed.”

Residents choose among:
- long restoration;
- partial abandonment;
- sealed agriculture;
- importing food indefinitely;
- risky re-engineering.

## Economy

Creates:
- cleanup contractors;
- ecological debt;
- land-value inequality;
- black markets for uncertified food/soil;
- veteran/refugee claims tied to environmental exposure.

## Political split

- restoration coalition;
- resettlement/compensation bloc;
- industrial interests wanting usable zones reopened;
- local residents who refuse abandonment because home/community matters.

## Military relevance

A commander who “saves” the planet by denying an enemy route can still create a generation-long ecological loss.

This is a useful check against scorched-earth abstraction in GA7.

## Story use

- GA5/7 aftermath;
- GA10 reconstruction example.

# CASE ECO-R5 — Extreme Home Environment / Limited Integration World

Planning tags:
- likely Middle/Frontier historical-majority system;
- `ENV-4 or ENV-5 / BIOE-2/3/X / ECO-X / MS3 locally, MS0–MS1 for visitors`.

## Premise

A technologically mature nonhuman people evolved in an environment that is expensive for most other species to inhabit.

Possible physical version:
- high pressure;
- dense atmosphere;
- unusual temperature;
- fluid or buoyancy support;
- chemistry safe to natives but incompatible with standard mixed spaces.

Exact species assignment remains HOLD.

## Key design rule

Isolation is primarily **logistical**, not because the people are xenophobic or technologically backward.

Visitor infrastructure:
- orbital mixed-service port;
- sealed descent/transfer vehicles;
- limited visitor enclaves;
- remote legal/business interfaces.

Native infrastructure:
- normal, cheap and extensive for the local population;
- what outsiders call “MS3” is simply ordinary home infrastructure there.

## Economy

The world may export:
- specialized biological materials;
- high-pressure industrial products;
- knowledge/services;
- remote/digital work.

It imports less mass tourism because visitor support is costly.

## Political issue

Galactic policy may mistake low physical migration for low engagement or separatism.

Local debates can concern:
- who pays visitor compatibility;
- whether youth should migrate to standard habitats;
- protecting native ecology from imported organisms;
- offworld representation through remote institutions.

## Story use

- later GA5/8 regional encounter;
- demonstrates the galaxy is larger than “human-compatible planets with rubber-forehead aliens.”

## 8. Red-Team Result

PASS if:
- each world contains more than one biome/social constituency;
- ecology drives material cost rather than decorative wildlife;
- advanced technology reduces but does not erase time scales;
- non-sapient life is ecologically ordinary before it is spectacular;
- military damage can persist after a battle;
- extreme worlds are not primitive/exotic stereotypes.

Reject if:
- one monster species defines an entire planet;
- one terraforming machine controls every ecological variable;
- restoration happens within an episode;
- every native organism is useful to settlers;
- environmental politics has one morally pure side.

## 9. Current Verdict

> **PASS AS FRONT-STAGE ECOLOGY PROOF SET.**

Five cases are enough to validate the architecture. Do not create more generic ecology worlds until a specific GA/system requires a distinct function.
