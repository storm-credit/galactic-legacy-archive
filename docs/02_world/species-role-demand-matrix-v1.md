## 1. Matrix purpose

This document answers **what kinds of biological difference the series actually needs** before inventing species names, flags or lore.

Planning rule:

> every core species slot must generate at least one ordinary-life consequence, one infrastructure consequence, one political consequence and one technical/military consequence.

If a slot only changes appearance, remove it.

The current 8-slot test includes humanity as one slot. It is a design sample, not a fixed canon count.

# 3. Core 8-slot test matrix

## S1-01 — Humanity / Human-Derived Spectrum

Status:
- existing setting anchor; exact demographic share HOLD.

Body baseline:
- familiar bipedal human range with extensive natural, medical and engineered variation.

World function:
- provides reader anchor and existing Imperial/Academy history.

Difference engine:
- diversity comes not from one “human culture” but from 612-system regional, class, posthuman and institutional variation.

Infrastructure:
- much current infrastructure was historically optimized around common human ranges, creating political path-dependence rather than universal biological neutrality.

Red-team control:
- humans must not remain the invisible default against which all others are “special.”

GA use:
- GA1 anchor; later acts expose that human-standard infrastructure is historically contingent.

---

## S1-02 — High-Gravity Compact Tetrapod Tool-Users

Working descriptor only; no public name.

Biological premise:
- evolved/engineered under substantially higher gravity;
- compact torso, broad load-bearing pelvis/shoulder girdle;
- four major manipulator/locomotor limbs capable of switching between braced movement and fine work;
- redundant circulation and strong pressure regulation.

Not a “warrior race.”

Strengths:
- acceleration and impact tolerance;
- stable manual work in high-load environments;
- excellent bracing and close industrial control.

Costs:
- high caloric demand;
- heat rejection difficulty;
- chronic joint/circulatory problems in low-g unless actively loaded;
- larger restraint/support loads despite shorter stature.

Infrastructure consequence:
- furniture, ladders, handrails and cockpits designed for human two-arm posture are awkward;
- ships with aggressive acceleration may recruit them, but food/cooling/space costs rise.

Political possibility:
- historical stereotype as soldiers/industrial laborers conflicts with large civilian/artistic/service populations.

Frame consequence:
- shared humanlike frames can be piloted through translated controls, but some close-work frames may justify four-limb mapping or auxiliary control arms.

Story function:
- demonstrates that physiological advantage produces labor exploitation and doctrine assumptions, not destiny.

Suggested first strong window:
- GA2 ship/yard or GA3 industrial city.

## S1-03 — Low-Gravity Long-Limbed Pressure-Sensitive Species

Biological premise:
- low-g habitat/moon ancestry;
- elongated flexible body with three primary locomotor limbs and two fine manipulators;
- extensive pressure/vibration sensory web across skin and internal air/fluid cavities.

Strengths:
- exceptional perception of hull vibration, airflow and structural strain;
- efficient low-g movement;
- delicate remote maintenance and leak detection.

Costs:
- poor sustained high-g tolerance;
- blunt trauma risk;
- pressure transients and loud structural vibration can overload senses;
- requires larger reclining/rest volume despite low body mass.

Infrastructure consequence:
- alarms that use vibration can be painful;
- crash couches must distribute load differently;
- “standing room” has little meaning to them.

Cultural temptation to avoid:
- do not make them universally calm mystics because they sense vibration.

Military consequence:
- useful in damage control, reconnaissance and hull operations;
- weak point if commanders exploit their sensory capacity without respecting acceleration limits.

Frame consequence:
- remote/low-g service frames and sensor craft may suit them better than high-g ace dueling.

GA use:
- GA2/3 as worker, navigator, structural specialist; later evacuation stakes in GA6.

## S1-04 — Amphibious / Fluid-Supported Bilateral Species

Biological premise:
- evolved in dense fluid/shallow-ocean environments with periodic surface/gas exposure;
- can operate in gas atmosphere for limited/assisted periods but requires regular fluid-supported rest and skin/respiratory maintenance.

Body plan:
- bilateral body with two principal manipulators and powerful ventral/caudal locomotor structures;
- tool use does not assume legs.

Strengths:
- pressure tolerance;
- three-dimensional movement in fluid environments;
- resilience to some decompression injuries if correctly supported.

Costs:
- dehydration/skin damage;
- fluid mass is expensive to accelerate aboard ships;
- contamination control complex;
- medical transport requires fluid-support pods.

Infrastructure consequence:
- mixed habitats need wet modules rather than “aquarium rooms” as novelty;
- sanitation/water rights become serious political economy.

Political possibility:
- water-intensive life support makes them vulnerable during embargo/disaster, while their home habitats may be water-rich exporters.

Military consequence:
- boarding ships designed around dry corridors disadvantages them unless equipped;
- excellent in flooded/coolant/industrial environments where others require suits.

Frame consequence:
- cockpit may be fluid-supported, increasing mass but improving acceleration load distribution.

GA use:
- GA3 infrastructure politics, GA5 mixed fleet logistics, GA6 evacuation.

---

## S1-05 — Symbiotic Dual-Organism Person Lineage

Biological premise:
- one legal person normally develops through a lifelong obligate symbiosis between two biologically distinct organisms: a mobile host body and a smaller distributed regulatory/sensory partner.

Important:
- not a hive mind;
- not two personalities by default;
- legal personhood concerns the functioning life-history of the pair, while unusual separations can create hard cases.

Strengths:
- broad chemical sensing;
- strong immune adaptation and environmental memory;
- flexible metabolism.

Costs:
- medicine must treat both partners;
- antibiotics, sterilization and quarantine can save one component while harming the other;
- reproduction/development requires complex care.

Infrastructure consequence:
- medical systems that record “one body = one patient” fail;
- border quarantine law becomes politically important.

Archive consequence:
- identity compression can mistakenly treat symbiont markers as two people, family lineage or equipment provenance.

Military consequence:
- chemical/radiological reconnaissance possibilities, but exposure can damage the symbiont long before host incapacity.

Frame consequence:
- cockpit biomonitoring requires two-layer physiology; no automatic combat advantage.

GA use:
- GA4 law/citizenship, GA8 Archive identity, GA9 classification.

## S1-06 — Slow-Maturing Long-Lived Radial Species

Biological premise:
- approximately radial body plan with five primary limbs around a central torso;
- lifespan several times common human span;
- maturation substantially slower, with long apprenticeship/juvenile phases.

Strengths:
- long institutional memory at individual scale;
- broad manual redundancy;
- stable low-speed endurance.

Costs:
- low replacement rate after war/disaster;
- children/dependents remain vulnerable for long periods;
- fast political institutions can cycle repeatedly within one individual’s adult life;
- high-speed movement/acceleration not exceptional.

Political consequence:
- outsiders may stereotype them as conservative; actual long-lived individuals may instead be radical because they personally remember institutional failures.

Succession consequence:
- terms of office, inheritance, military retirement and punishment lengths cannot simply copy human years.

Military consequence:
- casualty aversion has demographic logic but does not imply cowardice;
- may prefer repair, remote operation and heavily documented doctrine.

Frame consequence:
- five-limb direct mapping is possible for special industrial frames, but common fleet equipment normally uses translated control.

GA use:
- GA4 historical claims, GA5 casualty politics, GA8 living witness, GA10 long-memory transition.

---

## S1-07 — Thermally Variable Burrow/Station Species with Electrosense

Biological premise:
- ancestry in subsurface or enclosed mineral habitats with large thermal cycles;
- low visual dependence, strong electric/magnetic-field and contact sensing;
- compact segmented body with two manipulators and multiple anchoring limbs.

Strengths:
- works in darkness/dust;
- perceives electrical leakage and some machine-state changes directly;
- strong anchoring in microgravity maintenance.

Costs:
- dense EM/noisy machinery can overwhelm perception;
- standard visual interfaces are inefficient;
- thermal regulation requires scheduled warm/cool cycles;
- strong magnetic fields can be physiologically disruptive.

Infrastructure consequence:
- human “quiet” spaces may be electrically deafening to them;
- accessibility includes EM cleanliness and tactile/data channels.

Political/economic possibility:
- stereotyped as engineers because of electrosense, creating occupational channeling and certification discrimination.

Military consequence:
- potentially strong in mine detection, maintenance and close sensor warfare, but enemy electronic jamming can physically harm them.

Frame consequence:
- sensor fusion should translate rather than simply stream raw EM data.

GA use:
- GA2 salvage/maintenance, GA3 production, GA7 EW war.

---

## S1-08 — Developmentally Engineered Free-Lineage Species

Biological premise:
- current self-reproducing biological people descended from an intentionally engineered historical population whose original labor/environment purpose is known or disputed.

This slot is **not** “created slave race” by default; exact origin is HOLD.

Design purpose:
- tests creator-descendant rights, inherited patents, gene ownership and whether designed function defines identity.

Potential biology:
- modular metabolic organs or environment-specific developmental switch;
- robust enough to be a stable people, not dependent on constant creator maintenance.

Strengths/costs:
- depends on final selected adaptation; must include at least one inherited liability caused by original design constraints.

Political consequence:
- competing narratives: engineered heritage as pride, exploitation history, creator claim, or irrelevant ancient fact.

Legal consequence:
- genetic sequence cannot be treated as corporate equipment title;
- “designed purpose” cannot determine employment or military obligation.

Archive consequence:
- old records may classify ancestors as assets/production batches rather than people, creating GA8–9 relevance.

Frame consequence:
- no automatic special machine; any equipment lineage must arise from actual environment or institutions.

GA use:
- GA3 industrial-rights foreshadowing, GA4 legal standing, GA8 record reveal, GA9 classification.

# 5. Major blind spots found before naming

## B-01 — Existing cast visibility

If the galaxy is genuinely multi-species, why does the existing opening cast appear human-coded?

Recommended answer:
- K-13 is a regional/institutional demographic concentration, not a galaxy sample;
- its recruitment, medical infrastructure and local population skew human/posthuman;
- nonhuman citizens are normal elsewhere and may appear in background roles only after explicit continuity review.

Do not retroactively recolor named characters.

## B-02 — Imperial origin

Need to decide whether the Empire:

- began as human-led and incorporated other peoples;
- began as a multi-species covenant;
- inherited an older multi-polity network.

This is a high-ripple author decision. HOLD.

## B-03 — First-contact chronology

If nonhuman species are common, “first contact” cannot be a late-series shock.

Need a chronology layer distinguishing:
- first evidence;
- first physical encounter;
- first trade;
- first diplomatic recognition;
- first citizenship treaty.

## B-04 — Reproduction and mixed heritage

Do not default to universal interbreeding.

Each pair may be:
- biologically impossible;
- possible only through advanced reproductive medicine;
- compatible within some human-derived clades;
- socially recognized as family without genetic mixing.

## B-05 — Language density

Six to eight core species can create noun overload.

Control:
- reader-facing species gets one easy public name + one self-name only when needed;
- do not introduce multiple species names in the same early scene unless plot requires it.

## B-06 — Species power creep

Any biological advantage must be counterweighted by ecology/logistics and must not obsolete trained individuals from other species.

## B-07 — Ancient species temptation

Do not assign current eight slots as descendants of one precursor. Deep-time diversity must remain multiple-source and uncertain.

# 7. Recommendation before naming

Keep the 8-slot matrix as a **stress-test portfolio**.

Next pass should:

1. compare each slot against known SF trope similarity;
2. test evolutionary/engineering plausibility;
3. assign preliminary demographic bands without changing 76.0b total;
4. assign 2–4 candidate home clusters per slot, then reject overconcentration;
5. map infrastructure MS0–MS3 demand;
6. map one ordinary focal, one expert, one political actor and one antagonist/critic per core slot;
7. map frame/ship adaptation only where mechanically justified;
8. create 10–15 S2 regional slots after S1 passes;
9. name only after phonetic and trademark/reference screening.

## 8. Current verdict

> **PASS AS NONCANON ROLE MATRIX.**

The eight slots are intentionally different in biology and world consequence. None is yet a canon species, and none receives a guaranteed state, faction, signature frame or superior technology simply by existing.
