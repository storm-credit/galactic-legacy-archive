## 1. Node Function

Ardis is a phase-reference, traffic-control and authentication system—not a magical portal ring.

A successful lattice transit requires:

1. physical phase-anchor structures aligned and powered;
2. a stable local reference solution;
3. authenticated route/ship identity and slot allocation;
4. energy/thermal reserve for transition and recovery;
5. traffic separation and emergency abort capacity;
6. destination-side reference agreement.

Failure in any layer can cause:
- refused entry/exit;
- misalignment and hull stress;
- traffic collision or emergence error;
- communication/data desynchronization;
- legal/identity rejection;
- hardware damage or complete route loss.

No single key can substitute for physical repair, power, operators and destination cooperation.

## 2. Ardis Node Architecture

### A. Three Phase-Reference Spines

- `Spine A — Civic/Imperial`: most stable hardware, legal authentication and current traffic control.
- `Spine B — Foundry/Service`: physically repairable, old industrial/emergency interface, damaged alignment.
- `Spine C — Transit/Legacy`: partly offline, old AUXILIA/Continuity-compatible service layer, politically suppressed and physically fragmented.

Pre-damage design:
- any two spines could support limited traffic.
- all three provided high-capacity L2-class synchronization and redundancy.

GA3 arrival state:
- Spine A operates at reduced capacity and is overloaded.
- Spine B can support service windows after repair but lacks trusted authentication.
- Spine C contains key historical/emergency functions but cannot safely run alone.

Strategic conflict:
- Imperial/Civic actors prefer A-led restoration.
- Foundry/Open Standard actors prefer A+B with local maintenance control.
- Continuity/legacy actors want A+C or central master synchronization.
- joint trust seeks split functions across all three without one permanent master.

### B. Anchor Crown

Components:
- external phase vanes/field projectors.
- structural trusses and thermal radiators.
- traffic-control arrays.
- authentication/data vaults.
- energy storage and switching.
- crew/operator habitats and emergency locks.

Scale:
- several kilometers of distributed structures, not one easily captured room.

### C. Surface and Orbital Support

- Surface Works provides energy, propellant and thermal mass.
- Foundry fabricates/repairs structural and service parts.
- Garden/Civic systems supply operators and city services.
- ships/tugs/frames align external structures and manage traffic.

Node battle cannot ignore the city economy.

## 3. Current Damage Model

### Damage N-1 — Spine B alignment drift

Cause:
- old impact, overload or deliberate emergency disconnect.

Effect:
- transition windows narrow and require manual correction.
- high risk of asymmetric stress and false emergence solution.

Repair:
- replace/realign external vanes, structural truss and sensor reference.
- requires frames/tugs and local operators.

### Damage N-2 — Spine C segmented power/data loss

Cause:
- old shutdown and parts scavenging.

Effect:
- legacy emergency service layer responds inconsistently.
- some sections physically isolated and legally sealed.

Repair:
- restore power/data in stages; verify old protocols; no full activation before audit.

### Damage N-3 — Common thermal storage deficit

Cause:
- one thermal reservoir/radiator farm lost; remaining system patched.

Effect:
- node cannot sustain repeated transitions, defense and city power simultaneously.

Repair options:
- surface thermal storage expansion.
- temporary ship/industrial heat sinks.
- reduce traffic or city/defense load.

### Damage N-4 — Authentication split

- Civic/Imperial registry, Helix service certificates, Neutral route proofs, local operator keys and old service authorities disagree.

Effect:
- technically viable ships/routes can be rejected or dangerous identities accepted.

Repair:
- multi-signature translation/authority or central master reissue.

### Damage N-5 — Operator workforce collapse

- senior experts overworked; apprentices limited; knowledge compartmented.

Effect:
- restoration depends on individuals and creates hereditary/technocratic monopoly.

Repair:
- training, documentation, simulation and shared procedure; takes months/years.

### Damage N-6 — Defense/control coupling

- fixed batteries and traffic arrays share some sensors/power/control.

Effect:
- combat jamming or security seizure can degrade route safety.

Repair:
- segment networks and authority, but slower coordination.

## 4. Restoration Phases

### Phase 0 — Survival Mode

Capacity:
- 35–46% nominal.

Actions:
- reduce traffic and protect Spine A.
- manage food/medicine/thermal countdown.
- no structural political solution.

### Phase 1 — Diagnostic and Local Stabilization

Requirements:
- shared sensor/provenance model.
- external inspection via frames/tugs.
- worker/operator access and public failure state.

Reward:
- accurate damage map and slightly larger safe window.

Cost:
- exposes hidden damage, claims and old records.

### Phase 2 — Spine B Service Restoration

Requirements:
- Open Service/07/ship relay package.
- Foundry parts and operators.
- temporary authentication bridge.

Reward:
- alternative low-capacity traffic and external repair access.

Cost:
- open-standard risk, Helix/Imperial opposition and physical vulnerability.

### Phase 3 — Thermal and City-Service Decision

Requirements:
- choose energy/heat allocation between:
  - food/air/medical;
  - industrial production;
  - defense;
  - node transitions.

No solution maximizes all.

Reward:
- sustained local operations or military/traffic advantage.

Cost:
- one district/service bears shortage or delay.

### Phase 4 — Spine C Recovery / Legacy Audit

Requirements:
- Service Authority D + additional local/legacy/operator access.
- physical repair, consent to inspect suppressed systems and S-001 provenance.

Reward:
- emergency fallback, historical evidence and path toward multi-spine control.

Cost:
- Continuity protocol detection and political struggle.

### Phase 5 — Joint Reference Operation

Requirements:
- A/B/C spines run limited synchronized window under split authority.
- operators, Civic, workers, transit users, defense and external guarantors agree on traffic/abort rules.

Reward:
- stable enough for regional route and GA3 survival.

Cost:
- slower decision, limited throughput and contested access.

### Phase 6 — L2 Candidate, not completed in GA3

Requires:
- months/years of capital, training, external route agreements and political recognition.

GA3 can achieve:
- safe regional operations and a credible path, not full instant L2 restoration.

## 5. Control and Key Layers

### Physical control

- access to trusses, power, coolant, modules and operator stations.

### Operational control

- live traffic solution and technical stop.

### Authentication control

- decide which ship/data/identity is trusted.

### Political control

- choose priorities, users, prices, exclusions and emergency powers.

### Military control

- fixed defenses, approach zones and denial/destruction.

### Economic control

- supply, wages, repair, insurance and debt.

Joint trust principle:
- these layers cannot all belong to one actor.

Emergency override:
- one central key can temporarily coordinate faster, but:
  - creates trace and accountability;
  - expires automatically unless renewed by multiple actors;
  - cannot repair physical damage or create missing energy;
  - is vulnerable to capture/spoofing and institutional expansion.

This is an early model for later Continuity Seed conflict.

## 6. Why Maneuver Frames Matter at Ardis

Frames are necessary for:

- precise work on rotating/nonrotating anchor structures.
- moving between trusses, vanes, service cavities and damaged docking arms.
- carrying/holding parts while maintaining relative position.
- cutting/repairing under high electromagnetic/thermal noise.
- capturing or disabling defense/sensor points without destroying node.
- rescue and local security around habitats/industrial zones.

Ships cannot replace them because:
- large thruster plumes and weapon effects risk structures.
- ships cannot enter service geometry.
- capture and repair require articulated manipulation.
- close infrastructure creates sensor/line-of-fire constraints.

Drones cannot fully replace pilots because:
- lattice noise and jamming.
- authentication/network trust.
- unpredictable damage and human/local judgment.
- adversarial control of infrastructure.

Frames do not replace ships because:
- they lack endurance, long-range sensors/weapons, transport and strategic movement.

## 7. Node Battle Layers

A major GA3 conflict should operate across five linked layers:

### Layer 1 — Regional Approach

- fleets/convoys, node queues, blockade, reinforcement timing.
- command and support ships determine who reaches the city.

### Layer 2 — Anchor Crown Exterior

- frames, tugs, point defenses, service structures and sabotage.
- capture/repair/disabling objectives.

### Layer 3 — Node Interior and Operator Control

- human/utility-frame teams, authentication, power and technical stop.
- physical access and local operators decisive.

### Layer 4 — City Services and Districts

- food, medical, power, riots/strikes, transit and evacuation.
- battle choices change civilian survival and legitimacy.

### Layer 5 — Records and Public Authority

- identity, orders, claims, broadcast and who can lawfully command.
- forged/late information alters alliances.

P-001 can command one layer or coordinate links but cannot personally control all five.

## 8. Weapons and Infrastructure Rules

### Fixed batteries

- effective at denying predictable approach corridors.
- limited ammunition/heat and coupled sensors.
- cannot fire through habitats/relay structures safely.

### Missiles

- strong in open approach, weaker near dense structures with interception/civilian risk.
- node destruction threat can deter but also delegitimize actors.

### Lasers/beam weapons

- line-of-sight and thermal constraints.
- effective against sensors/radiators but risk cascading infrastructure damage.

### Kinetic weapons

- debris and orbital persistence make use near city politically/physically dangerous.

### Electronic/cyber warfare

- affects classification, keys and traffic.
- physical/local fallback prevents instant total hack.
- bad data can be more dangerous than direct shutdown.

### Scuttling/denial

- several partial-denial options:
  - destroy a spine;
  - misalign route;
  - dump thermal storage;
  - erase keys;
  - damage anchor geometry.
- no single “red button” destroys entire node safely.
- denial still kills/displaces and can create long-term hazards.

## 10. Key Technical Choices

### Choice A — Traffic vs city services

- more transitions bring supplies/revenue but consume heat/energy and risk failure.

### Choice B — Defense vs open infrastructure

- stronger centralized security improves response but creates seizure risk.

### Choice C — Certified closed repair vs provisional open repair

- certified slower/controlled; open flexible/risky.

### Choice D — Repair one spine deeply vs multiple shallow fallbacks

- deep repair efficient but concentrated.
- shallow plural systems resilient but lower capacity.

Recommended GA3 direction:
- restore Spine B and limited Spine C fallback, keep Spine A, achieving plural low/medium capacity rather than one maximum-output spine.

### Choice E — L2 ambition vs local stability

- pursuing L2 status attracts capital, fleets and obligations.
- remaining L3 limits growth and regional independence.

GA3 ending:
- choose candidate path with explicit local control and future review, not instant upgrade.

## 11. Failure and Casualty Logic

Node failure can cause:
- stranded ships/people and route isolation.
- structural/thermal damage.
- decompression/fire in Anchor Crown.
- loss of food/medical supply.
- dangerous emergence accidents.
- identity/authentication collapse.

It should not routinely cause:
- arbitrary planet explosion.
- instant universe-wide effects.
- every ship in route disappearing with no technical chain.

Casualties:
- tied to specific structures, traffic, shifts and decisions.
- local operators/workers/residents are named agents, not background numbers.
