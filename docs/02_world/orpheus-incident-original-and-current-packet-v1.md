## 1. Core Design Rule

Orpheus is not “save soldiers or save innocent civilians.”

Both sides contain:
- civilians and dependents;
- state/route/medical/record institutions;
- military crews and weapons;
- people who chose and people who were compelled;
- long-term consequences beyond immediate body count.

The choice is between:
- preserving the state’s ability to coordinate the wider war and services;
- preserving the largest immediate vulnerable population;
- attempting a distributed third plan that saves many but destroys strategic assets, accepts slower war and still loses people.

No option saves everyone.
No late superweapon or master key creates a clean fifth answer.

# PART I — Original Orpheus Incident

## 2. Original Timeline Location

Working site:
- `Orpheus Junction`, a damaged L1/L2 military-civil node on the approach to the capital corridor.

Original-time context:
- imperial succession/civil war had already fragmented commands.
- P-001 was acting admiral of an irregular coalition/fleet.
- a capital/state fleet and institutional convoy attempted retreat/reposition through Orpheus.
- refugee/civilian fleets accumulated from collapsed routes.
- enemy/hostile forces and node damage narrowed viable transit windows.

## 3. Original Forces and Populations — Working Reconstruction

### State/Capital Group — O-STATE

- 58 combat ships.
- 31 support, hospital, command, archive and logistics ships.
- approximately 220,000 military crews and embarked forces.
- approximately 410,000 civil servants, medical staff, technicians, families and institutional evacuees.
- total people: approximately 630,000.

Critical functions carried:
- General Staff/theater command core.
- Treasury/payment and identity backups.
- 3 major hospital ships and medical production staff.
- route-authentication and node technicians.
- war reserve data, spare command keys and industrial specialists.

Strategic forecast at the time:
- if O-STATE survives, capital defense and several regions can continue 6–14 months longer.
- if destroyed/captured, projected wider deaths over following year rise by millions, but estimates depend on institutional assumptions.

### Refugee/Route Group — O-CIV

- 360–520 civilian, merchant, habitat, tug and improvised transport hulls.
- approximately 1.35–1.75 million people.
- 55–65% civilians displaced by war.
- 10–15% medical/elderly/children requiring priority support.
- remaining population includes merchant crews, local militia, workers, families, unregistered people and some infiltrators/armed groups.

Critical functions carried:
- food/repair/medical labor.
- provincial/community leadership and records.
- surviving populations of several lost habitats.
- merchants and route operators.

Weakness:
- uneven life support, poor formation discipline, incompatible identities and insufficient escort.

### P-001 Coalition — O-P001

- 35–50 combat-capable ships, many damaged.
- 25–40 support/rescue ships.
- limited ammunition and repair.
- P-001 held effective battlefield authority but not uncontested political/legal control.

### Hostile Pressure — O-HOST

- 70–110 combat ships across enemy/rejecter forces, not all able to enter node zone simultaneously.
- sabotage/infiltration threat inside civilian traffic.
- long-range fire and route denial rather than simple annihilation battle.

## 4. Original Node Windows

Reconstructed viable windows over approximately 21 hours:

### Window 1 — 3.5 hours

Capacity:
- enough for most O-STATE if prioritized and tightly controlled.
- or approximately 350,000–500,000 civilian-equivalent transit mass.

Risk:
- low/moderate before hostile fire intensifies.

### Window 2 — 2.0 hours after 5–7 hour instability

Capacity:
- approximately 45–60% of Window 1.

Risk:
- high; route solution vulnerable to sabotage and thermal overload.

### Window 3 — uncertain 1.0–1.8 hours

Capacity:
- approximately 20–35% of Window 1.

Risk:
- extreme; depended on emergency node work and sacrificing thermal/defense capacity.

Original official model:
- Window 1 to O-STATE.
- remaining coalition/escorts hold node long enough for partial Window 2 civilian passage.

What happened:
- O-STATE received Window 1 priority.
- civilian classification/authentication was restricted after infiltration warning.
- Window 2 opened late and at lower capacity.
- hostile action/node failure collapsed most Window 2/3 rescue.
- large civilian population was stranded, scattered, captured or killed.

## 5. P-001’s Original Decision

P-001 decision:
- support O-STATE Window 1 and deploy coalition to protect state fleet transit.
- accept that uncontrolled civilian entry could collapse the route or bring hostile elements through.
- plan a later partial rescue/holding action.

Why rational:
- O-STATE contained irreplaceable command, medical and authentication systems.
- immediate models predicted state survival would prevent wider deaths.
- civilian fleet could not physically pass in one window.
- hostile infiltration warning was credible.
- P-001 lacked authority over node operators, civilian traffic and state commanders.

Why responsible:
- he chose to concentrate escorts and information on O-STATE.
- he accepted authentication restrictions and did not force an earlier limited mixed transit.
- he withheld uncertainty from civilians/crews to prevent panic.
- he assumed later rescue capacity would exist despite weak evidence.

## 6. Original Hidden/Systemic Truth

There was no secret perfect route.

But a limited alternative existed:

- Window 1 could have been mixed: approximately 70–80% O-STATE plus 180,000–320,000 high-risk civilians/critical route workers.
- a lower-output node configuration could have preserved more Window 2 probability at the cost of delaying and fragmenting O-STATE.
- several heavy state ships could have been abandoned/scuttled to free thermal/traffic capacity and rescue crews/passengers.
- neutral/merchant ships were denied earlier physical access because state security feared infiltration and loss of control.
- an information office exaggerated confidence in capital-fleet necessity and suppressed uncertainty about later civilian rescue.
- Neutral/route actors had debts and prior seizure history that made them refuse unlimited risk.

P-001 did not know all of this.
He knew enough uncertainty to be morally responsible for choosing the clearer centralized plan, but not enough to be a sole murderer.

## 7. Original Outcome Envelope

Estimated immediate outcomes:

O-STATE:
- 70–85% of people and key institutions survive transit.

O-CIV:
- 350,000–600,000 pass/escape through mixed routes.
- 300,000–600,000 killed, captured or permanently missing within weeks.
- 400,000–700,000 stranded/dispersed with later high mortality.

P-001 coalition:
- severe losses; later history credits/condemns actions under simplified labels.

Long-term:
- state survives longer but still loses war/order.
- Archive classifies the event as a continuity success or less-bad outcome because institutions persist.
- survivors remember abandonment rather than modelled future lives.

## 11. Option Models

### OPTION 1 — State Continuity Priority

Plan:
- Window A mostly S-STATE.
- Common fleet defends node and organizes partial civilian B/C/Grey transit.

Immediate likely outcome:
- 700,000–900,000 S-STATE people/institutions survive.
- 600,000–1.0 million civilians pass/escape through later/side routes.
- 700,000–1.3 million civilians killed, captured, missing or stranded at lethal risk.
- common fleet suffers heavy but controlled losses.

Long-term benefit:
- Compact command, medicine, payroll and war effort remain coherent.
- projected downstream deaths lower in central models.

Long-term risk:
- legitimacy collapse, rebellion, route/community refusal and moral repetition of original Orpheus.
- forecasts may overvalue institutions and undervalue distributed alternatives.

Main supporters:
- Protector, Regent/service institutions, Continuity Custodian, some civilians dependent on systems.

### OPTION 2 — Civilian Priority

Plan:
- Window A/B prioritize highest-risk civilian groups and route workers.
- S-STATE fragments, delays and fights/uses side routes.

Immediate likely outcome:
- 1.4–2.0 million civilians pass/escape.
- 300,000–700,000 civilians still die/captured/stranded.
- 250,000–500,000 state/military/institutional people killed/captured/stranded.
- 18–35 major combat/support ships lost or abandoned.

Long-term benefit:
- immediate human survival and political legitimacy; communities/workforce preserved.

Long-term risk:
- Compact/fleet command, hospitals, Registry/Treasury and industrial coordination fragment.
- wider war may last longer or be lost; downstream deaths may exceed immediate savings.

Main supporters:
- civilian/Transit/Humanitarian/Independence blocs, some provincial forces, H-001/M-001 leanings under conditions.

### OPTION 3 — Distributed Sacrifice / Mixed Evacuation

Plan components:

1. Window A mixed but state fleet stripped/fractured rather than passed intact.
2. transfer institutional data/personnel/functions across many smaller ships and Ardis/provincial nodes.
3. abandon/scuttle 12–24 heavy combat/support hulls to create thermal/traffic/defense capacity.
4. use Common Fleet Charter formations on separate fronts rather than one escort mass.
5. preserve Window B probability by limiting A output.
6. force/open Grey routes with H-001/H-002/S-001/M-001 infrastructure.
7. local shelter/holdout for groups who cannot transit.
8. accept loss of central command and strategic initiative.

Likely immediate outcome:
- 1.55–2.15 million civilians escape/survive immediate phase.
- 520,000–760,000 S-STATE people/functions escape in distributed form.
- 450,000–850,000 total people killed/captured/missing/left at severe risk across all groups.
- 25–45 combat/support ships destroyed/abandoned, including high-value hulls/tenders.

Long-term benefit:
- more immediate lives than Option 1 and more institutions than Option 2.
- plural infrastructure survives in several regions.
- political legitimacy strengthened among civilians/provinces.

Long-term cost:
- central military/administrative capacity collapses or shrinks sharply.
- war fronts lose support; enemy gains initiative.
- distributed services are slower and unequal.
- some regions/people are effectively abandoned.
- no guarantee downstream deaths are lower.

This is the recommended direction, but it is not a clean best answer.

### OPTION 4 — Central Continuity Override

Plan:
- Custodian/Protector uses master emergency coordination to schedule routes, assign ships/people and suppress refusal.

Likely outcome:
- highest modeled throughput and lower immediate chaos if obeyed.
- can optimize a mixed evacuation better than human coalition.

Cost:
- removes consent, freezes identities/movement, treats ships/people as continuity assets and creates preservation-regime precedent.

Narrative role:
- credible tempting alternative, not final chosen answer.
- P-001 may use a narrow bounded part but refuses full override.

## 12. Recommended P-001 Choice

P-001 supports/commands a version of Option 3 under time-limited charter authority.

Required sacrifices:

- orders or accepts abandonment/scuttling of strategic state/charter ships, possibly including a beloved/important vessel or first ship module.
- distributes command/institution personnel instead of preserving one capital fleet.
- prioritizes civilians and critical services through mixed criteria rather than hero/future value.
- allows local/ship/community refusals within standing constraints.
- accepts that one front/group will receive insufficient rescue.
- refuses full Continuity override even after delay/chaos kills people.

He is not absolved:
- his allocation causes named deaths and wider war costs.
- survivors from all sides can condemn him.
- future models cannot prove he chose best.
