## 1. Purpose

The original S2 pool contains 15 functional ideas. The series does **not** need all fifteen to become recurring named peoples.

Target:
- 8–12 strong S2 regional peoples;
- 3–5 S3 rare/isolated/extinct/disputed peoples;
- additional background diversity only when a front-stage region needs it.

Selection criteria:
1. not redundant with S1;
2. ordinary-life consequence;
3. distinct infrastructure demand;
4. different political/economic story function;
5. memorable without one-trait caricature;
6. can appear in a concrete GA rather than only a codex.

## 3. Active S2 Portfolio

### S2-A1 — Cryothermal Slow-Metabolism People

Biological demand:
- low-temperature native/engineered ecology;
- metabolic rate can slow substantially during cold rest but does not equal suspended animation.

Infrastructure:
- cooler housing;
- food/medication timing;
- mixed-habitat heat burden;
- emergency warming protocols.

Story engine:
- remote cold industry and long-route shift scheduling;
- wage/time disputes when human labor law assumes one sleep/work cycle.

Anti-cliché:
- not emotionally “cold.”

Suggested window:
- GA2 frontier route or GA5 logistics.

### S2-A2 — Heat-Shedding Membrane People

Biology:
- high-temperature ancestry;
- large vascularized cooling surfaces that can fold/protect.

Infrastructure:
- hotter comfortable spaces;
- humidity/airflow critical;
- narrow cold tolerance.

Story engine:
- industrial heat zones;
- shared-habitat energy politics: one group's comfortable temperature is another's medical risk.

Anti-cliché:
- not “desert nomads” by default.

### S2-A3 — Microbody Tool Users

Biology:
- adult mass perhaps 8–20 kg provisional;
- high surface-area/heat-loss and injury issues.

Infrastructure:
- rail gaps, doors, seats, safety sensors, crowd crush, equipment force feedback.

Story engine:
- accessibility and scale;
- cargo/passenger economics;
- security scanners and prisons built for larger bodies.

Control:
- never comic mascot species.

### S2-A4 — Macrobody Low-Acceleration People

Biology:
- adult mass several hundred kilograms;
- low native gravity or buoyant support means body size does not imply Earth-elephant strength.

Infrastructure:
- floor load;
- berth volume;
- rescue cranes;
- acceleration limits;
- medical imaging.

Story engine:
- shipping and evacuation economics.

Control:
- no “giant brute” stereotype as core identity.

### S2-A5 — Acoustic-Primary People

Biology:
- echolocation / active acoustic spatial mapping is dominant.

Infrastructure:
- acoustic privacy;
- noise pollution;
- silent emergency alarms fail them differently;
- stealth architecture becomes accessibility issue.

Story engine:
- privacy, surveillance and ship noise.

Control:
- acoustic sense has clutter/range limits.

### S2-A6 — Wide-Spectrum / Polarization Vision People

Biology:
- sees polarization and broader spectral information than human baseline.

Infrastructure:
- public screens/lighting;
- visual evidence can contain information invisible to humans;
- some human lighting can be visually noisy.

Story engine:
- evidence standards, art, interface design.

Control:
- no automatic lie detection or perfect hidden-object vision.

### S2-A7 — Seasonal Metamorphic People

Biology:
- recurring adult-life morphology/physiology shift driven by long biological cycle, not one irreversible juvenile metamorphosis.

Infrastructure:
- clothing;
- medicine;
- body ID;
- work eligibility;
- housing.

Story engine:
- identity law when one person's biometric profile changes drastically on schedule.

Control:
- one legal person across forms unless a specific continuity case says otherwise.

### S2-A8 — Short-Lived Rapid-Generation People

Biology:
- provisional adult life around 20–40 standard years, maturation faster than humans.

Infrastructure/society:
- education compressed;
- retirement/inheritance/term limits differ;
- generation turnover faster.

Story engine:
- institutional memory versus biological generation change;
- a treaty remembered by humans may be several generations old to them.

Control:
- short life does not mean impulsive or less attached to long projects; institutions can outlive individuals.

### S2-A9 — Mineral-Shell Renewal People

Biology:
- grows/renews a protective mineralized external layer using environmental minerals;
- shell is living/maintained body tissue, not equipment.

Infrastructure:
- dietary/mineral supply;
- seating/contact surfaces;
- medical shell repair;
- scanners that mistake shell for armor.

Story engine:
- body/equipment boundary in law and security.

Control:
- shell is not bulletproof combat armor.

### S2-A10 — Dense-Atmosphere Buoyant People

Biology:
- evolved in dense atmosphere where partial buoyancy supports body;
- body plan poorly suited to ordinary thin-gas ship compartments without support.

Infrastructure:
- dedicated pressure/density habitat;
- heavy atmospheric gas mass;
- transport pods;
- large transition cost to space travel.

Story engine:
- shows why a technologically capable civilization can remain regionally concentrated: spaceflight integration itself is expensive.

Control:
- not primitive or isolationist by personality.

Suggested window:
- later regional act, not early cast.

## 5. S3 Design Principles

S3 is not “secret final boss species.”

Functions allowed:
- deep-time history;
- ecology;
- migration;
- failed civilization;
- isolation due physical infrastructure cost;
- disputed identity/provenance;
- current tiny populations.

Forbidden:
- one precursor species invented lattice, AI, frames, Archive, Seed and AXIOM;
- extinction equals moral superiority;
- lost technology solves current production constraints;
- late alien invasion changes the genre after GA8.

## 6. S3 Portfolio Candidates

### S3-01 — Extinct Civic/Infrastructure Civilization

Status:
- extinct biological people; material culture survives.

Distinctive function:
- ruins reveal public infrastructure, ordinary households, maintenance failures and political choices rather than only weapons.

Cause of extinction:
- HOLD, but must be multi-causal/material and not a mystical disappearance.

Story use:
- GA8 archive/archaeology.

Critical control:
- their technology can be advanced in some fields and obsolete/impractical in others.

### S3-02 — High-Pressure Isolated Living Civilization

Status:
- living; low galaxy population/contact due extreme habitat transition cost.

Biology:
- high-pressure/dense-atmosphere life;
- ordinary lattice ship interfaces expensive.

Story use:
- demonstrates that political isolation can be logistical rather than ideological.

Difference from S2-A10:
- if A10 survives as regional integrated people, S3-02 should be more extreme and only lightly connected; otherwise merge them.

### S3-03 — Diaspora Remnant with Lost/Uninhabitable Homeland

Status:
- living small population distributed across several systems;
- home biosphere destroyed or no longer accessible.

Story function:
- identity without pure-blood homeland;
- ecological loss and claims politics;
- tests whether Archive defines a people by extinct territory.

Control:
- do not make them culturally frozen refugees for centuries.

### S3-04 — Disputed Classification People

Status:
- living or recently extinct group whose official taxonomy is contested: species, engineered clade, composite lineage or several related peoples.

Story function:
- GA8/9 classification conflict;
- exposes why administrative labels are not ontological truth.

Control:
- dispute must have biological/historical evidence, not “maybe they are secretly gods.”

## 8. Reader-Facing Quantity Control

The galaxy can contain more species than the reader must memorize.

### Active memory budget

GA1:
- 0–1 nonhuman public species names foregrounded.

GA2:
- 2 S1 names active;
- max 1 S2 regional name if needed.

GA3:
- 3–4 S1 active;
- 1–2 S2 active.

GA4–7:
- reuse > introduction;
- no more than 5–6 species names central in one subact.

GA8:
- S3 opens;
- max 2 new deep-time names in one 25-episode block.

GA9–10:
- new species creation nearly stops; payoff known diversity.

## 9. Visual Atlas Rule

A future species visual atlas should group by:
- body scale;
- support environment;
- movement;
- sensory emphasis;
- clothing/equipment adaptation.

Do not group as:
- good aliens / bad aliens;
- Empire species / rebel species;
- warrior / merchant / scientist species.
