## 2. Distribution Principle

Do not spread species evenly by percentage.

A believable lattice uses overlapping settlement histories:

1. old metropolitan mixing;
2. historical-majority homelands/diasporas;
3. industry/environment-driven concentrations;
4. route migration;
5. refugee/postwar redistribution;
6. infrastructure-compatible clustering.

Therefore:
- a species can be common in a city but rare in the macroregion;
- a species-majority system can still be politically mixed;
- a biologically diverse population can live in one standard environment if bodies are compatible;
- a small population with extreme needs can drive disproportionate MS2/MS3 infrastructure.

## 3. Core Region Pattern

Canon base:
- 24 systems / 24.0b;
- old anchors, capital, archives, high industry.

Recommended diversity profile:
- very high demographic mixing in major habitats;
- strong immigrant/diaspora presence from most S1 peoples;
- comparatively few systems whose political identity is species-majority;
- historical human-default infrastructure remains visible in old districts and institutions.

Service profile:
- MS1 expected almost everywhere in major inhabited zones;
- MS2 concentrated but common enough that most Core systems have multiple specialist centers;
- MS3 available at a few flagship medical/transit/research facilities, not every neighborhood.

Ecology profile:
- high share of heavily engineered habitats and old transformed worlds;
- native biospheres, where present, are intensively regulated;
- imported species and invasive-life controls are politically mature.

Conflict engines:
- old building standards versus modern access law;
- expensive specialist service hidden behind wealth/class;
- historical districts protected for heritage but physically exclusionary;
- central medical capacity drawing patients away from provinces.

Story use:
- GA4, GA9, GA10.

## 4. Inner Region Pattern

Canon base:
- 96 systems / 25.0b;
- mature colonies, shipyards, agriculture, administration.

Recommended diversity profile:
- highest combination of old settlement populations + large diasporas;
- several historical-majority systems plausible;
- mixed industrial cities common;
- many cross-species households/workplaces.

Service profile:
- MS1 routine in regional cities and trunk ports;
- MS2 common near medical, yard, university and transport centers;
- smaller settlements may offer only MS0/MS1 plus transfer guarantees.

Ecology profile:
- widest mix of mature naturalized/agricultural/engineered ecosystems;
- long-running invasive-species disputes;
- food export worlds must distinguish native, imported and closed-loop production.

Conflict engines:
- accommodation cost in older industrial infrastructure;
- labor channeling by physiology;
- agricultural biochemistry and food-label standards;
- local pride versus Core certification.

Story use:
- GA2 transit, GA3 support flows, GA4 succession constituencies.

## 5. Middle Region Pattern

Canon base:
- 180 systems / 18.0b;
- mixed industry, regional capitals, military corridors.

Recommended diversity profile:
- greatest system-to-system variation;
- some strongly mixed capitals, some historical-majority worlds, some industry-built demographic clusters;
- ideal region for nonhuman-majority polities that are still integrated into wider Imperial/Neutral systems.

Service profile:
- MS1 variable but expected in major L2 cities;
- MS2 often one or two regional hubs rather than universally available;
- transport to specialist care becomes a political/logistics issue.

Ecology profile:
- mix of native biospheres, partial terraforming, sealed cities and extraction settlements;
- ecological regulation varies sharply by polity.

Conflict engines:
- who pays for regional MS2 capacity;
- military requisition of specialist transports;
- ecological externalities exported to poorer habitats;
- local standards versus Imperial/Helix certification.

Story use:
- GA3, GA5, GA7.

## 6. Frontier Region Pattern

Canon base:
- 312 systems / 9.0b;
- small nodes, resource worlds, mobile settlements, infrastructure gaps.

Recommended diversity profile:
- **not uniformly diverse** despite high travel/migration;
- individual settlements may be nearly single-environment/species because supporting multiple biologies is expensive;
- neighboring habitats can have completely different demographics;
- mobile/irregular populations are proportionally more visible.

Service profile:
- MS0 common minimum at recognized ports;
- MS1 concentrated in major route/service hubs;
- MS2 often portable, contract-based or reached by transfer;
- MS3 normally absent outside specialized settlements/home environments.

Ecology profile:
- highest share of unfinished terraforming, closed habitats, extraction damage, fragile imported ecologies and native-life contact zones;
- ecological failure can strand a settlement before military help arrives.

Conflict engines:
- “unsupported passenger” pricing and discrimination;
- emergency refuge incompatible with physiology;
- black-market medicine/adapters where lawful supply is absent;
- local autonomy because Core rules arrive without infrastructure funding;
- conservation versus survival extraction.

Story use:
- GA2 strongest; GA5–7 route war; GA8 isolated/deep-time sites.

## 7. Free Route / Mobile Layer

Not a fifth census region.

Population:
- drawn from the ~6b irregular/mobile/stateless layer plus registered mobile workers/passengers.

Demographic character:
- can be more visibly mixed than many fixed frontier settlements;
- mobility selects for people whose service needs are supported by route infrastructure or who can afford portable adaptations.

Service profile:
- Neutral/major commercial ports can become MS1/MS2 islands;
- small ships may only support a declared manifest envelope.

Political consequence:
- broad compatibility becomes economic power;
- route networks can discriminate through service capability without formally denying citizenship.

## 8. Species Placement Rules

Before assigning a core species to a region, answer:

1. Is this a homeland, old diaspora, labor migration, refugee movement, military settlement or ordinary metropolitan population?
2. Which service band makes settlement viable?
3. What industry/resource/history explains concentration?
4. What other species live there?
5. What internal political disagreement exists?
6. Does population concentration create a sovereign polity, local district, or no political unity at all?

No species receives:
- one region exclusively;
- one universal home polity;
- one faction alignment.

## 9. Working S1 Distribution Tendencies — NONCANON ONLY

These are broad placement pressures, not ethnic maps.

### Braxi
Likely visible in:
- Inner/Middle high-industry corridors;
- Core metropolitan diaspora;
- selected higher-gravity settlements.

Avoid:
- making all high-gravity industry Braxi-owned.

### Seia
Likely visible in:
- low-g habitats across Inner/Middle/Frontier;
- route communities where acceleration policy is managed.

Avoid:
- concentrating all navigation jobs under Seia.

### Numar
Likely visible in:
- systems with mature wet-support infrastructure;
- some water-rich historical-majority settlements;
- Core/Inner specialist districts.

Avoid:
- “Numar only live on ocean planets.”

### Ivra
Likely broad diaspora if shared atmosphere is compatible.

Primary concentration driver:
- medical/quarantine law and historical migration rather than habitat physics alone.

### Tesar
Likely broad long-established urban/provincial populations.

Primary concentration driver:
- long historical settlement and institution continuity, not special environment.

### Kelik
Likely visible in:
- station/habitat networks;
- Middle/Frontier industrial clusters;
- Core/Inner urban populations with EM-access standards.

Avoid:
- all mining/engineering worlds becoming Kelik-coded.

### Verin
Placement depends on unresolved ontology/history.

Safe current rule:
- diaspora can be broad;
- no “creator colony” or homeworld is assigned until classification/history is approved.

## 10. Ecology Service Cross-Tags

Future front-stage system expansion may use two independent tag families.

### Ecology tag
- ECO-N: substantially native biosphere;
- ECO-M: mixed/native + imported managed ecology;
- ECO-T: major terraformed/engineered planetary ecology;
- ECO-H: sealed/closed habitat ecology;
- ECO-D: damaged/recovery ecology;
- ECO-X: extreme/rare biology environment.

### Multi-species service tag
- MS0 baseline access;
- MS1 common mixed service;
- MS2 specialist regional capability;
- MS3 rare/extreme dedicated capability.

Example planning description:

`Middle L2 city / ECO-M / MS2 regional hub`

This is enough for author-side planning without inventing exact species percentages.

## 11. Front-Stage System Minimum Packet

When one of the 612 systems becomes front-stage, derive:

- ecology tag;
- service tag;
- dominant settlement pattern P1–P4;
- 2–4 demographic constituencies, not necessarily species;
- one service bottleneck;
- one ecological dependency;
- one ordinary-life public space;
- one crisis failure mode;
- one institution responsible for repair/appeal.

Only after that consider exact species percentages.

## 12. Red-Team Findings

### RT-01 — Core diversity can erase historical path dependence
Keep visible old human-standard buildings/systems alongside modern adapters and contested retrofits.

### RT-02 — Frontier can become “alien zoo”
Do not use Frontier as dumping ground for every exotic species. Extreme biology may actually reduce mobility.

### RT-03 — ecological tags can become biome hats
One ECO tag is a planning dominant condition, not “the whole planet is one biome.”

### RT-04 — MS3 can become luxury-only shorthand
Some MS3 facilities may be public/home-community infrastructure, not elite tech.

### RT-05 — species-majority does not mean political unity
Maintain faction/culture/house/class separation.

## 13. Verdict

> **PASS FOR NONCANON MACROREGION SCAFFOLD.**

The 612-system canon remains untouched. The new layer gives future system expansion a deterministic way to derive multi-species and ecological depth without pre-writing 612 species censuses or 612 biospheres.
