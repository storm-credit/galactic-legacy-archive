## 1. Core Visual Principle

Visual identity must reveal:

- what a place or object does;
- who maintains it;
- what resources are scarce;
- which authority controls access;
- how old layers remain visible;
- how ordinary people modify official systems.

Prohibited defaults:

- smooth white science-fiction surfaces everywhere;
- faction identity reduced to one color;
- poverty represented only by dirt;
- wealth represented only by gold;
- every military object shaped for intimidation regardless of function;
- culturally coded clothing with no pressure, gravity, work or maintenance logic;
- one-to-one resemblance to a recognizable franchise silhouette.

## 2. Shared Material Vocabulary

### Structural materials

- matte ceramic-metal panels around heat, radiation and pressure boundaries;
- exposed replacement ribs and bolted repair plates in long-lived infrastructure;
- woven restraint fabric, soft storage and handholds in inhabited microgravity spaces;
- translucent service membranes around clean medical and precision-industrial zones;
- layered radiator surfaces that visually change with thermal state;
- painted ownership, inspection and hazard marks accumulated rather than perfectly replaced.

### Wear vocabulary

- polished grip zones where hands/boots repeatedly contact;
- mismatched panel tone from service generations;
- abrasion around cargo paths;
- faded legal/insurance seals beneath newer authorities;
- locally fabricated brackets visible beside certified modules;
- emergency patches that remain because removal would require shutdown.

Rule:
- wear communicates use history, not moral worth.

## 3. Color and Marking Logic

Color is functional before symbolic.

- orange/amber: heat, temporary hazard, active maintenance;
- blue/cyan: breathable-water/medical service paths, not universal goodness;
- red/magenta: isolation, weapons-safe boundaries, emergency denial;
- green: life-support/agriculture or cleared route state;
- white: inspection/clean work or unassigned authority, not purity;
- black: optical/thermal treatment, sealed evidence or refusal—not automatic villain coding;
- yellow: movement/cargo/impact hazard;
- violet: high-energy/field or archive provenance only where locally established.

Faction colors appear through combinations, materials and placement rather than recoloring whole objects.

## 4. Imperial Visual System

### Silhouette

- axial symmetry and repeated modular bays;
- visible hierarchy through access depth and protected central spines;
- long horizontal/vertical sightlines in administrative spaces;
- older anchors contain ceremonial voids around functional control rooms.

### Materials

- high-quality standardized panels;
- dark service frames behind pale inspection surfaces;
- durable woven uniforms with replaceable rank/office tabs.

### Clothing

- office authority shown by collar/wrist authentication fields and document pockets, not capes;
- military uniforms prioritize restraint harness compatibility, pressure-seal interfaces and layered duty modules;
- provincial personnel mix standardized core pieces with local repair and climate layers.

### Internal variation

- Crown/Continuity offices: restrained, high maintenance, low visible clutter;
- provincial forces: more repair marks and regional insignia;
- civil service: practical data/identity tools;
- elite ceremonial wear exists but cannot replace work clothing in operational scenes.

### Shadow

- architecture makes routes to authority physically controlled;
- human-scale waiting areas contrast with large inaccessible service cores.

## 5. Helix Visual System

### Silhouette

- compact high-efficiency modules;
- rounded service shells around standardized interior cores;
- visible replaceable cartridges and diagnostic light paths;
- product families share interfaces rather than identical exteriors.

### Materials

- clean composites, sealed fasteners, durable printed service labels;
- wear is quickly covered or module-swapped in high-service regions;
- grey-market areas show official shells around incompatible local interiors.

### Clothing

- role-specific contract kits with embedded access/medical/tool modules;
- quality is genuinely high;
- employment tier visible through service options, not cartoon luxury.

### Shadow

- inaccessible fasteners, license seals and remote-service ports visually show dependency;
- a clean module may sit beside a failing unaffiliated life-support line.

## 6. Route, Neutral and Mobile Visual Systems

### Silhouette

- external cargo, tow, docking and repair points are prominent;
- ships/habitats look designed to connect with strangers;
- additions remain visible as family/route history.

### Materials

- mixed certified and locally repaired systems;
- textile dividers, personal handholds, route tokens and maintenance boards;
- high contrast around emergency paths.

### Clothing

- multi-layer garments usable across docks, pressure corridors and social spaces;
- patches indicate ships, households, rescue qualifications and mourning;
- personal items must be secure under acceleration.

### Internal variation

- wealthy merchant household: excellent repair and customized storage;
- marginal mobile family: fewer spares and narrower safety margin, not universally filthy;
- neutral professionals: standardized rescue/claims markers layered over local clothes.

## 7. Independence and Frontier Visual Systems

There is no single rebel aesthetic.

### Common pressures

- resource reuse;
- local manufacturing;
- concealment;
- rapid role conversion;
- variable gravity and atmosphere;
- distrust of permanent central marks.

### Design families

- Open Passage: visible exits, rescue grapples, bright route markings;
- Red Wake: heat-stained fuel and propulsion structures, mobile defense additions;
- Ash Choir/Quiet Harbor: physically disconnectable data/service modules, local-only markings;
- Broken Loom: modular fabrication frames and exposed tooling;
- Outer Lattice Watch: long-baseline sensors, sparse protected crew cores.

### Prohibition

- no universal scavenger spikes, skulls or ragged clothing;
- a frontier object may be better maintained than a core object when local survival depends on it.

## 8. K-13 and White Dock

### K-13

Spatial character:
- correctional institution adapted from traffic/service infrastructure;
- teaching, confinement, labor and emergency functions overlap;
- corridors show multiple authority eras and improvised student ownership.

Visual anchors:
- numbered pressure sectors;
- inspection windows into inaccessible service trunks;
- restraint-compatible classroom furniture;
- work boards carrying real maintenance debt;
- small personal territories created from storage, fabric and light.

### White Dock

- large service dock with three primary work volumes and an obstructed central approach;
- repair gantries, cargo rails, damaged shutters and pressure-control boundaries define tactics;
- white refers to inspection coatings and overexposure under work lighting, not pristine emptiness;
- every major tactical path must be drawable from the locked map.

## 9. 07 Frame Visual Lock

### Core silhouette

- 10–13 m mixed-lineage service/capture/rescue frame;
- narrower torso than dedicated assault frames;
- long forearm service rails and replaceable hand/tool units;
- asymmetrical sensor head caused by surviving modules;
- shoulder/hip hardpoints visibly support rescue, tags, cable and limited weapon pods;
- radiator and thermal shutters are readable in motion.

### Avoid

- giant heroic crest;
- wings with no thermal/sensor/function role;
- oversized sword as default identity;
- clean matching armor after restoration;
- silhouette too close to a famous humanoid mecha franchise.

### Visual progression

- GA1: incomplete panels, exposed service marks, training restraints;
- GA2–3: mixed modules and route/rescue hardware;
- GA4–5: authority/communication modules grow more important than weapons;
- GA6: permanent damage and reduced strategic mobility become visible;
- GA7–9: configuration diverges by mission and shared operators;
- GA10: unique combat configuration removed/split; public training/rescue variants retain recognizable service geometry without personal ownership mark.

## 10. 파루스 Visual Lock

### Hull

- approximately 134 m obsolete relay/utility escort;
- forward traffic/sensor wedge, central crew/service cylinder, aft paired drive structures;
- one restored frame cradle and evidence of removed second bay;
- external tow, cargo and repair points more visually prominent than weapons;
- common coolant trunk and patch generations visible in maintenance diagrams.

### Interior

- tight crew routes but real domestic adaptation;
- repaired cabins and cargo partitions expose status disputes;
- infirmary is stabilizing space, not full hospital;
- engineering has physical local controls beside incompatible digital layers;
- bridge supports captain, mission, navigation and systems roles without one throne-like command seat.

### Progression

- never becomes a pocket battleship;
- additions make the hull more useful and more maintenance-complex;
- GA6 strategic propulsion loss permanently changes aft silhouette;
- later school/rescue use adds classrooms, workshops and public access without erasing scars.

## 11. Ardis Visual Lock

### System identity

Ardis is multiple habitats and industrial structures, not one city planet.

- Anchor Crown: nonrotating traffic/node structures, phase spines and layered customs/control;
- Civic Ring: rotating mixed housing, markets, hospitals and civic offices;
- Foundry Cylinder: heat, material movement, heavy repair and worker districts;
- Garden Chain: water, agriculture, biological processing and variable-light environments;
- Transit Belt: temporary habitats, cargo, refugee ships and improvised services;
- Surface Works: mining, propellant, heat sinks and heavy power;
- Outer Field: debris, abandoned construction, defense and salvage.

Class differences appear through:
- access to rotation comfort;
- air/water reserve;
- transit time;
- private volume;
- noise and heat;
- maintenance response;
- view and natural-light access.

## 12. Palimpsest Visual Lock

- archive sites are materially different: cold storage, witness habitat, damaged transport archive, living-margin settlement and sealed infrastructure memory;
- no universal glowing library aesthetic;
- provenance appears through physical containers, seals, gaps and conflicting annotations;
- refused testimony is represented by protected absence, not spooky black corruption;
- visitors see maintenance and survivor life around records.

## 13. Preservation Regime Visual Lock

Benefit must be visible:
- repaired clinics;
- stable lights/air;
- orderly food and identity access;
- functioning transit;
- reduced improvisational danger.

Cost must also be visible:
- access lanes that reject uncertain identities;
- model-confidence signage;
- synchronized housing/work patterns;
- sealed local modifications;
- service interfaces that are simple when accepted and inaccessible when excluded.

Do not render it as a universally grey dystopia.

## 14. GA10 Transition Visual Families

- T0 synchronized: highly reliable shared interfaces, central markers remain;
- T1 delegated: central service backbone with visible regional operators;
- T2 joint transition: doubled signage, temporary seals and shared control rooms;
- T3 local plural: incompatible but negotiated local systems;
- T4 asynchronous: delayed updates, physical couriers and local caches;
- T5 contested: overlapping checkpoints, repair gaps and competing records;
- T6 re-centralized: restored order with heavy security and reduced local modification.

Each region should show one service improved and one service worsened during transition.

## 15. Civilian Clothing Categories

- habitat domestic wear: soft secure layers, pockets/anchors, thermal variation;
- industrial workwear: replaceable pressure/abrasion zones, visible qualification marks;
- medical/care wear: hygiene and rapid role identification without erasing personal style;
- route wear: modular pressure/temperature layers and secure personal items;
- formal wear: derived from local materials, family/office rituals and gravity—not historical Earth costume copy;
- youth/school wear: institutional base pieces modified by district, household and peer culture.

## 16. Class and Regional Variation Matrix

For every costume/location sheet vary at least four axes:

1. function;
2. wealth/service access;
3. region/culture;
4. age/body/disability;
5. formal vs ordinary use;
6. new vs repaired generation.

A poor core resident and wealthy frontier operator must not collapse into one visual stereotype.

## 17. Concept Brief Template

Every future image brief includes:

- subject and narrative function;
- location/gravity/environment;
- technology era and compatibility layers;
- materials and maintenance state;
- authority/ownership markings;
- ordinary-life evidence;
- scale references;
- silhouette requirements;
- prohibited similarities;
- accessibility and body variation;
- what must remain readable at thumbnail/mobile size.

## 18. Similarity Control

Before approving visual canon:

- compare silhouette, head/face, weapon, limb proportion and color blocking against major franchises;
- reject a design that remains identifiable as one existing franchise after logo removal;
- transform at least three structural premises, not only surface decoration;
- preserve setting-specific service, repair, authority and logistics functions.
