## 1. Locked population boundary

Current Working Canon:

- 612 inhabited systems;
- 76.0 billion registered population;
- approximately 6 billion irregular/mobile/stateless population.

This proposal does **not** change those totals.

Species composition is a subordinate distribution layer.

---

## 2. Three demographic scenarios

These percentages are planning scenarios only. `human-derived` includes baseline humans and stable human-derived clades; ordinary cybernetic/augmented people remain within their biological lineage.

### DEM-A — Human-heavy lattice

- human/human-derived: 68–76%
- S1 nonhuman core species combined: 18–25%
- S2 regional/rare biological peoples: 4–9%
- AI/composite persons: tracked separately from biological population where census law permits; some jurisdictions include them in registered-person totals.

Strength:
- lowest continuity cost.

Weakness:
- species diversity can feel peripheral.

Verdict:
- safe but undershoots author’s desired galaxy breadth.

### DEM-B — Human-majority plural lattice

- human/human-derived: **50–60%**
- S1 nonhuman core species combined: **30–40%**
- S2 regional/rare peoples: **8–15%**
- no single nonhuman species needs to exceed 10–12% galaxy-wide.

Strength:
- humans remain a plausible historical/reader anchor while nonhuman people are ordinary, economically and politically unavoidable.

Weakness:
- existing early documents need explicit local-demography explanation.

Verdict:
- **RECOMMENDED PROPOSAL**.

### DEM-C — No biological majority

- human/human-derived: 30–42%
- S1 nonhuman species combined: 42–55%
- S2 regional/rare: 10–18%

Strength:
- strongest “true multi-species galaxy” impression.

Weakness:
- existing Imperial, Academy, culture, naming and military documents become too human-default; high rewrite cost.

Verdict:
- HOLD unless author explicitly wants broader canon revision.

## 3. Recommended demographic logic under DEM-B

Do not distribute species evenly across 612 systems.

Use four settlement patterns.

### P1 — Mixed metropolitan / route hubs

- no species majority or only soft majority;
- strong MS1/MS2 infrastructure;
- high intermarriage/household/cultural mixing where biologically/socially possible;
- common in Core/Inner major nodes and Neutral routes.

### P2 — Historical-majority systems

- one species/lineage has 55–85% local share due homeworld or old settlement history;
- significant minorities remain;
- politics cannot assume ethnic unanimity.

### P3 — Functional/industrial settlement concentrations

- demographics shaped by labor, environment, clinic, military, yard or migration history;
- may correlate with physiology but must not be genetically assigned occupations.

### P4 — Frontier patchwork

- small habitats with dramatic local variation;
- some nearly single-species because infrastructure supports only one environment;
- neighboring habitat can be entirely different.

Rule:
- “one planet = one species” is exception, not default.

## 4. Irregular/mobile population

The ~6 billion irregular/mobile/stateless population should not become a hidden dumping category for alien peoples.

Potential composition:
- mobile human/human-derived crews;
- nonhuman diasporas;
- refugees;
- mixed households;
- AI/composite persons with disputed registry status;
- postwar populations;
- sanctuary communities.

Red-team control:
- no species is statistically “mostly unregistered” without a historical/political cause and agency-focused treatment.

## 5. Contact-history architecture

Do not use one simplistic “First Contact Day.”

Every major species pair/history can have separate milestones:

1. **Remote Evidence** — signal, artifact, biosignature or recovered craft.
2. **Uncontrolled Encounter** — rescue, crash, conflict or accidental route contact.
3. **Sustained Exchange** — repeated trade/scientific communication.
4. **Diplomatic Recognition** — recognized political counterpart.
5. **Mobility Treaty** — controlled travel/migration.
6. **Service Compatibility** — medicine, identity, trade and technical standards work across groups.
7. **Citizenship/Personhood Recognition** — people can hold standing inside another polity.
8. **Military/Institutional Integration** — cross-species command, service and public office.

History can disagree on which milestone counts as “first contact.”

This supports Archive ambiguity without retconning physical facts.

## 6. Four Imperial-history scenarios

### IMP-A — Human expansion empire

- Empire began primarily human and later incorporated/treatied with other species.

Strength:
- continuity with human-coded institutions.

Risk:
- easy colonial-metropole cliché; nonhumans become “added subjects.”

### IMP-B — Multi-species founding covenant

- early state created by several species/polities together.

Strength:
- plural legitimacy from origin.

Risk:
- conflicts with current dynasty/founding-house language unless heavily redesigned.

### IMP-C — Relay-service federation that became empire

- practical route/service agreements among several peoples gradually centralized into Imperial institutions.

Strength:
- fits setting’s emphasis on service, routes and authority layers.

Risk:
- can make the Empire’s origin too thematically identical to the ending.

### IMP-D — Layered transformation

- early Imperial core was human-dominant or human-led;
- expansion encountered already-developed nonhuman polities;
- treaties, wars, annexations, shared route crises and public-service integration transformed the state over centuries;
- current Empire is genuinely multi-species but still carries human-default institutional path dependence.

Strength:
- explains both existing human-default infrastructure and present diversity;
- creates historical injustice and genuine integration without making either the whole story.

Risk:
- requires nuanced chronology and avoids “humans civilized everyone.”

Verdict:
- **RECOMMENDED PROPOSAL; author-level decision required before canon promotion.**

## 7. Technology/contact relation

Species contact should affect technical eras without making all innovation cross-species borrowing.

Possible historical effects:

- pressure/gravity standards broaden;
- emergency service connectors gain adapter requirements;
- medical identity expands beyond human anatomy;
- cockpit and industrial controls gain translation layers;
- legal authentication distinguishes body/lineage/person;
- food and cargo safety standards broaden;
- ship rescue modules become modular.

Foundational prototype relation:
- multi-species ergonomics can be a secondary design pressure on some 01–20 experiments;
- the prototype program should still be driven by general frame/industry problems rather than existing solely because aliens appeared.

## 8. S2 regional role slots — first pass

These are deliberately less detailed than S1. Names/origins remain HOLD.

### S2-01 — Cold-environment slow-metabolism lineage

Function:
- cryogenic industrial zones, long-duration remote stations, different sleep/work cycles.

### S2-02 — High-temperature membrane-cooling lineage

Function:
- hot industrial worlds; cooling infrastructure becomes political.

### S2-03 — Very-small-body tool-using species

Function:
- tests scale/accessibility without turning small body into comic relief.

### S2-04 — Large-body low-acceleration species

Function:
- freight, architecture, rescue and passenger economics; shows size is costly, not automatically strong.

### S2-05 — Echolocation/acoustic-primary species

Function:
- sound privacy, ship noise, translation and stealth conflicts.

### S2-06 — Polarized-light / wide-spectrum vision species

Function:
- visual UI, evidence and art differ; human displays may hide information.

### S2-07 — Seasonal metamorphic life-cycle species

Function:
- legal identity, work, clothing, medicine and adulthood vary across life stages.

### S2-08 — Short-lived rapid-generation species

Function:
- institutional memory, inheritance and command succession contrast with long-lived S1-06.

### S2-09 — Colony-origin but individual-person offspring species

Function:
- biology includes temporary collective developmental phases, but adults are legally individual; prevents hive-mind cliché.

### S2-10 — Atmosphere-reactive mineral-shelled species

Function:
- body carries protective shell grown/renewed biologically; armor/equipment boundary becomes culturally interesting.

### S2-11 — Photosymbiotic supplementary-energy species

Function:
- light/environment affects health but does not eliminate food needs; tests habitat lighting and medical prejudice.

### S2-12 — Gas-rich buoyant-habitat species

Function:
- home infrastructure and body plan optimized for dense atmospheres; space travel requires high adaptation cost.

### S2-13 — Extreme-radiation niche lineage

Function:
- can work where others need shielding, but reproduction/development remains radiation-sensitive.

### S2-14 — Multi-stage aquatic/terrestrial developmental species

Function:
- children and adults need different environments; family migration becomes difficult.

### S2-15 — Engineered rescue/settlement descendant population

Function:
- a stable nonhuman or human-derived lineage created for emergency colonization; historical purpose no longer determines current identity.

Rules:
- some S2 slots may be cut;
- S2-15 must not duplicate S1-08 unless one is human-derived and the other independent biological origin;
- every surviving S2 slot requires ordinary-life and political representation before naming.

## 9. Homeworld and diaspora controls

A homeworld does not automatically function as:
- capital;
- sacred center;
- species government;
- largest population center.

Possible states:
- inhabited homeland;
- politically fragmented;
- ecologically damaged;
- lost/destroyed;
- protected reserve;
- mostly symbolic while diaspora dominates;
- unknown/disputed for engineered peoples.

This prevents one-species-one-homeworld simplicity.

## 10. Census-tagging design

Future `612-system` species extension should add **separate data**, not rewrite the current authoritative census fields.

Proposed supplementary fields:

- dominant biological service profile(s);
- demographic concentration band, not exact ethnicity count initially;
- MS service capability (MS0–MS3);
- mixed-habitat index;
- specialist medical capacity;
- historical home/diaspora tag;
- species-politics sensitivity flag;
- posthuman service prevalence.

Do not add reader-facing species names to all 612 rows.

## 11. Red-team findings

### Finding 1 — DEM-B best balances breadth and continuity

It makes nonhuman people normal rather than rare while retaining existing human-centered opening plausibility.

### Finding 2 — Imperial history is the largest unresolved ripple

Named species should not be canonized before the state’s multi-species history is selected.

### Finding 3 — S2 roster must stay regional

Fifteen S2 slots are a design pool, not fifteen mandatory lore entries.

### Finding 4 — population does not equal story prominence

A 2% species can have a major character; a 10% species may be mostly background in one GA.

### Finding 5 — AI census needs separate normalization

Existing “registered population” may include or exclude different recognized AI/composite persons by jurisdiction. Do not force a biological demographic percentage onto them until the census-law interpretation is audited.
