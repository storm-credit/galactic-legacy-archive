## 1. Scale Philosophy

- “아르젠 제국”은 은하 전체를 균일하게 지배하지 않는다. 격자망으로 연결된 제한된 정착권을 제국·기업·지역세력이 불완전하게 통치한다.
- 전쟁은 모든 세계가 동시에 움직이는 총력전이 아니라, 병목 노드·조선소·항로·정비망·인증권을 두고 벌어진다.
- 함대 숫자가 늘어도 이동·정비·승무원·탄약·정당성 제약이 사라지지 않는다.
- 수치는 장면과 전략을 통제하는 범위이며, 미래 지도 제작 후 약 ±20% 조정 가능하다.

## 3. Lattice Node Hierarchy

Total active/partly active nodes and relays:
- 950–1,350.

### L0 — Continuity Anchors

Count:
- 7–12.

Function:
- oldest major phase-reference anchors, authentication roots, high-capacity route synchronization, deep archives.

Population/economy:
- usually core capital or heavily defended system.

Strategic value:
- losing one can reroute months/years of traffic and identity/command trust.

Risk:
- centralization and single-point failure.

### L1 — Trunk Nodes

Count:
- 35–55.

Function:
- connect major regional corridors, fleet logistics, high-volume communications.

Typical served population:
- hundreds of millions to several billions across linked systems.

Defense:
- fixed defenses, fleets, multiple repair facilities.

### L2 — Regional Nodes

Count:
- 160–240.

Function:
- connect 3–12 local systems/routes; administrative and industrial center.

Typical served population:
- 5–300 million.

Story use:
- GA3 joint-trust city is damaged L2 or strong L3 upgraded candidate.

### L3 — Local/Frontier Relays

Count:
- 600–900.

Function:
- one system, small cluster, industrial route, habitat chain.

Typical served population:
- 10,000–8 million.

K-13 school context:
- relay itself is valuable L3 near L2 corridor; Academy/White Dock support node traffic.

### Dormant/Broken Nodes

Count:
- hundreds more known/uncertain.

Function:
- salvage, mysteries, dangerous alternate routes, lost civilizations/records.

Rule:
- restoring a node requires phase reference, power, hardware, trust/authentication and traffic support—not one key press.

## 4. Travel Model

### 4.1 In-system conventional travel

- habitats/moons/inner system: hours to days.
- outer-system transfer: days to weeks without local relay shortcut.
- strategic location depends on node placement and orbital windows.

### 4.2 Lattice transition

A ship does not instantly teleport from anywhere.

Steps:

1. reach node capture/approach volume.
2. receive route slot and authentication.
3. match velocity/phase window.
4. enter corridor/transition.
5. emerge and recalibrate navigation/thermal systems.

Typical times at healthy nodes:

| Stage | Local node | Regional/trunk node |
|---|---:|---:|
| queue/authorization | 1–18 h | 0.5–12 h priority, days civilian congestion |
| alignment/approach | 1–8 h | 1–6 h |
| transition | 20 min–6 h | 10 min–4 h |
| post-transition recovery | 0.5–4 h | 0.5–3 h |

At damaged/frontier nodes:
- alignment and recovery can take 6–72 hours.
- route may open only at intervals.

Route-chain travel:

- neighboring healthy systems: 0.5–2 days operational travel.
- regional crossing 3–8 nodes: 4–16 days military priority, 2–6 weeks civilian/merchant.
- Core to middle frontier: 3–8 weeks military, 2–4 months civilian under stable conditions.
- edge-to-edge across known lattice: 3–8 months priority, 8–24 months commercial/irregular.

Narrative implication:
- distant factions cannot respond in hours.
- fleet concentration reveals itself through node queues and logistics.
- local wars can be decided before central authority arrives.

## 5. Communication Model

Communication uses:
- in-system radio/laser with light delay.
- node packet synchronization at transition windows.
- physical couriers for high-security/large/uncertain data.

Priority communication speed:
- generally 3–8× faster than ordinary ship movement along stable trunk routes, not instantaneous.

Typical delay:

- same system: seconds to hours depending distance/interference.
- neighboring nodes: 2–18 hours.
- regional capital: 1–4 days.
- Core↔middle frontier: 4–14 days under stable priority.
- edge regions: weeks.

Costs/limits:
- bandwidth, authentication, queue priority, node damage, censorship, spoofing.
- full neural/AI/model data may require physical transfer or long packet windows.

Strategic consequence:
- governors/admirals have real autonomy.
- orders arrive after context changes.
- propaganda and forged records can precede correction.
- P-001 cannot simply call future capital allies for immediate solution.

Population:
- 0.5–20 million.

Needs:
- diversified industry, ship repair, hospitals, governance.

Military:
- local fleet, hundreds of frames/defense units, node batteries.

Population:
- 50 million–5 billion.

Military/economy:
- shipyards, multiple fleets, large industrial/agricultural base.

GA3 target recommendation:
- damaged L2/L3-upgrade node city with 1.1–1.8 million permanent residents plus 80,000–250,000 stranded/transient population.
- large enough for governance/production, small enough that one node crisis is existential.

## 7. Ship Class Envelope

Automation reduces crew but AI law and damage control keep humans/limited agents necessary.

### Utility/Patrol Cutter

Length:
- 50–100 m.

Crew:
- 12–35.

Passengers:
- 20–80.

Frames:
- 0–1 externally/limited cradle.

Roles:
- customs, courier, rescue, short patrol.

### Utility Escort / Light Corvette — GA2 first ship target

Length:
- 100–180 m.

Crew:
- 35–75.

Passengers:
- 60–180 depending life support.

Frames:
- 2–4 maneuver/utility frames.

Endurance:
- 30–90 days between major resupply depending load.

Roles:
- convoy escort, rescue, cargo/mission support, frontier contracts.

Weakness:
- limited missiles, armor, repair, crew redundancy.

### Corvette / Frigate

Length:
- 160–320 m.

Crew:
- 90–350.

Frames:
- 4–12.

Roles:
- patrol, screening, escort, raiding, node security.

### Destroyer

Length:
- 280–520 m.

Crew:
- 350–1,100.

Frames:
- 8–24 or specialized missiles/sensors instead.

Roles:
- fleet screen, missile combat, anti-ship, node assault support.

### Cruiser

Length:
- 500–950 m.

Crew:
- 1,200–4,500.

Frames:
- 20–60 depending design.

Roles:
- independent command, regional power projection, carrier/arsenal variants.

### Battleship/Heavy Carrier

Length:
- 1–2.4 km.

Crew:
- 5,000–18,000 plus embarked forces.

Frames/craft:
- 60–240 or heavy arsenal.

Roles:
- trunk-node battle, strategic defense, command, siege.

Limits:
- immense maintenance and supply; cannot patrol everywhere.
- vulnerable to node denial, logistics, political loss and asymmetric capture.

### Support ships

Types:
- tankers, repair tenders, hospital ships, ammunition/food transports, sensor pickets, tugs, mobile yards, refugee carriers.

Rule:
- a combat fleet without support loses endurance quickly.
- support ships are collection targets and strategic characters/spaces, not invisible background.

## 8. Fleet Scale

### Cell/Flight

- 3–8 frames or small craft.

### Squadron

- 4–12 ships or 12–36 frames depending type.

### Task Group

- 8–30 combat ships plus 5–20 support.

### Fleet

- 30–120 combat ships plus substantial support and attached local forces.

### Grand Fleet / Theater Concentration

- 150–500 combat ships plus support.
- rare, visible months in advance, strategically expensive.

### Empire working force estimate at R0

Front-line combat hulls:
- 1,400–2,000 across known lattice.

Patrol/security/customs combat-capable hulls:
- 2,500–4,500.

Major support/logistics/repair military hulls:
- 2,000–3,500.

Readiness:
- only 45–65% of front-line hulls fully ready at one time.
- forces dispersed across 600 systems and key nodes.

Why rebels can fight:
- local concentration, captured ships, defectors, industrial conversions, node denial, communication delay.
- not equal total fleet strength.

### Helix and major corporate security

Helix direct combat/security hulls:
- 160–280.

Armed industrial/patrol/rescue craft:
- 500–900.

Leverage:
- parts, software, yards, contracts and trained personnel exceed direct military count.

### Independence at R0

No unified navy.

Combined known armed hulls across cells/polities:
- 250–600 combat-capable, mostly small/converted/dispersed.

Frames:
- thousands of industrial/military mixed units, uneven readiness.

Strength:
- local networks and hidden support.

Weakness:
- incompatible command, parts, goals, records.

### Neutral Assembly

Armed rescue/security hulls:
- 50–90.

Civilian rescue/merchant/member vessels:
- 700–1,400 with varying affiliation and availability.

Strength:
- routes, insurance, credit, medical capacity, legitimacy.

Weakness:
- cannot concentrate like navy without losing neutrality/commerce.

## 9. Frame Scale and Cost

Typical maneuver frame:
- 9–14 m.
- 30–90 t operational mass.
- 1 pilot plus external support team.

Large/strategic variants:
- 15–22 m and 100–250 t, rare and specialized.

Small utility suits/frames:
- 2–7 m for interiors, rescue, cargo, engineering.

Cost reference using Academy annual budget = `100`:

| Asset | Build/major acquisition index | Annual operating/support index |
|---|---:|---:|
| utility suit/small frame | 0.05–0.25 | 0.02–0.10 |
| basic training/industrial frame | 0.4–1.2 | 0.15–0.5 |
| military mass-production frame | 1.2–3.5 | 0.4–1.2 |
| elite/special frame | 4–10+ | 1–4 |
| 07 restoration in GA1 | 0.5–1.5 cumulative equivalent | high labor/compatibility cost rather than cash alone |

Maintenance team:
- 4–12 direct maintainers per operational frame in sustained combat organization, plus shared specialists/logistics.

Sortie limits:
- heat, propellant, pilot sync, maintenance, ammunition/tools.

No loot fantasy:
- capturing frame without cradle, keys, parts, crew, legal cover and transport may be burden not reward.

## 11. Logistics Consumption — Relative

For a task group:

- reaction mass and thermal consumables.
- missiles/projectiles and countermeasures.
- frame parts and medical supplies.
- food/air/water processing spares.
- trained crew replacement and rest.
- encryption/authentication updates.
- docking/route fees and insurance.

Operational endurance:

- patrol/low combat: weeks to months.
- high-intensity fleet combat: missile/countermeasure stocks can deplete in days.
- damaged fleet without repair tender loses readiness sharply after 1–3 major engagements.

Strategic implication:
- H-001/H-002/support ships are not secondary to ace battles.
- node/shipyard/route collection matters more than adding one elite pilot at scale.

## 12. Fleet Battle Information Limits

Sensor horizon:
- physical detection may be long-range, but classification/fire solution is degraded by lattice noise, decoys, heat masking, orbital bodies, data trust.

Communication:
- local fleet command seconds/minutes depending range and interference.
- detached task groups may have minutes/hours delay.
- node-separated fleets receive outdated orders.

AI:
- bounded tactical agents allowed within command envelopes.
- unrestricted autonomous strategic target selection illegal/rare.

Command implication:
- admirals issue intent, priorities, engagement limits, retreat conditions.
- subordinate commanders make irreversible choices.
- P-001 cannot pilot and command a theater simultaneously without cost/gap.

## 15. Strategic Bottleneck Rules

A region’s power depends on combination:

1. node access and authentication.
2. shipyard/repair capacity.
3. trained crew/pilots/maintainers.
4. missiles/parts/propellant.
5. food/medicine/life support.
6. records/citizenship/contracts.
7. public legitimacy and local cooperation.

No single collection target solves all seven.

Examples:
- battleship without route/repair is trapped prestige asset.
- open standard without quality control causes failures.
- node control without local legitimacy invites sabotage.
- hero concentration drains other fronts.

## 17. Red-Team Checks

### Is 76 billion too large for named politics?

Control:
- story works through nodes, institutions, representatives and local populations.
- no claim that protagonist personally governs everyone.
- final distribution explicitly rejects one uniform solution.

### Why doesn’t Core send overwhelming fleet early?

- school/07 initially low-priority/ambiguous.
- travel/communications take days/weeks.
- fleets dispersed and politically divided.
- covert actors avoid public escalation.
- local seizure can damage asset/evidence/legitimacy.

### Why don’t ships replace frames at nodes?

- ship weapons risk infrastructure/civilians.
- frames access structures, capture keys, repair/rescue, operate under degraded fire control.
- fleets control approach and denial; frames decide local capture/use.

### Why are old ships affordable?

- distressed purchase/lease value low, operating and repair burden high.
- ownership remains split and debt-based.

### Why can Independence threaten Empire?

- not in total hull count.
- local concentration, infiltration, node denial, defections, industrial conversions, communication delay and imperial fragmentation.
