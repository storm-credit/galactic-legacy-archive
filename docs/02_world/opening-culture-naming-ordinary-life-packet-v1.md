## 1. Cultural Design Rule

- 세력과 문화는 동일하지 않다. 제국인·독립전선·헬릭스 직원 안에도 여러 지역문화가 존재한다.
- 문화는 옷·음식·이름만이 아니라 가족, 기록, 죽음, 노동, 시간, 권위, 기술을 대하는 방식으로 드러난다.
- 첫 100화에서는 최소 세 문화권이 같은 학교에서 충돌·혼합한다.
- 문화설명은 인물 선택·오해·의례·생활습관으로 보여주고 백과사전식 소개를 피한다.

## 2. Opening Cultural Strata

### CUL-A — 중앙 등록권 문화 / Core Registry Culture

Origin:
- 오래된 제국 행정·교육·노드도시.

Values:
- 이름, 기록, 학력, 직책, 적법한 승계.
- 공공규칙과 문서가 개인적 신뢰보다 오래간다고 믿음.

Family:
- 법적 가족관계와 후견인 등록 중요.
- 입양/후견도 강한 법적 지위를 가질 수 있으나 서류가 없으면 존재하기 어려움.

Death/memory:
- 공식 사망확인과 기록봉인, 추모명부.
- 기록에 이름이 남는 것이 인간 존엄의 일부.

Speech:
- 직책·경칭·책임 주체를 명확히 함.
- 공개 자리에서 직접 감정보다 절차·원칙 언어.

Food/life:
- 표준 영양식, 지역별 정교한 조미, 정시 식사.
- 낭비보다 정량과 품질 관리.

Potential characters:
- Imperial observer, parts of S-001, some instructors/administrators.

Shadow:
- 기록 밖 사람을 무질서/위험으로 봄.
- 적법성을 도덕성으로 착각.

### CUL-B — 변경 항로공동체 / Frontier Route Communities

Origin:
- 작은 노드, 수송선단, 정착지, 수리항.

Values:
- 누가 실제로 와서 고치고 운반하고 구조했는가.
- 계약보다 반복된 상호의무와 평판.

Family:
- 혈연보다 선단·작업반·돌봄공동체.
- 아이·노인·환자의 이동책임을 여러 가구가 나눔.

Death/memory:
- 사망자의 이름보다 마지막 항로·작업·구조 대상이 함께 기록됨.
- 시신이 없을 때 개인 물건이나 호출부호를 공동 보관/순환.

Speech:
- 짧고 실용적, 지역·선박 은어.
- 직책보다 작업역할·신뢰이력.

Food/life:
- 보존식과 공동조리, 교환 가능한 향신료·발효재료.
- 자원 순환과 배급 감각.

Potential characters:
- H-001, E-001, community focal people.

Shadow:
- 외부법과 전문표준 불신.
- 내부 평판이 폐쇄적 처벌·연좌로 변할 수 있음.

### CUL-C — 공방·산업 디아스포라 / Workshop-Industrial Diaspora

Origin:
- 조선소, 공방도시, 계약노동 이주, 기업학교.

Values:
- 제작자 표시, 수리 이력, 안전, 기술 계승, 공구 소유.
- “누가 설계했는가”보다 “누가 유지했는가”를 중시하는 하위문화와 특허/인증을 중시하는 상위문화가 충돌.

Family:
- 가족공방, 작업반, 견습관계, 노조/길드.
- 공구·설계·안전사고 책임이 세대를 넘어감.

Death/memory:
- 고장 원인·사고보고·마지막 수리기록을 남기는 것이 추모.
- 사망자 이름을 공구나 작업대에 새기기도 함.

Speech:
- 상태·원인·책임·시간을 구체적으로 말함.
- 과장된 영웅담보다 “누가 뒤에서 고쳤는가”를 따짐.

Food/life:
- 교대식, 뜨거운 고열량 음식, 손으로 먹기 쉬운 간식.
- 기름·금속냄새를 없애는 강한 차/향.

Potential characters:
- H-002, Helix evaluator, many Ring C workers.

Shadow:
- 안전·전문성을 명분으로 외부인을 배제.
- 기술을 가문/기업 소유로 고정.

### CUL-D — 자유항·중립선 문화 / Free-Port Neutral Culture

Origin:
- 구조선, 상선, 중립항, 보험조합, 망명망.

Values:
- 함내 규칙, 무장해제, 손님/승객 보호, 채무와 상호성.
- 어느 국가보다 선박·항로 규칙이 우선하는 순간이 있음.

Family:
- 장기승선 동료, 다중국적 가족, 항구마다 다른 친족망.

Death/memory:
- 항해기록, 구조/거부 결정, 미지급 채무까지 남김.
- 선내 추모는 짧고 다음 항해 안전절차와 연결.

Speech:
- 범위·수용량·시간·보증을 정확히 말함.
- 정치적 호칭보다 선내 역할.

Food/life:
- 여러 항로 음식의 혼합, 교환 선물, 승무원별 작은 사치.

Potential characters:
- Neutral representative, future ship crew.

Shadow:
- 지불·보증 없는 사람을 위험/부담으로 환원.
- 중립이라는 이름의 상업적 자기보존.

## 3. Academy Mixed-Culture Policy

Official language:
- Imperial Standard for legal, command, safety and education.

Permitted:
- local languages/cant in private and cultural spaces.

Controlled:
- encrypted/unknown slang during mission and security incidents.

Student reality:
- mixed jargon combines military terms, workshop signs, route slang, detention codes.
- translation errors can be genuine conflict, not comic accent only.

Naming in records:
- one legal name plus aliases, route name, workshop mark, call sign.
- administrative systems may compress or reject multi-part identities.
- Archive may confuse title, call sign, inherited workshop name, and person.

## 4. Naming Palette Options

The final palette should prioritize Korean readability, cultural distinction and long-series memory.

### Palette A — Clean Eurasian Space Opera

Pattern:
- short given name + 2–3 syllable family/house name.
- examples are structural only: `카이 렌바`, `세라 노반`, `미라 케일`.

Strength:
- readable and familiar.

Risk:
- generic Western-SF impression and similarity to existing works.

### Palette B — Registry Name + Cultural Alias

Pattern:
- legal short name, local alias/call sign, optional habitat/workshop identifier.
- example structure: `이안 벨 / 항로명 “백선” / K-7 공동체`.

Strength:
- identity/record theme becomes visible.
- one person can be known differently without confusing if controlled.

Risk:
- too many names.

### Palette C — Constructed Syllable Families

Pattern:
- each cultural stratum uses controlled consonant/vowel families.
- Core: liquid/clear multi-syllable names.
- Frontier: short clipped names and route epithets.
- Industrial: inherited workshop names/marks.
- Neutral: mixed origin and ship names.

Strength:
- original cultural texture.

Risk:
- author burden and reader pronunciation.

### Palette D — Korean-Accessible Hybrid — Recommended

Rule:
- primary reader-facing personal name is 2–4 syllables in Korean transcription.
- one family/house/workshop/habitat identifier only when socially relevant.
- call signs limited to pilots/operations, not every character.
- avoid apostrophes and excessive consonant clusters.
- distinguish cultures through name order, inheritance, titles and aliases more than difficult phonetics.

Working structure:

- Core: `[given] [registered family/house]`, formal surname use.
- Frontier: `[given]`, plus community/route byname when needed.
- Industrial: `[given] [workshop/family mark]`, mark can transfer by apprenticeship.
- Neutral: `[given] [ship/port affiliation]` in formal contexts.

Status:
- Palette D recommended for sample testing; no current codes renamed yet.

## 5. Forms of Address

Academy official:
- student/cadet + registered surname or number in punitive settings.
- instructors use given/call sign to humanize, but this is political.

Core bureaucracy:
- title + surname/registered name.

Frontier:
- given name or role; full registered name signals distrust/legal dispute.

Workshop:
- given name + craft role; workshop mark in contract/technical context.

Neutral ship:
- ship role on duty; personal name off duty.

Relationship signal:
- changes in address must track trust, power, ownership and identity—not arbitrary intimacy.

## 6. Everyday Time and Ritual

Academy day — ordinary baseline:

- 6–7.5 hours sleep officially, often reduced by shifts/mission.
- two academic blocks.
- one physical/simulation block.
- one industrial/maintenance/service shift depending cohort.
- meals in 3 main windows, ration/medical exceptions.
- weekly cell inspection and debt/credit update.
- monthly family/protector message window, often delayed/edited.
- memorial roll after fatal incidents, language carefully controlled.

Student micro-rituals:
- checking gravity/rotation hum after waking.
- trading ration flavors and heat tokens.
- marking tools/lockers against silent confiscation.
- leaving a small object at the empty bunk of transferred students because official status may not say dead.
- recording who owes whom a shift, meal, medical favor, or message slot.

## 7. Core Character Ordinary-Life Anchors

### P-001

Food:
- eats too quickly and finishes emergency rations before noticing taste.
- privately likes overly sweet hot tea/drink associated with original-life quiet watch.

Sleep:
- sleeps seated or near exits; wakes at changes in machinery hum.
- cannot initially tolerate soft civilian bedding without feeling exposed.

Personal object:
- keeps a plain training tag or damaged fastener from first 07 repair, not grand relic.

Nonwar interest:
- old navigation board/route puzzles used recreationally, though others accuse him of turning leisure into work.
- secretly poor at music or drawing despite strong spatial skill.

Shame unrelated to grand plot:
- teenage body and social rules make ordinary embarrassment harder than battle; avoids communal showers/medical examination due loss of command privacy.

Humor:
- deadpan literal replies; becomes unintentionally funny when treating dorm problems like operations.

No-mission behavior:
- reorganizes communal supplies until others tell him to stop controlling them.

### H-001

Food:
- saves part of every meal, combines leftovers carefully, remembers others’ dietary needs.
- enjoys making one unexpectedly good communal dish from ration scraps.

Sleep:
- light sleeper during shortages, deep sleeper after everyone’s count is confirmed.

Personal object:
- small paper/plastic route token or handwritten list from protector/community.

Nonwar interest:
- cooperative board games or route-building games; dislikes elimination games.
- keeps a tiny low-maintenance plant/seed packet but worries about wasting water.

Private shame:
- cannot ask for personal luxury without calculating who else could use it.

Humor:
- quiet observations about queues, schedules, and people taking the longest route to avoid one another.

No-mission behavior:
- fixes food/shift schedules without permission, which can irritate others.

### H-002

Food:
- strong bitter/acidic drink or candy used to cut metal/solvent smell.
- forgets meals during work, then eats intensely.

Sleep:
- can sleep through noise but wakes if a machine changes pitch.

Personal object:
- one inherited nonessential craft tool, unsuitable for current repair but emotionally important.

Nonwar interest:
- makes miniature kinetic ornaments/toys from scrap for aesthetics, not function.
- likes loud old worker music and has poor singing voice but participates.

Private shame:
- formal theory gaps and reading/writing conventions; hides them behind contempt for paperwork.

Humor:
- names bad repairs after bureaucratic departments and contracts.

No-mission behavior:
- trades repairs for personal stories or small decorations, not only credits.

### S-001

Food:
- prefers individually wrapped/dated food and dislikes communal unlabeled containers.
- hoards receipts/labels.

Sleep:
- checks message/log queues before bed and on waking.

Personal object:
- handwritten notebook or old mechanical stamp because it cannot be remotely edited.

Nonwar interest:
- word puzzles, annotation, collecting contradictory versions of rumors or stories.
- may enjoy low-stakes radio drama.

Private shame:
- poor memory for faces despite excellent records; fears not recognizing someone whose life depended on a file.

Humor:
- jokes about forms that require dead people to sign corrections.

No-mission behavior:
- organizes shared photos/messages with too many metadata rules.

### M-001

Food:
- insists on hydration/meal timing for others but skips own meal after loss.
- enjoys growing herbs/greens in tiny habitat garden and cooking one simple fresh dish.

Sleep:
- can fall asleep quickly during short safe windows; wakes to breathing/coughing nearby.

Personal object:
- seed packet, cloth wrap, or simple music player from accident-response days.

Nonwar interest:
- habitat gardening and singing/quiet rhythm used for breathing exercises, not formal therapy every time.

Private shame:
- hates memorial ceremonies because once froze and said nothing; fears being seen as cold.

Humor:
- dry medical skepticism about heroic symptoms and people lying about pain.

No-mission behavior:
- tends plants, cleans personal space slowly, avoids communal praise.

### I-001

Food:
- drinks the same cheap beverage/meal from graduate days; students mock it.

Sleep:
- keeps strict routine when possible, becomes irritable if paperwork cuts it.

Personal object:
- alumni patch or list of past cells kept hidden, not displayed as heroic medal.

Nonwar interest:
- follows an ordinary sport/game or repairs an old analog timepiece badly.

Private shame:
- remembers protégés’ scores better than their chosen post-school lives.

Humor:
- understated instructor sarcasm and embarrassing old cohort stories.

### E-001

Food:
- carries strong-smelling preserved frontier food; shares carefully but not ceremonially.

Sleep:
- sleeps well in machinery/ship noise, poorly in silent formal rooms.

Personal object:
- worker identification tag or tiny cut piece from dismantled home yard.

Nonwar interest:
- low-gravity dance/acrobatic work tradition, competitive docking/line-throw games.

Private shame:
- cannot read some formal documents fluently and once signed a harmful contract.

Humor:
- worksite teasing; distrusts solemn revolutionary slogans.

## 9. School Social Microgroups

- cohort/cell identity.
- workshop and specialty circles.
- sponsor-backed students.
- family/protector networks.
- language/culture groups.
- debt/credit mutuals.
- medical/patient networks.
- alumni/faction recruiters.

No single “popular kids/bullies” hierarchy explains school.

Conflict examples:
- sponsor-backed students have better equipment but less exit freedom.
- frontier students share food/routes but distrust records.
- workshop students value repair labor and resent ace pilots.
- medical students know hidden injury rates and can be socially isolated.
- informant rumors can target innocent students and protect real handlers.

## 11. Red-Team Controls

Avoid:
- one culture = one political opinion.
- accents used to mark intelligence.
- all frontier people as warm/community and all core people as cold/bureaucratic.
- every hobby foreshadowing later plot.
- food descriptions detached from resource and social meaning.
- name complexity used as fake depth.

Required diversity within strata:
- a Core person opposing central records.
- a Frontier person supporting stable imperial/Seed order.
- an Industrial worker defending Helix standards.
- a Neutral member choosing humanitarian exception over solvency and another rejecting it.
