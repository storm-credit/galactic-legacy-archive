## 1. Core principle

A large lattice civilization can have excellent translation technology without having perfect communication.

Separate:

- signal reception;
- lexical translation;
- grammar;
- technical terminology;
- legal scope;
- cultural implication;
- identity/name mapping;
- speaker intent;
- evidence provenance.

Machine translation may solve the first three extremely well and still fail the last five.

---

## 2. Communication layers

### L1 — Native/community language

- household;
- local culture;
- species/city/ship/community identity.

### L2 — Regional common language

- education;
- public administration;
- regional trade/media.

### L3 — Interregional trade/service lingua franca

- routes;
- ports;
- military/technical exchange;
- contracts.

### L4 — Institutional controlled language

Examples:
- Imperial administrative standard;
- military command syntax;
- Helix certification terminology;
- Neutral claims/custody vocabulary.

Purpose:
- reduce ambiguity in high-stakes domains.

### L5 — Machine-mediated translation

- real-time speech/text/sign/sensory translation where models exist.

### L6 — Certified interpretation

Needed when consequences require accountable human/person expertise:
- court;
- treaty;
- medical consent;
- guardianship;
- surrender;
- complex historical evidence.

### L7 — Historical/uncertain reconstruction

- extinct languages;
- damaged records;
- old machine interfaces;
- nonstandard sensory modes.

This is strongest in GA8 but has earlier roots.

---

## 3. Why one galactic language does not erase others

A common language can dominate administration/trade while local languages survive because of:

- family;
- religion;
- art;
- identity;
- regional law;
- delayed communication and autonomous institutions;
- species sensory/body differences;
- political resistance;
- professional vocabulary.

Do not make language survival depend only on nationalism.

---

## 4. Machine translation capability bands

### MT0 — dictionary/subtitle support

- reliable common vocabulary;
- poor context.

### MT1 — conversational translation

- ordinary travel/service dialogue;
- strong for widely represented languages.

### MT2 — professional domain translation

- engineering, medicine, finance, military;
- requires certified domain model and current terminology.

### MT3 — legal/diplomatic assist

- proposes equivalents and flags uncertainty;
- cannot alone create binding interpretation where law requires accountable interpreter/witness.

### MT4 — historical/reconstructed translation

- confidence-weighted;
- multiple plausible readings;
- source/provenance must be shown.

Rule:
- no translation system reads thoughts, emotion or truth.

---

## 5. Non-auditory communication

Core species may communicate using combinations of:

- sound;
- gesture;
- color/polarization;
- vibration;
- chemical cues;
- touch;
- electric/field modulation;
- data-linked symbolic systems.

Design rule:
- a communication channel is not automatically a language.
- involuntary biological signals must not be mistaken for explicit statements.

Mixed spaces need:
- visual/text/data backups;
- accessible warning modes;
- interpretable consent interfaces.

---

## 6. Names and identity mapping

A person may have:
- self-name;
- legal registry rendering;
- translated/transliterated public name;
- house/lineage name;
- ship/call sign;
- office/title;
- local nickname.

Archive risk:
- title + translated name + call sign can be compressed into one person or split one person into several records.

Rule:
- legal systems keep stable identity IDs beneath reader-facing names; name change does not imply person change.

---

## 7. Species naming controls

Before naming a species:

1. define whether public name is self-name, exonym or translated descriptor;
2. keep Korean reader pronunciation simple enough for active-window memory;
3. one public species name first;
4. self-name only when cultural/political difference matters;
5. avoid apostrophe/glyph overload;
6. no species name should encode its entire biology (“Four-Arm People”).

Reader-facing rule:
- profession/name usually comes before species label unless species matters in the scene.

## 8. Translation errors that matter

### Semantic mismatch

- one word spans several legal/technical meanings.

### Scope mismatch

- translated order sounds broader/narrower than source authority.

### Cultural-pragmatic mismatch

- polite refusal translated as uncertainty;
- ritual statement mistaken for contract.

### Sensory mismatch

- color/field/vibration component omitted.

### Identity mismatch

- title treated as personal name;
- collective name treated as individual.

### Historical drift

- old term changed meaning across centuries.

### Model bias

- rare dialect/species has lower training data and higher confidence error.

Major plot rule:
- important translation failures must be discoverable from planted evidence; no arbitrary “actually that word meant X” twist.

---

## 9. Legal interpretation

Binding contexts require:

- source text/record preserved;
- translation version;
- interpreter/model identity;
- uncertainty notes;
- participant confirmation where possible;
- appeal/correction.

High-risk domains:
- treaty;
- surrender;
- citizenship;
- marriage/guardianship;
- medical consent;
- inheritance;
- salvage/title;
- military command.

No translated summary alone should silently transfer major rights.

---

## 10. Medical communication

Medical systems must distinguish:
- pain description;
- sensory differences;
- body-state signals;
- consent;
- cultural preference;
- species physiology.

Machine translation helps but cannot substitute for species/profile-specific clinical knowledge.

A patient who cannot use the dominant auditory language still retains decision rights.

---

## 11. Military communication

Military forces use controlled vocabulary to reduce ambiguity.

Layers:
- tactical brevity code;
- mission intent;
- authentication;
- coordinates/targets;
- surrender/civilian warning;
- technical damage reports.

Multi-species issue:
- not every member perceives the same alarm channel or speaking speed.

Rule:
- standardized data packets matter more than everyone speaking one language.

Failure modes:
- translation latency;
- jamming;
- corrupted terminology table;
- local call sign collision;
- command scope mistranslation.

---

## 12. Trade and labor

Language ability creates class advantages:
- better contracts;
- professional certification;
- migration;
- court access;
- command roles.

Counterinstitutions:
- public translation services;
- union interpreters;
- portable terminology packs;
- certified AI models;
- community translators.

Exploitative practice:
- signing workers into contracts only available in unfamiliar institutional language.

---

## 13. Language politics

Possible conflicts:
- official-language requirements;
- local language schooling;
- species-accessibility standards;
- translation funding;
- naming/exonym disputes;
- archive correction;
- military language dominance;
- corporate proprietary terminology.

Do not make every language conflict ethnic nationalism; class/profession/service access often matters more.

---

## 14. AI/composite-person languages

AI/composite persons may use:
- human-readable languages;
- compressed machine protocols;
- shared symbolic workspaces;
- embodiment-specific signals.

Rule:
- machine-native communication does not imply telepathy or perfect consensus.
- two AI persons can misunderstand values/context even if data exchange is exact.

Legal systems require human/nonhuman-auditable representation for binding decisions.

## 16. Species-codex link

Every S1 species entry eventually needs:
- primary communication channels;
- native language diversity;
- common-language acquisition constraints;
- name structure;
- translation failure mode;
- one ordinary multilingual setting.

No species gets one universal language unless a strong historical reason exists.

---

## 17. Reader prose control

Deep language design should not make prose unreadable.

Use:
- Korean narration as transparent reader rendering;
- occasional noted accent/register only when meaningful;
- translated-language markers sparingly;
- no phonetic dialect caricature for entire species;
- no multi-line alien script unless plot-critical.

Reader should experience consequences, not decode a conlang.

## 19. Red-team stop conditions

Reject if:
- universal translator is perfect and free;
- every alien species speaks one language;
- translation error appears only to manufacture twist;
- machine translation knows intent/truth;
- legal consent is valid through a low-confidence translation with no appeal;
- accents map to intelligence/morality;
- alien words flood reader memory;
- names become impossible to distinguish;
- language difference disappears outside exposition scenes.
