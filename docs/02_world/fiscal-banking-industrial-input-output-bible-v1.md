## 1. Fiscal Principle

No government or corporation collects one universal cash tax across the lattice.

Public and private extraction may occur through:

- registered income/payroll deductions;
- berth, air, water and energy charges;
- cargo and node tolls;
- insurance and liability premiums;
- service obligations and emergency labor;
- production quotas and procurement prices;
- family/dependent guarantees;
- contract-linked housing, medicine and certification;
- currency conversion and identity-access fees.

A low nominal tax polity can be more coercive when it monopolizes medicine, identity or route access.

## 2. Major Fiscal Regimes

### 2.1 Imperial Core Registered Regime

Typical annual effective burden:
- low-income household: 12–24% cash/withholding plus service fees;
- skilled/upper-middle household: 22–38%;
- high office/capital income: 30–52% before exemptions;
- strategic firms: negotiated 18–35% plus procurement/service obligations.

Revenue mix:
- 28% payroll/income;
- 18% consumption/service;
- 14% corporate/profit;
- 12% route/customs;
- 11% property/habitat;
- 17% state enterprise, fees and other.

Strength:
- predictable pensions, health and identity service in stable regions.

Shadow:
- accounts and status can be frozen through succession or security classification.

### 2.2 Imperial Provincial Regime

Typical burden:
- 15–35% formal tax;
- 5–25% additional local fees, compulsory purchase or arrears.

Problem:
- local governments may remit tax to the center while delaying wages and medicine.

### 2.3 Helix Contract Regime

Nominal tax:
- often 5–18%.

Real deductions:
- housing 8–25%;
- treatment/insurance 5–35%;
- tools/certification 3–18%;
- education/dependent support 2–15%;
- exit/debt service variable.

Real effective burden:
- 25–65% of total compensation.

Benefit:
- high-quality service and predictable supply where the contract remains valid.

Shadow:
- leaving employment may simultaneously terminate housing, care, identity access and certification.

### 2.4 Neutral Charter Regime

Revenue:
- route tolls;
- claims/arbitration fees;
- insurance pool contributions;
- rescue levy;
- member subscription.

Typical burden:
- 2–8% cargo value or 0.5–4 BSC per passenger-day in protected transit;
- emergency rescue levy can rise to 12–20% of voyage value.

Strength:
- cross-border enforceability.

Shadow:
- people without insurable identity or collateral may be excluded.

### 2.5 Local Assembly / Mutual Credit Regime

Cash tax:
- 5–20%.

In-kind/service:
- 4–25 workdays/year;
- food, water, repair or watch contributions;
- emergency ration/transport pooling.

Strength:
- can function during currency failure.

Shadow:
- local elites can define “voluntary” work or deny exit.

### 2.6 Preservation Service Regime

R0-equivalent tax is replaced partly by automatic service allocation.

Typical:
- 18–45% of registered productive output assigned to continuity services;
- medicine, identity, ration and housing minimum delivered in return;
- movement and discretionary consumption constrained in unstable regions.

Benefit:
- reduces catastrophic service gaps.

Shadow:
- people outside the model or refusing synchronization may lose access and political voice.

## 4. Settlement Finality

Payments have five states:

1. instruction issued;
2. local balance reserved;
3. node/clearing authentication;
4. counterparty acceptance;
5. service or asset physically delivered.

A character who has been “paid” at stage 2 may still lack usable money elsewhere.

During route delay:
- local provisional credit may circulate;
- exchange rate and fraud risk rise;
- families can suffer before official arrears are recognized.

## 5. Credit and Interest Bands

Stable annualized design bands:

| Credit | Secured stable node | Frontier/damaged route | Crisis/war |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| household housing/service | 3–8% | 8–20% | unavailable or 20–60% equivalent |
| skilled-worker tool/certification | 4–12% | 10–28% | 25–80% plus employer control |
| merchant voyage | 5–14% | 15–40% | 40–150% or profit share |
| ship repair/mortgage | 6–16% | 18–45% | restructuring, lien or mission assignment |
| government/node bond | 2–9% | 10–35% | forced subscription/default risk |

Interest may be charged through:
- cash;
- cargo share;
- priority rights;
- data/inspection rights;
- labor/service time;
- title or route control.

## 6. Insurance Structure

Insurance classes:

- hull and machinery;
- cargo;
- crew wage/death/disability;
- passenger and medical transport;
- environmental/habitat liability;
- node-transition liability;
- political/sanctions risk;
- data/provenance and certification.

Claims can be denied for:
- unauthorized parts;
- false manifest;
- expired identity;
- war exclusion;
- command outside charter;
- missing maintenance record;
- sanctions;
- deliberate route violation.

Insurance therefore creates real safety and coercive control simultaneously.

## 11. Key Interregional Input Flows

- Core: certification roots, high-end electronics, finance, advanced medicine.
- Inner: major yards, precision industry, pharmaceuticals, high-volume agriculture.
- Middle: repair, refining, frames, water and regional logistics.
- Frontier: raw resources, fuel, rare materials, salvage and route knowledge.
- Mobile/neutral: transport, insurance, cross-border settlement, rescue and information.

No region is self-sufficient in every strategic input.

## 12. Industrial Conversion Rules

Civil-to-military conversion can produce:
- cargo missile racks;
- sensor pickets;
- tugs and boarding carriers;
- frame cradles;
- armored transports;
- mobile workshops.

It cannot instantly produce:
- military fire-control quality;
- hardened magazines;
- trained damage-control crews;
- fleet networking;
- legal command authority;
- sustained ammunition supply.

## 13. Wartime Controls

Possible controls:
- rationing;
- export licenses;
- price ceilings;
- compulsory procurement;
- labor retention;
- conversion controls;
- capital and currency restrictions;
- route priority;
- insurance exclusion.

Failure modes:
- black market;
- maintenance deferral;
- hidden output;
- local hunger despite aggregate supply;
- political favoritism;
- flight of skilled people.

## 15. Budget Stress Rules

Every institutional plan must state:

- cash/credit source;
- physical input source;
- trained labor source;
- route and time;
- liability/insurance bearer;
- who loses current allocation;
- maintenance after acquisition.

A plan missing any two fields is not executable.
