## 1. Goal

The species catalogue needs names readers can remember after dozens of episodes.

The target is not linguistic simulation for its own sake.

The target is:
- one short reader-facing name;
- optional self-name when it adds meaning;
- recognizable naming rhythm without every individual sounding identical;
- no dependence on apostrophes, punctuation or unpronounceable consonant piles;
- no species name that sounds like a faction title or mecha model by accident.

## 2. Four Naming Directions

### N-A — Fully Humanized Exonyms

Example pattern:
- short Korean-friendly public names only;
- self-names rarely shown.

Strength:
- lowest reader load.

Weakness:
- galaxy can feel linguistically flat;
- outsiders appear to name everyone.

Verdict:
- useful for early exposure, insufficient alone.

### N-B — Self-Name First

Pattern:
- each species introduced by native autonym and native personal-name grammar.

Strength:
- strongest linguistic identity.

Weakness:
- webnovel noun overload;
- early exposition cost;
- reader pronunciation risk.

Verdict:
- reject as default.

### N-C — Dual Name System

Pattern:
- reader-facing common/exonym;
- self-name used in ceremonial, political or intimate contexts.

Strength:
- preserves readability and cultural depth.

Weakness:
- two names per species can still overload memory.

Verdict:
- strong.

### N-D — Layered Dual System

Pattern:
1. first exposure uses one short public name;
2. ordinary characters keep using it;
3. self-name appears only when the difference matters;
4. historical/old exonyms appear only in Archive/contact-history scenes.

Strength:
- maximum reader control;
- translation history itself can become plot.

Weakness:
- requires disciplined reveal tracking.

Verdict:
- **RECOMMENDED**.

## 3. Reader-Facing Phonetic Rules

### 3.1 Species public name

Preferred Korean length:
- 2–3 syllables normally;
- 4 syllables only if rhythm is very clean.

Preferred Latin transcription:
- 4–7 letters normally.

Avoid:
- apostrophes;
- repeated punctuation;
- three or more consecutive consonants;
- names differing from another active noun by one final consonant;
- direct Earth ethnic/religious naming echoes unless intentional and justified.

### 3.2 Active-window rule

Within one 5-episode reader window:
- introduce at most one new S1 species name unless the scene function absolutely requires two;
- do not introduce public name + self-name + homeworld + state + religion in one scene.

Suggested reveal sequence:
1. public species name;
2. individual name;
3. ordinary biological need;
4. local culture/faction;
5. self-name or deep history later.

## 5. Why These Working Labels Survive First-Pass Reader Test

### 브락시 / Braxi

Pros:
- short;
- hard consonant onset fits compact visual impression without making them “warriors” by definition;
- distinguishable from Rian/Haren/Nera/Serin/Mia/Ern.

Risk:
- may sound too intentionally “heavy.”

Control:
- personal naming should include soft/long forms so species does not sound culturally monolithic.

### 세이아 / Seia

Pros:
- light vowel structure;
- easy Korean pronunciation;
- distinct from faction/mecha terms.

Risk:
- human personal-name feel.

Control:
- species public name can be an old route exonym; self-name grammar provides alien distinction later.

### 누마르 / Numar

Pros:
- stable pronunciation;
- not explicitly aquatic in the word itself;
- works as species adjective in Korean prose.

Risk:
- possible similarity to existing SF/fantasy proper nouns must be externally screened later.

### 이브라 / Ivra

Pros:
- short and memorable;
- does not advertise “symbiosis.”

Risk:
- can sound like an individual human/fantasy name.

Control:
- self-name and personal names need a different grammatical pattern.

### 테사르 / Tesar

Pros:
- solemn without literally meaning elder;
- easy to decline in Korean.

Risk:
- long-lived species can slip into wise-elder cliché.

Control:
- first named Tesar proposal should not be a sage, historian or admiral.

### 켈릭 / Kelik

Pros:
- compact;
- tactile/technical sound without copying machine model syntax.

Risk:
- could resemble a surname.

Control:
- public species name should rarely appear adjacent to model codes.

### 베린 / Verin

Pros:
- smooth, non-threatening sound helps avoid “engineered race = ominous” coding.

Risk:
- high probability of external name reuse because phonetics are simple.

Control:
- external collision screen mandatory before promotion.

## 6. Self-Name Strategy by Slot

Do not design final self-language yet. Define only what kind of naming problem exists.

### S1-02 브락시

Self-name challenge:
- four-limb body permits gesture/contact components in formal identity.

Reader policy:
- audio/text public name remains sufficient 95% of the time.

### S1-03 세이아

Self-name challenge:
- pressure/vibration cues may distinguish meanings not represented in ordinary speech.

Reader policy:
- write translated spoken name; vibration marker appears only in ritual/legal misunderstanding scenes.

### S1-04 누마르

Self-name challenge:
- underwater/fluid phonation may not map cleanly to gas-atmosphere speech.

Reader policy:
- public exonym normal on mixed ships;
- self-name used in home/wet-module scenes.

### S1-05 이브라

Self-name challenge:
- legal person has two-organism biology but one social identity.

Reader policy:
- never write dual personal names merely to signal symbiosis unless that individual culture actually does so.

### S1-06 테사르

Self-name challenge:
- long lives may accumulate life-stage titles and historical names.

Reader policy:
- one current name in normal prose;
- old names only when legally/historically relevant.

### S1-07 켈릭

Self-name challenge:
- electrical/contact signals may be part of intimacy or technical shorthand.

Reader policy:
- do not transliterate EM signals as gimmicky punctuation.

### S1-08 베린

Self-name challenge:
- historic production/asset designations may compete with self-chosen lineage name.

Reader policy:
- asset-era labels appear as offensive/archival historical terms, not neutral species name.

## 7. Personal-Name Grammar — Minimal Distinction Only

Species personal names should not require seven fully constructed languages.

Use a small number of naming signatures.

### G1 — single core name + optional household/service name

Good for:
- mixed urban populations;
- Braxi/Numar/Verin working proposals.

### G2 — personal name + place/route affiliation

Good for:
- mobile Seia communities;
- Neutral/route citizens across species.

### G3 — personal name + chosen adult-name replacement

Good for:
- cultures where developmental stage matters;
- some Tesar/S2 life-cycle groups.

### G4 — personal name + institution/workshop lineage

Good for:
- Kelik industrial communities;
- Maker/Steward cultures across species.

### G5 — translated public name + protected self-name

Good for:
- species whose native communication is difficult to render.

Important:
- naming grammar is culture-dependent, not species destiny.
- members of the same species in different regions may use completely different naming customs.

## 8. Species Adjective / Dialogue Use in Korean

Preferred prose:
- `브락시 정비사`
- `세이아 항법사`
- `누마르 의료모듈`

Avoid constant repetition:
- once a named character is established, use their name/role rather than species tag every paragraph.

Avoid:
- “브락시 특유의 성격” unless a biological fact is genuinely relevant;
- narration that explains every behavior through species.

## 9. Homeworld Naming Separation

Species name does not automatically equal homeworld name.

Forbidden default:
- Braxi from Brax;
- Numar from Numa;
- every species state named after species.

Preferred:
- homeworld may have a local ancient name;
- diaspora can outgrow homeworld political importance;
- several species may share a system.

## 10. Faction Naming Separation

Never create:
- “Braxi Empire” merely because Braxi exist;
- “Numar Fleet” as universal species military;
- “Tesar Council” representing the whole species.

A species-majority polity may use a geographic/institutional name independent of species identity.

## 11. Candidate Name Similarity Risks — Internal Only

Before final promotion perform two screens:

### Internal collision screen

Search repository for:
- exact Korean form;
- Latin form;
- one-syllable-near names;
- frame/hull/faction names with similar sound.

### External reference screen

Search public fiction/game/brand naming environments for strong collisions.

A generic coincidence is not automatically disqualifying; direct recognizable genre collision is.

This external screen has **not yet been completed**.

## 13. First-Exposure Recommendation

Do not introduce all seven through a cantina-like parade.

Recommended:
- GA1: hints/background at most;
- GA2 early: one Braxi or Kelik ordinary worker/crew member;
- GA2 middle: Seia or Numar through actual ship/service problem;
- GA3: second/third species become city residents and workers;
- GA4: Ivra or Tesar becomes legally/politically relevant only after ordinary nonhuman life is already normalized;
- GA8: Verin history can intersect Archive classification after present-day Verin people have already appeared.
