## 1. Design Principle

Advanced medicine expands possible families and treatments but does not remove:

- bodily risk;
- time;
- trained staff;
- compatible facilities;
- consent;
- legal parenthood and guardianship;
- economic inequality;
- route and identity access;
- uncertainty about copies and continuity.

No reproductive or genetic technology may appear as a surprise twist without using this bible and prior clueing.

## 2. Reproductive Options

### 2.1 Unassisted and assisted biological pregnancy

Available across most settled regions.

Support may include:
- fertility preservation;
- hormonal and immunological support;
- embryo screening for severe known conditions;
- ectopic/placental monitoring;
- emergency transfer to partial external support.

Limits:
- route isolation and habitat gravity affect care access;
- high acceleration, radiation exposure and military deployment require restrictions;
- no technology makes pregnancy consequence-free.

### 2.2 In vitro conception

Common in Core/Inner and regional medical nodes; limited but available through route clinics elsewhere.

Typical cycle:
- 1–4 months preparation/retrieval;
- multiple attempts often required;
- genetic contributors and intended parents may differ.

Cost:
- 180–900 BSC per standard cycle;
- 1,500–8,000+ BSC for complex genetic/immunological treatment;
- public, employer and mutual-care coverage vary.

### 2.3 Partial ectogenesis

Use:
- premature rescue;
- severe maternal health risk;
- interrupted pregnancy after accident;
- staged support during medical evacuation.

Availability:
- regional hospital and some hospital ships.

Limit:
- not a portable incubator that can be improvised in a small infirmary;
- transfer requires stable power, sterile support, specialist monitoring and legal consent.

### 2.4 Full artificial gestation

Capability:
- established but infrastructure-intensive.

Typical duration:
- 32–40 weeks biological-equivalent development;
- accelerated growth beyond safe developmental windows is prohibited and unreliable.

Facility requirements:
- dedicated gestation unit;
- continuous nutrient/gas exchange;
- endocrine and immune monitoring;
- developmental sensory protocol;
- neonatal and family-integration staff;
- identity/parentage record and contingency guardianship.

Cost:
- 2,500–12,000 BSC in stable public/contract systems;
- frontier or private emergency cases can exceed 20,000 BSC.

Risks:
- facility interruption;
- developmental variation;
- contract ownership claims;
- parent/guardian disputes;
- discriminatory access.

Rule:
- an artificial gestation facility is a strategic medical service, not a baby factory.

## 3. Genetic Medicine Categories

### G0 — Screening and counseling

- carrier/risk screening;
- family and environmental history;
- nonbinding probability, not destiny.

### G1 — Somatic correction

- treats cells/tissues of the current person;
- common targets: blood disorders, immune defects, selected cancers and metabolic disease;
- changes are not generally inherited.

Availability:
- widespread in stable systems, limited by disease and facility.

### G2 — Germline prevention

- narrowly permitted for severe, well-characterized inherited conditions;
- requires multi-party consent and independent review;
- enhancement claims are excluded from standard care.

### G3 — Polygenic/complex intervention

- uncertain, socially controversial and tightly restricted;
- cannot reliably create genius, loyalty, combat skill or personality;
- environment and development dominate many outcomes.

### G4 — Adaptive/prosthetic integration

- immune and neural adaptation for implants, organs and prosthetics;
- can be medically necessary and identity-relevant;
- requires maintenance and may create vendor dependency.

### G5 — Experimental lineage engineering

- rare research/illegal programs;
- high uncertainty, multi-generational risk and political abuse;
- cannot be used as a hidden universal origin for major groups without change control.

## 4. Cloning and Biological Duplication

### Tissue and organ cloning

- common for compatible grafts where facilities exist;
- takes weeks to years depending organ complexity;
- not instant battlefield replacement.

### Whole-body gestational clone

- biologically possible through normal developmental times;
- legally a new person, not property or automatic continuation;
- memories/personality are not copied by DNA.

### Adult body reconstruction

- no rapid full adult clone with intact mind;
- partial body/organ reconstruction remains long-term medicine.

### Neural-state transfer or restoration

- bounded by separate continuity/personhood rules;
- a model, memory archive or behavioral reconstruction is not automatically the same person;
- legal continuity requires current process, consent, history and recognized adjudication.

## 5. Parenthood and Guardianship

Possible legally recognized roles:

- genetic contributor;
- gestational parent;
- intended parent;
- registered parent/guardian;
- cooperative household guardian;
- ship/community guardian;
- state/corporate temporary custodian;
- AI/composite co-parent where personhood and capacity are recognized.

Rules:

- genetic contribution does not automatically grant custody;
- paying for gestation does not create ownership;
- facility failure does not erase intended-parent obligations;
- children may have more than two recognized parents/guardians in some jurisdictions;
- cross-node parentage requires portable records and appeal.

## 6. Consent

Separate consent is required for:

- genetic contribution;
- gestation;
- embryo/fetal intervention;
- data retention;
- research use;
- future contact/identity disclosure;
- parenthood/guardianship;
- neural or composite continuity procedures.

One consent cannot silently authorize all categories.

Emergency care may preserve life without resolving long-term guardianship or data use.

## 7. Reproductive Labor and Social Inequality

Potential exploitation:

- contract gestation tied to debt;
- employer-controlled fertility preservation;
- military pressure to delay or schedule family formation;
- genetic-risk discrimination;
- access to artificial gestation only through corporations/states;
- family registry denial for mobile/stateless people;
- extraction of reproductive or neural data.

Counterinstitutions:
- patient councils;
- cooperative care households;
- neutral parentage courts;
- mutual gestation funds;
- independent genetic counselors;
- right-to-delete and restricted-use records.

## 8. Military and Shipboard Rules

- active high-g pilots require contraception/fertility and pregnancy policies based on informed choice, not blanket ownership of bodies;
- pregnant crew may continue low-risk duty but high-g/combat limits apply;
- cryogenic storage of gametes/embryos is possible but power, custody and identity must be tracked;
- ship infirmaries can stabilize but generally cannot provide full ectogenesis without a dedicated module;
- casualty or missing status creates guardianship and inheritance disputes that may take months across routes.

## 9. AI and Composite Family Formation

Recognized AI/composite persons may:

- enter partnership;
- hold guardianship where jurisdiction recognizes capacity;
- contribute design, memory traditions or care without genetic contribution;
- create derivative processes/persons only under personhood, resource and consent rules.

A derivative AI/person is not automatically:
- a child;
- property;
- the same person;
- an institutional asset.

Possible kin terms:
- origin-relative;
- shared-memory sibling;
- derivative/descendant;
- co-maintainer;
- chosen household member.

## 10. Regional Variation

### Core/Inner

- high technical availability;
- stronger records and public/contract programs;
- greater surveillance, eligibility and institutional control.

### Middle regions

- regional centers offer advanced care;
- travel, insurance and credential barriers shape access;
- mutual funds and employer programs coexist.

### Frontier/mobile

- fertility preservation and basic assisted care may be available;
- full gestation/complex genetics require transport or rare hubs;
- cooperative guardianship and local records more common;
- failure to reach a facility is a real risk.

### Preservation Regime

- strong minimum maternal/neonatal service where recognized;
- model-based eligibility and compulsory continuity records create exclusion/coercion.

### GA10 transition regions

- parentage, identity and care portability become early tests of successful authority distribution.

## 11. Story Prohibitions

- no secret pregnancy/child twist based on technology absent prior clues;
- no genetic program explains morality, loyalty or genius as destiny;
- no clone is treated as disposable copy;
- no artificial gestation removes parenting, care or legal conflict;
- no infertility/disability is used solely as punishment or motivation;
- no institution obtains a child/person merely because it funded treatment.
