## 1. Core Problem

Second Orpheus proves two truths at once:

1. open routes, distributed support and local choice can save people central plans abandon.
2. open routes and shared infrastructure can be seized, overloaded, infiltrated or used to hold entire populations hostage.

After GA6, regions begin adopting incompatible survival doctrines:

- keep routes open under shared guarantees;
- control routes centrally;
- close routes during crisis;
- destroy or misalign nodes before capture;
- move populations and industry into mobile/distributed networks;
- choose local survival over regional continuity.

GA7 asks whether a route federation can prevent hostage crises without becoming an empire of logistics or a scorched network that destroys what it cannot control.

## 3. Route Federation Model

Working name:
- `상호항로연합 / Mutual Route Federation`.

Not a sovereign state at opening.
It is a network of nodes, communities, fleets, medical/industrial routes and local authorities with reciprocal minimum obligations.

### Core rights

- local route/node custody.
- informed refusal of specific traffic/military use.
- independent identity and records.
- exit from political alignment.
- technical stop and safety authority.

### Core obligations

- minimum humanitarian/medical passage.
- emergency warning and data sharing.
- contribution to rescue/repair/defense proportionate to capacity.
- replacement/compensation when withdrawing.
- no unilateral destruction affecting dependent regions without consultation where time allows.
- support for stranded/holdout populations.

### Shared institutions

1. route status and warning network.
2. mutual repair/parts/worker pool.
3. medical/rescue minimum service.
4. distributed authentication and records.
5. emergency convoy/defense mandate.
6. dispute and burden allocation.
7. denial-control registry and physical custody.
8. audit, casualty and responsibility process.

### Strength

- redundancy, local knowledge, consent and resistance to one seizure.

### Weakness

- slow, unequal and dependent on members honoring obligations.
- wealthy/secure nodes can free-ride or veto burdens.
- H-001/logistics authority can become de facto central power.
- denial mechanisms can proliferate beyond oversight.

## 4. Route Denial Doctrine Spectrum

### D0 — Open Passage

- normal trade/civil traffic and limited military transparency.

Benefit:
- economy, medicine, movement and legitimacy.

Risk:
- infiltration, overload and seizure.

### D1 — Controlled Passage

- quotas, inspection, disarmament, priority and escort.

Benefit:
- maintains route while reducing threat.

Risk:
- identity exclusion, delay and gatekeeping.

### D2 — Temporary Closure

- stop traffic for hours/days under local/federation rule.

Benefit:
- prevents immediate military capture/overload.

Risk:
- stranded people, supply collapse and political coercion.

### D3 — Degraded/One-Way Route

- reduce throughput, remove return capacity, limit ship class or physically alter path.

Benefit:
- evacuation or denial without total destruction.

Risk:
- long repair, unequal destinations and trapped groups.

### D4 — Hard Denial

- destroy/misalign one node spine, thermal system or authentication layer.

Benefit:
- prevents strategic capture for months/years.

Risk:
- mass displacement, service loss and regional deaths.

### D5 — Scorched Network

- coordinated destruction of route chain, depots, yards and records.

Benefit:
- denies occupier sustained use.

Risk:
- civilization-scale collapse, famine, medical/identity loss and permanent coercion by the destroyer.

Rule:
- GA7 can cross D4 in one or more cases, but D5 remains red-line/near-crossing rather than normalized solution.

## 5. H-001’s Post-Orpheus Authority

H-001 holds influence through:

- route-federation obligations and community networks.
- manifests, supply and evacuation expertise.
- relationships with Ardis, Doran groups, Neutral, provinces and charter fleet.
- trust among populations that central models abandoned.
- access to route data and denial consequences.

He does not automatically control:

- local node keys.
- civilian councils.
- provincial fleets.
- technical operators.
- P-001’s fleet mandate.
- Neutral members.

Potential formal role:
- elected/contracted `Federation Route Coordinator` with limited term and removal.

Danger:
- the person who can predict where food, medicine and people move can punish dissent without firing.

Required controls:
- independent route custodians.
- public models and dissenting forecasts.
- minimum-service guarantees.
- no secret blacklist.
- emergency closure audit/sunset.
- communities can maintain local records and appeal.

## 6. Major Regional Blocs

### RF-01 — Mutual Route Federalists

Goal:
- preserve connected routes through enforceable shared obligations and local custody.

Constituency:
- Ardis, some provinces, route communities, worker/medical networks and charter fleets.

Strength:
- distributed infrastructure and legitimacy.

Weakness:
- slow decisions and burden disputes.

### RF-02 — Continuity Corridor Authority

Goal:
- centralize key routes, authentication and traffic under emergency continuity management.

Constituency:
- State services, military, hospitals, node operators seeking stability and civilians exhausted by delays.

Strength:
- rapid coordination and reliable minimum flows.

Weakness:
- exclusion, permanent emergency and route-as-permission regime.

### RF-03 — Scorched/Denial Coalition

Goal:
- destroy or render unusable routes/depots before hostile or central seizure.

Constituency:
- abandoned/frontier communities, radical Independence cells, some military/node custodians and survivors.

Strength:
- credible deterrence and local denial.

Weakness:
- destroys shared inheritance and can coerce populations that want passage.

### RF-04 — Local Custodian League

Goal:
- local node control with minimal supra-regional obligation.

Constituency:
- node operators, municipal governments and local defense.

Strength:
- knowledge and accountability to local residents.

Weakness:
- free-riding, fragmented standards and refusal of regional burdens.

### RF-05 — Neutral/Humanitarian Route Compact

Goal:
- protected civilian/medical passage and rescue capacity without military ownership.

Strength:
- legitimacy, ships, medicine, insurance and records.

Weakness:
- capacity and member autonomy; may become selective gatekeeper.

### RF-06 — Mobile Fleet/Industry Network

Goal:
- keep fleets, tenders, workshops and populations moving rather than defend fixed nodes.

Strength:
- flexibility and resilience.

Weakness:
- abandons fixed populations/infrastructure and depends on fuel/repair.

## 8. Cascade Logic

Closing/destroying one node can cause:

- food/medicine/fuel shortages elsewhere.
- refugee/merchant shifts overloading another route.
- military fronts losing support.
- local nodes gaining/losing bargaining power.
- records and citizenship becoming unusable.
- hostile forces rerouting unpredictably.

Route denial must track:

1. direct people at node.
2. dependent populations/industries.
3. alternative capacity and travel delay.
4. repair time and technical labor.
5. military benefit and enemy adaptation.
6. political consent and who decided.
7. holdouts/stranded ships.
8. long-term environmental/economic effects.

No node destruction is merely a tactical explosion.

## 9. GA7 Strategic Conflict Progression

### Phase 1 — Closure Proliferation

- local nodes close independently after Orpheus.
- shortages and military gaps emerge.
- H-001 federation attempts coordination.

### Phase 2 — Denial and Rescue

- one route faces capture and D3/D4 decision.
- successful denial saves one population but harms others.
- Blood Admiral attribution begins.

### Phase 3 — Doctrine War

- Continuity corridor, federation and scorched coalition compete.
- records/propaganda merge several commanders and orders.
- H-001 gains/loses formal authority.

### Phase 4 — Cascading Node Crisis

- multiple branches face simultaneous capture/closure.
- P-001 and H-001 choose different priorities/authority.
- one real harmful H-001 decision must remain his.

### Phase 5 — Federation/Identity Resolution

- route federation survives with denial limits or fractures.
- Blood Admiral record decompressed into title, orders, propaganda and people.
- destructive capacity distributed/limited but not eliminated.

## 10. H-001’s Real Harmful Choice Requirement

Identity decompression cannot erase responsibility.

Recommended choice:
- H-001 authorizes a D4 hard denial of a strategically essential node/route after evacuation is incomplete, believing capture would create a larger hostage system.

Immediate benefit:
- prevents enemy/central seizure and saves another region/front.

Immediate cost:
- 80,000–250,000 people stranded, captured, missing or exposed to severe shortage depending final simulation.
- dependent medical/agricultural branch suffers later deaths.

Why H-001 owns it:
- he has meaningful alternatives and authority.
- information is incomplete but not absent.
- local representatives disagree.
- P-001 does not order the decision.

Why not mass-murder caricature:
- evacuation/alternatives were attempted.
- capture risk is real.
- other actors participate/refuse.
- outcomes remain contested.

This choice becomes one component of Blood Admiral history, not every atrocity.

## 11. Central Continuity Offer

Continuity Office offers:

- authenticated route priority.
- central minimum food/medical/military corridors.
- control of denial keys.
- suppression of unilateral closures.
- predictive allocation and rapid response.

Genuine benefits:
- fewer contradictory closures.
- stable hospital/food routes.
- prevents some local elites/radicals from holding populations hostage.

Costs:
- route access tied to identity/risk/compliance.
- low-value/unverified regions can be deferred.
- emergency corridor authority becomes permanent.

H-001 critique:
- central corridor can abandon undocumented/low-ranked communities invisibly.

Continuity critique of H-001:
- federation lets secure nodes veto burdens and permits charismatic logistics leaders to govern without clear law.

Both critiques must land.
