## 0. Scope

Current canon strongly defines **systems as political, economic and route entities**. This document adds the missing biological/environmental layer without changing the 612-system census or current story events.

Core rule:

> A settled world is not just a node with an economy. It is a life-support problem, an ecology, a history of settlement, and a set of things that cannot be rebuilt on military timescales.

This architecture covers:

- naturally habitable worlds;
- partially habitable worlds;
- artificial habitats;
- terraformed or adapted worlds;
- native/introduced biospheres;
- non-sapient flora/fauna analogues;
- agriculture;
- invasive species;
- disease ecology;
- drought/desertification and other environmental disasters;
- environmental damage from war/industry;
- conservation, restoration and ecological politics.

It does not assign a specific ecology to existing named systems yet.

# PART I — HABITABILITY AND SETTLEMENT

## 1. Settlement-environment classes

Do not classify worlds only as “habitable / uninhabitable.”

### ENV-0 — Fully artificial closed habitat

Examples:
- orbital cylinders;
- stations;
- sealed moon/mining habitats;
- ship-based settlements.

Ecology:
- engineered closed loops;
- agricultural modules;
- controlled microbes;
- imported companion/utility organisms where allowed.

Strength:
- predictable environment.

Weakness:
- life support is infrastructure; contamination or power loss can cascade rapidly.

### ENV-1 — Naturally near-compatible biosphere

- local atmosphere/pressure/temperature broadly supports one or more current species with manageable adaptation.
- native biosphere may be biochemically compatible, partially compatible or completely inedible.

Key rule:
- “breathable” does not mean “safe to eat or biologically familiar.”

### ENV-2 — Partially compatible world

- some regions/seasons/altitudes usable;
- controlled cities/habitats required elsewhere;
- settlement depends on climate control, masks, pressure support, radiation protection or water infrastructure.

### ENV-3 — Engineered-habitable / terraformed world

- habitability depends on centuries-scale or long-duration atmosphere, water, soil/substrate and biosphere engineering.
- maintenance remains necessary even after apparent success.

### ENV-4 — Extreme inhabited world

- settlement exists because resources, history, species adaptation or strategic location justify severe support cost.
- surface/environment may be lethal to most residents outside protected zones.

### ENV-5 — Multi-environment world/system

- several species/service profiles inhabit different pressure, gravity, aquatic, orbital or planetary zones.
- no single “planetary environment” describes lived reality.

This should be common enough in a multi-species civilization.

## 2. Settlement origin modes

Every front-stage settlement receives one or more origins:

1. **native home ecology** — a sapient species evolved there;
2. **early colony** — settled before mature lattice/service standards;
3. **planned state colony**;
4. **industrial/resource settlement**;
5. **refugee or exile settlement**;
6. **religious/civic intentional community**;
7. **military/route outpost grown into civilian settlement**;
8. **corporate/contract colony**;
9. **mixed migration hub**;
10. **post-disaster resettlement**;
11. **terraforming project settlement**;
12. **former abandoned world recolonized later**.

Rule:
- current government need not match settlement founder.

# PART II — BIOSPHERE CLASSES

## 3. Biosphere provenance

### BIOE-0 — Sterile / no significant native biosphere

- life is imported/engineered.
- ecological politics centers closed loops and contamination prevention.

### BIOE-1 — Native microbial/simple ecology

- microorganisms/simple multicellular life dominate.
- can still create major disease, corrosion, soil chemistry or contamination issues.

### BIOE-2 — Complex native biosphere, low biochemical compatibility

- rich ecosystems exist;
- most native food cannot directly nourish settlers or may be toxic/allergenic.
- preservation can conflict with imported agriculture.

### BIOE-3 — Complex native biosphere, partial compatibility

- some biochemical pathways overlap enough for food, medicine or disease interaction.
- creates the richest ecological/economic exchange and highest invasive-species risk.

### BIOE-4 — Heavily engineered hybrid biosphere

- native and introduced life are inseparable after centuries of intervention.
- “restore nature” has no simple historical baseline.

### BIOE-5 — Artificial closed-loop ecology

- crops, microbes, decomposers, pollinators/functional analogues and waste systems intentionally assembled.
- common in habitats and terraformed settlements.

### BIOE-6 — Damaged/collapsed biosphere

- war, industrial pollution, climate shift, introduced organisms or infrastructure failure has pushed the ecosystem below former function.
- restoration may take decades/centuries or be impossible.

---

## 4. Native-life rule

Native alien life is not automatically:
- edible;
- poisonous;
- monstrous;
- intelligent;
- sacred;
- useful technology;
- aggressive toward settlers.

Most organisms should be ecologically ordinary in their own environment.

Reader-facing rule:
- front-stage native life earns attention through **function or character experience**, not encyclopedia dumping.

# PART III — NON-SAPIENT LIFE DESIGN

## 5. Functional life categories

Avoid using Earth labels as literal biology where inappropriate. Author-side analogues are acceptable.

### Producers / primary energy capture

Possible mechanisms:
- photosynthetic analogues;
- chemosynthetic mats;
- radiation/thermal gradient exploitation;
- symbiotic mineral-biological systems.

### Consumers

- grazers/filterers/browsers/predators/scavengers or local equivalents.

### Decomposers / recyclers

- crucial for soil/substrate and closed-loop nutrient cycling.

### Structural/ecosystem engineers

- organisms that build reefs, mats, root networks, shells, tunnels, atmosphere chemistry or water channels.

### Mobile mutualists

- seed/spore/offspring transport;
- pollination analogues;
- cleaning/symbiotic relations.

### Pathogens/parasites

- not automatically cross-compatible with all species;
- can still harm through toxins, inflammation, microbiome disruption or ecological damage.

### Domestic/companion/working organisms

- may be native, imported or engineered;
- require law on welfare, disease, transport and invasive risk.

---

## 6. Flora/fauna codex rule

For every **front-stage** world with a substantial biosphere, design only:

- 2–4 visually memorable common organisms;
- 1 economically important organism;
- 1 ecological keystone or ecosystem engineer;
- 1 nuisance/hazard/pathogen/invasive issue if relevant;
- 1 culturally meaningful species where story-use exists.

Do not create 200 species per planet.

Background systems use ecological templates until needed.

---

## 7. Alien-animal danger rule

Large wildlife should rarely threaten military forces simply because it is large.

Credible danger comes from:
- unfamiliar toxin;
- swarm behavior;
- habitat structural damage;
- contamination;
- sensory interference;
- crop destruction;
- disease vectoring;
- protected-status conflict;
- terrain that makes conventional weapons dangerous.

No routine “space dinosaur beats combat frame” unless exceptional scale and physics are established.

# PART IV — AGRICULTURE AND FOOD SECURITY

## 8. Food-production modes

### AGR-1 Planetary open-field/native-climate agriculture

- highest ecological integration;
- vulnerable to climate, disease and local politics.

### AGR-2 Controlled-environment agriculture

- greenhouses, sealed towers, subterranean farms;
- predictable but power/material intensive.

### AGR-3 Orbital/habitat closed-loop agriculture

- tightly coupled water/nutrient/waste systems;
- resilient to planetary weather, vulnerable to systems failure.

### AGR-4 Aquatic/fluid agriculture

- algae/analogue producers, aquaculture, fluid-support food systems.

### AGR-5 Fermentation / microbial / cultured protein

- high reliability and transport efficiency;
- not a total substitute for dietary/cultural variety.

### AGR-6 Imported ecological package

- crops + microbes + decomposers + pollination/seed systems transported as one managed ecology.

### AGR-7 Native domestication

- local organisms cultivated after long adaptation and safety testing.

---

## 9. Multi-species food rule

A food economy must distinguish:

- calories/energy;
- essential chemistry;
- toxins/allergens;
- cultural food;
- medical diet;
- symbiont support;
- storage/processing compatibility.

A system can be a food exporter while still importing specialist diets for minority species.

This creates realistic trade interdependence.

---

## 10. Agricultural vulnerability ledger

Front-stage food systems may fail through:

- water shortage;
- soil/substrate contamination;
- pollinator/mutualist collapse;
- crop pathogen;
- seed/genetic stock loss;
- fertilizer/nutrient shortage;
- power interruption;
- cooling failure;
- transport embargo;
- labor loss;
- invasive species;
- climate shift;
- war requisition;
- deliberate ecological sabotage.

Rule:
- food shortage is not solved by one shipment if seed stock, water, labor or ecology remains broken.

# PART V — TERRAFORMING AND PLANETARY ENGINEERING

## 11. Terraforming philosophy

Terraforming is not a magic “make Earth” button.

It may include:

- atmosphere modification;
- pressure adjustment;
- greenhouse management;
- water relocation/retention;
- radiation shielding/magnetosphere support;
- soil/substrate creation;
- microbial seeding;
- producer/decomposer ecology;
- controlled introduction of complex life;
- long-term climate feedback management.

---

## 12. Terraforming scale bands

Exact years remain HOLD pending tech-history audit. Use qualitative bands first.

### TF0 — Local environmental engineering

- city/valley/dome scale;
- years to decades;
- current civilization common where resources permit.

### TF1 — Regional planetary engineering

- watershed/atmosphere-local/climate-zone intervention;
- decades to centuries;
- major infrastructure and political continuity required.

### TF2 — Whole-world partial transformation

- atmosphere/water/biosphere changes at planetary scale;
- centuries-scale working assumption until calibrated;
- vulnerable to regime collapse and incomplete feedback.

### TF3 — Mature inherited terraforming

- descendants live in an environment created by ancestors;
- current people may not know or agree with original target state;
- maintenance becomes public infrastructure.

Rule:
- no 6-year main-story terraforming miracle.

---

## 13. Terraforming ethical conflicts

- altering a native biosphere for settler compatibility;
- one species’ preferred atmosphere harming another;
- descendants inheriting maintenance debt;
- corporate ownership of environmental infrastructure;
- indigenous/non-sapient ecosystem rights;
- refugee need versus conservation;
- whether a failed terraforming world should be abandoned or stabilized.

No universal moral answer.

# PART VI — INVASIVE SPECIES AND BIOSECURITY

## 14. Invasive pathways

- cargo;
- food/seed shipments;
- ballast/water/fluid systems;
- soil/substrate;
- pets/working animals;
- medical microbiomes;
- terraforming packages;
- military logistics;
- refugee evacuation;
- salvage/wrecks.

Multi-species civilization increases pathway complexity.

---

## 15. Biosecurity tiers

### BS0 Routine quarantine

- common screening, cleaning and manifest controls.

### BS1 Ecological risk hold

- unknown organism/soil/fluid cannot enter open biosphere before analysis.

### BS2 Agricultural emergency

- crop/pathogen/invasive control with movement restrictions.

### BS3 Habitat life-support contamination

- sealed emergency; immediate public-service consequence.

### BS4 Planetary ecological crisis

- cross-region coordination; may last years/decades.

Rule:
- quarantine is not automatically villainous; abuse comes from evidence, duration, appeal and unequal burden.

# PART VII — DISEASE ECOLOGY

## 16. Cross-species pathogen rule

A pathogen adapted to one alien species should **not** automatically infect every other species.

Cross-species harm may instead occur through:

- shared biochemical receptor coincidence;
- toxin production;
- immune overreaction;
- microbiome disruption;
- symbiont injury;
- vector organisms;
- contaminated food/water;
- cybernetic/biological interface contamination.

This avoids generic “alien plague infects everyone.”

---

## 17. Disease reservoirs

Potential reservoirs:
- native wildlife;
- livestock/domestic organisms;
- environmental water/soil;
- artificial life-support microbiomes;
- symbiotic partners;
- hospital systems;
- terraforming biota.

Medical systems need ecology/provenance, not only patient genomes.

# PART VIII — ENVIRONMENTAL DISASTER TAXONOMY

## 18. Water crisis / drought

Planetary drought is possible even in advanced civilization because water availability is about:

- location;
- energy to move/purify;
- infrastructure;
- contamination;
- aquifer/ice access;
- legal ownership;
- ecosystem requirement;
- route imports.

Possible causes:
- climate oscillation;
- terraforming failure;
- damaged pumps/desalination;
- war;
- upstream diversion;
- aquifer depletion;
- contamination.

Advanced tech reduces risk; it does not make water free.

---

## 19. Desertification / soil collapse

Drivers:
- water mismanagement;
- atmospheric/climate change;
- introduced grazers/organisms;
- industrial contamination;
- war/debris;
- failed engineered soil ecology.

Recovery:
- years to generations, not episode-scale.

---

## 20. Biosphere cascade

Example chain:

```text
route embargo
→ replacement nutrient/pollinator stock delayed
→ farm yield falls
→ local overharvest of native producer
→ keystone collapse
→ water/soil chemistry shifts
→ medical/food crisis
```

Rule:
- major environmental disasters combine physical + ecological + institutional layers.

---

## 21. Atmospheric / climate-control failure

On engineered worlds/habitats:
- atmospheric processors;
- orbital mirrors/shades;
- heat exchangers;
- cloud/water systems;
- pressure retention;
- greenhouse management
can be strategic infrastructure.

Military rule:
- attacking them is equivalent to attacking civilian life-support and may create irreversible regional loss.

---

## 22. Radiation / stellar-event crisis

Can affect:
- crops;
- exposed habitats;
- reproductive health;
- sensors;
- power grids;
- native ecology.

No need to make every stellar event apocalypse; preparedness matters.

---

## 23. Ecological warfare

Possible acts:
- crop pathogen release;
- reservoir contamination;
- invasive release;
- atmosphere-processor sabotage;
- pollinator/mutualist destruction;
- scorched habitat/soil policy.

Control:
- not a cheap superweapon. Delivery, persistence, attribution, blowback and war-crime consequences matter.

# PART IX — WAR, INDUSTRY AND ECOLOGICAL COST

## 24. Military collateral

Fleet/frame wars can damage ecology through:

- orbital debris;
- reactor/propellant contamination;
- atmospheric entry debris;
- port/farm power interruption;
- fire and chemical release;
- habitat decompression;
- soil/water pollution;
- abandoned munitions;
- forced migration and overuse of refuge ecosystems.

Victory ledger should distinguish:
- hull losses;
- infrastructure losses;
- ecological losses;
- recovery time.

---

## 25. Industrial externalities

Mining/manufacturing impacts:
- tailings;
- heat;
- chemical waste;
- water use;
- habitat fragmentation;
- atmospheric emissions;
- introduced organisms;
- worker/community exposure.

Helix/open/local systems can each solve some problems and create others.

---

## 26. Restoration and reclamation

Restoration needs:
- current ecosystem baseline;
- seed/genetic/microbial stock;
- water/substrate repair;
- labor;
- monitoring;
- decades of governance;
- affected-community input.

Ancient “original nature” may be impossible after centuries of hybrid ecology.

# PART X — LAW, ECONOMY AND POLITICS

## 27. Environmental ownership

Separate:
- land/habitat title;
- water rights;
- atmosphere/service rights;
- biological resources;
- genetic/sequence patents;
- conservation trust;
- indigenous/native ecosystem custody;
- public access.

Owning a mine does not automatically mean owning the biosphere around it.

---

## 28. Environmental political actors

Possible institutions:
- watershed/atmosphere boards;
- farm cooperatives;
- terraforming authorities;
- biosphere trusts;
- indigenous/homeworld councils;
- industrial operators;
- public health agencies;
- conservation groups;
- settler/resident assemblies;
- insurers.

Do not reduce environmental politics to one green activist faction.

---

## 29. Environmental economy

Strategic assets can include:
- viable seed/genetic libraries;
- microbial substrate cultures;
- water infrastructure;
- pollination/mutualist stocks;
- soil restoration labs;
- ecological monitoring satellites;
- specialist ecologists/farmers;
- quarantine facilities.

These can be more strategically valuable than rare weapons in some arcs.

# PART XI — SPECIES CODex LINK

## 30. Sapient-species origin requirement

Every core biological S1 species eventually needs:

- an evolutionary/engineered ecology compatible with body plan;
- original gravity/pressure/temperature context;
- food/energy strategy;
- predators/pathogens/competition only if relevant;
- settlement transformation history;
- how modern off-world medicine replicates key environmental needs.

No species may exist only because a designer wanted an unusual silhouette.

---

## 31. Species diaspora ecology

When a species leaves its home ecology, it may depend on:
- imported microbiome;
- specialist food;
- symbionts;
- environmental light/pressure/fluid;
- developmental conditions.

This can create:
- trade dependencies;
- diaspora agriculture;
- political vulnerability;
- new hybrid ecologies.

# PART XIV — BLIND-SPOT GATE

## 35. Reject environmental design if

- every inhabited planet is Earthlike;
- every alien world is a single biome;
- native ecology exists only as monsters;
- terraforming completes within the 6-year main story;
- advanced technology makes food/water/ecology free;
- crop failure is solved by one shipment without restoring cause;
- all species can eat the same food by default;
- quarantine exists only as authoritarian villainy;
- environmental damage has no recovery time;
- mining leaves no externality;
- conservationists are always correct or always obstructive;
- military destruction resets after the battle;
- every planet receives dozens of useless creature names.

---

## 36. Front-stage planet packet requirement

Before a planetary/system location becomes a major setting, record:

- ENV class;
- BIOE class;
- gravity/atmosphere/temperature;
- water source;
- food source;
- one ecological dependency;
- one ecological risk;
- native/introduced life relation;
- settlement origin;
- major environmental institution;
- industrial pressure;
- disaster response;
- species compatibility;
- what is irrecoverable if destroyed.

This is enough for depth without over-simulation.
