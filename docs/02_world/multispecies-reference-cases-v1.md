## 1. Purpose

A species catalogue is not proven until it survives an ordinary ship and an ordinary city.

These two reference cases deliberately test:

- mixed atmospheres;
- gravity/acceleration;
- food;
- sanitation;
- medicine;
- cabins;
- translation;
- rescue;
- work;
- cost;
- social conflict.

They are **not** instructions to alter existing GA2/GA3 cards yet.

# CASE A — Mid-size Neutral/Contract Route Vessel

## 2. Scenario

Ship role:
- convoy/contract support vessel with passenger, cargo, rescue and limited frame-support functions.

Population test:
- 74 crew;
- 38 passengers/clients;
- 112 persons total during a busy route leg.

Biological mix for stress test:
- 62 human/human-derived;
- 12 S1-02 high-g compact;
- 10 S1-03 low-g pressure-sensitive;
- 8 S1-04 fluid-supported;
- 6 S1-05 symbiotic;
- 4 other/rare profiles;
- 10 mixed cybernetic/service needs overlapping the above.

Numbers are test-only, not GA2 canon.

---

## 3. Environment architecture

### A1 — Common spine

- one atmosphere selected for the largest compatible group;
- moderate gravity/acceleration baseline;
- adaptive lighting, tactile and visual alarms;
- environmental supplement ports at regular intervals.

Problem:
- “common” is not neutral. It favors people physiologically closest to the baseline.

### A2 — Dry adaptive cabins

For:
- human/human-derived;
- high-g compact;
- low-g pressure-sensitive with acceleration couches.

Variable:
- bed geometry;
- restraint;
- humidity;
- local floor/handhold configuration.

### A3 — Fluid-support cabin pair

For:
- S1-04 ordinary rest/skin-respiratory maintenance.

Cost:
- fluid mass;
- filtration;
- pump redundancy;
- contamination controls;
- acceleration management.

Lesson:
- only eight fluid-supported passengers can create a ship-design burden much larger than their headcount.

### A4 — Medical refuge

- two common monitored beds;
- four configurable casualty pods;
- one fluid-support emergency pod;
- one high-dependency cybernetic power/interface station.

This is not enough for every simultaneous emergency.

---

## 4. Acceleration conflict case

Situation:
- hostile contact creates a tactical opportunity to accelerate harder for 11 minutes.

Capability:
- S1-02 high-g crew tolerate it well;
- many human crew can tolerate with couch/med limits;
- S1-03 passengers face serious injury risk;
- fluid-supported cabins need pump/structural mode changes;
- cargo restraints are rated below the proposed peak.

Command question:
- ship captain decides safety;
- mission commander can request acceleration but cannot silently override passenger/hull constraints.

Narrative lesson:
- a species advantage does not turn into free ship performance.

---

## 5. Food and galley

Three logistics layers:

1. mutually compatible staples;
2. separate preparation because compounds harmless to one group are dangerous to another;
3. specialist nutrient/biological support.

Conflict:
- emergency ration plan preserves calories but cuts culturally meaningful fresh food and some symbiont-support diversity.

Ordinary-life payoff:
- mixed crews trade recipes/adapters rather than only discussing identity politics.

---

## 6. Sanitation and waste

Shared mistake to avoid:
- one toilet design with decorative adapters.

Required:
- variable body geometry;
- different waste chemistry;
- fluid-cabin recycling;
- contamination isolation;
- prosthetic cleaning/charging.

Economic consequence:
- older vessels may accept diverse passengers only by reducing cargo volume or paying for modular upgrades.

---

## 7. Work assignment

Do not assign by species stereotype.

Test crew:
- one S1-02 person is finance/contract staff, not security;
- one S1-03 person is structural technician, but another is a cook;
- one S1-04 person is route lawyer/claims officer;
- one S1-05 person is propulsion technician;
- human crew include pilots, cleaners, doctors, loaders and managers.

Biology can affect safe duty envelope; profession remains personal/social.

---

## 8. Rescue limit

After a collision:
- 9 people need controlled atmosphere;
- 3 need specialist species medicine;
- 2 need fluid support;
- 1 cybernetic casualty requires high current and diagnostic isolation.

Ship has insufficient specialist capacity.

Correct conflict:
- choose transfer order based on current survival window and available destination, not species hierarchy.

Wrong conflict:
- “alien patients are too expensive, leave them.”

---

## 9. Ship-design verdict

PASS if:
- mixed crew feels ordinary;
- differences create operational decisions;
- no species gets a free tactical bonus;
- adaptation consumes volume, mass, power, staff and training.

# CASE B — Ardis Mixed District Public-Service Node

## 10. Scenario

Location type:
- one GA3-scale district/service interchange, not the whole city.

Population test:
- 58,000 daily users/residents/workers in the catchment;
- biologically mixed due node traffic, foundry labor, hospital access and transit residency.

This does not change Ardis canon population.

---

## 11. Public-space architecture

### B1 — Shared concourse

- common atmosphere;
- low/moderate gravity chosen for broad accessibility;
- multi-height/shape service counters;
- visual, tactile, audio and data signage;
- rest bays with different support geometry.

### B2 — Environment pods

- bookable small MS2 rooms for fluid support, high-pressure rest, thermal cycling or sensory quiet.

Political issue:
- are these accessibility infrastructure or chargeable premium services?

### B3 — Transit segregation by environment, not citizenship

Some transport cars/modules require:
- different pressure;
- fluid containment;
- EM-clean conditions.

Rule:
- operational separation must not become political exclusion without scrutiny.

---

## 12. Hospital test

Hospital capacity:
- MS1 broad emergency medicine;
- limited MS2 specialist beds;
- no full MS3 capacity for every rare species.

Conflict:
- route closure traps three rare-profile patients whose specialist clinic is elsewhere.

Possible solutions:
- remote specialist guidance;
- open adapter medicine;
- Neutral transfer;
- temporary environmental ward conversion.

Cost:
- converting one ward displaces other patients and consumes staff.

This makes multi-species care a real public-budget question.

---

## 13. School test

Mixed public school needs:
- age/development equivalence rather than one chronological year;
- different rest cycles;
- adaptive physical training;
- food safety;
- medical access;
- translation support.

Blind spot:
- a long-lived species juvenile may be older in calendar years while developmentally equivalent to a human teenager. Law must not treat age number alone as maturity.

---

## 14. Foundry/workshop test

Open-standard equipment must support:
- variable reach;
- glove/tool shapes;
- sensory display modes;
- body clearance;
- emergency stop accessibility.

GA3 opportunity:
- Nera/open-standard work can become genuinely “public standard” only if it stops assuming one operator body.

Control:
- this is a future payoff option, not a retroactive GA3 canon change yet.

---

## 15. Civic politics test

Potential local blocs cut across species:

- workers who want subsidized MS2 infrastructure;
- taxpayers/businesses who fear cost;
- rare-profile residents who need guaranteed access;
- localists opposing external standard mandates;
- disability/posthuman activists aligning with multi-species accessibility;
- some members of majority species supporting universal service;
- some minority-species elites preferring private premium service.

Rule:
- species identity does not predict vote.

---

## 16. Disaster test

Event:
- coolant/atmosphere service failure affects one district.

Different impacts:
- common-atmosphere residents have hours;
- fluid-supported residents lose filtration faster;
- pressure-sensitive species suffer from emergency fan/vibration systems;
- electrosense species may be overwhelmed by damaged power infrastructure;
- cybernetic residents face charging/diagnostic outage.

Correct emergency design:
- vulnerability map by current service need.

Wrong design:
- evacuation by species category alone.

---

## 17. Ardis-case verdict

The species layer can strengthen GA3 because its existing themes are already:
- production;
- infrastructure;
- rights;
- joint trust;
- open standards.

But canon promotion requires:
- exact Ardis demographic decision;
- scene-card impact audit;
- hospital/school/foundry state changes;
- continuity check against existing local factions.

---

## 18. Cross-case blind spots

1. **cost visibility** — adapters consume real money/space/power;
2. **rare-service inequality** — equal law does not create equal facility density;
3. **majority baseline** — the “common environment” is itself political history;
4. **emergency scarcity** — specialist pods are finite;
5. **professional stereotyping** — physiology cannot assign jobs;
6. **accessibility coalition** — species, disability and posthuman-rights politics can overlap without becoming one movement;
7. **military spillover** — civilian service standards can become fleet logistics standards later.

## 19. Current verdict

> **PASS AS OPERATIONAL PROOF.**

The proposed species layer can be supported without magical infrastructure, but it creates meaningful volume, mass, medical, legal and budget costs that must be propagated before named species are canonized.
