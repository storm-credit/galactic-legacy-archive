## 1. Atlas Principle

GA8 takes place in inhabited political geography, not a sequence of empty ancient vaults.

Each major archive site must contain:

- current residents and workers;
- ordinary services and dependencies;
- several record custodians with conflicting rights;
- physical and network access limits;
- a reason to preserve the site besides protagonist discovery;
- a cost created by copying, moving or synchronizing its records;
- a future that continues after the expedition.

No single site contains the full truth or can activate the Seed alone.

## 2. Regional Route Overview

GA8 uses five principal sites linked through three corridor classes.

```text
                         CORE REGISTRY SPHERE
                                 |
                    [A1 Meridian Founding Vault]
                                 |
                      trunk / 2–5 days priority
                                 |
            [A2 Orsen Witness Mesh] -- medical corridor
                   |                          |
              5–12 days                  7–16 days
                   |                          |
           [A3 Ardis Route Mirror] ---- damaged outer trunk
                   |                          |
              12–28 days                 8–20 days
                   |                          |
          [A4 Palimpsest Reach] ===== low-capacity mirror chain
                   |

                   |
       [A5 Continuity Assembly Constellation]
```

Times assume:
- stable priority packet/ship access;
- current political permission;
- no large fleet concentration;
- no damaged-mirror synchronization delay.

Large evidence sets and AI/composite state move more slowly than people or signed summaries.

## 3. A1 — Meridian Founding Vault

### Identity

Working name:
- `Meridian Founding Vault / 자오선 건국보관권`.

Node class:
- Core L1 archive and ceremonial identity system linked to an old Crown administrative world.

Population:
- 38 million in the surrounding system;
- 640,000 directly employed or resident in the archive, court, university, memorial and service complex;
- 60,000–110,000 visitors, petitioners and staff in normal cycles.

### Physical form

The site is distributed across:

1. a surface capital archive district;
2. a subterranean thermal-stable vault lattice;
3. an orbital legal mirror and witness station;
4. university/curator habitats;
5. ceremonial memorial fields;
6. off-site disaster copies controlled by separate offices.

No one room contains “the original history.”

### Ordinary economy and life

- archivists, conservators, court clerks, teachers, maintenance crews, translators and memorial workers;
- tourism, pilgrimage and legal petitions;
- families live in archive districts across generations but are not hereditary owners;
- public schools teach simplified imperial history while universities contest it;
- food and daily life are Core urban rather than monastic isolation.

### Record holdings

- founding covenants;
- succession oaths;
- early continuity and emergency-service laws;
- curated war records;
- sealed diplomatic copies;
- ceremonial identities and state memorial rolls;
- indices pointing toward regional mirrors.

### Rights and control

- physical vault: Meridian Archive Service;
- legal use: courts and state offices;
- ceremonial access: Crown/public memorial bodies;
- research access: universities and licensed curators;
- sealed copies: succession and Continuity trustees;
- affected-person claims: limited and historically weak.

### Local factions

- State Curators — defend continuity and controlled release;
- Revision Historians — expose selection and exclusions;
- Memorial Families — protect names, honor and privacy;
- Civil-Service Continuity Bloc — prioritize executable legal records;
- Right-to-Forget Petitioners — oppose permanent use of family/intimate history;
- Security Trustees — fear forged or destabilizing releases.

### Access obstacles

- legal standing and court schedule;
- sealed-copy quorum;
- physical retrieval time;
- public protest and family claims;
- competing genuine versions;
- archival conservation risk;
- state security and succession conflict.

### Major GA8 choice

Retrieve and synchronize a sealed founding/continuity index that can authenticate deeper mirrors.

Tradeoff:
- full public release reveals elite manipulation and private family data;
- sealed operational use preserves service continuity but repeats invisible authority;
- redacted plural release slows synchronization and leaves disputes unresolved.

### Combat/security geometry

- orbital mirror can be isolated without controlling surface vaults;
- heavy bombardment destroys legitimacy and copies without guaranteeing secrecy;
- interior conflict uses small teams, legal seizure, information control and public crowds;
- frames are useful only around orbital service structures and disaster vault access.

### Continued existence

After GA8, Meridian remains:
- a functioning court/archive city;
- a political battleground over corrections and memorials;
- one source among many, not the capital of truth.

## 4. A2 — Orsen Witness Mesh

### Identity

Working name:
- `Orsen Witness Mesh / 오르센 증언망`.

Node class:
- medical/identity L3 cluster expanded from treatment habitats, Black Ward descendants and consent-controlled archives.

Population:
- 2.6–3.4 million across the wider Orsen system;
- 420,000 patients, long-term care residents, caregivers and protected identities;
- 190,000 medical/research/service workers;
- approximately 32,000 recognized AI or composite clinical/witness processes, only a fraction claiming personhood.

### Physical form

- hospital cylinders and low-gravity rehabilitation habitats;
- protected identity residences;
- clinical data mirrors;
- physical tissue/prosthetic libraries;
- consent and claims courts;
- old Black Ward isolation structures converted unevenly;
- Helix, public and Neutral facilities interlocked.

### Ordinary economy and life

- advanced medicine and prosthetics;
- patient housing and caregiver labor;
- pharmaceuticals and sterile manufacturing;
- medical tourism and contract treatment;
- schools and work programs for long-term residents;
- identity correction and family reunification services.

### Record holdings

- treatment histories;
- closed/duplicated identities;
- witness interfaces;
- patient-controlled memory fragments;
- neural and prosthetic continuity data;
- evidence of state/corporate transfers;
- old Authority C phrases and protocols.

### Rights and control

- patients and recognized persons hold consent rights;
- hospitals control current clinical operation;
- Helix holds parts, model and contract claims;
- courts/Neutral bodies control selected evidence use;
- families and guardians may have standing but not ownership;
- clinical AI systems may be tools, witnesses, institutions or persons depending current status.

### Local factions

- Patient Self-Rule Assembly;
- Clinical Continuity Coalition;
- Helix Human Performance and Service;
- Public/Neutral Medical Trust;
- Protected Identity Network;
- AI/Composite Personhood Forum;
- research and public-safety regulators.

### Access obstacles

- meaningful consent;
- current health and treatment dependency;
- intimate data minimization;
- conflicting guardian/family claims;
- AI identity uncertainty;
- proprietary models and parts;
- risk that synchronization changes current eligibility or exposes protected residents.

### Major GA8 choice

Use the Witness Mesh to prove that legal death, person continuity and Archive compression are linked.

Tradeoff:
- copying enough data to improve Seed identity reconstruction may expose or reclassify patients;
- refusing synchronization protects autonomy but leaves millions of disputed identities without service restoration;
- aggregate translation reduces intimacy but also erases individual testimony.

### Security geometry

- medical decks are protected but cannot house unlimited refugees/evidence;
- network isolation threatens treatment;
- physical seizure requires many habitats and clinical teams, not one server;
- combat near hospitals creates route, oxygen and medicine consequences.

### Continued existence

After GA8:
- Orsen becomes a central battleground over classification and service eligibility in GA9;
- some residents benefit first from Aurel;
- others become permanent review cases;
- patient and AI rights institutions survive into GA10.

## 5. A3 — Ardis Route Mirror

### Identity

Working name:
- `Ardis Route Mirror / 아르디스 항로다원경`.

Node class:
- living regional continuity mirror distributed across the Ardis Joint Trust, route federation, yards, ships and district records.

Population:
- 1.6–2.1 million residents after post-GA3 growth and migration;
- additional 300,000–700,000 people linked through route/fleet/community obligations;
- numerous mobile crews and mirror custodians.

### Physical form

The “mirror” is not one archive:

- Anchor Crown operational logs;
- Civic Ring legal/corrective records;
- Foundry maintenance and labor archives;
- Transit Assembly identity/witness ledgers;
- route-federation mirrors aboard ships and ports;
- Orpheus and Blood Admiral plural after-action records;
- patient/medical minimum-service ledgers.

### Ordinary economy and life

- node transit and repair;
- ship/frame production;
- food and cargo exchange;
- education and operator training;
- arbitration, identity correction and public-service accounting;
- political tourism and migration attracted by joint-trust reputation.

### Record holdings

- contradictory operational accounts with known authors and affected parties;
- command/withdrawal records;
- worker and maintainer contributions;
- evacuation and casualty chains;
- current corrections to official hero/war histories;
- local technical standards and rights implementation.

### Rights and control

- each source remains with its institution/person/community;
- joint mirror stores provenance and translation, not universal ownership;
- local courts and boards determine current legal use;
- external users can query only agreed scopes;
- emergency synchronization requires multiple domain keys.

### Local factions

- Civic and Joint-Trust Continuity;
- Transit/Unregistered Representation;
- Foundry and Standards Network;
- Route Federation;
- Haren-related but independent corridor blocs;
- military/fleet veterans and harmed-region assemblies;
- local privacy and refusal advocates.

### Access obstacles

- slow plural approval;
- incompatible systems;
- politically active affected communities;
- records that implicate current allies;
- local refusal to become a universal archive;
- traffic and service workload competing with GA8 expedition demands.

### Major GA8 choice

Export enough plural architecture and current corrections to prevent Meridian/Seed compression.

Tradeoff:
- exporting the model risks turning Ardis practices into a universal template they were not designed to be;
- refusing central integration protects local governance but weakens galaxy-scale reconstruction;
- faster summaries reproduce single-author history.

### Security geometry

- distributed sites cannot be seized quickly;
- control of Anchor Crown affects traffic but not all records;
- ships can carry copies away;
- local labor and institutions can refuse operation;
- attack creates immediate service and legitimacy consequences.

### Continued existence

Ardis remains:
- a powerful but imperfect regional model;
- resistant to both Imperial and Aurel monopoly;
- vulnerable to local elite/technical capture;
- one source for GA10 staged distribution.

## 6. A4 — Palimpsest Reach

### Identity

Working name:
- `Palimpsest Reach / 중첩기록 변경`.

Nature:
- an old, irregular outer region containing damaged S2 continuity mirrors, maintenance habitats, AI communities, descendants of custodians, salvage settlements and abandoned routes.

Geographic scale:
- 11 inhabited systems;
- 18–26 partial mirrors and service installations;
- 4 stable local routes, 7 intermittent routes and numerous dead references;
- 42–78 million residents across the wider region;
- only 3–6 million directly depend on mirror-service economy.

### Why it survived

- low strategic importance after later route changes;
- distributed physical copies;
- local communities kept specific functions alive;
- no faction could afford to restore or destroy the whole region;
- some mirrors were deliberately obscured after autonomous-war poisoning.

### Five internal zones

#### P-1 — Sable Index System

Function:
- source indices, checksum lineages and cross-archive location maps.

Population:
- 5.4 million.

Economy:
- navigation, index verification, salvage claims, education and data transport.

Conflict:
- index guilds sell access and hide dangerous/valuable locations;
- local government fears becoming an expedition colony.

#### P-2 — Loom Habitats

Function:
- record translation, format emulation and old AI/model maintenance.

Population:
- 9–12 million across linked habitats.

Culture:
- translation apprenticeships, machine-language craft and strong authorship rituals.

Conflict:
- translators can become hidden sovereigns over meaning;
- some AI processes claim continuity/personhood.

#### P-3 — Ash Choir Worlds

Function:
- preserve autonomous-war and collapse records through deliberately isolated, mutually checking archives.

Population:
- 18–27 million.

Culture:
- public distrust of unified networks; local rituals mark periods of deliberate silence.

Conflict:
- synchronization is viewed as existential danger;
- current economies still need outside medicine and route data.

#### P-4 — Vesper Maintenance Chain

Function:
- power, cooling, physical mirror repair and old Authority F/G hardware.

Population:
- 3.2–4.8 million.

Economy:
- heavy service, mining, thermal materials, repair crews and route tenders.

Conflict:
- workers own practical continuity but lack legal recognition in Core histories;
- equipment failure creates a hard synchronization deadline.

#### P-5 — Quiet Harbor

Function:
- refuge for deleted, disputed or voluntarily unsynchronized identities and AI/person communities.

Population:
- 7–13 million, many irregular or locally recognized.

Culture:
- current chosen identity and limited disclosure valued above historical restoration.

Conflict:
- Seed activation could restore rights to some and destroy chosen privacy for others;
- hostile actors call it a sanctuary for forged identities and dangerous AIs.

### Regional governance

No unified state.

Common institutions:
- mirror custodian councils;
- route and maintenance cooperatives;
- local governments;
- AI/person assemblies;
- salvage and index guilds;
- Quiet Harbor identity covenants;
- temporary regional congress under external threat.

### Regional military/security

- 22–40 local combat ships, mostly corvette/frigate class;
- 70–130 armed patrol/rescue/industrial craft;
- 180–350 military/security frames and many utility frames;
- strong fixed local knowledge, weak unified command;
- route denial and data quarantine more important than fleet battle.

### Major GA8 choices

1. which mirrors synchronize;
2. whether index locations become public;
3. whether AI/person continuity data may move;
4. whether Quiet Harbor identities participate;
5. how much power/heat/traffic the region sacrifices;
6. who receives Authority F/G translation access.

### Cost of expedition

- fuel, medicine and specialized parts diverted from residents;
- outside fleets and collectors arrive;
- old conflicts become executable legal claims;
- synchronization can damage or expose current systems;
- local communities may lose control of their reason for survival.

### Continued existence

Palimpsest does not become a museum or capital.
It remains:
- a contested multi-system region;
- central to plural record, personhood and right-to-forget politics;
- partially synchronized, with some zones permanently refusing Aurel.

## 7. A5 — Continuity Assembly Constellation

### Identity

Working name:
- `Continuity Assembly Constellation / 연속성 합의성좌`.

Nature:
- a dormant/limited L0–L1 node constellation and distributed compute/authority site that can form the Seed consensus layer only when multiple mirrors, operators and institutions participate.

Location:
- at the edge of an old trunk intersection beyond Palimpsest;
- reachable by two damaged routes and one long Core chain;
- not hidden from history, but its actual function was fragmented and politically suppressed.

Resident population:
- 2.1–3.6 million across service habitats, miners, maintenance families, observers and displaced populations;
- 80,000–140,000 direct technical/operational workers;
- several AI/institutional processes with disputed standing.

### Physical form

- six widely separated node/compute anchors;
- thermal and power moons/habitats;
- traffic-control arrays;
- physical vaults and model-training facilities;
- operator communities;
- no single central chamber.

### Required inputs

- Meridian legal/founding index;
- Orsen identity/person continuity constraints;
- Ardis plural/correction architecture;
- Palimpsest provenance and translation;
- current route/node state;
- physical power, cooling, operators and secure communication;
- Authority F for translation;
- bounded Authority G for emergency curator invocation.

### Local constituencies

- service workers and families;
- regional residents dependent on power and routes;
- Continuity Office expedition;
- AI/model custodians;
- Neutral and state observers;
- local autonomy and anti-activation coalitions;
- people hoping activation restores identity, pay or medicine.

### Material constraints

- activation consumes enormous power/heat and route capacity;
- synchronization takes hours/days and can fail by domain;
- current services may be reduced during operation;
- damaged anchors create asymmetric model confidence;
- physical operators can stop or alter local participation;
- military seizure cannot manufacture missing mirror consent or data.

### GA8 climax function

The protagonists and factions do not “enter the archive.”
They assemble a temporary, politically bounded consensus across five sites.

The activation decision must specify:
- included sources;
- excluded/refusing regions;
- confidence disclosure;
- correction and appeal;
- operational domains;
- emergency expiry;
- who can invoke Authority G;
- who bears activation losses.

### Transition to GA9

Initial bounded activation:
- restores selected identity, medicine, pay, route and command functions;
- stops immediate record/command collapse;
- creates strong public demand for expansion;
- gives Continuity institutions practical authority;
- begins classification and synchronization pressure.

### Continued existence

In GA9–10 the constellation becomes:
- one technical root of Aurel, not the whole regime;
- a strategic target and service dependency;
- eventually split into domain and regional authorities;
- unable to retain one permanent master key.

## 8. Travel and Data Classes

### People and small teams

- Core to Meridian: hours to days.
- Meridian to Orsen/Ardis: days to weeks.
- Ardis to Palimpsest: 12–28 days stable priority; longer under war.
- Palimpsest internal travel: 1–14 days.
- Palimpsest to Assembly: 4–12 days when route available.

### Data

- signed index or order: hours/days along active chain;
- compressed archive summary: days;
- full high-resolution evidence: weeks/months or physical courier;
- AI/composite continuity state: physical and consent-controlled transfer preferred;
- live consensus synchronization: requires temporary dedicated bandwidth and route stability.

## 9. Faction Logistics

### Imperial/Continuity expedition

Strength:
- legal standing, trained operators, secure transport, public-service need.

Weakness:
- Core legitimacy weak in Palimpsest; heavy fleets slow and provocative.

### Helix

Strength:
- parts, medical support, compute, models and maintenance.

Weakness:
- contract/data suspicion and lack of universal legal standing.

### Neutral Assembly

Strength:
- transport, claims, witness and cross-border legitimacy.

Weakness:
- no unified command and limited willingness to fight.

### Independence/local networks

Strength:
- routes, local allies and anti-seizure legitimacy.

Weakness:
- uneven services and internal disagreement over synchronization/destruction.

### Ardis/plural networks

Strength:
- operational plurality and correction practice.

Weakness:
- slow, resource-intensive and vulnerable to being treated as universal answer.
