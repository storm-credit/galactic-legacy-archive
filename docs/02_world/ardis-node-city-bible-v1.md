## 1. Core Identity

Working name:
- `아르디스 / Ardis`.

Node class:
- damaged high-capacity L3 relay with an incomplete, politically contested path to L2 regional-node status.

Permanent population at GA3 start:
- approximately 1.42 million.

Stranded/transient population:
- approximately 170,000, fluctuating between 120,000 and 220,000.

Central contradiction:

> 아르디스는 ‘구조를 기다리는 난민도시’가 아니라 이미 스스로 살아남기 위해 고장 난 노드와 공장과 제도를 운영하는 도시다. 외부 세력은 도움을 주는 동시에 그 생존 능력을 자기 소유권으로 바꾸려 한다.

Strategic value:
- Silex bypass can reduce Lumen Gate monopoly.
- medium ship/frame repair and agricultural transfer.
- existing worker/utility knowledge compatible with Open Service lineage.
- regional population and local legitimacy sufficient to become alternative political center.

Why it was minor in original history:
- node failure, blockade, evacuation and record reclassification prevented L2 restoration.
- later histories treated Ardis as a failed transit camp or collateral route loss.

Changed-timeline significance:
- GA2’s Silex window, open-standard network and community migration give the city people, tools and external claims earlier.
- its survival now changes fleet logistics, frontier politics and succession-war routes.

## 2. Physical System

Ardis is not one planet-city.

System structure:

1. `Anchor Crown` — nonrotating lattice node/traffic complex around a mined inner moon.
2. `Civic Ring` — main rotating habitat and administration/markets.
3. `Foundry Cylinder` — industrial O’Neill-style cylinder/shipyard cluster.
4. `Garden Chain` — agricultural and water habitats in linked orbits.
5. `Transit Belt` — cargo depots, temporary habitats, refugee ships and service platforms.
6. `Surface Works` — mining, heat sinks, energy and propellant facilities on/inside the moon.
7. `Outer Defense and Salvage Field` — fixed batteries, tug points, debris and abandoned construction.

Approximate population:

| Zone | Permanent | Transient | Main function |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Anchor Crown | 62,000 | 35,000 | node operations, customs, command, traffic |
| Civic Ring | 520,000 | 35,000 | housing, governance, commerce, hospitals, education |
| Foundry Cylinder | 410,000 | 25,000 | repair, frame/ship components, worker communities |
| Garden Chain | 210,000 | 15,000 | food, water, biological processing |
| Transit Belt | 120,000 | 60,000+ | cargo, temporary residents, unregistered settlements |
| Surface Works | 98,000 | small | mining, propellant, energy, heavy engineering |

Total may vary with evacuation and ship traffic.

## 3. Spatial Relations and Travel

Approximate local travel under normal operation:

- Anchor Crown ↔ Civic Ring: 18–35 minutes by shuttle/tether route.
- Anchor Crown ↔ Foundry: 35–80 minutes.
- Civic Ring ↔ Foundry: 25–60 minutes.
- Civic Ring ↔ Garden Chain: 45 minutes–3 hours depending habitat/orbit.
- Anchor Crown ↔ Surface Works: 20–50 minutes.
- Transit Belt platforms: 15 minutes–6 hours from main zones.

During siege/node instability:
- traffic control, debris and orbital alignment can multiply times by 2–5.
- a character cannot cross the whole city in one short scene.

Maneuver frames:
- useful around Anchor Crown, Foundry, Surface Works, debris and docking structures.
- large frames cannot enter many Civic/Garden interiors.
- utility suits/drones/humans handle interior repair and security.

Ships:
- control approach, blockade, convoy and long-range fire.
- cannot safely seize/repair Anchor Crown or habitats through bombardment.

## 4. Node Condition at Arrival

Nominal traffic capacity:
- 46% of pre-damage rated throughput during stable window.

Reliable daily window:
- 2 short transitions plus low-bandwidth packet synchronization.

Unstable/emergency window:
- possible through Silex with manual/open-service calibration, but heat, phase drift and authentication errors accumulate.

Main failures:

1. one of three phase-reference spines damaged and politically isolated.
2. cooling/energy storage below sustained L2 requirement.
3. traffic/authentication stack split among local, Imperial and creditor keys.
4. old emergency service bus undocumented or suppressed.
5. structural ring/anchor alignment requires external frame/tug work.
6. operator workforce understaffed and divided.
7. corrupted identity/traffic data creates legal and safety delays.

Countdown at E211:
- food: 24–38 days without reliable Vela/Lumen/alternative convoy.
- advanced medicine: 12–20 days for critical items.
- node cooling reserve: 16–28 days before capacity must be reduced further.
- municipal credit/payroll: 20–35 days before essential-worker strike/default.
- transit-hab life support: several sectors only 8–15 days without parts/relocation.

These clocks do not all expire simultaneously; factions prioritize different ones.

## 5. Economy

### Primary sectors

- ship/frame overhaul and component fabrication.
- cargo transfer and storage.
- agricultural/biological processing.
- mining/propellant/thermal materials.
- local utility-frame production.
- node traffic, customs and data services.

### Self-sufficiency at GA3 start

| Resource | Local coverage | Vulnerability |
|---|---:|---|
| basic food/calories | 72–82% | fertilizer cultures, power and water links |
| water/air recycling | 91–96% | spare membranes, energy, habitat damage |
| basic medicine | 55–70% | reagents and sterile production |
| advanced medicine/neural/prosthetic | 25–38% | Orsen/Helix dependence |
| common frame/ship parts | 58–72% | advanced electronics/materials short |
| missiles/countermeasures | 20–35% | specialized imports and legal controls |
| energy/propellant | 80–110% normal | distribution/cooling and node demand |
| high-grade authentication/data | 35–55% | Lumen/Crownline/Helix/Neutral keys |

### Currency and debt reality

- Imperial currency, local service credits, worker mutual accounts and contract scrip coexist.
- local government owes Imperial/Helix/Neutral creditors.
- workers are often paid partly in housing, food and claim shares.
- stranded residents create both labor and fiscal/medical pressure.

Economic conflict:
- restore node first to regain revenue.
- feed/treat population first.
- preserve industrial assets for long-term survival.
- accept creditor takeover for immediate supplies.

No solution funds all four.

## 6. Social Classes and Status

### Registered civic residents

- legal vote/claims, housing and services.
- fear that mass inclusion will dilute scarce rights/resources.

### Foundry and utility workers

- operate critical systems but ownership/representation fragmented.
- certified, guild, independent and unregistered workers conflict.

### Transit residents / displaced people

- range from documented merchants to stateless refugees.
- provide labor and traffic knowledge but often lack vote/housing.

### Node operators and technical custodians

- small high-skill group with disproportionate practical power.
- accused of withholding knowledge and creating hereditary technical class.

### Creditors/contract officials

- Helix, Imperial and Neutral agents controlling supplies, claims and route access.

### Security/militia/military

- local defense, Imperial detachments, union guards and faction cells.
- divided command and ammunition.

### Garden and care communities

- food/water/health labor often politically undervalued.
- can stop the city without firing.

## 7. Civic Culture

Ardis identity:
- “the city that repairs what others abandon.”

Local practices:
- public boards record repair/shift contributions and unresolved failures.
- major systems carry multiple repair marks rather than one owner logo.
- annual/seasonal `Restart Day` commemorates systems brought back after the original node accident; official version minimizes casualties and labor conflict.
- transit residents have separate markets, foods and message walls.
- Foundry communities value visible maintenance labor; Civic Ring values legal continuity and public services.

Memory conflict:
- official memorial lists registered casualties.
- worker/transit memorial includes names, aliases, work tags and unknown counts.

Food/life:
- Garden Chain produces fresh but rationed greens/fermented protein and rare fruit.
- transit districts use mixed route cuisine and informal cooking.
- energy/cooling shortages change meal times, water and public space.

Ordinary stakes:
- school enrollment, ration queues, heating/cooling, shift safety, housing claims, identity correction, funerals and wage payment.

## 13. City Success/Failure States

### Total Imperial/Helix takeover

Pros:
- immediate supplies, authentication, security and capital.

Costs:
- local ownership/representation lost, open system contained, unregistered population excluded or relocated.

### Independence seizure

Pros:
- breaks Imperial/creditor monopoly, opens route.

Costs:
- weak recognition, sanctions, internal blocs, possible militarization/destruction.

### Neutral trusteeship

Pros:
- route/evidence/claims credibility.

Costs:
- expensive, limited defense, member capture and weak democratic legitimacy.

### Local joint trust — recommended direction

- physical operators/workers, civic residents, transit/unregistered groups, external guarantors and users hold different rights.
- no one actor owns full node/title/authentication/production.

Costs:
- slow and conflict-heavy.
- requires minimum-service obligations, audit, succession and enforcement.
- can still be captured by local elites.

### Destruction/abandonment

- avoids strategic capture but kills/displaces people and deepens regional collapse.
- must remain credible option for radicals and desperate residents.
