## 2. Four Reader-Memory Directions

### A — `Rotating Institution`

Core memory:
- K-13 is remembered first through **gravity changing as one moves through authority layers**.

Visual/sensory grammar:
- long curved horizon in student/civic rings;
- radial transfer tower changes floor/body orientation;
- central spine shifts to microgravity and handhold movement;
- emergency rotation change makes a familiar corridor suddenly dangerous.

Why it works:
- a school/correctional institution becomes physically distinct without decorative sci-fi architecture.
- Rian’s body limits, Haren’s route memory, Mia’s medical work and siege blocking all gain physical texture.

Risk:
- overusing “gravity weirdness” every scene becomes gimmick.

Use:
- introduction, transfer scenes, one or two crisis payoffs per block.

### B — `Three Faces of One Academy`

Core memory:
- same institution has three materially different public faces.

Ring A — Civic Face:
- bright, quiet, standardized, visible cameras, legal/visitor/sponsor spaces.

Ring B — Student Habitat:
- shared dorms, ration/dining, classrooms, sim halls, community spaces; controlled but lived-in.

Ring C — Industrial Works:
- lower gravity, machinery vibration, repair smells, workers, market/storage, parts and hidden maintenance opportunity.

Reader effect:
- the academy is not one sterile military corridor.

Risk:
- color-coding A/B/C into three theme-park zones.

Control:
- material/function differences, not arbitrary palette differences.

### C — `Jurisdiction Written On Space`

Core memory:
- ownership and authority are visible in **doors, floor lines, cages, access panels, checkpoints and separate control stations**.

Existing anchors:
- sponsor-owned ammunition/tools;
- floor markings in Dock D;
- parts cages;
- software/charging zones;
- access routes to Black Ward;
- public clinic vs controlled medical corridor;
- White Dock as distinct civilian facility rather than “academy dock.”

Reader effect:
- later themes of split authority are learned physically before they become politics.

Strength:
- supports entire-series ending without exposition.

Risk:
- signage/UI overload.

Control:
- every scene foregrounds at most one jurisdictional obstacle.

### D — `Old Yard / White Dock Scar Layer`

Core memory:
- current institution is built beside systems older, less tidy and differently governed than itself.

Old Yard:
- skeletal trusses, storage pods, sealed workshops, detached hull sections, partial power, old contractor systems.

White Dock:
- civilian repair/cargo/identity/transport archive function with different traffic and authority from K-13.

Reader effect:
- 07 is not found behind a mystical secret door. It is buried in **mismatched registration, old service infrastructure and forgotten labor history**.

Strength:
- collection fantasy comes from re-reading ordinary infrastructure.

Risk:
- Old Yard becomes “ancient dungeon in space.”

Hard control:
- no ancient omnipotent tech, no endless secret hangars, no intact legendary fleet.

Why foreground:

- social hierarchy;
- ordinary student life;
- siege rationing later.

Memory function:

No need for a new proper name.

### 4.2 Transfer Tower T2 / Transit Core

Why foreground:
- changing gravity;
- official vs covert travel;
- mixed passengers/checkpoints;
- emergency closure isolates rings.

Memory function:
- recurring movement landmark rather than decorative atrium.

### 4.3 Ring C Shared Workshop / Parts Cage

Why foreground:
- Nera;
- 07 repair;
- ownership and certification physically visible;
- industrial ordinary life.

Memory function:
- collection progression becomes “what can be legally/physically touched and repaired.”

### 4.4 Dock D / 07 Berth

Why foreground:
- 07 does not begin in a sacred hangar.
- low-priority berth near old service trunk better supports its identity.

Visual rule:
- nearby sponsor equipment looks more standardized; 07’s survival should read through service accessibility and mismatched history, not glowing uniqueness.

### 4.5 Black Ward Access Corridor

Why foreground:
- near public medicine but physically isolated by controlled low-g route.

Memory function:
- genuine care and coercive research coexist spatially.

Hard veto:
- horror-lab red lighting shorthand.

### 4.6 Old Yard

Why foreground:
- first large “history is infrastructure” environment.

Memory function:
- a later return should let readers recognize specific truss/service logic, not a generic debris field.

### 4.7 White Dock

Why foreground:
- civilian repair/cargo/identity archive;
- E14–20 enemy ace/retrieval objective;
- first proof that an enemy can have a legitimate rescue/record mission.

Memory function:
- exterior repair space, archive/custody function and civilian traffic should coexist.

## 5. Sensory Grammar

### Gravity/body

- Ring A/B/C should not all feel identical underfoot.
- radial travel can produce brief Coriolis/inner-ear discomfort.
- spine scenes use handholds/rails/controlled push rather than normal walking.

### Sound

Cause-based only:
- ring machinery and balance correction;
- transfer/elevator lock cycles;
- workshop/recycling vibration;
- Dock D clamp/pressure cycles;
- Old Yard partial-power silence punctuated by active service segments.

### Smell/air

Ring C already supports:
- lubricant;
- metal;
- ozone;
- recycling/processing smells.

Ring B/Civic should not be odorless, but do not invent a scent catalog.

### Light

Do not use one “futuristic blue” palette.

Light should follow function:
- public inspection visibility in Ring A;
- circadian/domestic utility in Ring B;
- work/safety visibility in Ring C;
- service/maintenance lighting in Old Yard;
- docking/external task lighting at White Dock.

## 7. Three State Changes

### K0 — Controlled normality

- routine works;
- coercion exists inside functioning school/service systems;
- spaces are not dystopian ruin.

### K1 — Inquiry / Black Ward exposure

- familiar circulation routes gain new restrictions and meanings;
- access layers become visible.

### K2 — Siege / charter aftermath

- same rings/towers/docks become evacuation/defense/service fronts;
- damage and emergency markings remain after battle;
- reopening does not restore pristine status quo.

## 8. Collectible-Desire Loop

1. `Where am I allowed to go?`
2. `What exists behind the next authority boundary?`
3. `What can Nera/Rian actually repair/use here?`
4. `What history is hidden by ordinary registration rather than a secret vault?`
5. `Which route remains usable when the institution turns hostile?`
6. `How does a place first seen as coercive become something students choose to preserve under a new charter?`

The place itself changes meaning; this is the collectible reward.
