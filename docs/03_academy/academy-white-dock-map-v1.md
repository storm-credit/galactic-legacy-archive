## 1. Spatial Principle

The academy must support:

- ordinary student life under partial gravity.
- industrial labor and frame maintenance.
- hidden but physically plausible surveillance gaps.
- rapid official movement and slower covert movement.
- separated legal jurisdictions and ownership zones.
- multi-front siege where no single pilot can control every ring.

No teleport-like travel:
- every scene transfer has a normal route, covert alternative, time, checkpoint, and life-support dependency.

---

## 2. Orbital Context

Working location:
- attached to a medium lattice relay and decommissioned shipyard in stable orbit around a populated frontier moon/planet.
- the relay is strategically useful but not top-tier galactic infrastructure.
- civilian White Dock, academy station, relay control, and old yard are distinct facilities connected by shuttle/tether/service routes.

Local objects:

1. `Lattice Relay K-13` — navigation/communication/traffic synchronization node.
2. `Academy Habitat 13` — rotating school/correction/industrial complex.
3. `White Dock` — civilian repair, cargo, and identity/transport archive facility.
4. `Old Yard` — decommissioned shipyard sections and storage trusses.
5. `Utility/Power Cluster` — reactors, radiators, propellant, recycling.
6. `Neutral Rescue Anchorage` — designated safe approach berth, not permanent Neutral territory.

Relative distances — provisional:

- Academy hub to relay control: 1.8 km structural/tether link.
- Academy external dock to White Dock: 9–14 km depending orbital/tether geometry.
- Academy to Old Yard closest access: 2.5 km external truss; legal route 5–7 km through controlled shuttle path.
- White Dock to relay control: 4–6 km.
- Neutral rescue anchorage from White Dock: 15–30 km safety orbit during conflict.

Travel times:

- normal crew shuttle Academy→White Dock: 8–14 minutes plus docking/checks.
- utility tug: 18–35 minutes.
- maneuver frame external transit: 6–12 minutes with carrier/boost assistance; 15–25 minutes under own conservative propulsion.
- emergency high-output frame transit is possible but consumes major thermal/propellant budget before combat.

Rule:
- a frame arriving quickly may enter battle already hot and fuel-limited.

## 3. Academy Overall Form

Recommended structural layout:
- a 2.4 km central nonrotating axial spine.
- three partial/concentric rotating habitat/industrial rings linked by six radial transfer towers.
- external docks, radiators, tanks, and Old Yard trusses attached to the spine.

Artificial gravity:

- Ring A: radius ~720 m, 0.82–0.88 g.
- Ring B: radius ~680 m, 0.75–0.82 g.
- Ring C industrial: radius ~610 m, 0.58–0.68 g depending segment and load.
- central spine: microgravity/low artificial acceleration.

Rotation:
- roughly 0.95–1.05 rpm depending operating state.

Sensory effect:
- curved horizons in long corridors.
- coriolis discomfort when moving radially/vertically.
- machinery hum and periodic balance corrections.
- emergency rotation changes make familiar routes physically dangerous.

## 5. Ring A — Civic Face

Functions:
- public administration.
- sentence/contract offices.
- visitor court/hearing rooms.
- public clinic and rehabilitation.
- sponsor evaluation rooms.
- Neutral/insurance audit offices.

Security:
- highest visible camera coverage and identity checks.
- clean, bright, quiet, standardized signage.
- few student-only areas; movement requires escort/appointment.

Story uses:
- hearings, contract negotiations, public facade, component auction/legal claims.
- Episode 21–45 inquiry and charter meetings.

Combat vulnerability:
- not built for military defense.
- civilian/visitor population and transparent walls/atriums make heavy weapons unacceptable.
- emergency bulkheads can partition sections but trap people.

Travel:
- Ring B dorm to Ring A hearing: 6–12 minutes official route depending checkpoint.
- covert service route: 15–25 minutes, low gravity transitions, risk of maintenance detection.

## 6. Ring B — Student Habitat

Segments:

- B1 first-year cells/dorms.
- B2 upper-cohort dorms.
- B3 classrooms and libraries.
- B4 dining, ration, market/recreation.
- B5 simulation/training halls.
- B6 counseling/chapel/memorial/community rooms.

Cell layout:
- 5–7 students share linked sleeping area and common sanitation/storage.
- individual lockboxes but limited privacy.
- team penalties and sensors encourage mutual monitoring.

Key opening spaces:

### Classroom / mission dispatch

- ordinary lessons can become instant test calls.
- screens/doors route students to different paths.

### Elevator cluster T2

- official vertical/radial transit.
- mixed passengers, checkpoints, changing gravity.
- Episode 4 trap: floor/button choice is a behavioral test, not magical building.

### Service corridor B2-S

- cable/air/waste maintenance access around habitat shell.
- H-001 knows crew schedules and route bottlenecks.
- not completely hidden; sensors sparse, physical seals logged.

### Dining/ration zone

- real power through food queues, dietary tiers, community exchange.
- social and ordinary-life anchor.

Travel estimates:
- across one sixth of Ring B on foot: 4–7 minutes.
- half-ring walk: 12–18 minutes.
- internal tram: 2–5 minutes plus wait/check.
- service route usually 1.5–3× slower but avoids some checkpoints.

Siege use:
- large student population, rotating ring, fire/air risks.
- evacuation cannot funnel everyone through one transfer tower.

## 7. Ring C — Industrial Works

Segments:

- C1 heavy maintenance and frame parts.
- C2 recycling/smelting and waste processing.
- C3 utility-frame yards.
- C4 electronics/sensor shop.
- C5 food/air/water processing support.
- C6 informal market and worker storage.

Environment:
- lower gravity, hotter/cooler zones, lubricant/metal/ozone/recycling smells.
- noisy vibration and partial visibility.
- protective clothing and shift signals.

Surveillance gaps:
- machinery creates sensor noise.
- old subcontractor networks have unregistered service buses.
- blind spots change with maintenance, not permanent author convenience.

H-002 spaces:

### Shared student workshop

- limited tools, scheduled access, cameras.

### Hidden calibration niche

- not secret laboratory; a disused access bay between old power converters.
- can hide small components/tools, not an entire frame.

### Parts cage

- ownership/serial/contract conflicts physically represented.

Travel:
- Ring B to main workshop: 5–9 minutes official.
- workshop to Dock D through T3/T5: 7–12 minutes.
- covert heavy-part movement requires carts, shifts, forged logs, or 20–40 minutes.

Combat use:
- cover, machinery, civilians, volatile systems.
- disabling power/rotation has station-wide consequences.

## 8. Central Spine

Low/microgravity zones:

### Spine North — Command and Security

- station control, security armory, surveillance, detention.
- hardened and separately powered.

### Spine Mid — Transit Core

- zero-g transfer hub linking ring towers, docks, relay/tethers.
- high traffic; closure isolates rings.

### Spine Medical — Black Ward

- near public medical tower but isolated through controlled zero-g corridor.
- avoids rotating gravity for some neural/patient systems.
- separate data/power links to Helix/Imperial systems.

### Spine South — Power and Life Support

- reactors/energy conversion, coolant, water/air processing connections.
- physical redundancy but central controls.

Security rule:
- no one route reaches Black Ward directly from student dorms.
- access requires public clinic transfer, maintenance path, or external lock.

Travel:
- Ring B to Spine via normal elevator: 3–6 minutes plus checkpoint.
- moving along full 2.4 km spine by rail: 3–5 minutes; handline/zero-g emergency 10–25 minutes.

Combat use:
- microgravity changes frame/person movement.
- projectiles/heat/debris risk systems far beyond target.
- small utility frames and suits more practical than full 10m frame in narrow interior segments.

## 9. Dock D — Academy Military/Frame Area

Functions:
- maneuver-frame bays.
- shuttle and utility craft.
- training locks and external ranges.
- ammunition/tools under separate sponsor ownership.

Capacity:
- 12–16 frame berths, only 8–12 fully serviced simultaneously.
- additional storage/cradles in Old Yard/industrial sections.

07호 location:
- initially at outer low-priority berth or storage cradle near old service trunk.
- hidden lineage is not behind secret door; it is lost in mismatched registration/parts history.

Ownership zones:
- floor markings, locked parts cages, separate software/charging stations for sponsors.
- visually demonstrates fragmented control.

Travel:
- student habitat to bay: 10–18 minutes through training/security route.
- industrial workshop to bay: 7–12 minutes.
- emergency scramble can cut this but requires preauthorized lifts and suit prep.

Frame deployment sequence minimum:

1. pilot medical/identity check: 1–3 min if ready.
2. suit/seat/sync: 3–8 min.
3. power/cooling/tool checks: 3–10 min.
4. lock cycle and external release: 2–5 min.

Even emergency deployment usually 8–15 minutes; not instant summoning.

## 10. Old Yard

Structure:
- skeletal shipyard trusses, storage pods, sealed workshops, detached hull sections.
- partial power and old contractor systems.
- separated from current station safety network.

Access:
- official tug route from Dock D.
- industrial external service locks.
- dangerous maintenance crawl/line route.

What can be hidden:
- frames, large parts, logs, old service keys, tools.

What cannot be hidden easily:
- active high-power reactor use, large crew movement, repeated shuttle traffic.

Hazards:
- debris, radiation pockets, frozen coolant/volatile residue, dead sections, spoofed maps, weak structure.

Narrative use:
- 07 records/components.
- salvage/auction/ownership.
- later siege/infiltration route.
- visual link between academy’s industrial past and military present.

## 11. White Dock

Identity:
- civilian/industrial dock serving relay traffic, cargo transfer, repairs, contractor identity/transport records.
- legally separate from Academy even if Academy provides security contracts.

Population:
- normal permanent/shift workers: 450–700.
- transient ship crews/passengers: 200–900.
- mission-day total: roughly 800–1,400.

Major zones:

1. cargo spines and pressure warehouses.
2. civilian repair arms.
3. identity/transport archive and customs office.
4. worker habitat and food/medical spaces.
5. relay-control interface annex.
6. emergency shelter and rescue locks.
7. school security/training perimeter.

Simplified plan:

White appearance rationale:
- high-reflectivity thermal coating and old repair panels, not aesthetic codename alone.

Why records are there:
- transport, customs, work contracts, medical clearance, cargo/passenger identity authentication intersect.
- correctional transfers can be disguised as contractor/medical movement.

Why Independence attacks:
- authenticated record junction and transport schedule.

Why Academy uses it for mission:
- existing security contract and complex civilian environment valuable for training/data.

Why not simply destroy archive remotely:
- records split among local physical authentication, network hashes, external insurers/authorities.
- destruction removes evidence but creates traffic, insurance, and political crisis.

## 12. Episode 14–20 Battle Geometry

Starting positions:

- P-001/07 team at School Security Berth or external patrol route.
- E-001 enters through Repair Arm/maintenance exterior based on insider map.
- radical escort approaches cargo/archive access.
- Academy control at security berth/remote command.
- civilians distributed in worker habitat, repair arms, cargo spines.
- Neutral rescue vessel outside weapons envelope, 15–30 km.

Key constraints:

- firing through central archive risks decompression/data destruction.
- repair arms create cover and anchor points.
- lattice noise degrades long-range classification.
- white reflective panels create sensor/thermal confusion under emergency heating.
- 07 enters with limited heat/propellant from transit.
- E-001 has better local-map access but receives falsified/changed routes.

Movement scale:
- dock overall span: 1.2–1.8 km.
- frame can cross exposed distance quickly but braking/turning/heat and civilians matter.
- interior archive corridors require dismounted team, suits, drones, or utility frame—not full-size mecha.

Battle layers:

1. external frame maneuver around repair arms.
2. interior record/data operation.
3. civilian decompression/medical response.
4. remote orders and ownership claims.
5. rescue vessel approach and legal custody.

Victory condition:
- preserve civilians and authenticated fragments while preventing single-faction control.
- enemy destruction is not primary.

## 13. Episode 85–100 Siege Geometry

Simultaneous fronts:

### Front 1 — Ring B / students

- protect dorms, prevent forced transfer, move noncombat students.

### Front 2 — Ring C / power and workshops

- prevent remote lockout, maintain life support, distribute tools/frames.

### Front 3 — Spine / Black Ward and records

- protect patients/AI witness, stop deletion/seizure.

### Front 4 — Dock D / external attack

- stop faction transport/boarding forces and preserve escape/rescue access.

### Front 5 — Ring A / legal/public broadcast

- charter negotiation, evidence, visitors, officials, public legitimacy.

Why P-001 cannot solve all:
- travel between fronts takes 5–20 minutes under normal conditions, longer during shutdown/rotation change.
- 07 cannot enter many interior corridors.
- command communications may fragment.
- choosing one front means another actor owns decisions elsewhere.

Final emergency:
- central command can synchronize rotation, power, life support, doors, identity, frames quickly.
- distributed plan uses segmented authority and manual fallbacks, slower and riskier.
