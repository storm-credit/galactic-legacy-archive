## 4. Staff Ratios and Constraints

Instructor ratio:
- approximately 1 instructor per 7 active students overall, but many are technical/classroom rather than trusted mentors.

Direct cell supervisors:
- 45–60 field/training officers for 180–220 operational cells.
- each supervises 3–5 cells, creating selective protection and information gaps.

Security ratio:
- one security worker per 6 students, but most guard infrastructure/data rather than personally watch students.

Medical ratio:
- one medical/research staff per 6–7 students/resident patients.
- only 18–28 are physician/surgeon-equivalent; many are technicians, nurses, neural monitors, therapists, researchers.

Frame maintenance:
- school possesses/leases 44–58 maneuver/industrial training frames.
- 20–28 combat-capable at any moment.
- 12–18 industrial/rescue.
- 8–12 cannibalized/storage/experimental.
- safe sustained operation requires 6–10 maintenance labor-hours per frame training hour; older mixed frames can require 12–20.

Implication:
- H-002 cannot maintain 07호 alone.
- tool access, worker cooperation, parts scheduling, and documentation are real resources.

## 6. Student Compensation and Debt

Base student labor credit:
- 100 nominal units per month for accounting reference.

Typical allocations:
- 25–35 living/food/equipment charge.
- 15–30 family/protector debt or support.
- 10–25 medical/insurance.
- 10–25 sentence reduction reserve.
- 5–20 discretionary account.

Common result:
- many students do not receive spendable cash despite productive labor.
- successful missions can produce large credits but equipment damage and treatment create countercharges.

Common starting debt equivalent:
- 18–60 months of base credits.

High-risk family/medical debt:
- 80–200 months, effectively impossible without sponsorship or mission bonus.

Legal constraint:
- debt cannot formally increase without documented service/treatment/equipment cost.
- inflated valuations, joint liability, and proprietary treatment are the abuse mechanisms.

Student mutual fund feasibility:
- 50–100 members contributing 3–5 discretionary units can support legal filing, temporary family ration, or small guarantee—not purchase freedom.

## 7. Official Outcome Statistics and Propaganda

Publicly advertised:

- 4% “medical or voluntary transfer.”
- less than 1% annual fatal incident.

Reality behind 78%:
- includes long-term military/corporate contracts, extended debt service, research continuation, and conditional citizenship.
- fully free civilian exit estimated 18–27% of cohort.

Why students still hope:
- outcomes are often better than stateless labor, prison, starvation, or local militia.
- alumni visibly achieve rank, income, treatment, and citizenship.
- success is real but uneven and purchased through control.

## 8. Jurisdiction Layers

### Layer 1 — Personal/custodial jurisdiction

- academy/correction authority controls student sentence and daily custody.
- cannot transfer to research/military/corporate ownership without documented contract/order.

### Layer 2 — Medical custody

- licensed neutral medical personnel may temporarily prevent transfer when movement creates immediate health risk.
- standard initial protection: 12 hours emergency, extendable to 72 hours with documented condition and notification.
- longer protection requires neutral court/claims body, host authority, or guardian agreement.

### Layer 3 — Military/security hold

- Imperial Intelligence or military authority can seize evidence/person under national-security warrant.
- covert action without warrant risks jurisdiction scandal and admissibility loss.

### Layer 4 — Property/contract claim

- Helix can claim parts, data rights, unpaid equipment, or injunction against use.
- cannot legally own a person, but can own contract rights, training debt, treatment access, and data licenses.

### Layer 5 — Neutral rescue/ship jurisdiction

- inside active neutral medical deck, no combat/arrest without captain/medical officer approval except immediate threat.
- does not erase underlying sentence or property claim.

### Layer 6 — Node/station sovereign law

- academy station falls under Imperial correction law plus node safety law and external mission contracts.
- White Dock may have separate civilian/industrial authority, creating contested jurisdiction.

## 10. Episode 100 Temporary Charter Plausibility

Working legal object:
- `제13학원 임시공동운영·보호헌장`.

Duration:
- 180 days renewable, with 30/90-day audits.

Authority allocation:

### Academy/correction authority
- daily education, sentence administration, internal discipline within revised rules.

### Student/staff assembly
- consultative vote and limited veto over black missions, mass transfer, data release, family-contract changes.
- initially 20–30 elected/selected representatives, not direct democracy for every tactical decision.

### Neutral trustee/auditor
- medical minimums, casualty/evidence preservation, transfer review, insurance/claims.

### Imperial/state observer
- national-security and legacy-material oversight, but no unilateral student seizure without emergency record.

### Industrial partners
- equipment, treatment, training and claims under transparent contract/audit; no exclusive school ownership.

### External mission board
- approves live operations with risk classification, medical/training sign-off, student-cell consent/contract, and emergency exceptions.

Student rights added:
- informed mission-risk summary.
- independent medical stop/appeal.
- contract copy and debt ledger access.
- representative counsel in transfer/discipline.
- limited exit/alternative-placement application.
- data access/correction and consent tiers.

Limits:
- sentences and debts remain.
- state can revoke charter in major security crisis.
- students may still choose/need dangerous missions.
- funding and enforcement remain weak.

Why not permanent solution:
- provisional emergency bargain between mutually blocking actors.
- political factions plan to capture/reshape it.
- other academies and frontier institutions are unaffected.

## 11. Security and Surveillance Capacity

Cameras/sensors:
- high coverage in public corridors, docks, classrooms, medical/security areas.
- weaker in old industrial/service zones due obsolete systems, maintenance, privacy rules, deliberate gaps.

Human monitoring:
- impossible to watch all streams continuously.
- AI filters classify anomalies but can be spoofed/bias-limited.

Student devices:
- restricted local communicators; external traffic relayed/logged.
- physical notes, tool tags, maintenance buses, and line-of-sight links create covert channels.

After D-001:
- service paths monitored more heavily.
- coercion shifts to contracts/hidden transfers instead of obvious tests.

Why revolt does not immediately succeed:
- students divided by goals, debts, health, families, factions, language, cohort.
- life support and transport are centralized.
- some staff/students benefit or hope to graduate.
- outside powers would seize the facility or scatter students.

## 12. Mission Rate

Per operational week:
- 40–60 classroom simulations across school.
- 15–25 facility/industrial exercises.
- 8–14 live-frame training sorties.
- 3–7 external industrial/rescue/security contracts.
- 0–2 covert/live high-risk missions disguised or compartmented.

Per student:
- first-year: one significant field/facility mission every 2–4 weeks.
- advanced: one external operation every 3–8 weeks depending specialty.
- combat-selected cell: can be overused at 1 mission per 1–2 weeks, causing fatigue/injury.

Narrative rule:
- the protagonist team’s high event density is an anomaly caused by value, crisis, and faction attention; other teams’ schedules show consequences.

## 13. Casualty and Investigation Response

1 death:
- internal inquiry, family/insurance notice, training review; can be minimized.

3–5 linked deaths:
- mission suspension, external insurer or military review likely.

10+ deaths or civilian involvement:
- major political/contract crisis; cannot be quietly ignored if multiple institutions have records.

Data manipulation needed to hide White Dock original:
- split deaths across industrial accident, enemy attack, delayed medical transfer, and unregistered contractor categories.
- pressure witnesses and control transport/medical logs.

Changed timeline:
- Neutral evidence and distributed hashes make this split-cover harder, explaining public inquiry and multi-party conflict.

## 14. Red-Team Checks

### Why not use regular cadets?

- regular cadets are politically/financially protected and costly.
- correctional students provide edge-case neural data, labor, and legal deniability.
- regular forces still exist and outperform them in many tasks.

### Why invest in disposable youth?

- they are not disposable economically; institutions seek data, labor, and future contracts.
- risk is externalized to family debt, insurance, and legal categories.
- mortality kept high enough for danger but low enough for investment logic.

### Why does Neutral law matter to Empire?

- neutral routes, insurance, rescue, credit, and evidence are needed across borders.
- ignoring it imposes trade, route, hostage, and legitimacy costs—not magical moral authority.

### Why can the charter exist?

- no faction can seize the school cheaply after public multi-party crisis.
- charter is a temporary mutual-veto bargain, underfunded and reversible.
